## Midday News

After making a moderate impact in some areas of West Bengal, Severe cyclonic storm Remal has weakened into a cyclonic storm. The Met department today said that the cyclonic storm is expected to weaken further in the day. The state administration continues to be on high alert following forecast of heavy to very heavy rainfall. Officials said, there had been reports of the uprooting of several trees and the collapse of mud houses from certain coastal belts in the state. Coastal areas of South and North 24 Parganas, Sunderban, Kolkata, Howrah and Hooghly are receiving heavy rainfall. More from our correspondent:

“Severe cyclonic storm Remal played havoc in North 24 Parganas and South 24 Parganas in West Bengal. Rain continues in several districts of South Bengal, including Kolkata. Some areas are still waterlogged and without electricity. Works are on to remove the uprooted trees and drain out excess water from the affected areas. One old woman of 80 years died at Mausuni island of South 24 district last night when a tree fell on her mud-house. Another person died in Kolkata last night when a part of a building collapsed and fell on him. However, some local train services have been restored and air services at Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose international airport has been resumed. Campaigning by the political leaders for the last phase of Lok Sabha election in the state have also been affected. Meanwhile, some parts of North Bengal are witnessing rainfall. SUSMITA MANDAL, WITH KALYAN LAHA FOR AKASHVANI NEWS, KOLKATA.”

Meanwhile, relief and rescue operations are in full swing in affected areas. Officials said, over one lakh people have been evacuated from vulnerable areas, especially in the coastal belts. Train services of Sealdah have been restored in South section and Barasat – Hasnabad line. Howrah – Amta local has been cancelled in the Howrah division. The truncated Metro Rail service is being operated between Dakshineswar and Girish Park, as well as between Mahanayak Uttam Kumar and Kabi Subhash. The flight services at Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose International Airport have also resumed.

Talking to media, DIG of National Disaster Response Force (NDRF), Mohsen Shahidi said, the wind speed which was earlier about 110 to 120 kilometres per hour during landfall, now has come down to 60 to 70 kilometres per hour. Mr Shahidi said, it is expected that by evening today, this will further change into a deep depression. The DIG said, restoration work is in progress and 14 teams of NDRF are deployed in major affected areas in West Bengal and six SDRF teams are also deployed to assist.

The Indian Coast Guard has also deployed its assets for any emergency. Ports, Railways and Highways have been kept on high vigil to avoid any untoward incident.

Meanwhile, the sea conditions off the coast of northern Odisha have become rough to very rough with high waves and strong winds. The India Meteorological Department has predicted thunderstorms and gusty winds with moderate rain in some areas of Balasore, Bhadrak, Kendrapara and Jagatsinghpur districts of the state. Many areas in these districts experienced heavy rain yesterday under the impact of cyclonic storm Remal. However, there are no reports of any damage due to rains or thunderstorms.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi yesterday chaired a meeting in New Delhi to review preparedness for cyclone “Remal” over North Bay of Bengal. IMD is also extending information support to Bangladesh with regular updates.
