## Morning News

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has said the Cabinet accepting the recommendations of the High-Level Committee on Simultaneous Elections is an important step towards making Indian democracy even more vibrant and participative. In a social media post, Mr Modi complimented former President Ram Nath Kovind for spearheading this effort and consulting a wide range of stakeholders.

Earlier yesterday, Union Cabinet headed by the Prime Minister approved the proposal for ‘one nation, one election’ as recommended by the panel headed by Mr Kovind. After the Cabinet meeting, Information and Broadcasting Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said the high-level committee under the chairmanship of the former President held extensive deliberations with stakeholders, including political parties and experts. He said it will be implemented in two phases.

Union Home Minister Amit Shah said India has taken a giant stride towards landmark electoral reforms with the Cabinet’s decision approving One Nation One Election. In a social media post, he said, this reflects the Prime Minister’s iron will to bolster our democracy through clean and financially efficient elections and accelerate economic growth through more productive allocation of resources.

BJP President and Union Health Minister JP Nadda said the Cabinet’s decision aims to streamline the electoral process and ensure better governance. He said, the current system of holding elections at different times across states hampers development efforts and burdens the national treasury. Mr Nadda said, adopting “One Nation, One Election” will help lower election-related expenses and financial burden on the government.

Jammu and Kashmir has recorded the highest voter turnout in last 35 years, with over 61 percent polling in the first phase of assembly elections yesterday. Voting was peaceful across all 24 Assembly Constituencies spanning seven districts of Pulwama, Shopian, Kulgam, Kishtwar, Anantnag, Ramban, and Doda.

The Election Commission noted that youth and women voters shined brightly at the polling stations, a testament to the deepening embrace of democracy in Jammu and Kashmir. It added that their enthusiasm to cast their votes is an emphatic response in favor of the ballot against boycotts and violence. Long queues of voters at the polling stations showcased to the world the deep trust and confidence of the people of J&K in the democratic exercise. Along with webcasting in all polling stations, 32 Central Observers kept a vigilant eye on the election process on the ground, ensuring no disruptions. Kashmiri migrant voters were also empowered to exercise their franchise through 24 Special Polling Stations set up in Jammu, Udhampur, and Delhi. Home-voting facilities were introduced for the first time in assembly elections in J&K.

J&K Lieutenant Governor Manoj Sinha has extended hearty congratulations to the voters for the record turnout in the first phase of assembly elections. In a social media post, he said, the enthusiastic participation of voters is a testament to the strength of Indian democracy and people’s faith in democratic values. Mr Sinha said, the record polling in a peaceful, free and fair election shows the strength of the Indian democracy.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi will address two election rallies in Jammu and Kashmir today. Mr Modi’s first rally will be in Srinagar this morning, while the second one will be held at Katra, the Base Camp of Shri Mata Vaishno Devi Shrine in the afternoon.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi is arriving on a day’s visit to Srinagar today. Upon reaching Srinagar, Mr Modi is scheduled to address a big election rally at Sher-e-Kashmir Cricket Stadium in Sonwar to encourage the people especially the youth of the Union Territory to exercise their right to franchise in the remaining two phases of J&K Assembly elections scheduled to be held on 25th of September and on 1st of October. Meanwhile, the area around the venue has been put under a multi-layer security blanket to ensure smooth visit of Prime Minister Modi. This is Sunil Koul for Akashvani News from Srinagar.

A four day long mega event World Food India 2024 will begin today at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi. The event promises to be a major convergence of innovation, technology, and sustainability in the food processing sector. The US central bank, the Federal Reserve, has cut interest rates for the first time in over four years. It has reduced the key lending rate by 0.5 percentage points to a range of 4.75 to 5 percent. The US Federal Reserve announced its sixth policy decision for 2024 last night after a two-day Federal Open Market Committee meeting.

Federal Chair Jerome Powell stated that this significant move was necessary as inflation eases and concerns about the job market grow.

The rate cut is expected to provide relief to US borrowers who have faced the highest interest rates in more than two decades.

In Lebanon, at least 32 people have been killed and thousands injured in a series of explosions during the past two days. The blasts involved communication devices, some of which are used by militant group Hezbollah. According to the country’s health ministry, the death toll in yesterday’s walkie-talkie explosions in Lebanon has risen to at least 20, with more than 450 injured. Earlier on Tuesday, pager blasts killed at least 12 people and wounded about 3000 others.

The latest explosions occurred near a large crowd gathered for the funerals of victims from the earlier blasts.

The first round of explosions began in Beirut and several other areas at approximately 3:45 PM local time on Tuesday, followed by another wave around 5 PM on Wednesday.

Canada has announced a further reduction of international students to manage the volume of temporary resident arrivals. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada said in 2025 new international student study-permits will be reduced by 10 per cent from the 2024 target of 4.85 lakh. It said, the number of study permits issued in 2026 will remain the same as in 2025. This announcement followed a previous measure back in January when the federal government said it will approve about 3.6 lakh undergraduate study permits in 2024, a 35 per cent reduction from the nearly 5.6 lakh issued in 2023.

The first Test of the two-match series between India and Bangladesh will begin today at MA Chidambaram Stadium in Chennai. This will be the first Test series that India will play after Gautam Gambhir took over as the India coach.

Bangladesh enter the series with high confidence after a historic 2-0 clean sweep of Pakistan on its home soil – its first-ever against the rival. On the other hand, India’s last Test assignment was a convincing 4-1 series win over England at home earlier this year.

In China Open 2024 Badminton tournament, India’s Malvika Bansod stunned world No. 7 and Paris Olympics bronze medallist Gregoria Mariska Tunjung in the women’s singles opening round in Changzhou yesterday. Malvika defeated the Indonesian shuttler by 26-24, 21-19. Malvika will next face Kristy Gilmour of Scotland, this morning.

The World Health Organization, WHO today declared Jordan as the first country in the world to eliminate leprosy, marking a significant milestone in global public health efforts. WHO said that Jordan has not reported any autochthonous cases of leprosy for over two decades. The declaration was made after commissioning an independent team to assess this situation.

Congratulating Jordan on this milestone, WHO Director-General, Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said, Leprosy has afflicted humanity for millennia, but efforts have been made to stop its transmission and freeing individuals, families, and communities from its suffering and stigma country-by-country.
