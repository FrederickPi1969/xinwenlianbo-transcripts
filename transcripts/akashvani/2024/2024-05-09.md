## Midday News

Campaigning for Lok Sabha elections is in full swing. Voting in the fourth phase will take place on the 13th of this month in 96 seats spread across ten states and a union territory. As many as 1,717 candidates are in the fray for this phase.

BJP Senior Leader and Union Minister, Amit Shah today said the present election is between Modi’s development guarantee and Rahul Gandhi’s own family welfare guarantee. He appealed to give one opportunity to BJP by providing it double digit seats from the state.

Addressing an election rally in support of BJP candidate, Dr Boora Narsaiah Goud in Bhongir Lok Sabha constituency, he alleged that the BRS, which ruled the state for a decade, took care of its personal interests, while the Congress government, which got public mandate for five years, has made the state as an ATM.  Mr Shah alleged that MIM, BRS and Congress have formed a triangle for appeasement politics in the state.

Meanwhile, Congress senior leader Rahul Gandhi will be addressing election rallies at Narsapur and Saroornagar later this evening. BRS chief K. Chandrasekhar Rao will be holding a roadshow in Karimnagar.

As mercury levels skyrocket across the nation, India finds itself in the grip of an unprecedented heat wave, with temperatures soaring to alarming heights. April 2024 has etched its name in history as the warmest April globally since at least 1940. India is experiencing above-normal temperatures in vast swathes of the country. More on this from our correspondent:

Eastern and northeastern India have borne the brunt of the scorching heat, with April registering unparalleled temperatures. As per India Meteorological Department, Minimum temperatures surged 1.78 degrees Celsius above normal, while mean temperatures soared a staggering two degrees higher than historical averages, last month. Now while the heatwave conditions have been abated in Eastern India due to rainfalls, it has gripped parts of Western India including Gujarat and Rajasthan. IMD has cautioned that that even as El Niño weakens and La Niña sets in the temperatures may remain elevated, intensifying the already sweltering conditions. Talking to Akashvani News, Senior IMD scientist, R.K. Jenamani said that for the next five to seven days northwest and central India will experience above 40 degrees temperature.

President Droupadi Murmu today presented the Padma Awards at the second Civil Investiture Ceremony at Rashtrapati Bhavan in New Delhi.

The President conferred Padma Vibhushan upon renowned artist Dr. Vyjayantimala Bali, and Telegu film actor Konidela Chiranjeevi. The first woman Supreme Court Judge in Asia, M Fathima Beevi was also presented with Padma Bhushan posthumously. Renowned cardiologist Dr. Ashwin Balachand Mehta, and veteran Political Leader O. Rajagopal were also given the third highest civilian honour. Togdan Rinpoche was also given Padma Bhushan posthumously for his exemplary contribution to the field of Spiritualism.

Padma Shri was conferred upon Joshna Chinappa for her contribution to the field of sports. K Chellammal received the Padma Shri award in the field of agriculture. Hindustani classical vocalist Som Datt Battu and sculptor A Velu Ananda Chari also received Padma Shri amongst others.

“Padma Awards – one of the highest civilian Awards of the country, are conferred in three categories – Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan and Padma Shri. The Awards are given in various disciplines and fields of activities including art, social work, public affairs, science and engineering, trade and industry, medicine, literature and education, sports, and civil service. Suparna Saikia, Akashwani News, Delhi.”

India has seen the second most significant decline of 7.82 per cent in the majority population, only next to Myanmar, within the immediate South Asian neighbourhood from 1950 to 2015. A working paper of the Economic Advisory Council to the Prime Minister, EAC PM, said India has witnessed an increase in the share of Minority groups – Muslim, Christian, Buddhist and Sikh and a decline in the share of Jain and Parsis. The PM panel said the Hindu population share was 84.68 per cent in 1950, which came down to 78.06 per cent in 2015. Between 1950 and 2015, the Muslim population increased by 43.15 per cent, and the Christian population rose by 5.38 per cent.
