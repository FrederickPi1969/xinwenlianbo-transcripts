The Election Commission has said that one thousand 210 candidates are in fray for the second phase of Lok Sabha polls across 13 States and UTs. A total of 2633 nominations were filed for 88 constituencies. After the scrutiny, 1428 nominations were found to be valid. Yesterday was the last date for withdrawal of candidature. Voting for this phase will be held on the 26th of this month.

In second phase, Kerala has a maximum of 500 nominations from 20 Parliamentary Constituencies, followed by 491 nominations from 14 Constituencies in Karnataka. Minimum of 14 nominations were received from one constituency in Tripura. Nanded Parliamentary Constituency in Maharashtra received a maximum of 92 nominations.

The Election Commission has directed the Union Home Ministry to deploy 100 more companies of Central Armed Paramilitary Forces CAPF in West Bengal to conduct free and fair Lok Sabha elections. Sources said, 55 companies of Central Reserve Police Force and 45 companies of Border Security forces are being deployed by the Home Ministry on the instructions of the Commission. The sources said, officials have been directed to complete the deployment of an extra 100 companies of CAPF in West Bengal on or before the 15th of this month.

The Election Commission, which is set to hold the biggest democratic exercise on earth, has adopted people friendly technology to make things easy for the voters.

Understanding the candidates is similar to having a map before starting a journey. It guides voters in selecting leaders who share their vision and values. In addition to this, being aware of any past legal troubles or controversies empowers voters to hold candidates accountable. The Election Commission has developed an app called Know Your Candidate or KYC, which tells voter if a candidate has ever been in trouble with the law. It’s available on both Android and iPhones and is user-friendly. Voters just need to select the type of election and the name of the area. Then, user can see a list of candidates or search for a specific one. The app will show if the candidate has any criminal cases against them, details about the cases, and their status. It’s a tool for voters to learn more about the people who want to represent them and make better choices when they vote. Durgesh Bhadauria Akashvani News, New Delhi.

The Delhi High Court today dismissed the plea moved by Chief Minister Arvind Kejriwal, challenging his arrest by the Enforcement Directorate (ED) in the money laundering case related to the alleged liquor policy scam case, as well as the remand order passed by the trial court sending him to ED custody.

Justice Swarana Kanta Sharma upheld his arrest and subsequent remand holding that ED was able to place enough material, statements of approvers and AAP’s own candidate stating that Kejriwal was given money for Goa elections.

On Kejriwal’s arguments challenging the timing of arrest ahead of General elections, the Court held that challenging the timing of arrest, in the absence of any mala fide on the part of the ED, is not sustainable.

Mr. Kejriwal is currently in judicial custody. He was arrested on the night of March the 21.

The National Investigation Agency (NIA) has arrested the key accused, identified as Harwinder Singh in the Attari drug seizure case. Indian Customs had seized over 102 kilograms of heroin worth seven hundred crore rupees in April 2022. The case relates to a major conspiracy hatched by the international drug cartel to circulate drugs in India through various distributors and channelize the proceeds to the foreign-based masterminds.

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) has provisionally attached one thousand 807 acres of land worth over 52 crore rupees in a bank loan fraud case of Punjab and Maharashtra Co-operative Bank. ED has initiated investigation on the basis of an FIR registered by Mumbai Police against the then directors of Punjab and Maharashtra Co-operative Bank Joy Thomas, Waryam Singh and other unknown persons.

Director of NASA’s Goddard Institute for Space Studies, Gavin Schmidt has said, if temperatures do not fall by the end of the year, Climate change could move “into uncharted territory”. He said, if record breaking temperatures are witnessed in the North Atlantic or elsewhere by the end of the summer this year, then the weather system will move into uncharted territory.

The warning came as new data showed that March 2024 was the warmest month on record, extending the run of monthly temperature records to ten in a row since June last year.
A weather system called El Nino and human caused climate change are behind some of the recent heat.

The European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) in France today delivered a landmark ruling and found that Switzerland’s failure to adequately tackle the climate crisis is a violation of human rights.

The court ruled in favour of a group of elderly women from Switzerland, who argued that the Swiss government’s inadequate efforts to combat climate change put them at risk of dying during heatwaves.

The case was brought by more than 2,000 Swiss women the majority of whom are in their 70s.

The National Green Hydrogen mission was launched in January last year with an outlay of 19 thousand 744 crore rupees up to the financial year 2029-30.
