Prime Minister Narendra Modi has said the decision to implement One Rank One Pension (OROP) was a significant step towards addressing the long-standing demand and reaffirming the nation’s gratitude to its heroes. As the scheme completed 10 years today, in a social media post, the prime minister said, the initiative was a tribute to the courage and sacrifices of the veterans and ex-service personnel who dedicated their lives to protect the nation. He said, over the decade, lakhs of pensioners and pensioner families have benefited from the landmark initiative. Prime Minister Modi said, beyond the numbers, OROP represents the government’s commitment to the well-being of the armed forces.

OROP is a transformative scheme implemented by the Government to ensure fair and equal pension payments for Ex-servicemen. The scheme ensures that veterans who retire at the same rank and with the same length of service receive equal pensions, regardless of their retirement date. The Ministry of Defence said the total financial implication for OROP scheme in the current financial year is over Four thousand 468 crore rupees. It said, over 895 crore rupees has been disbursed to Armed Forces pensioners till 30th September, this year.

The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change has doubled penalties against stubble burning. The government notified the new rules under, Commission for Air Quality Management in National Capital Region and Adjoining Areas (Imposition, Collection and Utilization of Environmental Compensation for Stubble Burning) Amendment Rules, 2024 yesterday.

As per the new rules, farmers with less than two acres of land holding will have to pay five thousand rupees now which was earlier two thousand five hundred rupees. The farmers having an area of land of two to five acres will have to pay an environmental compensation of ten thousand rupees per incidence. Those farmers who hold land more than five acres have to pay an environmental compensation of thirty thousand rupees. Earlier, the compensation was five thousand for those with two to five acres and 15 thousand for those with more than five acres for burning stubble.

The Supreme Court today held that recruitment rules for appointments in government jobs cannot be changed midway unless it is prescribed.

A five-judge bench headed by Chief Justice DY Chandrachud held that ‘rules of the game’ once set before the start of the recruitment process cannot be tinkered with midway. The bench also said that the selection rules should not be arbitrary and should be in accordance with Article 14 of the Constitution.

The top court unanimously held that transparency and non-discrimination should be the hallmarks of the public recruitment process and candidates should not be taken by surprise by change of rules midway.
