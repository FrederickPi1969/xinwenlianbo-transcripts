President Droupadi Murmu will attend the First Asian Buddhist Summit in New Delhi today. This two-day summit, beginning today, will celebrate the rich heritage of Buddhism. It will feature esteemed Sangha leaders, scholars, and practitioners from across the continent. The theme of the summit is “The Role of Buddha Dhamma in Strengthening Asia.”

The Supreme Court today upheld the constitutional validity of the Uttar Pradesh Board of Madarsa Education Act, 2004. A bench comprising Chief Justice of India DY Chandrachud, and Justices JB Pardiwala and Manoj Misra gave the verdict against the Allahabad High Court’s March 22 judgement that declared the Act as “unconstitutional” and violative of the principles of secularism. The Apex Court said,  the statute can be struck down if a state lacks legislative competence. The ruling means that madarsas would continue to function in Uttar Pradesh with the state regulating the education standards.

In another decision today, the Supreme Court held in a majority ruling that not all private properties are part of the “material resources of the community” and that the State must redistribute under Article 39(b) of the Constitution. However, it noted that some private properties could fall under this category if they are significant and serve the community. The nine-judge bench, led by Chief Justice of India DY Chandrachud, included Justices Hrishikesh Roy, B.V. Nagarathna, Sudhanshu Dhulia, and others. The 8:1 majority verdict was authored by the CJI, with Justice Nagarathna partially agreeing and Justice Dhulia dissenting.
