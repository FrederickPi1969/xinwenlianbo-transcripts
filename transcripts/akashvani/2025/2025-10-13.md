The Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences for 2025 has been awarded to Joel Mokyr, Philippe Aghion, and Peter Howitt for their groundbreaking work on explaining innovation-driven economic growth. We have a report:

Dutch-born American-Israeli economic historian Joel Mokyr from Northwestern University in US has played a crucial role in identifying the prerequisites for sustained growth through technological progress. He used historical evidence to understand how societies moved from stagnation to self-sustaining economic growth. On the other hand, French economist Philippe Aghion of the Collge de France and Canada born Peter Howitt of Brown University were honoured for developing the influential theory of sustained growth through creative destruction. Their 1992 paper presented a model showing how economic growth occurs through a cycle in which new innovations replace old technologies and products. The Nobel committee said the trio’s research has helped economists and policymakers better understand the roots of economic progress. Shagun Chopra, News Desk.
