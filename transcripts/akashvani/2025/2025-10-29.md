## Morning News

Severe cyclonic storm Montha crossed the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between Machilipatnam and Kalingapatnam last night, clocking wind speed of 90 to 100 kilometres per hour. India Meteorological Department (IMD) said, Montha has weakened after making landfall. The storm triggered very heavy rainfall in Andhra Pradesh, which has damaged 43 thousand hectares of crops in the state.

Cyclone Montha caused major damage in Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Visakhapatnam, Anakapalle, Nellore, Konaseema, and Kakinada districts. One person died in Konaseema after a tree fell in the strong winds. More than 38,000 hectares of crops and 1.38 lakh hectares of horticulture fields were destroyed. Roads, power lines, and beach roads were badly affected.

Over 76,000 people were moved to safe shelters. The government set up more than 3,000 relief centres and 200 medical camps. 11 NDRF and 12 SDRF teams were on the ground for rescue and repair works. Control rooms in 54 divisions are monitoring the situation round the clock.

Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu is supervising relief operations through the Real-Time Governance Society. The government sent over 3.6 crore alert messages and arranged food, water, and medical help for evacuees. The IMD has warned of more heavy rain and urged people in low-lying areas to stay alert.Allu Murali, Akashvani News, Vijayawada.

In Odisha, cyclone Montha’s outer bands brought torrential rain and winds through southern districts of the state including Gajapati and Ganjam. A large number of trees were toppled and few roads were blocked under the impact of cyclone Montha. However, there were no reports of any casualty and major damages.

As cyclone Montha roared ashore on the Andhra Pradesh coast last night, it has spared Odisha with no reports of any major damages in the southern districts of the state as expected before. Cyclone Montha has weakened into a cyclonic storm after making a landfall last night in Andhra Pradesh coast following which it is expected to bring heavy rainfall in southern Odisha today as the system moves inland. The Bhubaneswar Indian Meteorological Department office has said rains will taper off by Friday, with lingering showers till tomorrow in interior Odisha. Meanwhile, continuous rain has caused landslides in several places of Gajapati, Malkangiri and Rayagada districts of Odisha. The district administrations have cleared most of those areas this morning. Deputy Chief Minister and Agriculture Minister of Odisha KV Singh Deo has said cyclone Montha has done extensive damage to crops and promised aid once reports were received within a week. Prakash Dash, Deputy Director (News), Akashvani, Bhubaneswar.

In Tamil Nadu, Regional Met office has said that there will be no rains in the state. Fishing activity will resume after announcement from the Met Department.

After three days of gloomy weather , it is bright and sunny in Chennai and surrounding areas. The people living in the low lying areas sheltered in the camps will be returning to their homes after a medical check up at the camps. Schools are to resume their normal days from today. Though the monsoon season is in place, it will be a temporary break from rains for now.Joy,Akashvani,Chennai.

The GST Bachat Utsav kicked off on the 22nd September as the Narendra Modi government slashed GST rates in many sectors across the country. Today, our correspondent brings a report on how the reduced GST rates on tourism sector are benefiting citizens.

The next-generation GST reforms have provided relief to the tourism sector by reducing tax burdens and making travel, accommodation, and cultural experiences more affordable for citizens.  GST on hotel stays priced at under seven thousand five hundred rupees per night has been reduced from 12 percent to 5 percent, making accommodation more accessible. The step has boosted weekend travel, pilgrimage circuits, heritage tourism, and eco-tourism. Furthermore, it has encouraged investment in hotels and homestays, creating jobs and improving infrastructure. Meanwhile, the GST rate on buses with a seating capacity of more than 10 persons has been reduced from 28 percent to 18 percent, lowering public transport costs. These reforms have reduced the upfront cost of buses and minibuses, making them more accessible for fleet operators and tour providers.  These reforms are aimed at boosting domestic and foreign tourist inflows, promoting and supporting sustainable tourism growth. By fostering greater accessibility, this move will also generate significant economic growth and promote India’s image globally as a vibrant and inclusive destination for traveling.
