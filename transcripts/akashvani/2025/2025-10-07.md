GST Bachat Utsav, which was announced by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, and commenced on the 22nd of last month, is benefiting all sections of the society. Today, our correspondent brings a report on the GST reforms that will boost the economy of Maharashtra.

“An economy with strong agriculture, industry, and services sectors, Maharashtra is set to gain significantly from the recent GST reforms.  The new GST reform is a major relief for Maharashtra’s farmers, craftsmen, and small businesses. Under the revamped tax structure many goods in the state will now attract just 5 percent GST instead of the earlier 12 percent tax slab, making them more affordable . This move is particularly meaningful for Maharashtra’s sugar belt, where refined sugar will become 6-7 percent cheaper, benefitting more than 50 lakh sugarcane farmers and 2 lakh mill workers. In the coastal and Konkan regions, processed fish and marine products are reduced from 12 percent to 5 percent, providing over 2 lakh fishermen greater market edge. The handloom, textile, and craft sectors also stand to gain greatly. Industries centred in Ichalkaranji, Solapur, and Paithan along with traditional crafts like Kolhapuri chappals, Warli paintings, and Paithani sarees will benefit from the reduced GST rate of 5 percent on fabrics, leather, and handicraft goods. Shivang Misra, Akashvani News, Delhi.”

In Manipur, Mera Hou Chongba festival, which symbolises Hill-valley unity, is being celebrated today. Village chiefs of different indigenous tribal villages of hill districts have already reached Manipur Royal Palace in Imphal to participate in the festival. Manipur titular King and MP (Rajya Sabha) Maharaja Sanajaoba Leishemba formally received them.

“Mera Hou Chongba festival is celebrates in Manipur on 15th lunar day of Mera month of the Meetei calendar every year. Manipur titular King- Maharaja Sanajaoba Leishemba will lead a ritual march involving tribal village chiefs from Manipur Royal Palace to the historic Kangla where traditional rituals including Mera Men Tongba, Yenkhong Tamba, exchange of gifts among the tribal village chiefs and valley people would be held. The festival will culminate with showcase of cultural dances and a grand feast marking the brotherhood among different tribes in the state. Mera Hou Chongba is only festival in Manipur in which all indigenous communities participate and it has a significant important in the unity and communal harmony of Manipur. Three US-based scientists, John Clarke, Michel Devoret and John Martinis have been awarded the 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics for groundbreaking experiments involving a chip that demonstrated quantum mechanical effects on a macroscopic-or human-scale-level. We have a report:

“The work of scientists John Clarke, Michel Devoret, and John Martinis has paved the way for advancements in next-generation of quantum technologies, including quantum computers, quantum sensors, and quantum cryptography. The Nobel winners carried out experiments in the mid-1980s with an electronic circuit built of superconductors and demonstrated that quantum mechanical properties could be made concrete on a much larger, macroscopic scale. Quantum mechanics describes how matter behaves differently on incredibly small scales. Their experiments demonstrated quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electrical circuit large enough to be held by hand. The trio are affiliated with the University of California and Yale University. The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the trio a prize of 11 million Swedish kronor. Experts hailed the achievement as a major step in the ongoing development of quantum technologies that could transform digital communications and computing worldwide. This continues the long tradition of physics breakthroughs shaping modern technology and deepening scientific understanding. Abhineet Shukla from News Desk”
