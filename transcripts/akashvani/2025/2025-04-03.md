## Morning News

The Lok Sabha has passed the Waqf (Amendment) Bill, 2025 negating the amendments moved by opposition. Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla announced that the Bill was cleared by the House with 288 Members voted in favour of it while 232 Members voted against the Bill.

The Lower House held discussion over the Bill for over 12 hours till late night.

The Waqf (Amendment) Bill, 2025 aims to streamline the management of Waqf properties, with provisions to safeguard heritage sites and promote social welfare. The legislation also aims to improve governance by enhancing transparency in property management, streamlining coordination between Waqf Boards and local authorities and protecting stakeholders’ rights. The Bill also seeks to improve the economic and social status of Muslim women, particularly widows and divorced women.

Replying to a debate on the Bill, Minority Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju said, such assumption that non-Muslims will interfere in the Waqf Board dealings is wrong. Rejecting the allegation leveled by opposition of government being anti-minority, he said, Narendra Modi government has taken care of even the smallest minority community like the Parsis. Responding to the allegations of opposition members who termed the Bill unconstitutional, the Minister said, the opposition members have not been able to give any reason behind it.

Earlier in the day, moving the Bill, the Minister said, it has nothing to do with the religious practice of Muslims and it only deals with the properties related to Waqf Boards. He said, in the present Act, some provisions were misused to declare any land as Waqf property in the country.

Krishna Prasad Tenneti of TDP supported the Bill. He said Waqf properties worth 1.2 lakh crore rupees and over 36 lakh acres of land present an opportunity for economic and social transformation for minorities. JD (U) Leader and Union Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh (Lalan) said the legislation will bring transparency in management of waqf properties and stop their misuse.

Intervening in the debate, Union Home Minister Amit Shah said, no non-Muslim will come into the Waqf. He said, there is no such provision to include any non-Muslim among those who manage the religious institutions.

Mr Shah said, this is a huge misconception that this Bill will interfere with the religious conduct of Muslims and interfere with the property donated by them. Mr Shah said, this Bill was not necessary if the then Congress Government had not amended the Bill in 2013.

Asaduddin Owaisi of AIMIM claimed that the bill goes against the constitutional provision of equality of all religions. Arvind Sawant of Shiv Sena (UBT) alleged that clause-by-clause discussions were not held in the JPC.

Shrikant Shinde of Shiv Sena said, the legislation has been named as Unified Waqf Management Empowerment, Efficiency and Development (UMEED) and it will give hope to the minorities for progress.

Meanwhile, various organizations across the country have welcomed the Waqf Amendment Bill 2025, terming it a new dawn of reforms and gesture of goodwill from Narendra Modi government towards deprived and backward muslims.

In Kerala, residents of the coastal village of Munambam near Kochi, took out a procession in support of the Narendra Modi government around midnight last, as news about the Lok Sabha passing the Waqf Amendment Bill came in. Over 600 odd residents of the village have been agitating for more than 160 days, against the State Waqf Board’s claim over their residential properties in Munambam. After Lok Sabha passed the bill, they raised slogans and burst crackers, expressing hope that they may not face eviction from their homes, where they were living for decades.

The Lok Sabha adopted a Statutory Resolution confirming imposition of President’s Rule in Manipur. The House held a brief discussion on the resolution and raised the issue of violence in the State and the hardships faced by the people there. Replying to a debate on the Statutory Resolution in the House, Union Home Minister Amit Shah said that the government is making all out efforts to restore normalcy in Manipur.

## Midday News

The Rajya Sabha has taken up the Waqf (Amendment) Bill, 2025 for consideration and passing. The Waqf  (Amendment) Bill, 2025 aims to streamline the management of Waqf properties, with provisions to safeguard heritage sites and promote social welfare. The legislation also aims to improve governance by enhancing transparency in property management, streamlining coordination between Waqf Boards and local authorities and protecting stakeholder’s rights. The Bill also seeks to improve the economic and social status of Muslim women, particularly widows and divorced women. The Bill is intended to make the Waqf Board more inclusive, having representation from different Muslim sects for better Waqf governance and decision-making.

Moving the bill, Minority Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju said that the bill has been brought after a detailed consultation with the stakeholders as well as in the Joint Parliamentary Committee.  He urged the opposition parties to support this bill. Rejecting the opposition’s allegations, he said, only Muslim can get the benefit of Waqf and it is not correct that any non-muslim will interfere in the management of Waqf properties. He highlighted that the bill is intended to ensure better management of Waqf properties and not to interfere in the religious practice of Muslims. The Minister also highlighted that the Waqf boards will have representations of all different sects of Muslim and women to make it inclusive.

On the Waqf Amendment Bill 2025, Union Minister and LJP (Ramvilas) Chief Chirag Paswan has accused the opposition leaders of spreading lies on the Bill saying that this bill is against Muslims. Talking to reporters outside parliament, Mr Paswan said, the bill is pro-poor Muslim and women. He said, the systems will be made transparent through this Bill.

Prominent leaders across the nation have welcomed the Waqf (Amendment) Bill, 2025 after it was passed by the Lok Sabha yesterday. The experts, ranging from legal domain to intellectual groups, have extended their support, saying the Bill will solve reported issues of corruption, mis-governance and a lack of judicial oversight afflicting the Central and State Waqf Boards.

Talking exclusively to Akashvani News, Legal Expert and Former Chairman of Karnataka State Minorities Commission, Anwar Manippadi said that loose rules and regulations have festered unchecked embezzlements. He added that the Bill has empowered people to approach the higher courts over any dispute on the verdicts of the Waqf Board.
