## Morning News

Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal has emphasized that national interest cannot be compromised just to meet trade agreement deadlines. The Minister said, trade agreements should be win-win for both sides. Mr Goyal was speaking at the 9th Global Technology Summit in New Delhi yesterday, where he highlighted the opportunities that lie ahead for India in reshaping global trade, especially with trusted partners such as the United States.

Speaking on Free Trade agreements, the Minister stated that while timelines are aspirational, national interest cannot be compromised to meet trade agreement deadlines, asserting that it must be equitable, fair, and mutually beneficial.

The Minister highlighted that India’s current tariff protection measures are directed mainly at non-market economies that engage in unfair trade practices. He added that India is well-positioned to engage in bilateral partnerships with countries that value reciprocity, trust, and fair play.

India is currently working on trade agreements with many countries and blocs of nations, including with the European Union, the United Kingdom, and the United States.

China’s 125 per cent tariff on United States’ goods comes into effect from today. The move came after US President Donald Trump raised the tariffs for China to 145 per cent earlier this week.

On the other hand, the US President has said that he will impose reciprocal tariffs again on countries that fail to reach an agreement with America by 9th of July, when the 90-day pause ends.

The Centre has operationalised a dedicated ‘Global Tariff and Trade Helpdesk’ to assist stakeholders in navigating emerging trade issues. The Commerce and Industry Ministry in a statement said that the Helpdesk will look into issues relating to Import and Export Challenges, Import Surges or Dumping, Logistics or Supply Chain Challenges, Financial or Banking issues and Regulatory or Compliance Issues among others.

The Supreme Court has ruled that the President must decide on State Bills, reserved by Governors for Presidential assent, within three months. The Apex Court set aside Tamil Nadu Governor R.N. Ravi’s decision to withhold assent to ten pending Bills and, in the process, ruled that the President should also not take more than three months in arriving at a decision on Bills referred by Governors.

A bench of Justices JB Pardiwala and Justice Mahadevan clarified that if there’s any delay beyond this period, valid reasons must be provided and shared with the concerned State. The Court stated that just like Governors, the President cannot sit indefinitely on Bills or exercise an “absolute veto.” If no action is taken within the set timeframe, States have the right to move the Courts and seek a writ of mandamus. The judgment emphasizes cooperation, urging States to engage in pre-legislation discussions with the Centre, especially on sensitive constitutional matters. Similarly, the Central government has been asked to treat State proposals with seriousness and speed. In case a Bill is withheld due to constitutional concerns, the Court recommended that the President seek the Supreme Court’s advice under Article 143. VAIBHAV JYOTSANA SRIVASTAV, AKASHVANI NEWS, DELHI

The Supreme Court has said it is not undermining the office of the Governor in fixing a timeline for their actions under Article 200, but they must act with due deference to the settled conventions of parliamentary democracy. Article 200 empowers the Governor to give assent to the Bills presented to him, withhold the assent, or reserve it for the consideration of the President.
