Indian Railways has successfully completed the final high-speed trial of the indigenously designed and manufactured Vande Bharat Sleeper Train. The trial was conducted on the Kota-Nagda section, during which the train achieved a maximum speed of 180 Kilometer per hour, marking a significant milestone in the country’s journey towards advanced and self-reliant rail technology.

Union Railways Minister, Ashwini Vaishnaw, shared a video of the high-speed trial on social media. The video also showcased a water-glass stability demonstration, in which glasses filled with water remained steady without spillage even at high speed, underscoring the advanced ride quality, superior suspension, and technological robustness of this new-generation train. Our correspondent reports that this development underscores Indian Railways’ commitment to innovation, safety, and the advancement of indigenous rail manufacturing under the vision of Atmanirbhar Bharat. A report;

“The 16-coach Vande Bharat Sleeper rake used in the trial has been designed to cater to long-distance passenger travel and is equipped with state-of-the-art passenger amenities. These include comfortable sleeper berths, automatic doors, modern toilets, fire detection and safety monitoring systems, CCTV-based surveillance and digital passenger information systems. These features are aimed at providing passengers with a safe, comfortable, and world-class travel experience. During the trial, comprehensive technical evaluations were carried out, including assessment of ride stability, emergency braking system, safety systems, and other critical parameters under the supervision of the Commissioner of Railway Safety. The train’s performance at high speed was found fully satisfactory, and the trial was declared successful by the Commissioner. With Dipendra Kumar, Saklen Akhtar, Akashvani News Delhi.”

The Union Cabinet has approved two major highway projects to boost road infrastructure in Maharashtra and Odisha. Briefing the media in New Delhi today, Information and Broadcasting Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said, the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs cleared the construction of a 374-kilometre, six-lane access-controlled greenfield Nashik-Solapur-Akkalkot corridor in Maharashtra.

“Today, the Nashik–Solapur corridor has been approved. This corridor is very important. It will connect many major cities and several large industrial centers. Its construction will be completed in two years. It is a 374 km project, and an investment of ₹19,142 crore will be made in it. So this is the largest value of beauty project ever taken up in our highway system.. This project will reduce the distance between Nashik and Solapur by 14%, and travel time will be significantly reduced—by about 45%—between Chennai and Surat.”

Mr Vaishnaw said, the project will generate about 251.06 lakh man-days of direct employment and 313.83 lakh man-days of indirect employment. The Union Cabinet also approved the widening and strengthening of existing 2-Lane to 2-Lane with Paved Shoulder of NH-326 in Odisha.

National Highway 326 connects the interior areas of Odisha. Today, the Cabinet has approved the widening and strengthening of this highway. It is a 206-kilometer project with an investment of 1,526 crore rupees. Gajapati, Rayagada, and Koraput are all aspirational districts, and all three are tribal districts. “The launch of two Pralay missiles which India successfully test-fired today followed the intended trajectory, meeting all flight objectives as confirmed by tracking sensors deployed by the Integrated Test Range at Chandipur off Odisha coast.
