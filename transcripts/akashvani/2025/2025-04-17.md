The Supreme Court today recorded the Union government’s assurance that no fresh appointments will be made to waqf boards or the Central Waqf Council under the Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025, until the next hearing. The three-judge bench, led by Chief Justice Sanjeev Khanna, also noted that the status of existing waqf properties, including those listed as ‘waqf by user’, will remain unchanged for now.

The apex court granted the Centre seven days to file a reply over the pleas filed against the Waqf Amendment Act, after which the petitioners will be granted five days to review the reply. The matter will now be heard on May 5th. Due to the large number of petitions, the bench asked the petitioners to identify five lead cases to be heard, while the remaining may be treated as applications or considered disposed of. A total of 10 pleas were listed in the Supreme Court challenging the constitutional validity of the Waqf Act. Yesterday, the bench had indicated it would pass an interim order but deferred it after the Solicitor General Tushar Mehta requested a hearing before any decision was made. The Waqf Act regulates the management of Islamic endowments used for religious and charitable purposes such as mosques, schools, and hospitals. This is R.S Raghu with desk report for Akashvani news.

Vice President Jagdeep Dhankhar today questioned recent ruling of the Supreme Court prescribing a timeline for the President to decide on bills and said there can not be a situation where Court directs the President of India. He said, the Apex Court can only interpret the Constitution. Addressing the 6th batch of Rajya Sabha interns at the Vice-President’s Enclave in New Delhi, Mr Dhankhar said India had not bargained for a democracy where judges will legislate, perform executive functions and act as what he called the Super Parliament. He underlined that one has to be extremely sensitive while exercising power.

The Vice President remarks came days after Apex Court’s verdict in which it set a deadline for the President and the Governors to give assent to legislations.
