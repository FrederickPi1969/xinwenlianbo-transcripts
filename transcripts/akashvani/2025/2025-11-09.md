The country is celebrating the 150th anniversary of its national song, Vande Mataram. A song that has inspired generations of Indians to unite, rise and celebrate the spirit of the nation. Today, our correspondent brings a report on how Vande Mataram ignited India’s struggle for freedom.

“The power and passion of the song Vande Mataram came alive in 1905, a year when the British Raj made the attempt to divide Bengal. During this time,  Swadeshi Movement was launched in which Vande Mataram emerged as the most prominent and unifying slogan, echoing through the streets, schools, and hearts of freedom seekers. Vande Mataram was not just a slogan but a chant that united protestors, becoming the heartbeat of India’s freedom struggle. It was this song that ignited the idea of self-rule and self-reliance among the people of India. Inspired by the awakening spirit of Vande Mataram in 1905, Indian painter Abanindranath Tagore immortalized that sentiment on canvas, creating the iconic portrait of Bharat Mata which depicted India as a goddess, and a symbol of Indian nationalist movement. Great visionaries like Aurobindo Ghosh, Rabindranath Tagore, and Lala Lajpat Rai urged the people of India to raise their voices in unison and use Vande Mataram as a rallying cry for freedom and faith. Reciting Vande Mataram was not just a political act but a sacred invocation and a spiritual connection that united hearts beyond regions and religions, binding every Indian to a shared identity and purpose. With Shivang and Saumya, this is Abhishek Mukhopadhyay for Akashvani News, Delhi.”

The GST Bachat Utsav kicked off on the 22nd of September as the Narendra Modi government slashed GST rates in many sectors, benefitting citizens across the country. Today, our correspondent brings a report on how the new GST rates in the footwear sector is helping citizens. A report.

“Under the next-generation GST reforms, the government has simplified the tax structure across industries, including apparel and footwear. The taxes levied on chamois leather, composition leather with a basis of leather or leather fibre, and leather prepared after tanning or crusting has been reduced to 5 per cent. Earlier, these were taxed at 12 per cent. This has reduced input costs for manufacturers and enhanced production within the country. Also, the Goods and Services tax on footwear priced upto 2 thousand 5 hundred rupees per pair has been brought down to 5 per cent. The move is directly benefitting young manufacturers and has made products more accessible to the consumers.

The new reforms have made stylish and durable footwear accessible to young and middle-income consumers. Tax cuts in the leather and footwear sector has reduced the burden on young manufacturers and helped to increase overall consumption. With Rishu Jaiswal, Saklen Akhtar for Akashvani News, Delhi.”
