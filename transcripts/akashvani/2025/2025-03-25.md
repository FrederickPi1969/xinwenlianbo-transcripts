## Morning News

India has achieved the highest-ever growth in indigenous defence production during the financial year 2023-24 since the launch of the Make in India initiative, reaching a record one lakh 27 thousand crore rupees. The value of defence production has surged 174 per cent increase from 46 thousand 429 crore in 2014-15. A report:

”65 percent of defence equipment is now manufactured domestically, a significant shift from the earlier 65 to 70 percent import dependency. The Defence exports have also surged from 686 crore rupees in 2013-14 to an all-time high of over 21 thousand crore rupees in 2023-24, marking a 30 fold increase over the past decade. This growth has been bolstered by the Make in India initiative, which has enabled the development of advanced military platforms.

The Defence Ministry said, once dependent on foreign suppliers, the country now stands as a rising force in indigenous manufacturing, shaping its military strength through homegrown capabilities. The Ministry said, the government has introduced several measures to improve ease of doing business in the defence manufacturing sector. India now targets 3 lakh crore rupees in defence production by 2029, reinforcing its position as a global defence manufacturing hub. Adarsh for Akashvani News, Delhi.”

The Centre has notified an increase in the salary, daily allowance and pension of Members and former Members of Parliament. The monthly salary for Members of Parliament has been increased from one lakh rupees to one lakh 24 thousand. The daily allowance has been raised from two thousand rupees to two thousand 500 rupees. ## Evening News

The Parliament has passed the Disaster Management (Amendment) Bill 2024 with Rajya Sabha approving it today. The Lok Sabha had passed it in December last year. It aims to enhance disaster preparedness, response and recovery across the country. The Bill amends the Disaster Management Act, 2005 and intends to strengthen the efficient working of the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) and the State Disaster Management Authorities (SDMA). The bill empowers the NDMA and SDMA to prepare the disaster plan at national level and state level instead of the National Executive Committee and the State Executive Committee.

Replying to a discussion on the Bill, Union Home Minister Amit Shah said, the size and scale of disasters have changed and the responses must change accordingly.

On the opposition’s allegation on centralization, Mr Shah said, the greatest power under the Bill has been given to the District Disaster Management Authority.

Earlier, initiating the discussion, Neeraj Dangi of Congress asked that the Bill does not specify details on the PM Cares fund or how it will be used. The treasury benches protested over Mr Dangi’s comment on the government’s COVID-19 management.

Taking part in the debate, Brij Lal of BJP said, the main responsibility of disaster management lies on the state governments. He said, the Centre provides all help to states in case of disasters.

Ritabrata Banerjee of TMC accused the NDA ruled Central government of withholding funds of opposition ruled States.

Praful Patel of NCP lauded the efforts of the government to strengthen disaster preparedness at every level.

A A Rahim of CPI (M) alleged that the Central Government is not allocating enough funds to states affected by natural disasters. CPI MP Sandosh Kumar P suggested sending the Bill to the joint parliamentary committee for more scrutiny.

The Parliament also passed the Boilers Bill, 2024 with the Lok Sabha approving it today. The Bill repeals the Boilers Act, 1923 and seeks to provide for the regulation of boilers and safety of life and property of persons from the danger of explosions of steamboilers. The Rajya Sabha had already passed the Bill in December last year. The Legislation also provides for uniformity in registration and inspection during the manufacture and use of boilers in the country. For Ease of Doing Business, the Bill will benefit boiler users including those in the MSME sector as provisions related to the decriminalisation have been incorporated in the Bill.

Replying to a debate on the Bill in the Lok Sabha, Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal said, the bill has been brought in Parliament after holding discussions with all the stakeholders. He said, the Modi government believes in cooperative federalism and there is no question of encroaching the rights of States.

The Lok Sabha has passed the Finance Bill, 2025, incorporating 35 government amendments aimed at tariff rationalisation and boosting domestic manufacturing. The Bill seeks to give effect to the financial proposals of the Central Government for the financial year 2025-2026. Replying to the discussion on the Bill, Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said, the Finance Bill 2025 provides unprecedented tax relief to taxpayers and ease of doing business.

Ms. Sitharaman said, under the new tax regime, there will be zero income tax upto the income of 12 lakh rupees. She announced that the New Income tax bill is likely to be taken up for discussion in the next monsoon session. She informed that this Finance bill also proposed to remove seven custom tariff rates for industrial goods. Ms. Sitharaman said, the government has also proposed to extend the period of incorporation of Start ups by five years to allow the benefits available to them which are incorporated before 1st April 2030.

Earlier, participating in the discussion, S Jothimani of Congress raised the issue of unemployment in the country. Jagdambika Pal of BJP said, India is the fastest growing economy in the world.
