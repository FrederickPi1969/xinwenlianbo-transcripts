## Morning News

Russian President Vladimir Putin will visit India in December for an annual summit with Prime Minister Narendra Modi, according to the Kremlin. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed that the visit is scheduled to take place before the New Year. The spokesman  added that preparations for the visit are in full swing. This will be the Russian leader’s first visit to India since the start of the Ukraine conflict in early 2022.

The Russian President has said he is looking forward to his upcoming visit to India early December and meeting with his dear friend and Russia’s trustworthy partner, Prime Minister Modi. Speaking at the 2025 Valdai Discussion Club meeting in Sochi, President Putin reaffirmed Moscow’s long-standing trust in New Delhi and described Prime Minister Modi as a very reasonable, wise leader, strongly oriented toward the interests of his nation. He praised special relations with India rooted in historical ties dating back to the Soviet era.

Noting that current trade turnover between the two countries falls short of its true potential, President Putin emphasized the need to build effective, mutually beneficial trade ties. He said the focus now must be on resolving key issues in order to unlock greater opportunities. This includes addressing logistics challenges, improving financing mechanisms, and finding new solutions for cross-border payments within the bricks instruments, on bilateral basis, using the rupee or a third country’s currency or using the electronic payment methods.

President Putin acknowledged the existing trade imbalance with India and said he has already instructed the Russian government, including Deputy Prime Minister Denis Manturov, head of the Intergovernmental Commission, to come up with concrete measures to strengthen trade and economic ties.

India and China have agreed to resume direct air services connecting designated points in both countries by late October. In a statement, the External Affairs Ministry said that the civil aviation authorities of the two countries have been engaged in technical-level discussions on resuming direct air services and on a revised Air Services Agreement. This is being done since early this year as part of the government’s approach towards gradual normalization of relations between India and China. Following these discussions, the decision to resume direct air services has been taken, in keeping with the winter season schedule, subject to commercial decision of the designated carriers from the two countries and fulfilment of all operational criteria.

At least two people were killed and several others injured in a mass stabbing and vehicle-ramming attack outside Heaton Park Hebrew Congregation in Manchester, UK, on Yom Kippur. Police shot dead a third man believed to be the attacker. Authorities declared the incident a terrorist attack.

India has condemned the terror attack on the Heaton Park Synagogue in Manchester. In a statement, the Ministry of External Affairs expressed solidarity with the people of the United Kingdom in this moment of grief. The Ministry also conveyed its sadness that this heinous act was committed on the International Day of Non-Violence.

GST Bachat Utsav announced by Prime Minister Narendra Modi which commenced on 22nd of last month is benefiting all sections of society. The next-generation GST reforms represent a significant step towards simplifying India’s indirect tax system. The reforms are bringing substantial relief to citizens by reducing the tax rate on various goods and services. Today we are presenting a report on the GST cut on stationery items.

Under the Next Generation GST reforms, the Government has announced major relief for stationery items. Students and retailers are benefiting under the latest GST reforms. The GST on pencils has been reduced from 12 percent to nil. Notebooks and copies, including exercise books, graph books, and laboratory notebooks, are now completely tax-free, down from 12 percent. Pencil sharpeners now attract no GST, reduced from 12 percent.

Additionally,Geometry boxes and mathematical instrument sets, which earlier had a 12 percent GST, will now attract only 5 percent. These changes are expected to reduce the cost of essential stationery and educational tools, benefiting students, parents, and teachers across the country. Retailers and small shopkeepers are also likely to see increased sales. Talking to Akashvani News , Harsh Vardhan express their gratitude towards the government and said that the reduction in gst rate will benefit to the middle man.

The Government emphasizes that this move is part of a larger initiative to support education, promote learning, and make essential materials affordable and accessible to all. Nitika Gupta, Akashvani News, Delhi.

Centre has issued an advisory to all States and Union Territories on rational use of cough syrups in paediatric population. Union Health Ministry has advised that Cough and cold medications should not be prescribed or dispensed to children below two years. It said, these are generally not recommended for children below five years, and above that, any use should follow careful clinical evaluation with close supervision and strict adherence to appropriate dosing and avoiding multiple drugs combinations. The Ministry has written a letter to the Director Health Services of all States and Union Territories in this regard. It said that most acute cough illnesses in children are self-limiting and often resolve without pharmacological intervention. In view of the recent reports suggesting that child deaths in Madhya Pradesh were linked to consumption of cough syrups, a joint team consisting of representatives from National Centre for Disease Control, National Institute of Virology and Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation visited the site.

The next-generation GST reforms represent a significant step towards simplifying India’s indirect tax system. The reforms are bringing substantial relief to citizens by reducing the tax rate on various goods and services. Today we are presenting a report on the GST cut on wood substitutes. A report:

Under the Next Generation GST Reforms, the government has reduced the GST on Agro-based and eco-friendly wood substitutes to ease the financial burden on small and medium businesses. Under GST 2.0 tax rates on various wood based products such as rice husk board, glassfibre reinforced gypsum board and jute particle board has been lowered from 12 percent to 5 percent. Similarly, the GST on Sheets for veneering, bamboo flooring, casks, barrels, vats, tubs of wood have also been brought down to 5 percent from the previously taxed 12 percent. The reduction aims to boost production of eco-friendly alternatives, promoting sustainability. Along with this the GST rates on other wood items including cement bonded particle board, and sisal fibre board will now attract 5 percent tax slab. The rationalization under the new GST structure will create a range of positive effects for the agroforestry sector and consumers. The move will further encourage sustainable manufacturing and MSME competitiveness. With Drishti, Rohan for Akashvani News Delhi.
