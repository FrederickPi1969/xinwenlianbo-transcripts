Election Commission (EC) has asked political parties, candidates and campaign representatives for responsible use and disclosure of synthetically generated information and AI-generated content during elections. Through an advisory, it has asked to label any synthetically generated or AI-altered image, audio, or video used or disseminated for campaigning purposes as AI-Generated, Digitally Enhanced, or Synthetic Content, covering at least 10 percent of the visible display area.

Additionally, such content shall prominently disclose the name of entity, responsible for its generation in the metadata or accompanying caption. The commission has also called for restraint on publishing or forwarding any content that is unlawful and misrepresents the identity, appearance, or voice of any person without their consent, and created with the intent to mislead or deceive the electorate.

The GST Bachat Utsav, announced by Prime Minister Narendra Modi and launched on the 22nd of last month, is benefiting people across all sections of society. Today, our correspondent brings a report on how the revised GST rates in the footwear sector are benefiting consumers.

“The next-generation GST reforms have provided major relief to the leather and footwear sector. The reforms aimed at easing the burden on young manufacturers, have made production more cost-effective, and ensured that quality footwear becomes more affordable for consumers. Under the new tax structure, GST has been reduced from 12 percent to 5 percent on chamois leather, composition leather made from leather fibre, and leather prepared after tanning or crusting. This rationalisation has significantly reduced input costs for manufacturers and encouraged great production within the country. Footwear priced up to 2 thousand and five hundred rupees  per pair now attract just 5 percent GST instead of 12 percent, making stylish and durable footwear more accessible to young and middle-income consumers. Similarly, the GST on job work related to skins and leather has been cut from 12 percent to 5 percent, helping MSMEs lower production expenses and operate more competitively.  These measures have not only boosted domestic manufacturing but also strengthened India’s export potential, generated new employment opportunities, and supported the government’s vision of promoting youth-led entrepreneurship and self-reliance in the manufacturing sector. With Samarth and Rohan, Aman Yadav for Akashvani News, Delhi.”
