Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India report related to Aam Aadmi Party government’s liquor policy has highlighted that the policy led to a loss of revenue of over two thousand crore rupees to government exchequer. The report which was tabled in the Delhi Assembly by Chief Minister Rekha Gupta today flagged several discrepancies in the way City Excise Department monitored and regulated the supply of liquor in Delhi.

The report found the violations in award of licenses to Wholesalers, Retailers, Hotels, Clubs and Restaurants. It added that the Department was issuing licenses without checking various requirements relating to Excise Rules and Terms and Conditions for the issue of different type of licenses.The CAG report also pointed  out issue of  inadequate Quality Control.

Over two hundred pages report points out poor execution of Enforcement function ranging from incorrect inspection reports to deficient show cause notices and incorrect collection of security deposit from zonal Licensees and other lacuna in the liquor policy.The report also talks about lack of transparency in pricing of Indian Made Foreign Liquor. The Central Auditor highlighted inadequate Quality Control of liquor during the previous regime and stressed on the need for proactive monitoring of the quality of alcohol and framing of stringent quality standards. Talking about the issues in implementation of the Excise Policy, the report said while some retailers retained licenses till the expiry of the policy period, some surrendered the same before the policy period was over which  caused disruption in supply leading to revenue loss of around nine hundred crore rupees.

The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) has conducted extensive searches at over 60 locations across the country in connection with the GainBitcoin crypto currency scam. The coordinated search operations are being carried out in major cities, including Delhi NCR, Pune, Chandigarh, Nanded, Kolhapur, and Bengaluru, targeting premises linked to key accused.

GainBitcoin was an alleged Ponzi scheme launched in 2015 by Amit Bhardwaj, along with Ajay Bhardwaj and their network of agents. The fraudulent scheme lured investors by promising lucrative returns of 10 percent monthly in Bitcoin for 18 months. Investors were encouraged to purchase bitcoin from exchanges and invest them with GainBitcoin through cloud mining contracts.
