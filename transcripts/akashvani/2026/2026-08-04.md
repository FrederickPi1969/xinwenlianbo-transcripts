## Evening News

The Parliament has passed the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026 with the Rajya Sabha approving it today. Lok Sabha has already passed the Bill. Minister of State for Home Nityanand Rai moved the bill in the Upper House, which aims to amend the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969. Following the passage of the bill, the Rajya Sabha was adjourned for the day. More from our correspondent:

“The objective of this bill is to prevent delays in the registration of births and deaths and to encourage people to register in a timely manner. The Bill provides that if a birth or death is reported to the Registrar more than one year but within two years, it may be registered only with the approval of the District Magistrate, Sub-Divisional Magistrate, or an Executive Magistrate. The Bill further states that if a birth or death is reported to the Registrar more than two years, it will be registered only on the order of a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class. The Bill also mandates that the designated authority must verify the authenticity of the reported birth or death before granting permission for delayed registration. The registration of births and deaths is mandatory, and the certificate issued provides legal identity to an individual. With Dipendra Kumar, Anand Kumar, Akashvani News Delhi”

The Supreme Court today directed the Reserve Bank of India to prepare and circulate a standard operating procedure (SOP) for dealing with mule accounts and bank accounts linked to cyber fraud. The court also issued a series of directions to states, Union Territories, law enforcement agencies and banks to strengthen grievance redressal, speed up the restoration of defrauded money and improve the handling of digital fraud cases. More from our correspondent:

The apex court asked the RBI to provide copies of the SOP to the Registrars General of all high courts and directed states, Union Territories and law enforcement agencies to ensure the expeditious adoption and operationalisation of grievance redressal and money restoration modules. The court also directed states and Union Territories to operationalise the grievance redressal and money restoration modules and take steps to create public awareness about them. It asked the Registrars General of all high courts to apprise subordinate courts of the availability of the modules.

The court sought a state-wise and bank-wise report on grievances registered and resolved, directed authorities to expedite cases involving frozen bank accounts, and asked the Inter-Departmental Committee to consult banks and intermediaries on preventing digital arrest scams and recovering defrauded money. Reshma Tiwari for desk report, Akashvani News”

In another decision today the Supreme Court directed the central government to develop a pilot project linking fuel sales to valid third-party motor insurance, proposing that uninsured vehicles be denied fuel at petrol pumps until coverage is obtained. The court directed the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India and the Centre to pilot Automatic Number Plate Recognition, ANPR-based insurance verification to identify uninsured vehicles and improve compliance with the Motor Vehicles Act.

India has brought back 274 fugitive criminals from 36 countries between 2019 and 2026. The Ministry of Home Affairs said that to strengthen action against fugitives, the government enacted the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act in 2018. It added that India has developed an integrated, intelligence-led and technology-driven model to combat fugitive criminals.

The Ministry said that before 2014, India had extradition treaties with only 37 countries and there was also no dedicated law to deal with economic fugitives or to confiscate their assets. It informed that between 2004 and 2013, India secured the extradition of only four fugitives per year on average, while 110 extradition requests remained pending. The Ministry highlighted that previous governments lacked the political will to bring back fugitives but the current government has made the extradition of fugitives a ‘national priority’.
