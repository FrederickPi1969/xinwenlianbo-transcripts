The Government has constituted a committee to examine issues raised by sugarcane farmers and recommend appropriate measures for addressing their concerns. Former Member of NITI Aayog, Professor Ramesh Chand will chair the committee which included Agriculture Commissioner, Joint Secretary (Sugar), Department of Food and Public Distribution and Joint Secretary (Legal Metrology), Department of Consumer Affairs as members. The constitution of the Committee follows the decisions taken during a meeting of representatives of sugarcane farmers from Karnataka and Maharashtra with the Union Minister of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare and the Union Minister of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution.

“The Committee will examine the demand for an increase in remunerative returns to sugarcane farmers, taking into account cultivation costs and other relevant factors. It will also examine the demand for revision of the ex-mill Minimum Selling Price of sugar in the context of sugar production costs, market realisations, and the financial viability of sugar mills. Other key areas of examination will include existing mechanisms for testing and reporting sugar recovery  and prevailing sugarcane weighment practices at sugar mills.

The Committee will also examine prevailing practices, relating to deductions from the weight of mechanically harvested sugarcane and assess the extent of actual losses, if any, arising from mechanised harvesting. It will evaluate the financial impact of the present practices on the cane price payable to farmers and may consider any other related matters deemed necessary. The Supreme Court has ruled that individuals converting from Hinduism to Islam or other non-Hindu religions cannot automatically claim reservation benefits under the Backward Class category. Dismissing the Tamil Nadu government’s plea against a Madras High Court verdict, a bench of Justices Prashant Kumar Mishra and Shree Chandrashekhar held that social backwardness suffered in an antecedent caste cannot be carried forward after conversion. ​

The apex court affirmed that conversion to a non-Hindu social order does not by itself confer membership in any notified Backward Class community. It declared that statutory benefits under Article 16(4) of the Constitution cannot be extended solely on the basis of a person’s pre-conversion status. The court also reiterated that anyone professing a religion other than Hinduism, Sikhism, or Buddhism ceases to hold Scheduled Caste status.
