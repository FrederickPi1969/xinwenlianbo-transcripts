Prime Minister Narendra Modi has arrived in Chennai this morning, where he will inaugurate projects worth 13 thousand crore rupees. The Prime Minister was given a warm welcome by Tamilnadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay, his cabinet colleagues, and AIADMK General Secretary and former Chief Minister Edappadi Palanisamy, along with others. The Prime Minister held a roadshow en route to the venue of the event at Porur in Chennai, where he flagged off the metro rail service from Porur to Poonamallee. The Prime Minister traveled along with the Tamilnadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay in the metro rail.

The 14.5-km metro rail service features a unique 3.7-km double-decker structure with advanced signalling systems designed to support driverless operations. The Prime Minister will dedicate and inaugurate railway infrastructure projects worth over 750 crore rupees, including five Amrit Stations at Palani, Vellore Cantonment, Ambasamudram, Paramakudi and Namakkal, which have been redeveloped under the Amrit Bharat Station scheme.

He dedicated the Sudha Gopalakrishnan Brain Centre at IIT Madras, a pioneering global scientific mission to map more than 100 human brains into three-dimensional atlases at cellular resolution across the human lifespan, from the foetal stage to old age.

New Delhi strongly condemned the repeated attacks on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport over the past few days, including the dastardly attack of yesterday, which have led to loss of lives, injuries and damage to property. In a statement, the Ministry of External Affairs expressed condolences to the families of the deceased and wished for the speedy recovery of the injured persons. New Delhi said that repeated attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure are completely unacceptable and must cease immediately. It added that India stands in solidarity with the people and the government of Saudi Arabia in defence of their sovereignty, and hopes that peace and normalcy returns to the region at the earliest.

Twelve people were killed and 309 injured in a Houthi attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh yesterday, Saudi Arabia’s General Authority of Civil Aviation (GACA) said in a statement issued in the wee hours today. The victims included 4 Saudi citizens and 8 foreigners includes 2 from Bangladesh and 1 each from the USA, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Palestine and Sudan. Some of the injured were in critical condition, while the attack also caused property damage. Airport operations were temporarily suspended for damage assessments and safety inspections. The Saudi-led coalition condemned the attack as a terrorist act and vowed a decisive response. More from our correspondent:

“Saturday’s Houthi attack on Riyadh Airport was the third attack on the Saudi Capitals’ airport this week. It was also one of the deadliest strikes on Saudi Arabia in almost a decade. No threat alert was reported to be sounded by Saudi Arabia’s warning system. Almost 260 flights to & from Riyadh were reported to be cancelled on Saturday. The earlier attacks this week had also caused fatalities and damaged a Saudi commercial plane on the tarmac. The Saudi-led military coalition had also reported attempted attacks on Dammam Airport and Al-Kharj. They’ve vowed a decisive response to these attacks on Saudi territory. Manish Shiva, Akashvani News, Dubai.”

US President Donald Trump said the USA may consider joining Saudi-led strikes against the Houthis.

The Supreme Court today directed authorities to ensure that people gathering peacefully are not illegally detained and that the free movement of people is allowed, subject to traffic regulations. It also called for a review of barricading arrangements in Delhi and directed the Centre to ensure that metro services operate according to their normal schedule and that internet services remain uninterrupted, subject to law and order requirements.

The court said metro and train services should function normally in accordance with the prevailing law and order situation. It further directed that lawyers must not be obstructed from discharging their professional duties.

Issuing notice to the Centre on a plea seeking initiation of contempt proceedings against authorities over alleged violations of its directions, the Supreme Court said it would hear all parties and ensure that no inconvenience was caused.

Solicitor General Tushar Mehta informed the court that entry and exit had been restricted at only 45 of the 303 stations across the metro network on October 10.

The Consulate General of India in Dubai has confirmed that all 22 Indian crew members aboard oil tanker MT GEM No. 2 have been rescued and are safe. This follows a fire incident on the vessel off the Ras Al Khaimah coast. In an update, the Consulate said it was in touch with UAE authorities and extending all possible assistance and support to the Indian seafarers. According to the Emirates News Agency (WAM), the Coast Guard Division of the UAE National Guard Command had rescued 22 Asian nationals after receiving a report of a fire aboard an oil tanker on October 10. The Coast Guard located the vessel and rescued all crew members, who received first aid before being handed over to the relevant authorities. All those rescued were reported to be in stable health.

Authorities in Nepal have issued evacuation warnings for settlements along the Budhi Gandaki River in Gorkha district after a sudden rise in water levels swept away several houses early this morning. According to preliminary reports, five to seven houses in the riverbed at Jagat have been swept away, while several other houses are feared to have been washed away downstream. No casualties have been reported so far. The cause of the sudden rise in water levels remains unclear. However, officials suspect that a landslide above Siuribas in Chumnubri Rural Municipality may have temporarily blocked the river, with the subsequent breach of the blockage triggering the surge.

Following floods in Nepal, vigilance has been stepped up in Bihar’s border district of West Champaran. Although the impact of this rise on Bihar is expected to be negligible. District Magistrate Tarnjot Singh said that a significant rise in the water level of the Gandak River is not expected in the district. However, an alert has been issued for low-lying areas as a precautionary measure.

Nepal has been hit by flash floods once again, as the Budhi Gandaki river swept Gorkha district in the wee hours today washing away settlements in Chumnubri Rural Municipality. Nepalese authorities have rescued more than 80 people so far, including nine foreign nationals. Five people still remain missing. The flood swept away houses and bridges and damaged several hydropower projects.

The International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development has suggested that the flash flood may have been triggered by the sudden failure of a temporary blockage formed by a landslide near Chumchet in Gorkha district. Floodwaters carrying mud, sediment and debris surged downstream, prompting authorities to issue warnings for communities along the Budhi Gandaki, Trishuli and Narayani river systems.

Pakistan has declared an emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province amid a deteriorating law and order situation in the northwestern region, effectively bringing the provincial government to an end. According to the presidential proclamation, President Asif Ali Zardari invoked emergency provisions, citing a breakdown of constitutional machinery in the province. The move allows Pakistan’s federal government to assume key executive and legislative powers.

The development comes amid an ongoing protest march led by Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi, who is demanding the release of former Prime Minister Imran Khan and access to medical treatment for him.

The nine-day festival of Shardiya Navratri began with religious fervour and devotion across the country today. People worship Goddess Durga in her nine forms during the festival. Thousands of devotees thronged temples to offer prayers and seek blessings, as special rituals, traditional ceremonies and devotional celebrations marked the auspicious occasion.

In Gujarat, thousands of devotees visited the 400-year-old Juna Ambaji Temple at Gopipura in Surat to attend the morning Mangala Aarti and offer prayers to Goddess Amba. State-wide celebrations kicked off with Ghatasthapana and the lighting of traditional Garbo lamps. More from our correspondent.

“The festival of Navratri, which literally means ‘Nine Nights’, is one of the biggest dance festivals in the world. In Gujarat, Navratri is celebrated by playing the traditional folk dance Garba in groups, observing fasts, and performing Aarti of Goddess Amba in communities. Adorned with vibrant lighting, cities across the state have taken on a festive look as streets reverberate with traditional Garba music. The Gujarat Police has made elaborate security arrangements across more than 22,000 Garba venues. Special ‘She-Teams’ and police personnel are deployed at party plots and street Garba grounds.  The Cyber Cell is monitoring social media 24/7 to prevent rumors. With Aparna Khunt’s report from Ahmedabad, Reshma Tiwari, Akashvani News.”

The festival culminates with Vijayadashami, also known as Dussehra, symbolising the victory of good over evil.
