The United States has struck Iranian military targets on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz last night. American forces said, they targeted two Iranian launchers on Larak Island after Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) troops were seen preparing to launch rockets carrying sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian state broadcaster IRIB reported that the attack killed and wounded several Iranian personnel and civilians.

The Larak strike was the first known US attack inside Iran since late July, when President Donald Trump halted a two-week American military campaign linked to the strait. A US official said, American forces are monitoring the area closely and remain prepared to protect the free flow of commerce through the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran has retaliated, with IRGC launching ballistic missiles on US bases in Jordan in response to the US strike. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they launched an attack on the King Hussein and Al Azraq US bases in Jordan using ballistic missiles, The latest development marks a sharp escalation in the confrontation, which had remained relatively calm since June.

In Nepal, the death toll from last week’s devastating floods and mudslides has risen to 903, while more than 4,247 people remain untraceable. According to the Nepal’s National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority, the figure has increased from Sunday’s figure of 2,502. Bodies have been recovered from all eight affected districts of Nepal as rescuers race against time to search for those missing and rescue those stranded.

Meanwhile, at least 933 workers are believed to be trapped at around half a dozen hydropower projects. Rescue teams backed by India and other experts are searching around half a dozen blocked hydropower tunnels, including Trishuli-3A.  In Tibet, the death toll currently stands at 16, with 546 reported to be missing. The disaster sent a torrent of ice, rock, mud and debris through towns and villages along the Nepal-Tibet border.

A preliminary study by Nepali scientists found the floods were triggered by a massive ice-rock avalanche at around 5,200 metres in Rasuwa’s Langtang-Lirung area. The debris entered the Lhende River before surging through the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli.

Nepali Prime Minister Balendra Shah called the floods an “unprecedented and devastating natural disaster”, with nearly 21,000 security personnel involved in rescue operations. The Red Cross estimates more than 90,000 people were affected, while authorities continue recovering and identifying bodies.

The West Bengal government has urged the Centre to protect the state’s interests before renewing the 30-year Ganga-Padma water-sharing agreement with Bangladesh, which expires in December. The state has stressed the need for adequate dry-season flow through Farakka to maintain the navigability of the Hooghly and protect shipping operations at Kolkata’s Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port. It has also raised concerns over riverbank erosion, siltation and flooding in Malda, Murshidabad and Nadia.

The state has sought priority for environmental protection and future water needs in the new agreement and called for detailed discussions through the India-Bangladesh Joint Rivers Commission.
