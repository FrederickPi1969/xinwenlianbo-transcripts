## Morning News

Election Commission has directed 17 States and Union Territories to conduct a Special Summary Revision (SSR) of electoral rolls with 1st January next year as the qualifying date. These states and UTs are Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Manipur, Puducherry, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and West Bengal.

“The Commission said in its letter that it had issued instructions on 29th of September to undertake a special drive for the enrolment of left out, young and first time eligible electors.  EC also said that the schedule for SSR in States and UTs where the Special Intensive Revision is ongoing or has recently been completed will be announced later. It added that for Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, where SIR has not been started so far, schedule for roll revision will be announced in due course of time.

The Commission further said that Electoral Registration Officer of the concerned assembly constituency shall be responsible for ensuring that no eligible citizen is left out while no ineligible person gets included or remains in the Electoral Roll. During the Special Summary Revision, Chief Electoral Officer, District Election Officers, Electoral Registration Officers will ensure that every eligible person is enrolled as an elector. With Suparna Saikia, Dilnashi Arzoo, Akashvani News, Delhi.”

Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal has launched the National Strategic Trade Command Centre (NSTCC) in New Delhi. NSTCC is a strategic initiative aimed at strengthening the monitoring, analysis and governance of India’s international trade ecosystem through integrated data analytics and evidence-based decision-making. The Ministry of Commerce and Industry said that the Command Centre has been conceptualised to provide senior policymakers and government officials with a consolidated view of India’s trade performance, export promotion schemes, regulatory approvals, application processing and operational indicators.

A two-day national conference on e-Governance reforms will begin today in Dharamshala, the district headquarters of Kangra in Himachal Pradesh. The conference will bring together senior officials and experts from the Central and State Governments to deliberate on various aspects of making governance more transparent, efficient and citizen-centric through digital technology. Union Minister Jagat Prakash Nadda, Union Minister of State Dr Jitendra Singh and Chief Minister Sukhvinder Singh Sukhu are scheduled to attend the conference.

“The conference is being organised jointly by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances, Government of India, and the Government of Himachal Pradesh. Around one hundred delegates from across the country are expected to participate in the event. The conference aims to facilitate the exchange of innovative technologies, administrative reforms and best practices in the field of e-Governance.

During the conference, Himachal Pradesh’s initiatives, including Him Parivar, Sukh Ashray, Vidya Samiksha Kendra and Green Himachal, will be showcased. Discussions will also focus on the use of technology in agriculture, healthcare services, judicial administration, digital services and the effective implementation of government schemes.

In addition, special sessions will be held on topics such as cyber security, data analytics, artificial intelligence and citizen-centric digital services. Ritesh Kapoor, Akashvani News, Shimla.”

“The Rann of Kutch is a vast salt flat extending as far as the eye can see, and for most of the year, it remains a pristine, empty expanse. Following the devastating 2001 earthquake, the region was severely affected, offering almost nothing that the local communities living at its edge could leverage for livelihood. In response, then Chief Minister of Gujarat Narendra Modi established a winter tent city on the salt flats near Dhordo village in 2005, inviting travelers from across the nation to witness its unique topography. Along with the influx of visitors came local folk musicians, dancers, artisans, and traditional cooks, transforming the remote desert into a vibrant cultural epicentre. Today, lakhs of visitors arrive each season, providing Kutch artisans with a thriving, self-sustaining market right at their doorstep.

The Government has raised the export duty on petrol to 2.5 rupees per litre from 50 paise, while the export duty on diesel has been reduced from 16 rupees to 8.5 rupees per litre. According to a notification issued by the Finance Ministry, the revised rates have come into effect from today. The rate of duty on exports of ATF continues to remain at 10.5 rupees per litre.  There is no change in the existing excise duty rates on petrol and diesel cleared for domestic consumption. The rates are prescribed based on the average international prices of crude oil, petrol, diesel and ATF prevailing during the period since the last review. The rates are being revised on a fortnightly basis and the last such revision was undertaken on 1st of this month.

The Press Council of India (PCI) has issued guidelines for journalists covering protests and demonstrations. The guidelines have been issued in view of the prevailing scenario and the need to facilitate the orderly and effective coverage of protests and demonstrations. Under the guidelines, journalists have been advised to carry identity cards issued by their media organisations or Press Information Bureau (PIB) and Directorate of Information and Publicity accreditation, as applicable, and keep them visibly displayed while covering demonstrations. More from our correspondent:-

“The Press Council of India has advised media persons to maintain a reasonable and safe distance from confrontations, particularly when tensions escalate or police action is anticipated. The council has also advised the journalists to maintain neutrality and professionalism and not to participate in demonstration or interfere with protesters, counter-protesters, or police. It urged journalists to be alert to changes in crowd behaviour and identify escape routes in advance. The guidelines stated that journalists should report events accurately, objectively, and impartially. They have been advised to distinguish verified facts from allegations, opinions, and unconfirmed claims and follow the applicable norms of journalistic conduct on accuracy and fairness. The Council further suggested to verify information through multiple sources wherever possible. PCI has asked the journalists not to broadcast or publish real-time police positions, tactical movements, or escape routes as doing so can compromise safety or operational security. WITH ANAND KUMAR, SHIVANG MISRA, AKASHVANI NEWS, DELHI”
