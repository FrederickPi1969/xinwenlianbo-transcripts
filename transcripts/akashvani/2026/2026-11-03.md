Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu makes landfall near Ongole in Prakasam district of Andhra Pradesh early this morning. The Storm brought heavy rainfall across South Coastal Andhra Pradesh, impacting districts including Prakasam, Bapatla, Palnadu, SPSR Nellore, and parts of adjoining states. Gale-force winds reaching speeds of 100 to 110 km per hour, gusting up to 120 km per hour, are moving along and off the South Andhra Pradesh coast. A storm surge of 1 to 1.5 metres above the astronomical tide is likely to inundate low-lying areas of South Coastal Andhra Pradesh, with the maximum peak surge expected over Bapatla, Krishna and Prakasam districts. Residents in Prakasam and other affected districts can reach out to the dedicated disaster support line, Mana Mitra, at 9552300009 for assistance or to report emergencies.

Heavy rainfall is being reported across parts of South Coastal Andhra Pradesh. Nellore, Prakasam, Bapatla, Palnadu, Guntur, Krishna, NTR, Eluru and West Godavari districts are among the affected areas. Heavy to very heavy rainfall is also forecast in parts of Coastal Andhra Pradesh and Rayalaseema during the next 12 hours.Chief Minister Chandrababu Naidu reviewed the situation with officials and district collectors. He directed them to ensure public safety and prevent loss of life and property. Fallen trees in several areas have been cleared. People living in unsafe areas have been shifted to relief camps. Schools and colleges have been closed in four districts. Emergency and relief teams are on alert. The South Coast Railway has opened round-the-clock control rooms. The government has notified a new set of rules governing India’s civil aviation sector, replacing the nearly nine-decade-old Aircraft Rules, 1937. The new rules called the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026, will come into effect on 1st of January, next year. The rules provide the regulatory framework on various aspects of the aviation sector including aircraft registration, manufacturing, safety certification, maintenance, flight operations, personnel licensing and airport operations.

Airlines operating scheduled air services are required to determine fares based on factors such as operating costs and submit tariff details to the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA). The provision allows the Centre to issue written directions if it determines that an extraordinary situation has arisen and intervention is necessary in the public interest. The new rules also mandate greater transparency in airfare pricing, requiring airlines to publish their fares on official websites and other digital platforms. The DGCA will continue to monitor tariffs set by scheduled airlines. One of the key areas covered by the rules is the registration and certification of aircraft. They specify requirements for registering aircraft in India and obtaining certificates that establish whether an aircraft is safe to fly.

The Election Commission of India (ECI) has reviewed preparations for the 2027 Uttar Pradesh Assembly elections in 18 districts across four divisions and directed senior officials to strengthen voter lists, security arrangements and personnel training to ensure free, fair and peaceful polling.

The review meeting was chaired by Senior Deputy Election Commissioner Manish Garg in Meerut in the presence of Uttar Pradesh Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) Navdeep Rinwa and other senior commission officials, according to a statement issued by the CEO’s office.

Mr Garg emphasised the importance of the review for both first-time district election officers and police chiefs and experienced officials, noting that election rules and guidelines undergo periodic changes. He urged all officials to examine the issues discussed afresh and improve their preparations accordingly.

He also stressed ensuring minimum assured facilities at polling stations and launching EVM awareness programmes. The senior deputy election commissioner instructed authorities to maintain strict surveillance against the illegal transportation and distribution of liquor during the elections, including checks at distilleries’ source and exit points.

He also called for effective arrangements for mobile phone deposit boxes at polling stations on polling day, with the assistance of volunteers. Officials were asked to ensure training for state-level master trainers and familiarise relevant personnel with postal ballot-related instructions.

Rinwa directed officials to launch special drives in classes 11 and 12 and degree colleges to enrol young people becoming eligible to vote for the first time. He also asked authorities to re-examine rejected Form 6 applications to ensure that eligible citizens are included in electoral rolls.

Applicants filling Form 6 should provide their mobile numbers and complete addresses accurately, while Common Service Centre operators should be trained immediately in the online application process and measures to prevent multiple entries. The CEO further directed district election officers to recheck all Forms 6, 7 and 8 entered by computer assistants since the conclusion of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. The CEO also directed officials to ensure distribution of voter information slips five days before polling and record the distribution in booth-level officers’ registers.

The midterm elections in the United States will be held today. Americans will vote for all 435 seats in the House of Representatives, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships.

The poll will decide whether Republicans retain control of Congress or Democrats win back one or both chambers during the final two years of President Donald Trump’s term. The voting will start and close at different times across the United States, with results expected to begin coming in as votes are counted. The outcome of the midterm poll will decide which political party – Trump’s Republicans or opposition Democrats – controls Congress and how easy it is for the president to carry out his plans. Apart from the ongoing war between the United States and Iran, the state of US economy remains a key issue for voters ahead of the midterm elections. Immigration is another key issue with Republicans saying Trump has delivered on a major campaign pledge in 2024 by tightening the border and launching a widespread crackdown on illegal immigration.
