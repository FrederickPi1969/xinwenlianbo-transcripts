## Morning News

India and New Zealand Free Trade Agreement (FTA) comes into force on the occasion of Vijay Dashami today. The agreement will provide duty-free access for 100 per cent of Indian exports to New Zealand. Addressing a ceremony on India-New Zealand Free Trade Agreement in New Delhi, Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal said that the agreement is aligned with the vision of Prime Minister Narendra Modi for Viksit Bharat 2047. He said, the agreement opens significant new opportunities for India’s exporters, particularly MSMEs, artisans, farmers and pharma. The Minister highlighted that both the countries have agreed to work towards the goal of doubling bilateral trade in goods and services with New Zealand to approximately 35 thousand crore rupees by 2030.

Mr Goyal said that India’s farmers will gain wider market access for fruits, vegetables, spices and other agricultural produce. He said, the agreement will help improve productivity, support higher incomes and strengthen livelihoods.

The Minister said that New Zealand’s commitment to facilitate 20 billion US dollars in investment into India holds immense potential to strengthen manufacturing, infrastructure, services and innovation. Mr Goyal said, this agreement will also create employment opportunities.

On the occasion, the first-ever export of a JCB machine from India to New Zealand was flagged off by Mr Goyal. Flag-offs of a range of products across the agriculture, gems and jewellery, engineering, sporting goods, footwear and leather sectors are also planned for the day. More from our correspondent :

“India has steadily expanded its global trade partnerships to strengthen economic growth, create jobs, and enhance its global standing. In recent years, the country has signed nine Free Trade Agreements covering 38 developed countries. The India-New Zealand FTA marks a significant milestone in this effort. The FTA between the two countries was signed in April this year at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi. The agreement protects India’s farmers’ interests, boosts labour-intensive sectors like textiles and leather, and opens new opportunities for Indian professionals, students, and service providers. The Bilateral merchandise trade between India and New Zealand stood at around 1.1 billion US dollar in 2025-26. For the first time, New Zealand has facilitated trade in Ayurveda, yoga, and other traditional medicine services with India. With Dipendra Kumar, Anand Kumar, Akashvani News Delhi.”

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh has said the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) must convert the political vision of its leaders into practical cooperation that is visible in the lives of the people of the region. Addressing the SCO Council of Heads of Government meeting in Tajikistan today, Mr Singh said that the organisation’s 25-year journey provided an opportunity to review its achievements and discuss its future direction. The Minister emphasised that the three pillars of security, connectivity and opportunity are mutually reinforcing and essential for achieving tangible benefits for the people.

Mr Singh said terrorism remains one of the gravest threats to peace and development of the region. He said that terrorism causes the loss of innocent lives, disrupts investment and economic capabilities, radicalises societies and deprives young people of opportunities. Mr Singh asserted that there can be no justification for terrorism, no double standards in combating it, and no place for those who finance, support or provide safe havens to terrorists. He called for collectively dismantling the entire ecosystem of terrorism.
