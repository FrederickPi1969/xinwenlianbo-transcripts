## Morning News

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has said that he directed the departments to prepare a proposal for fast-track courts to tackle paper leaks and they worked continuously and submitted the draft to him late last night.  In a video message last night, Mr Modi said, this draft includes provisions for fast-track courts and stringent punishment, will be discussed by the Cabinet today. He said, after incorporating the suggestions of Cabinet colleagues, it will be given its final shape. The Prime Minister said, as the second week of Parliament begins on Monday, the Government will make every effort to get this Bill passed by the House at the earliest.

The Prime Minister said that over the past two and a half months since the paper leak incident, several steps have been taken. The culprits have been arrested and are in jail. Mr Modi said, Government’s foremost responsibility was to ensure that students did not lose an academic year and it was essential to conduct the examinations immediately.

The Delhi High Court has constituted a Fast Track Court to hear cases under the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024. The move is aimed at ensuring speedy disposal of cases related to unfair practices in public examinations.

The newly designated Fast Track Court will function at the Rouse Avenue Courts under the charge of judicial officer Anu Grover Baliga. According to a notification issued by the Delhi High Court, the court will hear cases registered under the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024, along with connected offences. Ms Baliga, who was serving as the judge in charge of the Mediation Centre at Tis Hazari Courts, has been transferred to the Rouse Avenue Courts to head the specially designated court. The initiative is aimed at ensuring expeditious adjudication of cases involving the use of unfair means in public examinations. The notification was issued hours after Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the Centre’s decision to establish fast-track courts to ensure swift and stringent punishment for those involved in examination paper leaks. The sacred city of Puri in Odisha is witnessing the Bahuda Yatra today, marking the ceremonial return journey of Lord Jagannath, Lord Balabhadra and Devi Subhadra from the Gundicha Temple to the Shree Jagannath Temple.

“The sacred city of Puri has come alive with an atmosphere of profound devotion and spiritual fervour as millions of devotees gather to witness the Bahuda Yatra, the auspicious homeward journey of Lord Jagannath, Lord Balabhadra and Devi Subhadra. The return journey symbolises the divine reunion of the sibling deities with their eternal abode after their annual sojourn at theShree Gundicha Temple. Amid the resonant chants of devotees and the rhythmic beating of traditional percussion, the deities will be ceremonially escorted in the Pahandi procession and seated on their magnificent chariots. During the journey, Lord Jagannath’s chariot will halt at the Mausi Maa Temple, where the Lord will be offered Poda Pitha, a traditional baked cake prepared from rice, lentils and jaggery. The Bahuda Yatra embodies Odisha’s timeless spiritual legacy and the enduring values of faith, compassion, inclusiveness and universal brotherhood. PRAKASH DASH FOR AKASHVANI NEWS”

## Evening News

The Union Cabinet today approved a draft Bill to set up fast-track courts and to ensure stricter punishment in cases of paper leaks and exam malpractices. The Cabinet meeting chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi at the Parliament House complex approved the draft bill. Sources told Akashvani News that the proposed legislation is expected to be introduced in the Parliament next week.

In a video message yesterday, Prime Minister Modi had announced about bringing a bill with provisions of strong action in cases of paper leaks.

Meanwhile, in a major bid to bring out serious examination reforms, the National Testing Agency has terminated 47 officials from service. According to sources, Legal and criminal actions will also be taken against some of these officials. This is part of a complete overhaul of NTA that has been in controversy over paper leaks.

Union Ministers J P Nadda and Dr Jitendra Singh today met the CJP delegation in New Delhi. Talking to media after the meeting Mr Nadda said, they gave a patient hearing to the demands of the CJP regarding reforms in the examination system. Mr Nadda said that the CJP had put forth 3 main demands and also suggested some reforms to curb examination paper leak. Mr Nadda added that the Government will hold another round of discussion with the delegation tomorrow.
Spokesperson of CJP Ashutosh Ranka, who was part of the CJP delegation claimed that the Government has in-principle agreed to two of their demands.
