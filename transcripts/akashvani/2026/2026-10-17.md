The Centre has made Aadhaar authentication or proof of possession of an Aadhaar number necessary to avail scholarship benefits under the PM CARES for Children Scheme. The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment has issued a notification in this regard. It said that beneficiaries of the Special Scholarship Scheme under PM CARES for Children, which provides assistance for continuing education from Class 1st to 12th, will be required to undergo Aadhaar authentication or furnish proof of possession of an Aadhaar number. The notification stated that no child will be denied assistance for failure to authenticate Aadhaar or for not having been assigned an Aadhaar number. In such cases, the benefit will be provided after verifying the child’s identity and establishing the relationship with the parent or legal guardian through the documents specified in the notification. Our correspondent has the details.

“The notification issued by the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment provides separate document requirements for beneficiaries below 18 years of age and those aged 18 years or above who have not been assigned an Aadhar number. Children below 18 years of age can establish their identity by submitting an enrolment acknowledgement containing the Enrolment ID and a document establishing their age, such as a birth certificate, Indian passport or a Class 10th or Class 12th certificate or marks statement issued by a recognised school education board. For beneficiaries aged 18 or above, the notification lists documents such as an Election Commission issued voter identity card, driving license, Indian passport, ration card along with other specified identity documents. Notification also provides for alternative authentication methods, including biometric or one-time password-based verification, wherever feasible, if Aadhaar authentication fails. SHIVANG MISRA, AKASHVANI NEWS, DELHI”

The Supreme Court has called for stronger technological and regulatory measures to detect and remove child sexual exploitation and abuse material from the internet. A Bench of Justices JB Pardiwala and KV Chandran directed the creation of a comprehensive national database to identify and remove such content across social media platforms. The Court expressed concern over the continued circulation of this material despite its 2024 directions. ​Highlighting that technological safeguards alone are insufficient, the Bench emphasized the need to equip children with awareness and safe internet practices. It called for digital safety and age-appropriate education to be included in the core curriculum. The Union Government has been directed through the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology and the Ministry of Law and Justice to frame necessary guidelines, standard operating procedures, and IT rules amendments. The government must file an affidavit detailing its implementation progress by the next hearing on December 10, 2026.

The Supreme Court has declared that the right to access and use the internet is a fundamental right guaranteed to every individual, including children, under Articles 21 and 19(1)(a) of the Constitution. A bench of Justice JB Pardiwala and Justice K Vinod Chandran observed that children, irrespective of their age, enjoy these constitutional protections. Reversing earlier judicial hesitation on whether internet access itself is a fundamental right, the apex court affirmed that protecting children from online harms cannot be a ground to deny them digital access altogether.
