The three works represent different aspects of India’s knowledge traditions. Rajatarangini is a historical account of Kashmir, the Mahabharata explores Dharma, duty and human values, while Arthashastra deals with governance, diplomacy and statecraft. The development was announced by Member Secretary, Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA),  Dr. Sachchidanand Joshi who played an important role in preparing the nomination dossiers for all three works.

The Supreme Court has ruled that the 24-hour constitutional deadline for producing an arrested person before a magistrate begins from the moment of detention, and not from the time the police formally records the arrest. A bench of Justices Ujjal Bhuyan and Atul S. Chandurkar held that there can be no artificial distinction between ‘arrest’ and ‘detention’ while interpreting Article 22(2) of the Constitution.

The bench observed that any interpretation allowing the police to exclude the period of detention before formal arrest would be contrary to the constitutional mandate. The court clarified that the only period that can be excluded while calculating the 24-hour deadline is the time reasonably required to travel from the place of arrest to the magistrate’s court. Time spent on inquiry, investigation, search or seizure before formal arrest cannot be excluded.
