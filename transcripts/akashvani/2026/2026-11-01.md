Prime Minister Narendra Modi has approved Northern Bullet Train Corridor which will connect Delhi to Guwahati via Siliguri. This was disclosed by Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw in Varanasi yesterday. The Minister was in the city to flag off Varanasi-New Delhi Vande Bharat Sleeper Express. The Northern Bullet Train Corridor will cover approximately 1,500 km and will pass through key cities across Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and West Bengal before extending into Assam. The key stations include Delhi, Agra, Kanpur, Lucknow, Ayodhya, Varanasi, Prayagraj, Patna, Siliguri, and Guwahati. Mr Vaishnaw said, Northern Bullet Train Corridor aims to revolutionize connectivity across northern and eastern India, cutting down the current 20-hour journey between Delhi and Siliguri to just six hours.

The Bengaluru-Mumbai Vande Bharat Sleeper Train will be inaugurated today. Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw will flag off the train from Sir M. Visvesvaraya Terminal in Bengaluru, Karnataka. Several important financial, tax, and administrative changes have come into effect from today. Small businesses selling goods online will find it easier to register across states, while taxpayers can expect faster refunds. The changes were approved at the 57th GST Council meeting last month. Small suppliers operating through e-commerce platforms will no longer need to maintain a physical office in every state where their goods are stored, provided they meet the prescribed conditions. A Winter Action Plan will come into effect in the national capital from today to combat air pollution. The Delhi Government has said that under the plan, vehicles from outside Delhi that do not comply with BS-VI emission standards will not be allowed to enter the national capital. The city government will also impose restrictions on construction and demolition activities to curb dust pollution. Construction sites will be monitored through AI-enabled cameras directly connected to the government’s Command and Control Centre. The Winter Action Plan also includes the implementation of 50 per cent work-from-home and the ‘No PUC, No Fuel’ policy. Under this policy, vehicle owners will not be able to purchase petrol, diesel, CNG or LPG at fuel stations unless their vehicles have a valid Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate

The Centre has withdrawn the special additional excise duty on petrol exports while increasing export levies on diesel and aviation turbine fuel ATF. According to notifications issued by the Ministry of Finance, under the revised rates, petrol exports will attract no export duty, compared to the earlier levy of two rupees 50 paise per litre. The export duty on diesel has been increased from eight rupees 50 paise to 11 rupees 50 paise per litre while the levy on ATF has been raised from 10 rupees 50 paise to 11 rupees per litre. The revised rates came as the government continues its fortnightly review of fuel export duties in response to fluctuations in international crude oil and refined petroleum product prices.

Aviation turbine fuel ATF prices has been increased by one rupee 37 paise per litre and commercial LPG rate has been increased by 10 rupees per 19-kg cylinder. ATF prices were increased to 138 rupees 50 paise per litre from 137 rupees 12 rupees per litre. With the increase, the price of a 19-kg commercial LPG cylinder has touched two thousand 820 rupees.
