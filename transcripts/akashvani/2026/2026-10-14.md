The First BIMSTEC Home Ministers’ Meeting concluded in New Delhi with adoption of Delhi Declaration. In the declaration, the BIMSTEC Nations have strongly condemned terrorism and violent extremism in all its forms and manifestations including cross-border terrorism. More from our correspondent;

“In the declaration, the BIMSTEC nations reiterated their determination to prevent, combat and eliminate terrorism and violent extremism conducive to terrorism in a comprehensive and sustained manner, in accordance with the UN Charter and international law. They also reaffirmed commitment to strengthening regional cooperation under BIMSTEC for realizing a connected, secure, resilient, developed and prosperous BIMSTEC region. They also expressed deepest condolences and solidarity with the victims of terrorist attacks in Member States and their families, and with the Governments of the affected States. The nations called for those responsible to be brought to justice in accordance with applicable domestic and international law. The Delhi Declaration also called for effective implementation of the finalized Plans of Action in areas of Human Trafficking, Narcotic Drugs and Precursor Chemicals and Anti-Money Laundering as well as finalisation of the Plan of Action by the Sub-Group on Cooperation in Countering Radicalization and Terrorism. It also called for effective implementation of the BIMSTEC Convention on Mutual Legal Assistance in Criminal Matters, which was signed in March 2022 at 5th BIMSTEC Summit in Colombo. The BIMSTEC nations also reaffirmed the principle that cooperation within the BIMSTEC in security sector is based on respect for the principle of sovereign equality, territorial integrity, political independence, non-interference in internal affairs, non-aggression, peaceful co-existence and mutual respect. With Shivang’s report, Yashashvi Akashvani News, Delhi.”

The Sela Tunnel in Arunachal Pradesh stands as a remarkable example of this commitment. A report;

Located at an altitude of approximately 13 thousand feet in Arunachal Pradesh, the Sela Tunnel represents a significant engineering achievement in the challenging terrain of the Eastern Himalayas. Built at a cost of around 825 crore rupees, the project comprises two tunnels, measuring approximately 1 thousand 3 metres and 1 thousand 595 metres in length. It has been designed to provide more reliable connectivity between Tezpur in Assam and Tawang in Arunachal Pradesh, particularly during periods of heavy snowfall and adverse weather conditions. The tunnel holds immense strategic significance as it helps overcome the challenges posed by the high-altitude Sela Pass. Improved connectivity facilitates the movement of armed forces, military equipment and essential supplies, thereby strengthening operational preparedness in this sensitive border region. Beyond its strategic importance, the project also carries significant benefits for local communities. More dependable road connectivity can improve access to healthcare, education, essential services and markets, while providing a boost to tourism and other economic activities. The Sela Tunnel reflects India’s continued emphasis on developing modern infrastructure in remote and border areas, ensuring that geographical challenges do not become barriers to progress. A vital link between security and development, the Sela Tunnel stands as a symbol of engineering excellence, improved connectivity and the nation’s commitment to strengthening its border regions. India’s Wholesale Price Index (WPI) based inflation rose sharply to 11.39 per cent in September, from 9.92 per cent in August, on an annual basis. According to the provisional data released by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, the annual inflation last month was driven by higher prices of mineral oils, food articles, crude petroleum and natural gas, coal and lignite, basic metals, chemicals and chemical products, textiles, rubber and plastic products and electrical equipment.

Among the major categories in WPI, year on year inflation in the Fuel and Power group surged to 25.85 per cent last month, from 22.93 per cent in August. Inflation in Manufactured Products rose to 9.76 per cent, from 8.37 per cent and inflation in Primary Articles edged up to 8.67 per cent, from 7.76 per cent during the same period.

India has recorded a 21 per cent decline in new tuberculosis cases between 2015 and 2024, compared to a 12 per cent global decline. According to the World Health Organisation’s Global Tuberculosis Report, the country’s TB treatment coverage has reached 92 per cent, against the global average of 78 per cent. Our correspondent reports that the government has intensified efforts towards eliminating tuberculosis through expanded screening, advanced technology and community participation.

India’s declining TB incidence reflects the impact of sustained public health efforts, strong community participation, and continued commitment to strengthening tuberculosis prevention and control initiatives across the country. Under the TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan, more than 28 crore people have been screened, over 20 lakh individuals have received TB Preventive Treatment, and five lakh 70 thousand new Ni-kshay Mitras have joined the initiative. The National TB Elimination Programme provides free screening and treatment services at all public health facilities and private healthcare centres. Health and Family Welfare Ministry has launched JOSH (Joint Squad for Health) to further strengthen community participation in India’s fight against tuberculosis. With Dipendra Kumar, Akshit Kumar Vaidyan, Akashvani News Delhi.
