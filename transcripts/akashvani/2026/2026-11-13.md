India has achieved a significant milestone in its fight against tuberculosis (TB), recording a 38 percent reduction in TB mortality since 2015. This is higher than the global reduction of 35 per cent, reflecting the country’s sustained efforts towards eliminating the disease.

India’s reduction in tuberculosis (TB) mortality highlights the country’s strong performance in reducing TB-related deaths. According to the World Health Organization’s Global Tuberculosis Report 2026, released yesterday, India has also achieved an impressive 94 per cent treatment success rate among patients suffering from tuberculosis. Health and Family Welfare Ministry said that the progress reflects the impact of intensified interventions under the National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP), including proactive case finding including use of AI enabled X-rays, rapid diagnostics, prompt treatment initiation, differentiated TB care and nutritional support. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has proposed reforms to simplify the rules for governing cab rental businesses, promote ease of doing business and strengthen passenger safety. Under the proposed reforms, operators will be allowed to offer cabs with or without drivers. The minimum fleet requirement will be reduced from 50 to five vehicles, with at least 2 cabs per place of operation. The requirement for branches in five tourist cities is removed. The reforms also include a single electronic licence, valid across the State or Union Territory for a uniform fee. Approved places of operation may include airports, stations and bus terminals. It further added that licence grants and renewals have a proposed 60-day timeline. Adding or removing cabs or places of operation has a 30 day deemed-approval mechanism.

The four-day long Chhath Puja will begin today with the ‘Nahay-Khay’ ritual in different parts of the country. Dedicated to Surya Dev or the Sun God and Chhathi Maiya, the festival is observed with strict purity, fasting, and rituals along riverbanks and ghats. The Nahay-Khay’ is marked by ritual bathing and preparation of food while observing strict cleanliness and purity. Traditionally, devotees prepare Kaddubhaat as prasad, marking the beginning of the four-day festival.

The second day will be observed as ‘Kharna’. Devotees will observe a day-long fast and break it in the evening after offering prayers and consuming prasad. This will be followed by the rigorous ‘nirjala’ fast.
On the third day, devotees will gather at rivers, ponds and other Chhath ghats in the evening to worship Chhathi Maiya and offer ‘arghya’ to the setting sun.

The festival will conclude on Monday morning with devotees offering ‘arghya’ to the rising sun.
