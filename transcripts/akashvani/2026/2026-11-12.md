## Morning News

Union Cabinet has approved the development of Yuge Yugeen Bharat Museum in the North and South Block complex in New Delhi. Briefing media after the Union Cabinet meeting, Information and Broadcasting Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said, the museum will integrate contemporary museum practices, immersive technologies and visitor-centric interpretation.

The Cabinet has also approved the Nutrient Based Subsidy rates for RABI Season, 2026-27 on Phosphatic and Potassic fertilizers. The tentative budgetary requirement for Rabi 2026-27 will be approximately 62 thousand 370 crore rupees. This is nearly 24 thousand 417 crore more than the budgetary requirement for Rabi 2025-26 season. Mr Vaishnaw said the subsidy will ensure smooth availability of these fertilizers to the farmers at affordable prices.

The Cabinet has also given nod to the continuation of Stand-Up India Scheme for a period of five years upto 31st of March 2031.

The Cabinet has also cleared the extension of Aqua Line Metro Rail Corridor from Noida Sector-51 to Greater Noida Sector-04 of Noida Metro Rail Project covering approximately 8.5 kilometres and comprising five elevated stations.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has stated that the Yuge Yugeen Bharat Museum will transform New Delhi into a global centre of cultural heritage, with the world’s largest museum display area. In a social media post, the Prime Minister noted that the Cabinet’s decision will give impetus to the transformation of the historic North and South Block buildings into a magnificent celebration of India’s civilisational journey.

Mr Modi said the museum will showcase thousands of years of India’s heritage through world-class galleries and immersive experiences. It will cover a wide range of subjects, including ancient knowledge systems, mathematics, astronomy, architecture, art, languages, and spirituality. He highlighted that these buildings, which have long served as centres of governance, will now become places where millions can discover, experience, and celebrate the timeless richness of India’s civilisation.

The Prime Minister described the initiative as a historic new chapter in India’s cultural heritage.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi will address Bharat Agriculture Conference 2026 at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi this evening. Around 2,000 farmers from across India, alongside policymakers, diplomats, industry leaders, and agricultural scientists are attending the 2-day event. Approximately 20 progressive farmers and Padma Shri recipients are scheduled to share their innovation and resilience stories. Discussions center on increasing farmer incomes, adopting precision farming and artificial intelligence-led resource management, developing climate-resilient crops, and expanding India’s agricultural exports.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi will be on a two day visit to Karnataka and Gujarat beginning tomorrow.

Mr Modi will participate in Golden Jubilee Celebrations of Prashanti Kutiram,  the residential headquarters campus of Swami Vivekananda Yoga Anusandhana Samsthana (S-VYASA) tomorrow morning. In the afternoon, the Prime Minister will flag off and undertake a metro ride from Tavarekere to IIM Bangalore metro station. After this, Mr Modi will lay the foundation stone, inaugurate and dedicate to the nation various development projects worth over 15 thousand 600 crore rupees in Bengaluru.

On the same day, the Prime Minister will travel to Mysuru where, he will participate in a tree plantation programme at Bharat Vann. In the evening, he will inaugurate COSMOS-Mysuru Planetarium.

Government has rationalized the visa regime by reducing the number of main visa categories from 13 to 9 and the number of sub-categories from 49 to 23. Ministry of Home Affairs said that the move is aimed at boosting foreign tourist arrivals, enhance medical value travel and foreign student arrivals. The step follows the earlier rationalisation undertaken in October last year when the main visa categories were reduced from 22 to 13 and sub-categories from 63 to 49. Ministry said that the required filed and column for all 23 categories and sub-categories of Visas have been reduced by 65 to 70 per cent.

Today is the Public Service Broadcasting Day. The day is observed every year on this day to mark the historic address of Father of the nation Mahatma Gandhi, through Akashvani. An important day in the History of Broadcasting, Mahatma Gandhi paid his first and only visit to the Broadcasting House in New Delhi on this day in 1947. More from our correspondent:

“On this day in 1947, Mahatma Gandhi addressed the grief-stricken people staying in Kurukshetra who were displaced during partition through a special broadcast. In the year 2000, then Union Minister for Information and Broadcasting, Sushma Swaraj announced that every 12th of November would henceforth be observed as Public Service Broadcasting Day. Since then, Akashvani has been celebrating the occasion every year with special programmes and events. Akashvani music artists render bhajans dear to Mahatma Gandhi to mark these celebrations. Throughout the year, Akashvani broadcasts and organizes a series of special programmes across the country based on the Gandhian Philosophy. Public Service Broadcasting Day inspires Broadcasters to carry forward the ideals and teachings of Mahatma Gandhi, to every corner of the country. With Arzoo, Samarth Singh for Akashvani News, Delhi.”

India has been ranked 28th in the Climate Change Performance Index. The annual index tracks the climate mitigation performance of 64 countries and the European Union. According to the report, India retains an overall medium rating, with medium scores in greenhouse gas emissions, energy use and climate policy, while receiving a high rating for its renewable energy trend.

The report highlights India’s progress in solar and wind energy, rooftop solar installations and electric vehicles. It notes that non-fossil fuel sources accounted for 50 per cent of India’s cumulative installed electricity capacity in June 2025, five years ahead of its earlier 2030 target.
