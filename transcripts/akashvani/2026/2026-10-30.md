Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) will launch the navigation satellite NVS-03 aboard the GSLV-F18 rocket at 11:51 PM IST tonight. The liftoff will take place from the Second Launch Pad at the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh. This mission marks the 20th flight of India’s Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV). The rocket will deploy the satellite into a Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (GTO). NVS-03 is the third, second-generation navigation satellite designed to maintain and boost the continuity of the Navigation with Indian Constellation (NavIC) system. To enhance precise positioning, the satellite carries an indigenous atomic clock alongside procured ones. The NavIC system provides positioning, navigation, and timing services covering the Indian mainland and up to 1,500 km beyond its borders. A successful deployment tonight will restore NavIC’s basic operational requirements to four satellites, laying the groundwork for upcoming NVS-04 and NVS-05 launches to fully stabilize India’s independent navigation capabilities.

The Depression (Remnant of Cyclonic Storm “Arnab”) over central Chhattisgarh and adjoining Odisha has moved nearly northwards and weakened into a Well Marked Low Pressure Area over central parts of Chhattisgarh. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) said that it is very likely to continue to move north-northeastwards, and weaken further into a low pressure area over Northeast Chhattisgarh and neighbourhood by today evening. IMD has issued a red colour warning for very heavy to extremely heavy rainfall over Bihar today.

The impact of Cyclonic Storm ‘Arnab’ has intensified in Bihar. Several parts of the state have been experiencing rainfall since yesterday. India Meteorological Department has issued a rain alert for 32 districts, including Patna, for today. A Red Alert has been issued for Madhubani and Sitamarhi districts, where extremely heavy rainfall is likely. Orange and Yellow Alerts have been issued for other districts of the state. Meteorological Department has also warned of thunderstorms with wind speeds of 40 to 50 kilometres per hour, along with the possibility of lightning strikes.

Floods triggered by incessant torrential post-cyclone rainfall have severely affected Ganjam and Gajapati districts of Odisha, with Aska, Dharakote, Sorada and Bhanjanagar among the worst-hit areas. Hundreds of villages have been affected, while thousands remain marooned, prompting large-scale rescue and evacuation operations. The Indian Coast Guard has launched helicopter rescue operations to evacuate people stranded in inaccessible Ganjam and Gajapati villages, while NDRF, ODRAF and Fire Service personnel are engaged in rescue and relief operations. The Rushikulya river is flowing above the danger mark at several locations, inundating villages and low-lying areas. Berhampur, Aska and Bhanjanagar have also reported waterlogging. More than one thousand villages have been badly affected by the current flood. A Central team is expected to arrive soon in Odisha to assess the damage due to Cyclone Arnab. East Coast Railway has cancelled nine trains and diverted fourteen others because railway tracks and bridges flooded. Educational institutions in Ganjam and Gajapati will remain closed till tomorrow, while schools and colleges in flood-hit Sundargarh district are also closed today. The IMD has forecast thunderstorms and lightning in parts of Odisha today, with heavy rain likely at isolated places along the south Odisha coast. Prakash Dash for Akashvani News, Bhubaneswar.

The Central Water Commission CWC has issued a special flood advisory for West Bengal and Assam following a reported Glacial Lake Outburst Flood at Shintaphu Lake in Bhutan, warning that water levels in Alipurduar and Dhubri districts could rise suddenly as floodwaters move downstream through the Sankosh River system. In its advisory issued yesterday, the CWC said the Glacial Lake Outburst Flood had been reported by the Indian Embassy in Bhutan and that a flash flood in the upper reaches of the Punatsangchhu River, known as the Sankosh in India, could lead to a sudden rise in water levels. The advisory said, the flood water will travel downstream and will reach the border in 6-8 hrs and is likely to impact the district of Alipurduar in West Bengal and Dubri in Assam.

In Alipurduar district, more than seven thousand people living in vulnerable areas along the river have already been evacuated and shifted to 18 relief camps as a precautionary measure.
