The Government has notified the Schedule of Permissible Works under the Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) [VB-G RAM G] Act, 2025, providing for 375 permissible works under the new rural works framework. Ministry of Rural Development said, the new Schedule represents a 41 percent expansion over the 266 works under the previous framework. A report::

“The works have been reorganised around four thematic pillars – Water Security, Core Rural Infrastructure, Livelihood-related Infrastructure and Special Works for Extreme Weather Events and Disaster Preparedness. The Water Security pillar comprises 128 works, the highest among the four categories. It includes check dams, gully plugs, underground dykes, recharge shafts, injection wells, rejuvenation of rivers, rivulets and nallahs, reclamation of waterlogged land and renovation of field water-distribution channels. Every work will originate through a bottom-up, decentralized planning process under the Viksit Gram Panchayat Plan, approved by the Gram Sabha, and will be tracked through the Viksit Bharat National Rural Infrastructure Stack. ANUPAM MISHRA, AKASHVANI NEWS, DELHI.“

“India’s maritime sector has been the backbone of the country’s trade and has grown manifold over the years. To harness its more than 11,000 -kilometre-long coastline, around 14,500 kilometres of potentially navigable waterways and strategic location on key international maritime trade routes, the Government launched the Sagarmala Programme in 2015 under the leadership of Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

The programme aims to promote port-led development by modernizing ports, improving connectivity, promoting coastal shipping and strengthening coastal communities. Prime Minister Narendra Modi has emphasized harnessing India’s maritime potential to drive economic growth and development.

He highlighted that the decade from 2015 to 2025 witnessed the highest sustained agricultural growth since Independence, surpassing all previous records. Mr Modi said that such transformation is possible when the intent is clear and policies are well-defined.

After nearly three-and-a-half years, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) today raised the key benchmark policy rate by 25 basis points to 5.5 per cent in a bid to contain rising inflation amid continuing global and geopolitical uncertainties. The last repo rate hike was in February 2023, when the RBI raised the rate by 0.25 per cent to 6.50 per cent. It kept the rate unchanged through 2023-24 before beginning its rate-cut cycle in 2025. Announcing the bi-monthly monetary policy, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the Monetary Policy Committee unanimously decided to raise the policy repo rate by 25 basis points.

The RBI also projected real GDP growth for the current financial year at 7.1 per cent, with growth projected at 7.2 per cent in the second quarter, 6.9 per cent in the third quarter and 6.8 per cent in the fourth quarter.
