Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal has said India is in negotiations with at least 8-9 more groups of countries and individual nations that together account for another 15 trillion dollars of GDP.  Addressing a round-table meeting with the Japan India Business Consultative Committee  of the Japan Chamber of Commerce and Industry  and the Embassy of India Mr Goyal added that the country’s free trade agreements would eventually cover about 75 per cent of global trade and help position India as a trusted partner in global value chains. He said India has a large domestic market and it opens its doors to the world with nine free trade agreements signed in the last four years.  The Minister added that these nine agreements cover economies with a combined GDP of 60 trillion dollars and 38 developed countries. The earlier trade pacts with countries, including Japan and Korea , and the Asean region have opened another US Dollar 10 trillion of GDP. Mr Goyal  said that large opportunities for investments in India are there in areas like data centers, manufacturing and artificial intelligence.

Mr Goyal is on a four-day visit to Japan leading an Indian business delegation of more than 200 representatives to boost trade and investment ties between the two countries.

The Indian Railways has launched a special cleanliness drive across the railway network. It focuses on cleaning and disinfection of drinking-water tanks and filtration systems to ensure the continued availability of safe and clean drinking water for passengers at railway stations. The Ministry of Railways informed that during the drive, Zonal Railways will undertake comprehensive inspection of water sources, treatment and filtration plants, underground and overhead tanks, PVC tanks, water booths, taps and water dispensing points. India has stressed the need for diversified, adequate, predictable and sustained international financing to support land restoration and strengthen resilience to drought. Union Environment, Forest and Climate Change Minister Bhupender Yadav said this in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia today. He was addressing the Ministerial Dialogue on “Innovative Financial Mechanisms for Healthy Land and Drought Resilience” during the 17th session of the Conference of the Parties (COP17) to the UN Convention to Combat Desertification. Mr Yadav said, land restoration is not a cost, but an investment in food, water, biodiversity, livelihoods and long-term resilience. The Minister highlighted India’s blended green-finance framework, which enables financing for climate-change adaptation, sustainable land use, forestry and agriculture, afforestation and biodiversity conservation. Concluding his address, the Minister reaffirmed India’s commitment to sharing its experience, safeguards, institutional arrangements, lessons learned and areas requiring course correction.
