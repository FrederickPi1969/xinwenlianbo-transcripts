## Evening News

The Parliament has passed the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026, with the Rajya Sabha returning the legislation to the Lok Sabha after discussion. The Lok Sabha had passed the bill on Monday. The legislation amends the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956, for increasing the number of Judges of the Apex Court from the present 33 to 37 (excluding the Chief Justice of India).

Union Law and Justice Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal had moved the bill in the Rajya Sabha.

Replying to the debate in the House, Mr Meghwal said, in view of the increasing workload of the judiciary, the evolving nature of judicial matters, and the emerging constitutional and legal interpretation issues, increasing the number of judges in the Supreme Court is the need of the hour and a timely reform.

Our correspondent reports that the strength of the Judges of the Supreme Court was last increased from 30 to 33 (excluding the Chief Justice of India) by amending the original Act through the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2019.

“The increase in judge strength of the Court is one of the viable solutions for tackling the pending backlog of cases in the Supreme Court. As on 1st of January this year, over 92 thousand cases are pending in the Supreme Court. Even while working at near-full sanctioned capacity of 34 Judges including the Chief Justice of India, since 2019, the Supreme Court recorded institution of 75 thousand 410 fresh matters last year against a disposal of more than 65 thousand cases. The volume of litigation in the Supreme Court has constantly been on the rise due to a persistent gap between institution and final disposal of cases in the Court. The increase in the number of judges in the Supreme Court is expected to expedite the disposal of cases and ensure timely justice for the people. With Dipendra Kumar, Anand Kumar, Akashvani News Delhi.”

After completing the business, the Deputy Chairman adjourned the Rajya Sabha for the day.

Earlier in the day, the Upper House witnessed repeated disruptions over several issues, including alleged police action during students’ protest on the NEET paper leak issue.

The Lok Sabha has passed the Bankers’ Books Evidence Bill, 2026. The bill seeks to provide a law relating to evidence with respect to bankers’ books and to align it with contemporary digital banking practices. It aims to modernise the legal framework to meet the requirements of the present banking system. The Bill also has a provision that an electronic or digital record of a banker’s book will be admissible, valid, and legally enforceable as evidence. Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman moved the bill when the House re-assembled after the first adjournment at 2 PM. The legislation was cleared by the Lok Sabha without any discussion amid continuous uproar by the Opposition members over several issues. Following the passage of the bill, the Lok Sabha was adjourned for the day.
