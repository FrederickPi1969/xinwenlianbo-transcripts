## Morning News

National Security Advisers and senior security officials from the BRICS nations called for stronger cooperation to address the widening range of global security challenges, including terrorism and cyber threats, during a high-level meeting hosted by India in the national capital. The 16th BRICS National Security Advisers’ Meeting, held under India’s chairship of the grouping, led by India’s NSA Ajit Doval, brought together top security representatives from member countries to discuss both traditional and emerging threats confronting the international community. According to India’s Ministry of External Affairs, the discussions focused on evolving security risks that increasingly transcend national borders. Delegates exchanged views on issues ranging from energy and food security to climate-related instability and the misuse of emerging technologies by terrorist organisations.

Speaking at the meeting, National Security Advisor Ajit Doval stressed the need for collective deliberation and cooperation among BRICS nations to address the emerging security risks and evolving forms of terrorism in an increasingly digitised world.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi received a telephone call from the Amir of Qatar Sheikh Tamim Bin Hamad Al-Thani. He expressed condolences over the loss of lives of 12 Indian nationals in the accident at Ras Laffan Industrial City in Qatar on Sunday and conveyed his wishes for the speedy recovery of those injured.

Mr Modi thanked the Qatari leader for his words of sympathy for the affected families and appreciated the prompt medical assistance extended to the injured. The two leaders also reaffirmed their commitment to ensuring the wellbeing and safety of their citizens and reiterated their solidarity and support for each other.

During discussions on the situation in West Asia, Prime Minister Modi appreciated Qatar’s constructive role in the ongoing peace efforts.

India has strongly criticized Pakistani Defence Minister Khawaja Asif’s remarks over the situation in Pakistan occupied Kashmir saying, these remarks are desperate attempts by the neighbouring country to cover-up its own failings and deflect attention away from its human rights abuses. Addressing media in New Delhi, Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said New Delhi categorically rejects these fabricated claims with the contempt they deserve.

On India-UAE defence partnership, the spokesperson said India’s relationship with the UAE in recent several years has seen a major upswing.

Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has called for stronger multilateral cooperation to build resilient economies, accelerate sustainable development and ensure inclusive growth. Addressing the Global Convergence for Growth Summit virtually in New Delhi yesterday, she said that while prosperity and challenges are shared in an interconnected world, the consequences of conflicts and uncertainty fall disproportionately on developing countries and the Global South. Ms Sitharaman stressed that coordinated global action is essential to address these challenges.

Highlighting India’s economic progress, the Finance Minister said the country’s growth is primarily driven by domestic demand, supported by a largely market-determined exchange rate. She added that India continues to be among the world’s fastest-growing major economies, with GDP growth projected to remain robust at around seven per cent over the medium term. The summit was chaired by French President Emmanuel Macron.

He said this while releasing the latest Edition of the Trade Watch Quarterly report in New Delhi. Dr Lahiri added that India’s exports reached 1.84 trillion dollars and imports grew by 6.5 per cent in the last quarter. Talking about the situation in West Asia, Dr Lahiri said that the crisis was temporary and expressed hope that soon peace will prevail in the world.

India and the United States opened high-level trade talks in New Delhi to salvage and recalibrate a proposed bilateral trade agreement after changes in US tariff policy disrupted a framework negotiated earlier this year. Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal met United States Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and US Ambassador to India Sergio Gor in New Delhi yesterday. More from our Correspondent:

“Both sides are aiming to conclude an interim trade pact before 24th July, when Washington’s temporary 10 per cent tariff on imports from trading partners is set to expire. On February 20, the US Supreme Court struck down sweeping tariffs. This led the US administration to impose a temporary 10% tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act on all countries for 150 days starting February 24, which is due to expire on July 24. Following a de-escalation in bilateral trade tensions, the U.S. reduced the reciprocal tariff on most Indian goods to 18% and removed a previous 25% penalty tariff. India had also proposed reducing or eliminating tariffs on a range of US industrial and agricultural goods, including dried distillers’ grains, red sorghum for animal feed, tree nuts, fruits, soybean oil, wine and spirits, among others. Kaumudee Parik, AkasHvani News, Delhi.”

Speaking at a Mack Trucks manufacturing facility in Pennsylvania, Trump said preventing Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon remained the central objective of his administration’s policy towards the Islamic Republic.

The International Maritime Organisation (IMO) announced an evacuation plan for more than 11 thousand seafarers still stranded in the Strait of Hormuz. IMO Secretary-General Arsenio Dominguez said in a statement that, the IMO has secured the necessary safety guarantees and verified safe navigation conditions to support the evacuation operation. The operation follows the signing of a memorandum of understanding between Iran and the United States and will be carried out in close cooperation with Iran, Oman, other countries in the region, and the maritime industry.
