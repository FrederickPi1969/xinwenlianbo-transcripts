## Morning News

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has emphasised the need for effective implementation of Swachh Bharat Mission 2.0 and underlined that the mission should move beyond infrastructure creation and ensure measurable outcomes through regular monitoring, citizen participation and convergence between various stakeholders. Chairing the 51st PRAGATI Meeting in New Delhi, Mr Modi asked States to expedite the completion of solid waste management-related infrastructure, including waste processing plants and GOBARdhan plants. During the meeting, the Prime Minister reviewed seven critical infrastructure projects across the Railways, Power and Road sectors covering nine States worth around 30 thousand crore rupees.

“While reviewing power sector projects, Prime Minister emphasized the need to accelerate rooftop solar adoption across urban areas, with a special focus on cities, residential clusters and public institutions. He underlined that rooftop solar should be taken up in mission mode to reduce electricity costs, improve energy security and promote clean energy at the household and community level. While reviewing road and port connectivity projects, it was emphasised that Vadhavan Port should be developed as a model of port-led, multi-modal development, where every major mode of transport is seamlessly integrated to create a future-ready logistics ecosystem. Mr Modi said, the project should not be seen merely as a port, but as a national gateway connected through coastal shipping, inland waterways, dedicated freight corridors, high-speed rail connectivity, highways and airport linkages. While reviewing Ken-Betwa River Inter-linking Project, Prime Minister observed that Ken-Betwa project should serve as a model for other States to resolve inter-State water issues through cooperation, timely clearances, technology-based monitoring and mission-mode execution. ANUPAM MISHRA, AKASHVANI NEWS, DELHI.”

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) approved the continuation of the scheme to modernise the public distribution system (PDS) including ration transport, dealer support and technology upgrades, till March 2031. Union Home Minister Amit Shah has directed authorities to strictly enforce ‘zero tolerance’ policy towards illegal constructions within 15 km of the country’s borders and demolish all such structures that have come up over the years. The Home Ministry has assigned enhanced responsibility to the district magistrates to ensure legal and financial compliance of banking transactions in the border areas by all banks, verify big business establishments, examine their funding sources, track mule accounts and false companies, identify fake Aadhaar cards and control cross-border smuggling.

Renowned Urdu poet and literary figure Bashir Badr passed away at the age of 91 in Bhopal today. According to his family, he breathed his last at around noon. Bashir Badr had been suffering from dementia for a long time. He was laid to rest at a cemetery near Bhopal Talkies this evening. Here is a tribute:

“Ujaale apni yaadon ke hamaare saath rehne do, Na jaane kis gali mein zindagi ki shaam ho jaaye; Mohabbaton mein dikhaave ki dosti na mila, Agar gale nahin milta to haath bhi na mila.

These timeless lines capture the soul of Bashir Badr’s poetry — simple yet deeply moving, intimate yet universal. His ghazals spoke of love, loneliness, relationships, separation, and the quiet emotions hidden within everyday life. Bashir Badr gave Urdu poetry a fresh identity by writing in a language that felt close to ordinary people. His couplets continue to echo in mushairas and conversations. In 1999, Bashir Badr received the Padma Shri and Sahitya Akademi Award for his poetry collection “Aas”.“

Bashir Badr was born on 15 February 1935 in Ayodhya. He completed his post-graduation from Aligarh Muslim University. He joined Meerut College as a lecturer in the Urdu department and taught there until 1990.
