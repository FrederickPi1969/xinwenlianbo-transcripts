## Morning News

In a major overhaul of Agnipath scheme, the Union Cabinet has approved a revised framework creating a pathway for 100 percent absorption of all willing Agniveers into the Defence Forces. Briefing media after the Cabinet meeting, Chief of Defence Staff General NS Raja Subramani said, the move follows an extensive evaluation by the armed forces that gave highly positive feedback on Agniveer efficacy, physical fitness, and technological adaptability.

The decision comes just before the first batch of 18,300 Agniveers completes its initial four-year tenure next month.

“As originally planned, the top 25% of Agniveers will be offered regular permanent employment immediately after their initial 4-year tenure based on merit. The remaining 75% of Agniveers will now be given an additional engagement period of two years, subject to candidate willingness and recommendations from the forces.

Upon finishing the additional two-year extension (bringing total service to 6 years), all remaining willing Agniveers will be offered permanent enrolment as regular soldiers, provided they meet standard selection criteria.

The newly added two-year extension period will feature increased monthly remuneration, an enhanced Seva Nidhi package, and cumulative seniority benefits.

The structural reforms effectively remove the mandatory 75% exit provision, ensuring that any Agniveer who wishes to serve permanently and meets the benchmark criteria will have a direct route to do so. YASHASVI KIRYAL, for Akashvani News, DELHI.”

Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has proposed amendments to the Drugs Rules, 1945, to provide for immediate suspension of manufacturing licences in respect of drugs declared Not of Standard Quality on specified critical quality parameters. The proposed amendments seek to enable prompt regulatory intervention upon detection of substandard medicines, prevent their continued manufacture and distribution, and strengthen the mechanism for ensuring compliance in the interest of patient safety and public health.

“The Health Ministry said that under the existing provisions, suspension or cancellation of a manufacturing licence is preceded by the prescribed regulatory procedure, including issuance of a show-cause notice. The Ministry added that the proposed amendment also provides for mandatory corrective measures before revocation of the suspension. The suspension shall continue until the manufacturer undertakes an effective Root Cause Analysis and implements appropriate Corrective and Preventive Action to the satisfaction of the concerned Licensing Authority. The Ministry said that the amendments are expected to strengthen post-marketing regulatory surveillance, facilitate timely action against substandard medicines, promote greater accountability among manufacturers and marketers. The draft amendments have been notified for public consultation and  objections and suggestions have been invited  from stakeholders and members of the public within 30 days. The Supreme Court today ruled that the 24-hour constitutional deadline for producing an arrested person before a Magistrate begins from the moment of actual detention, when the person’s liberty is curtailed, and not from the time of formal arrest recorded by the police. The judgment came on appeals filed by four persons challenging a Tripura High Court decision denying them bail in a case registered under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act. A Bench of Justices Ujjal Bhuyan and Atul S. Chandurkar held that the period of 24 hours prescribed under Article 22(2) of the Constitution must be calculated from the date and time when a person is actually detained, and not from the time when the investigating agency formally records the arrest.

The Supreme Court has directed the Centre to constitute a special cell and monitor the implementation of the provisions of the Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016, which prohibits the employment of child labour in hazardous industries. A bench of Justices SVN Bhatti and NV Anjaria directed the Ministry of Women and Child Development to issue directions to the enforcing agencies through the respective chief secretaries of the States in this regard.

The act imposes a complete ban on child employment and prohibited the employment of children below 14 years in all occupations and processes.

Indian Railways has geared up to manage the ongoing festive season rush of Diwali and Chhath Puja with extensive crowd management preparations underway at several railway stations. Ministry of Railways said that Indian Railways is developing dedicated passenger holding areas, known as Yatri Suvidha Kendras, at major railway stations across the country.

Our correspondent reports that the facilities have been designed to ensure orderly passenger movement and provide a comfortable waiting environment.

“The holding areas have been designed to accommodate passengers before they proceed to the platform area. Talking to Akashvani News, Northern Railway CPRO Himanshu Kumar Upadhyay said that in New Delhi Railway station, holding area is available for both reserved and unreserved class. He said additional RPF personnel have also been deployed at Railway stations to facilitate passengers.

At Anand Vihar Terminal, the Yatri Suvidha Kendra has 14 ticket counters, 16 CCTV cameras, four electronic display boards and seating for 20 passengers. The facility also has separate toilet facilities for male and female passengers, a Divyangjan toilet, an RO plant, 11 HVLS fans and a public announcement system. The aim is to reduce congestion on platforms, in circulating areas and at station approaches by providing designated spaces for waiting and ticketing.
