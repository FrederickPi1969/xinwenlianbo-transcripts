The first batch of 26 Indian traders is expected to enter the Chinese territory of Tibet through Lipulekh pass in Pithoragarh district of Uttarakhand tomorrow, marking the resumption of border trade after a gap of six years. According to the Dharchula administration in Pithoragarh district, the first batch of Indian traders will leave for the Taklakot market in Tibet tomorrow.

“Dharchula Sub-Divisional Magistrate and Trade Officer Ashish Joshi said that 26 trade passes have been issued in the first phase, including 17 traders and 9 assistants. A customs office has also been set up at Gunji to ensure the smooth conduct of trade. Traders have already transported their goods to warehouses near the Lipulekh Pass. He said that more than 103 traders have applied for trade passes. In the second phase, another 25 traders will be granted permission. It may be recalled that border trade between India and China was suspended in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Sakshi Singh, Akashvani News, Dehradun.

Samvidhaan Hatya Diwas is being observed today. It serves as a reminder of what happened when the country’s Constitution was trampled over in 1975. It is also a day to pay homage to each and every person who suffered due to the excesses of the Emergency. A report:

“On this day in 1975, then President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed proclaimed a National Emergency under Article 352 of the Constitution, citing threats arising from internal disturbances. The Emergency was declared amid growing political unrest and judicial developments that had undermined the legitimacy of the government led by then Prime Minister Indira Gandhi. As a result, the executive assumed sweeping powers, and greater central control was exercised over state authority. Following the proclamation, several constitutional safeguards were suspended. Citizens were denied the right to seek judicial remedies for violations of these rights. Pre-censorship was imposed on newspapers, and even the Press Council of India, the statutory body responsible for safeguarding press freedom, was abolished. Constitutional Amendments during that period barred the courts from questioning the President’s decision to declare Emergency and excluded the election of the Prime Minister and Speaker of the Lok Sabha from judicial scrutiny. The Ministry of Culture is organizing the closing ceremony of year-long programs in New Delhi today, SAKLEN AKHTAR FOR AKASHVANI NEW DELHI.”

A federal judge has permanently barred President Donald Trump’s administration from implementing most of his first executive order on elections, including a proposal requiring documentary proof of citizenship for voter registration. The ruling by US District Court Judge Denise Casper in Boston effectively converts a preliminary injunction she issued a year ago, in which she temporarily blocked many of Trump’s efforts to overhaul elections, into a permanent ban. Judge Casper ruled that the Constitution gives states and Congress-not the President-the authority to regulate elections adding that Trump’s requirements violated the separation of powers.

International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) has said that it will carry out inspections in Iran under the country’s preliminary peace agreement with the US. Talking to reporters in Japan, Director General of IAEA Rafael Grossi said, they will be working on the modalities – dates, procedures, places – very soon. The head of the global nuclear watchdog said, the agreement signed last week explicitly mentioned that the dilution of Iran’s highly enriched uranium would be carried out under International Atomic Energy Agency supervision.

However, Iran’s deputy foreign minister said, access to its damaged nuclear facilities and nuclear material would only be addressed within the framework of a final deal with the US.

Government has hiked the fees for application of passports, with a 36-page fresh passport or reissue of passport now costing 2,500 rupees. The Passports (Amendment) Rules, 2026 will come into force with effect from 1st of July. In a notification, the Ministry of External Affairs said that under the new fee structure, a fresh passport or reissue of passport under Tatkal will cost five thousand rupees. The fee for 60-page ordinary passport or reissue will be 3500 rupees, and under Tatkal, this will attract six thousand rupees. The existing fee for a 36-page passport is 1,500 rupees and for a 60-page passport is two thousand rupees.

In case of an ordinary passport in lieu of lost or damaged passport containing 36 pages, a sum of five thousand rupees will be charged. For an ordinary passport in lieu of lost or damaged passport containing 60 pages, the cost will be six thousand rupees.
