Prime Minister Narendra Modi has become the longest continuously serving democratically elected Prime Minister of India today. Mr Modi took the oath of office on 26th of May in 2014. He surpassed the record of Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru who first took oath as an elected Prime Minister on 13th of May in 1952 and served until 27th of May in 1964. Pandit Nehru’s tenure as an elected Prime Minister amounted to 4 thousand 398 days. Now Prime Minister Modi’s continuous tenure is 4 thousand 399 days, making him the longest-serving elected Prime Minister in India’s history. More details on this from our Correspondent:

The achievement of Prime Minister Narendra Modi is particularly noteworthy given the scale and complexity of governance in India, a nation of over 1.4 billion people characterized by extraordinary diversity in language, culture, religion and geography. At a time when many countries have witnessed political churn, frequent changes in government and policy uncertainty, India has experienced continuity in leadership and governance. It reflects sustained public trust across three consecutive national mandates in the world’s largest democracy. Independent global surveys have consistently ranked Prime Minister Modi among the world’s most popular democratic leaders. He has also been conferred with the highest civilian honours of more than 30 countries. Prime Minister Modi’s rise from a humble socio-economic background to the highest elected office without the support of a political dynasty is widely seen as an illustration of the opportunities and social mobility offered by India’s democratic system. During Prime Minister Modi’s tenure, nearly 250 million Indians moved out of multidimensional poverty and people saw rapid expansion of digital public infrastructure, large-scale infrastructure development, improvements in welfare delivery, and broad-based socio-economic progress. During this period, India has strengthened its position as the world’s fastest-growing major economy, concluded major trade agreements with leading economies, and emerged as a more influential voice on global issues, including development, technology, climate action and the priorities of the Global South. American and Iranian forces have traded military strikes in a sharp overnight escalation that has pushed the region’s fragile truce to its limits. A Report :

“US Central Command confirmed its forces completed self-defence strikes against Iran yesterday on the direction of President Donald Trump, in response to the downing of a US Army Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz the previous day. Trump said both crew members were safe and uninjured, but stressed the United States had to respond. CENTCOM said the strikes targeted Iranian air defence systems, ground control stations and surveillance radar sites near the strait, using precision munitions from US Air Force and Navy fighter jets. Iran’s response came swiftly. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed it launched drone strikes on the US Fifth Fleet in Bahrain and the Ali Al Salem airbase in Kuwait, as well as a long-range missile strike on the al-Azraq airbase in Jordan. Jordan’s armed forces confirmed they intercepted and destroyed all five Iranian missiles fired toward the Azraq region, with no casualties or material damage reported. The IRGC claimed to have struck 21 targets at US bases across the region – a figure that could not be independently verified. WITH VINOD KUMAR’S REPORT PAYAL SHARMA AKASHVANI NEWS, DELHI”

At least 13 people, mostly children, were killed in a Pakistani military airstrikes in Afghanistan’s Kunar, Khost, and Paktika provinces. In a statement, Taliban spokesperson Zabihullah Mujahid condemned the attack and said the attack targeting civilian homes took place yesterday night. He said, the Pakistani military once again violated Afghanistan’s airspace and called it a humanitarian crime and act of aggression. The latest incident comes amid escalating tensions between Pakistan and Afghanistan over the past few months, with repeated exchanges of fire and growing concerns over civilian casualties.

NASA has announced names of three US astronauts and one Italian astronaut as the crew for its next Artemis III, a spacecraft docking demonstration mission scheduled for late next year that will test lunar landers from SpaceX and Blue Origin in orbit for the first time. A report:

“US astronauts Andre Douglas, Frank Rubio, Randy Bresnik and Italian astronaut Luca Parmitano of the European Space Agency will be the crew for Artemis III. The four-man mission will test moon landers from Elon Musk’s SpaceX and Jeff Bezos’ Blue Origin for the first time in space, setting up a delicate docking demonstration one by one. At a ceremony in Houston, NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman announced that veteran astronaut Randy Bresnik will serve as commander, with Andre Douglas and Frank Rubio as mission specialists, and Luca Parmitano of the European Space Agency as pilot. NASA astronaut Bob Hines was named as a backup crew member and will train alongside the prime crew.  Italian Space Agency President Teodoro Valente said in a statement that Parmitano’s selection as pilot for Artemis III “confirms and enhances the role and capabilities of the European and Italian space system in the human exploration of the universe. With Payal sharma’s report, Jigyasa Pandey for Akashvani news.”

Facebook’s parent company META today announced that it will establish its first AI-enabled data centre in India. As part of the agreement, META and Reliance industries will build a data center with 168 MegaWatt capacity at Jamnagar in Gujarat which META will lease, with options to scale.

The Government has refuted several social media posts falsely claiming that the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) will withdraw paper currency notes and replace them with plastic currency notes from the 30th of this month. Fact Check Unit of the Press Information Bureau (PIB) has termed the claim as fake. PIB clarified that, according to the RBI, there are no plans to withdraw paper currency notes or replace them with plastic currency notes by the end of this month.

India Meteorological Department (IMD) has said that Delhi-NCR is expected to experience significant weather changes over the next three days, with thunderstorms, dust storms, and rainfall likely to bring much-needed relief from the scorching temperatures. Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Meghalaya, Sikkim, Coastal Karnataka and Sub Himalayan West Bengal are likely to witness heavy to very heavy rainfall at isolated places during the day.

Benchmark domestic equity indices were trading around 0.6 per cent up in the afternoon session today. The Sensex rose 568 points to 74 thousand 487 while the Nifty added 136 points to 23 thousand 378, when reports last came in.
