Bihar and Assam governments have decided to withdraw cases and other legal proceedings initiated against people who participated in protests over NEET examination.

Bihar’s Home Department assured that it will immediately initiate the legal process to withdraw all FIRs, criminal complaints, and show-cause notices against agitators registered before 6 PM on 26th of July.

According to a public notice issued by the Home Department, no punitive, retaliatory, or adverse legal action will be taken against any person who participated in the protests held anywhere in the State up to 6 p.m. on 26th July. The legal process to withdraw all First Information Reports (FIRs), complaints, and show-cause notices registered during this period will be initiated immediately. The Government has also announced that all persons arrested or detained in connection with these cases will be released at the earliest. It has further clarified that no direct or indirect action will be taken against the individuals named in these cases in the future. SAVITA PAREEK, AKASHWANI SAMACHAR, PATNA.

Assam government has decided to withdraw all criminal cases registered in connection with NEET protests. Authorities have also been instructed to review all arrests and detentions made in connection with these cases and expedite the release of the arrested persons. After reviewing the circumstances surrounding the protests and taking into account the need to strengthen accountability and undertake systemic reforms in the examination and higher education ecosystem, the state government decided not to pursue further legal action against those involved. According to the order, no adverse legal action will be initiated by any police authority in Assam against persons who participated in the protests. The government also directed that the process for withdrawing all the registered cases, irrespective of the stage of legal proceedings, should be initiated promptly in accordance with the law.

## Midday News

The Supreme Court today directed the release of student protesters who are under 18 and have no criminal records. The Court asked authorities to preserve electronic and digital evidence relating to the recent nationwide protests over the NEET paper leak. The Apex Court also observed that there was a need for a fair and independent investigation into the allegations of injuries to the students and police officials during the student protests last week. Hearing a batch of petitions alleging excessive use of police force during the demonstrations, the apex court issued a slew of interim directions.

The three-judge bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant directed all states, where protests were held to preserve CCTV footage, drone recordings, body-camera footage, wireless communication records, PCR logs and other digital evidence pertaining to the protests.

It also ordered that any digital data collected on protesters be preserved but not brought into the public domain.

The bench permitted the Delhi government to continue investigations into FIRs registered but said no coercive action be taken against eligible protesting students during the pendency of the proceedings. The bench also sought responses from the chief secretaries of Delhi, Maharashtra, Bihar, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh to the allegations raised in the petitions.

The Court said, the allegations prima facie warrant consideration of a fair and independent probe, which must also address the concerns of families of over 200 injured police personnel.

## Evening News

The Lok Sabha is holding discussion on the bill proposing stricter punishment and setting up of fast-track courts in paper leak cases. The Bill seeks to amend the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024. The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026  incorporates several significant provisions, including stringent penalties such as imprisonment for up to ten years, fines of up to ten crore rupees, and the seizure of the properties of convicted offenders. To ensure the expeditious disposal of cases, the legislation mandates that every State Government and Union Territory Administration establish Special Fast Track Courts for the day-to-day trial.

Moving the Bill, Minister of State for Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions Dr Jitendra Singh said, the new legislation reflects government’s resolve not to allow anyone to compromise with the future of the youth of the country.

Participating in the discussion, Congress MP KC Venugopal stressed the need for wider educational reforms and comprehensive changes in the examination system.

Several other members including, Bansuri Swaraj and Tejasvi Surya of BJP, Priyanka Gandhi Vadra and Gaurav Gogoi of Congress, Abhisekh Banerjee of TMC, NCP(SP) MP Supriya Sule, Dr Shrikant Shinde of Shiv Sena and Akhilesh Yadav and Dimple Yadav of Samajwadi Party took part in the debate.
