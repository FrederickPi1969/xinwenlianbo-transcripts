The government will introduce the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 in the Lok Sabha today. The Bill seeks to amend the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024. It will be introduced by Union Minister Dr Jitendra Singh.

We have a report:

The bill has strong penal provisions to deal with any case of paper leaks. According to the listed agenda of Lok Sabha, Union Minister Jitendra Singh will introduce the bill in the House. The Bill introduces seven key amendments designed to plug enforcement loopholes, establish dedicated judicial mechanisms, and accelerate criminal trials. It also seeks to significantly overhaul the 2024 framework by instituting strict time frame for both investigations and judicial proceedings. State Governments and Union Territory Administrations will be empowered to designate Courts of Session as Special Fast Track Courts dedicated exclusively to trying offences under the Act. According to the provisions of the bill, proceedings in Special Fast-Track Courts must be conducted on a day-to-day basis, with trials mandated to conclude within three months from the date the chargesheet is filed. The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 was introduced in the Lok Sabha today. The Bill seeks to amend the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024.

When the House met after the first adjournment at 12 noon, Minister of State for Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions Dr Jitendra Singh introduced the Bill in the House.

During the introduction of the Bill, the opposition members were on their feet demanding discussion on alleged police action during recent student protests over the NEET issue. We have a report:-

The legislation aims to strengthen the transparency, fairness, and integrity of the examination system by establishing a robust legal framework while safeguarding the future of students. It incorporates several significant provisions, including stringent penalties such as imprisonment for up to ten years, fines of up to ten crore rupees, and the seizure of the properties of convicted offenders. To ensure the expeditious disposal of cases, the legislation mandates that every State Government and Union Territory Administration establish Special Fast Track Courts for the day-to-day trial. It further provides that investigations into offences must be completed within two months from the date the information is recorded at the police station. The trials must be concluded within three months from the filing of the chargesheet. The legislation has been brought to eliminate malpractices and ensure people’s trust in recruitment tests and entrance exams in the country. Anupam Mishra, Akashvani News Delhi.
