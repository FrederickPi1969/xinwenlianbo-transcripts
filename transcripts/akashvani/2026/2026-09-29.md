## Midday News

External Affairs Minister Dr S Jaishankar said that India’s relationship with China is better today than it was last year. Speaking at an event organised by Asia Society in New York, Dr Jaishankar said the relationship started improving once the border standoff was resolved. He said that India has long been telling China that a dip in bilateral relationship does not help either country and a long standoff does not serve interests of any nation.

Speaking about the India-US relationship, and the trade deal negotiations, the External Affairs Minister said both nations have reached a situation where there is an understanding on the trade agreement.

The External Affairs Minister also stated that the Russia-Ukraine and the Iran-Gulf conflict had a devastating impact on Global South economies. He expressed concern about the energy situation. He also cautioned that there is likelihood of a major food crisis, and significant fertiliser shortages, in the coming months.

Civil Aviation Minister K Rammohan Naidu has said that India is emerging as a major global aviation hub and working towards becoming the world’s third-largest aviation market. Addressing the curtain raiser press conference on Routes Asia 2027 in New Delhi today, Mr Naidu said that India aims to increase the number of passengers six fold from around 24 crore currently to 130 crore by 2047. The Minister highlighted that the number of operational airports in the country has increased from 74 in 2014 to more than 166 currently.

Highlighting the regional connectivity scheme UDAN, Mr Naidu said the scheme has made air travel more accessible, available and affordable across the country. The Minister said that India will host the 23rd edition of Routes Asia 2027 from 16th March next year in New Delhi. The three days event will provide a platform for the region’s airlines, airports, tourism authorities and aviation stakeholders to develop network strategies and ensure future route development across Asia Pacific.

The Sonai-Rupai Wildlife Sanctuary in Assam’s Sonitpur district has once again become a topic of national discussion. Prime Minister Narendra Modi mentioned the Sonai-Rupai forest and the return of elephants during the 138th episode of ‘Mann Ki Baat’. Mr. Modi said that elephants had once abandoned this region due to increased human interference in the forest. However, following the clearing of forest encroachments about three years ago, elephants have returned to Sonai-Rupai.

“The Sonai-Rupai Wildlife Sanctuary located in the foothills of Himalayas covers an area of 220 square kiloimeters. It was officially notified as a wildlife sanctuary on September 4, 1998. According to the Assam Forest Department’s 2024 elephant census, 140 elephants were recorded within the sanctuary. The wildlife sanctuary once which used to be the home for hundreds of elephants had abandoned it due to various human activities and land encroachments in to the sanctuary. But with the eviction of these encroached lands and strong emphasis on restoring greenery through afforestation have restored the ecological balance of the sanctuary. As a result of which herds of elephants are now being seen even in areas where they had not been spotted for many years. The mention of the Sonai-Rupai Wildlife Sanctuary in the Mann ki Baat programme by Prime Minister Narendra Modi carries a great significance in terms of the conservation efforts being carried out in the sanctuary.”

Prime Minister Narendra Modi is completing 25 years in public service, a journey defined by the mantra of Jan Seva Se Desh Seva, and a relentless commitment that continues. Guided by this vision for Seva and Sushasan, the Government has been ceaselessly working towards achieving the goal of a Viksit Bharat by 2047. Through inclusive development and social empowerment, this dedication is transforming lives. Today, in our special series, we bring you an instance of how this vision has been translated into action through the Direct Benefit Transfer.

“Introduced in 2014, Direct Benefit Transfer, DBT has transformed welfare delivery by transferring funds directly from the government to beneficiaries. It has strengthened transparency, efficiency, and accountability, by  cutting out the role of intermediaries and curbing leakages, In 1985, the then Prime Minister had observed that of every one rupee meant for the poor, only 15 paise reached the intended beneficiary. This remained unchanged until 2014. DBT sought to change that equation – putting the full benefit directly into the beneficiary’s account. By August this year, DBT had transferred over 53 lakh crore rupees across 320 schemes. The elimination of duplicate or fraudulent beneficiaries and reduction of leakages are estimated to have resulted in savings of over five lakh crore rupees. Addressing an event Prime Minister Narendra Modi said that today direct benefit transfer is done under hundreds of government schemes and it has eliminated leakage from the system.

Talking to Akashvani News, a beneficiary Dinesh Kumar Rathore from Chhattisgarh said that through DBT, the government has provided great benefits to small farmers like him and he is receiving six thousand rupees annually under the PM-KISAN Samman Nidhi scheme.

DBT has enhanced efficiency, transparency, and accountability in the government system, while strengthening citizens’ trust and confidence in governance. It has also demonstrated how digital welfare infrastructure could deliver relief at an unprecedented speed. With Dipendra Kumar, Bhanu Pratap Singh, Akashvani News, Delhi.”
