The Indian Navy commissioned INS Mangrol, the third Anti-Submarine Warfare Shallow Water Craft (ASW-SWC) built by Cochin Shipyard Limited (CSL), Kochi, at the Naval Dockyard in Mumbai today. The induction of the indigenous warship marks an important step towards strengthening the Navy’s ability to detect and counter submarine threats in coastal and shallow waters. The commissioning ceremony was presided over by Vice Admiral Sanjay Vatsayan, Flag Officer Commanding-in-Chief of the Western Naval Command. Addressing the gathering, Vice Admiral Vatsayan said that by 2034, around 60 per cent of the Navy’s platforms were expected to have an average age of less than ten years. A report:

“The INS Mangrol is equipped with modern weapons and sensors, including the Abhay hull-mounted sonar, lightweight torpedoes, an indigenous anti-submarine rocket launcher and the Mareech Advanced Torpedo Decoy System. The ship can operate alongside maritime patrol aircraft, helicopters and other warships to detect and counter submarine threats. Named after Mangrol, a coastal town and prominent fishing harbour in Gujarat’s Junagadh district, the ship carries forward the legacy of the erstwhile INS Mangrol a minesweeper that served the Indian Navy until April 2004. Prarthna, Akashvani News, Mumbai.”
