## Morning News

Union Cabinet has approved eight multitracking  railway projects at total cost of over 20 thousand 800 crore rupees. Briefing media after the cabinet meeting, Information and Broadcasting Minister  Ashwini Vaishnaw said, of these projects, three multitracking railway projects have been approved at a total cost of  ten thousand seven hundred and 83  crore rupees. Mr Vaishnaw said, these projects cover 14 Districts across West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, increasing the existing network of Indian Railways by about 656 kilometres. Mr Vaishnaw said that these projects are Kharagpur to Jharsuguda fourth Line, Katni to Pendra Road fourth Line and Bilaspur to Pendra Road third Line.  These proejcts are planned to be completed up to 2029-30.

Mr Vaishnaw further informed that the other five multitracking projects have been approved at a cost of nearly 10 thousand and 21 crore rupees. The projects cover 17 Districts across the States of Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Telangana, increasing the existing network of Indian Railways by about 540 kilometres. These projects are planned to be completed up to 2029-30. Mr Vaishnaw said, these routes include Arakkonam to Renigunta third and fourth Line, Whitefield to Bangarapet third and fourth Line and doubling of Hosur to Omalur line. The approved multi-tracking projects will enhance connectivity to approximately two thousand one hundred villages having a population of about 52 lakhs.

Mr Vaishnaw informed that the government has decided to four lining or quadrupling of all the seven high density railway corridors. He said, these routes are Delhi to Howrah, Mumbai to Howrah, Delhi to Mumbai, Delhi to Guwahati, Delhi to Chennai, Howrah to Chennai and Mumbai to Chennai Route.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has said that the cabinet decision will ensure faster passenger movement, stronger connectivity and additional freight capacity. In a social media post, Mr Modi highlighted that the five railway projects worth over 10 thousand crore rupees will foster better rail connectivity in South India and also benefit lakhs of people.

The Prime Minister also said that the Cabinet decision pertaining to three multitracking projects worth over 10 thousand 500 crore will add additional railway network and improve connectivity across Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha and West Bengal. He added that it will also encourage tourism, especially heritage and wildlife related tourism and lower logistics costs.

International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) has referred Iran to UN Security Council for the first time in 20 years, citing Tehran’s failure to cooperate with a long-running probe into uranium traces found at undeclared sites. The IAEA’s 35-member board approved the resolution by 23 votes to five, with eight abstentions. Russia, China, and Niger voted against it. The resolution was proposed by the United States, Britain, France, and Germany. Referring Iran to the Security Council opens the country to possible sanctions and asset freezes. However, such punitive measures are unlikely because Iran’s allies, Russia and China, are permanent Security Council members and can veto any punitive action.

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) has warned countries about the growing misuse of gaming and gambling platforms for financial crimes. FATF said criminals are using gambling platforms to move money without actually gambling, splitting large amounts into multiple small transactions and placing unusual or coordinated bets to avoid detection. It has also flagged illegal and unlicensed online gambling as a major risk.

In Nepal, the death toll from flash floods rose to 1,377 today as rescue teams recovered more bodies, while more than 5,000 people remained missing, authorities said.

The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority said the highest number of deaths was reported in Chitwan, with 364, followed by Nawalparasi East with 228 and Nawalparasi West with 222. More than 13,600 people have been rescued from affected areas.

The floods, triggered by an ice-rock avalanche near the Nepal-Tibet border on August 26, swept through towns and villages, destroying homes, vehicles, roads and bridges and damaging hydropower projects. At least 43 people were also killed on the Chinese side, while more than 500 remain missing in Tibet, according to Chinese media. Meanwhile, India has intensified its humanitarian assistance to Nepal in the wake of devastating flash floods, delivering over 130 tons of relief material and deploying 54 expert personnel to support search, rescue, and forensic operations.
