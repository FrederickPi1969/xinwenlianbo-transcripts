## Morning News

The death toll in devastating Nepal-Tibet flash floods has risen to 392, while 1,468 people remained missing on both sides of the border. Of the 392 people killed in the disaster, 389 died in Nepal and three in neighbouring Tibet. Nepal authorities reported 910 people missing, while officials in Tibet said another 558 people remained unaccounted for, taking the total number of missing people on the two sides of the border to 1,468. As rescuers search riverbanks and isolated settlements for survivors and recover bodies carried downstream, the threat is far from over. A newly formed barrier lake near the source of the Bhote Koshi river system has raised fears that a breach could unleash another surge, potentially worsening the disaster and complicating rescue operations already underway. Nepalese security personnel have evacuated more than 800 people from affected districts.

The catastrophe has triggered a major rescue effort by India, with External Affairs Ministry saying 290 Indian nationals in Nepal remain uncontactable. New Delhi is making “urgent efforts” to ascertain their whereabouts and coordinate their rescue, with the missing Indians including hydropower workers and tourist groups from different states. External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar said urgent efforts are underway to ascertain the status and well-being of Indians caught in the flood-hit areas. He said External Affairs Ministry is coordinating with the Nepalese side, other ministries, state governments, tour operators and relevant authorities. External Affairs Ministry has also established a 24-hour control room to assist with tracing and evacuation efforts. India has so far rescued 84 nationals. This includes 63 workers from the Trishuli-I run-of-river hydroelectric project and 21 people from Tamil Nadu.

The flood threat in Bihar has, for now, receded following severe floods in Nepal. After a rise in the Gandak River’s water flow on Wednesday night, the discharge has been declining since yesterday. The Gandak’s discharge at Valmikinagar Barrage was recorded at 99,800 cusecs at 6 this morning.

Deputy Chief Minister and Water Resources Minister Vijay Kumar Chaudhary said the river’s flow is currently normal. He said the flow loses intensity as the water passes through the Bhotekoshi, Trishuli and Narayani rivers before reaching the Gandak.

As a precautionary measure, all eight Gandak-affected districts have been kept on alert. Water Resources Department teams are maintaining round-the-clock surveillance and patrolling of the embankments.

The Government has debunked a fake video circulating in social media, falsely claiming that the wall of the Bhakra Nangal Dam has developed a tilt of 1.77 inches. The video misleadingly claims that a breach in the dam could cause flooding in several cities, including Delhi. The Fact Check Unit of the Press Information Bureau (PIB) has clarified that the claim is false, and noted that the actual recorded deflection of the dam wall on 27th August 2024 was 0.86 inches.

## Evening News

India has stepped up humanitarian assistance to Nepal affected by flash floods as the third flight carrying ten tonnes of humanitarian assistance has departed for Kathmandu. External Affairs Minister Dr S Jaishankar informed this in a social media post. The Minister said that the assistance includes portable generator sets, dignity kits, food packets and water purification tablets. Dr Jaishankar said that an 11-member team of doctors, paramedics and Recce team for Tunnel rescue operations is also on-board. He also reiterated India’s support to Nepal’s ongoing relief and rescue efforts. Earlier, India had sent more than 47 tonnes of emergency relief supplies in two flights.

Meanwhile, the death toll from devastating flash floods in Nepal and Tibet has risen to 547, with 1,545 people still missing, as rescue teams search riverbanks and isolated settlements for survivors and bodies swept downstream.

Authorities have evacuated more than 800 people from affected districts and issued fresh warnings after a 5.1 magnitude earthquake struck southwestern China, raising fears of further flooding.

India has launched a major rescue and relief operation. The Ministry of External Affairs has said that 320 Indian nationals remain uncontactable or are reportedly missing following the flash flood tragedy on Wednesday. External Affairs Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said, around 100 people of Indian origin, holding other nationalities also remain uncontactable. Addressing the media in New Delhi today, Mr Jaiswal said that 63 Indian nationals working at Trishuli-1 Hydropower Project were rescued from the affected area. He said, another 96 Indian nationals who were stranded on the Chinese side have safely crossed into Nepal by land and efforts are underway to evacuate them. Mr Jaiswal informed the media that twenty-one Indian nationals from Tamil Nadu, who had reached Kathmandu from the flood-affected area yesterday, have arrived in New Delhi this afternoon.

External Affairs Minister Dr Jaishankar today met the group of 21 pilgrims belonging to Tamil Nadu in New Delhi who were rescued from Nepal.  Later talking to the media, he thanked the government of Nepal and its Army for the rescue efforts.

On the Chinese side, External Affairs Ministry spokesperson Jaiswal said that around 400 Indian nationals are safe and in contact with their families. He said, the Indian Embassy in Beijing is coordinating with local authorities, team leaders and travel agents to facilitate their evacuation.

Mr Jaiswal said that the government has set up a control room in the Ministry of External Affairs, which is working round the clock. He said, Indian embassies, both in Kathmandu and Beijing, have also set up 24 X 7 helplines.

The Government has said that there is no shortage of sugar in the country and all necessary measures will be taken for adequate availability of sugar at reasonable prices.
