## Morning News

Parliament has passed the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 with the Rajya Sabha approving it yesterday.The Bill was passed by the Lok Sabha on Wednesday.

The legislation incorporates several significant provisions, including stringent penalties such as imprisonment for up to ten years, fines of up to ten crore rupees, and the seizure of the properties of convicted offenders. To ensure the expeditious disposal of cases, the legislation mandates that every State Government and Union Territory Administration establish Special Fast Track Courts for the day-to-day trial.

Replying to the discussion on the Bill in the Rajya Sabha, Minister of State for Personnel and PMO, Dr Jitendra Singh said this issue touches every citizen of India and has stake with every citizen of this country. He said that the National Education Policy 2020 has been the biggest game changer over the last decade.

Moving the Bill in the House, Dr Singh reiterated the Modi government’s resolve of zero tolerance towards  malpractices and paper leaks of public examinations.

Initiating the discussion, Leader of the Opposition Malikarjun Kharge said that proposed amendments will not be effective to curb malpractices in the public examinations.

Leaders of the BJP, Congress, JD(U), Samajawadi Party, TMC, DMK, AIADMK, NCP, AAP, BJD, RJD, CPI(M) and others also participated in the discussion on the Bill.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has said, Government is continuously taking one step after another to build a trustworthy examination system. In a video-message on social media, Mr Modi said, for the past several decades, every state government and the Central Government have faced the problem of paper leaks and this has put the future of students at risk. He said, keeping all this in mind, reforms in the education and examination system have become essential across the country, both in the states and at the Centre. The Prime Minister said, Parliament has passed the Bill containing stringent legal provisions.

The Nation pays homage to revolutionary freedom fighter Shaheed Udham Singh on his 87th martyrdom day today. Belonging to Ghadar Party and Hindustan Socialist Republic Association, Udham Singh assassinated Michael O’Dwyer, former Lieutenant Governor of British Punjab on 13th March 1940 to take revenge of 1919 Jallianwala Bagh massacre of Amritsar.

“The disturbing memory of the Jallianwala Bagh massacre kept haunting him till the time he shot Michael O’Dwyer dead in London’s Caxton Hall. While in custody, he used the name ‘Ram Mohammad Singh Azad’, representing the three major religions in India and his anti-colonial sentiment. He was convicted of murder and hanged on this day in 1940.  To commemorate the martyrdom of the legendary revolutionary, a state-level programme will be organized at his birth place Sunam in Punjab. Haryana government is also paying tributes to him at a programme in Sanaur, today. Rajesh Bali, Akashvani News, Jalandhar.”

Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah today paid tribute to revolutionary martyr Udham Singh. Sharing a Sanskrit quote in a social media post, Mr Modi said that the life of martyr Udham Singh, marked by his extraordinary courage and sacrifice, will continue to inspire every generation of the nation to safeguard the country’s honour and sovereignty.

## Midday News

Both Houses of Parliament have been adjourned for the day following opposition uproar over several issues, including alleged police action during students’ protest over the NEET issue. When the Lok Sabha met after the first adjournment, at 12 noon, the presiding officer Dilip Saikia took up the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026 for consideration and Passage. Minister of State for Home Nityanand Rai moved the bill, amid opposition uproar. The bill was passed in the House amid ruckus without any discussion.

Meanwhile, Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju criticised the Opposition for not participating in the discussion. The Minister said, he had repeatedly appealed to the opposition, including the Congress, to ensure that such an important Bill is properly discussed.

Our correspondent reports that the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026 aims to amend the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969.

“The new bill has been brought to make provisions for delayed registration more stringent and to encourage timely reporting of events of birth and death. The Bill provides that if a birth or death is reported to the Registrar more than one year but within two years, it may be registered only with the approval of the District Magistrate, Sub-Divisional Magistrate, or an Executive Magistrate authorised by the District Magistrate, following verification of the authenticity of the birth or death. The Bill further states that if a birth or death is reported to the Registrar more than two years, it will be registered only on the order of a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class. The Bill also mandates that the designated authority must verify the authenticity of the reported birth or death before granting permission for delayed registration. The registration of births and deaths is mandatory, and the certificate issued provides legal identity to an individual. With Dipendra Kumar, Anupam Mishra, Akashvani News Delhi.“
