## Morning News

The Lok Sabha has passed the anti-Paper leak bill, proposing tougher penalties and fast-track courts to curb exam malpractices. The Bill seeks to amend the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024. The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 incorporates several significant provisions, including stringent penalties such as imprisonment for up to ten years, fines of up to ten crore rupees, and the seizure of the properties of convicted offenders. The legislation mandates that every State Government and Union Territory Administration establish Special Fast Track Courts for day-to-day trial.

Replying to the discussion on the bill, Minister of State for Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions Dr Jitendra Singh said, the amendment in the bill has been brought to make the law more stringent and ensure speedy justice in paper leak cases.

The  new legislation will  further strengthen the legal framework for preventing examination-related malpractices, including question paper leaks, impersonation and organised cheating, while ensuring swift investigation, speedy trial and timely disposal of cases. The Bill  seeks to uphold the integrity, fairness and transparency of public examinations and  protect the interests of deserving and meritorious candidates.The Bill was passed by the Lok Sabha yesterday after nearly two days of deliberations, spanning around ten hours. The Parliament has passed the Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026 with the Lok Sabha approving it today. The Rajya Sabha passed the legislation yesterday. The law will further amend the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971. The legislation seeks to give Vande Mataram the same statutory protection as the national anthem, Jana Gana Mana and make the insult to the National Song a criminal offense. These offences are punishable with imprisonment up to three years, a fine, or both. Meanwhile in the Rajya Sabha the discussion is underway on Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026.
