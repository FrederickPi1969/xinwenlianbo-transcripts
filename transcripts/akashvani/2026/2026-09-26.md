## Morning News

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has said that over the past 12 years, the gap between cities and villages in India has narrowed significantly. He highlighted the considerable improvement in rural infrastructure and noted that women across the country have also become increasingly self-reliant and economically empowered. Mr Modi made these remarks during a virtual interaction with Seva Yatris travelling on the Yatra from Varanasi to Vadnagar under Seva Sankalp Abhiyan. He emphasized the importance of social service, social reform and positive change. He said, Viksit Bharat can be realized only with the contribution and participation of everyone. Mr Modi highlighted that Vadnagar gave him the values that shaped his life, while Kashi added countless colours of service to those values. He expressed happiness that young people are also enriching this journey with the many colours of service.

During the interaction these yatris shared their experiences with the Prime Minister. Mr Modi also urged the yatris to maintain a diary and record their experiences during the Yatra.

Many rain related accidents have also been reported from different parts of the state.   In eastern Uttar Pradesh, a warning of very heavy rainfall has been issued along with heavy rainfall, thunder and lightning, and strong winds. In western Uttar Pradesh as well, heavy rainfall and thunder and lightning are likely till September 27.

Chief Minister Pushkar Singh Dhami has appealed to people and tourists to avoid unnecessary travel during this period. The Char Dham Yatra will continue for now, but pilgrims may be stopped at the nearest holding area in case of heavy rainfall.

According to the Ministry of Labour and Employment, 29 central laws have been subsumed into four codes under these reforms. The Code on Wages universalizes minimum wages and ensures timely payment, while the Industrial Relations Code simplifies dispute resolution and enhances flexibility in hiring. Meanwhile, the Code on Social Security extends coverage to gig workers, platform workers, and the unorganized sector. Similarly, the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code improves workplace safety and welfare standards.

In Chandigarh Tricity Building Kamgaar Union ‘s President Rampreet Sharma, said that the new labour laws are in the interest of the workers.

These four labour codes aim to modernize India’s labour ecosystem, bringing both workers and employers under a unified set of rules that simplify compliance, ensure safety, and increase flexibility in workforce management. For workers, this ensures robust social security, safe workplaces, and broader access to benefits; for enterprises, it creates an environment of simplified compliance, standardized processes, and a level playing field. External Affairs Minister Dr S Jaishankar has emphasized the need to harness the possibilities offered by AI, anticipate its consequences and mitigate its risks. The Minister made these remarks while co-chairing an event on ‘AI in the Context of International Peace and Security, with an Emphasis on Maritime Security’, alongside Foreign Minister of Greece George Gerapetritis.

During the event, Dr Jaishankar reaffirmed that AI and maritime security remain important priorities for India at the United Nations. He highlighted that the New Delhi Declaration emphasises democratic access to AI resources, building secure and trustworthy systems, investing in human capabilities and harnessing AI for economic growth and social good. He also highlighted that in the maritime domain, AI can enhance maritime domain awareness, search and rescue, disaster response, identification of suspicious activity and resilience of ports and critical infrastructure. The Minister added that it can also enable sophisticated reconnaissance, vulnerability mapping and cyber operations targeting vessels, communication networks and undersea infrastructure.

External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar will address the High Level Session of the 81st United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) in New York this afternoon. According to the UN, the 81st session will focus on revitalising global cooperation, advancing the Sustainable Development Goals and promoting collective action for peace, dignity and equality.

Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri has emphasized the urgency of UNSC reform to ensure greater voice and representation for developing countries. Delivering India’s statement at the 50th Annual Meeting of Foreign Ministers of the Group of 77 (G77) at the 81st session of the UN General Assembly in New York, Mr. Misri called for a collective efforts to enable the Global South to move from being rule-takers to rule-shapers. He also underscored the continued relevance of the G77 in advancing people-centric and development-oriented multilateralism.

Secretary, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Punya Salila Srivastava reaffirmed India’s commitment to strengthening global pandemic preparedness and response at the UN High-Level Meeting in New York. Addressing the meeting, the Secretary outlined India’s coordinated approach to pandemic prevention and response across government and society, which covers a wide range of potential health emergencies. She underlined the efforts taken by India to expand laboratory networks, emergency coordination, health workforce, stockpiles and clinical care capacity to ensure that systems are equipped to respond effectively to health emergencies. Ms  Srivastava  stressed the need to translate the lessons of the COVID-19 pandemic into sustained investment, institutional capacity and resilient health systems.

Iran has offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume talks on its nuclear program in seven days if the United States lifts its naval blockade of Iranian ports, waives sanctions on Iranian oil sales and observes a ceasefire that would include Lebanon.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi detailed the proposal to reporters outside the U.N. Security Council, saying it would also restart stalled talks with the U.S. on Iran’s nuclear program. The seven-day timeline will start as soon as the United States accepts this plan, he said.

US President Donald Trump has reportedly rejected an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end hostilities in the region, even as Iran awaits an official US response to its plans. An American Newspaper reported about Trump’s views quoting US officials.

Iran had earlier proposed a seven-day process under which hostilities would end on all fronts, while the United States would lift its blockade on Iranian ports, release frozen Iranian funds and waive oil sanctions. The Strait of Hormuz would then be reopened as per this proposal.
