The Prime Minister added that silos are not merely an organizational problem but also a problem of mindset. He further urged officials to work with a spirit of belongingness. Highlighting the importance of informal interaction and building team spirit among officers, Mr Modi called for greater engagement between government departments, universities and young people to bring fresh perspectives in policymaking. Prime Minister concluded by reiterating the need for a whole-of-government approach, greater coordination, innovative thinking and collective ownership to address complex challenges and shape India’s future with a long-term perspective. National Testing Agency (NTA) has said that its ‘answer key challenge’ system is designed to be fair to all students and rejected concerns about it being expensive. An NTA answer key challenge is an online process where candidates who take an exam (like JEE Main, NEET, or UGC NET) can object to errors in the provisional answer key. In a social media post, NTA said, if even a single candidate challenges a question, and the subject experts accept the challenge, the revised answer key applies to every candidate who wrote the exam. It said, the fee is charged for challenging an answer to discourage frivolous challenges and it is refunded if the challenge turns out to be right. NTA said, 200 rupees per question is refunded to the candidate whose challenge is upheld. The agency said, the fee exists to discourage frivolous challenges, not to profit from genuine ones. It said, an accurate challenge from any candidate can correct the record for lakhs of others.

The Trump administration has imposed fresh sanctions on Hezbollah and redesignated the group as an Iranian proxy. The US Treasury Department announced sanctions on 10 individuals alleged to be part of a network responsible for transferring cash to Hezbollah.

According to the Treasury Department, the network used couriers travelling on commercial airline flights between Lebanon, Turkiye, UAE and Iran to move large amounts of money across jurisdictions, providing an avenue outside the formal financial system for Hizballah to obtain foreign currency and evade sanctions. The redesignation is aimed at highlighting that Hezbollah operates on behalf of Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and is not a stand-alone operation. This formally recognises that Hezballah’s military operations, external security activities, and major financing networks function as an extension of IRGC, Iran’s primary instrument for conducting terrorism beyond its borders.

Somali pirates have hijacked two ships in four days, raising concerns that piracy is making a major comeback in waters off Somalia and Yemen amid growing regional instability. The latest incidents include the seizure of the Cameroon-flagged M/V LUTUF off Somalia’s Puntland coast and the hijacking of the Eritrean-flagged SIBU 1 tanker off Yemen. The LUTUF was reportedly carrying Turkish weapons and equipment bound for a military training facility in Mogadishu.

The LUTUF’s 10-member crew included six Indians, one Turkish national, one Georgian and two Serbian security guards. Eight pirates reportedly took the vessel toward the Nugaal coast near Garmaal in Puntland’s Dangoroyo district. Somali officials said, six ships are currently being held by pirates, including the two seized this week, with more than 100 crew members being held hostage across those vessels. More than 17 piracy-related incidents were documented across Somali waters and the Gulf of Aden during the first five months of the year, including the hijacking of at least three commercial ships and five dhow boats. Experts say regional conflict, economic difficulties for fishermen and reduced maritime security are contributing to the resurgence. Somali piracy previously peaked around 2010, when hundreds of vessels were seized and shipping companies and insurers faced billions of dollars in losses. Astronomers have discovered an unusually dense giant exoplanet orbiting a star far beyond our solar system, that challenges current theories of how planets are formed. The planet, named GJ 523b, is about 170 million years old. It is more than two-and-a-half times wider and the radius roughly 2.55 times that of Earth. But it has an extraordinary mass – about 23 times the mass of Earth – while remaining predominantly rocky. The planet has been informally dubbed a “mega-Earth,” a rare category of exceptionally massive and dense rocky worlds.

It will facilitate exchange of knowledge, expertise and best practices, as well as encourage collaboration in research, innovation and development of new-age communication technologies. India and Brazil have also emphasised the importance of strengthening cooperation in international telecommunications and technology forums.
