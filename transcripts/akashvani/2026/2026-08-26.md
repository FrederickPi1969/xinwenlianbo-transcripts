Prime Minister Narendra Modi reviewed infrastructure projects worth 30 thousand crore rupees at 53rd PRAGATI meeting in New Delhi yesterday. The Indian Medical Association (IMA) has urged people to remain vigilant amid a seasonal rise in Influenza A (H1N1) cases, saying that the majority of infections are mild and self-limiting and there is no cause for panic. The IMA has advised people not to take antibiotics over the counter for flu symptoms, stressing that H1N1 is a viral infection and antibiotics do not kill viruses. It specifically cautioned against self-medication with antibiotics such as azithromycin, amoxicillin-clavulanate and cefixime. According to the association, antibiotics should be taken only when prescribed by a qualified medical professional.

The IMA said H1N1 commonly presents with sudden onset of high-grade fever and chills, persistent dry cough, sore throat, severe muscle aches and fatigue. The association has recommended basic preventive measures including respiratory hygiene  by covering their mouth and nose while coughing or sneezing. wearing a well-fitted mask in crowded and enclosed spaces among others.

A massive flash flood swept through the northern part of Nepal near the border with Tibet today claiming forty six lives so far and leaving 500 people missing including over 100 Indians. The flood surged through the Bhote Koshi River this morning from the direction of Tibet. We have a desk report:

“The sudden rise in water levels caused widespread destruction in Timure in Rasuwa district of Nepal, near the Tibet border, and the nearby Syafrubesi area, washing away vehicles, goods and other property. Preliminary reports suggest that the flash flood may have been triggered by an earthquake that caused an avalanche, sending large quantities of melted snow into the Bhote Koshi river. Rescue and relief operations have been intensified, with the Nepal Army deploying helicopters and disaster response teams to support search operations. The Nepal Tourism Board has advised people in the affected areas to remain calm, follow official safety instructions and stay updated through verified channels. Swati Rakheja Akashvani News”

Security officials said, at least 93 people have been rescued from different parts of the affected areas.

According to the Nepal Tourism Board (NTB), those reported missing include foreign nationals and Nepali citizens. Among the foreign nationals, 105 are Indians, while 37 are NRIs, most of whom were travelling to Tibet for the Kailash Manasarovar Yatra. Other foreign nationals are from the United Kingdom, Australia, the United States, Malaysia, South Africa and the Netherlands.

Nepal Prime Minister Balendra Shah reviewed the situation and rescue and relief efforts with officials and federal parliamentarians from affected districts, including Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha and Chitwan.
