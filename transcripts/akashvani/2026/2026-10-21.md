## Morning News

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has said that all institutions and political parties of the country should unite for national interest and fight together against the enemies of the country. Participating in Vijaya Dashami celebrations in the national capital, Mr Modi reached out to political parties, urging all to join hands in national interest and to fulfill the aspirations of the people. The Prime Minister called upon citizens to adopt a ‘Nation First’ pledge to jointly identify and defeat the enemies of “Maa Bharati”. Mr Modi said ‘Ram Rajya’ is considered a benchmark of good governance even today, where there is no discrimination against anyone, everyone receives justice, and equal opportunities are available for all.

Underlining that his government’s goal is to make India self-reliant, the Prime Minister said, we must have faith in our resources.

Vijaya Dashami is being celebrated in West Bengal today, marking the culmination of Durga Puja celebrations in the state.

UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik) Scheme has completed ten years today. Launched in 2016, the scheme has expanded India’s regional aviation network, connecting unserved and underserved regions and improving access to air travel. Highlighting the rapid expansion of India’s civil aviation footprint in the last decade, the Civil Aviation Ministry noted that India is now the world’s third-largest domestic aviation market, with operational airports increasing from 74 in 2014 to 166 till date.

“During the first decade of UDAN scheme, 687 regional routes have been operationalised across the country. It has also facilitated air travel for 1.72 crore passengers. The Civil Aviation Ministry said that the scheme uses market-based selection, airfare caps, concessions, and Viability Gap Funding (VGF) to encourage airline participation. As of 15th of this month, more than five thousand crore rupees have been disbursed under VGF. Along with this the sectoral jobs have also grown from around 3 million in 2014 to nearly 7.7 million in 2026. Building on the progress, the next phase of UDAN was launched on 4th of July this year. The scheme now places greater emphasis on Centre-State and Union Territories partnerships. Saumya Sharan for Akashvani News, Delhi. “

Today is National Police Commemoration Day. On this day in 1959, ten valiant Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) personnel were martyred during patrol duty at Indo-China border in an ambush laid by heavily armed Chinese troops. Since then, 21st October is observed as Police Commemoration Day every year. Home Minister Amit Shah will lay a wreath at National Police Memorial in New Delhi today. Heads of Central Armed Police Forces, Central Police Organisations will also pay homage to the martyrs by laying wreaths.

The United Nations Environment Programme has warned that  the world must cut greenhouse gas emissions by 18 percent by 2030, 45 per cent by 2035 and 69 percent by 2040, compared with 2025 levels, to minimise global warming beyond 1.5°C and bring temperatures back to that threshold by the end of the century.

In its report title ‘The Emissions Gap Report 2026’, the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) said the immediate priority, must be to limit the scale and duration of this overshoot and pursue a pathway that could return warming to or below 1.5°C by 2100. It said global warming could worsen beyond earlier projections, with the United States’ withdrawal from the Paris Climate Agreement contributing to the rise. It said United States recorded the largest increase in greenhouse gas emissions among the world’s 20 major economies last year. Its emissions rose by 133 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent, driven by higher electricity demand and increased coal-fired power generation.

India has opposed the use of human rights as a political tool to selectively target countries on the basis of unverified allegations. Speaking at the United Nations General Assembly’s Third Committee, Indian representative  Barkha Tamrakar said human rights must not be used for political pressure or selective country targeting.

Ms Tamrakar said India remains willing to clarify specific, properly documented cases through established channels. She called for engagement with international human rights mechanisms based on dialogue, consultation, cooperation, non-selectivity and transparency. She also urged delegations to cite reports faithfully and not treat humanitarian communications as verdicts.

The United Nations is launching an initiative to address risks posed by the growing use of artificial intelligence in military operations, including potential threats to human control and nuclear security. The UN Institute for Disarmament Research (UNIDIR) plans to launch an international forum tomorrow to bring together key stakeholders to discuss military applications of AI.

Mr. Robin Geiss, head of the Geneva-based institute, warned that, the margin for human judgment is increasingly narrow, including in the nuclear domain. He also said AI was increasingly being used in early warning systems, intelligence gathering and reconnaissance, making it difficult to determine when human control could be compromised.
