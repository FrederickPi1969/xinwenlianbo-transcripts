## Morning News

Government has notified rules specifying that banks and payment system providers cannot impose charges on UPI transactions of up to 2,000 rupees. The Finance Ministry issued a gazette notification yesterday under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. It listed UPI transactions of up to 2,000 rupees and debit card payments made through RuPay as electronic payment modes for which customers cannot be charged.

“According to the Gazette notification issued by the Finance Ministry, no bank or system provider can impose any direct or indirect charge on a person making or receiving payments through these electronic modes. The Ministry has notified the changes under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act to clarify the rules regarding charges on UPI transactions. The notification specifically categorized RuPay-powered debit cards and UPI transactions of up to two thousand rupees as electronic modes of payment. Last month, the government clarified that consumers will not be charged for making payments through the Unified Payments Interface (UPI), while any future merchant discount rate (MDR) will be limited to select transactions above a specified threshold. The Finance Ministry also said that all person-to-person (P2P) UPI transactions will continue to remain free and there will be no blanket MDR on merchants. It further said that such charges would be significantly lower than the MDR applicable to debit or credit card transactions. The clarification comes amid concerns over the possibility of charges being imposed on UPI users following a recent amendment to the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. Dilnashi Arzoo, Akashvani News, Delhi.”

The prestigious ‘World Circular Economy Forum (WCEF) 2026’ is set to begin today at Mahatma Mandir in Gandhinagar, Gujarat. The conference which is being held for the first time in South Asia will be jointly inaugurated by Union Minister for Environment, Forest and Climate Change Bhupender Yadav and Gujarat Chief Minister Bhupendra Patel. Held under the theme ‘Circular Economy: Transition for People and Prosperity,’ the event will bring together over 2,000 stakeholders from more than 65 countries.

“The stage is set at Mahatma Mandir in Gandhinagar for the World Circular Economy Forum 2026, marking a major milestone in promoting sustainable economic transitions across South Asia. The forum aims to drive global circularity by emphasizing the reuse, refurbishing, and recycling of materials to reintegrate them as valuable economic resources. Across the two days, the forum will host 4 plenary sessions and 16 thematic sessions covering key areas such as the application of AI, green energy, sustainable finance, governance, and the start-up ecosystem. Additionally, an international expo featuring over 125 exhibitors will showcase innovative, practical solutions for circular business models. The global event is jointly organized by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Central Pollution Control Board, Finnish Innovation Fund Sitra, and the Government of Gujarat. Aparna Khunt, Akashvani News, Ahmedabad.”

India and Canada have begun the fourth round of negotiations for the proposed Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) in New Delhi, as both sides seek to boost bilateral trade to 50 billion US dollars by 2030. The five-day talks, which began yesterday, will continue until September 18 and cover key areas including trade in goods and services, rules of origin, and technical trade barriers, among others.

Bilateral trade between both the countries declined 8.22 percent to 7.95 billion US dollars in 2025-26 from 8.66 billion US dollars in 2024-25. India’s major exports to Canada include pharmaceuticals, iron and steel, seafood, cotton garments, electronics and chemicals, while key imports include pulses, pearls and semi-precious stones, coal, fertilisers, paper and crude oil. India’s main services sector exports to Canada include telecommunications, computer and information services, and other business services. Canada is also home to more than 425,000 Indian students and a large Indian-origin community. The third round of CEPA negotiations was held in Ottawa in July.

National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) will upgrade its existing 1033 National Highway Helpline with advanced technology to provide faster and better assistance to people travelling on National Highways. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways said that the upgraded 1033 helpline will use technologies such as Artificial Intelligence, real-time location tracking and smart emergency-response systems. These improvements will help the 1033 helpline identify the location of callers, understand calls in different Indian languages and send the right assistance to the location more quickly. The Ministry said that human operators will continue to play an important role, especially in emergencies where human judgment and decision-making are essential. The 1033 helpline was introduced in 2018 and is available 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine is ready to halt strikes on Russian territory if its allies can secure a genuine Russian commitment not to attack Ukraine’s critical infrastructure.

India’s public service broadcaster Doordarshan is celebrating its 67th foundation day today. The public service broadcaster marked its inception in 1959 as an experimental television service under All India Radio. Over the decades, it has grown into a vital platform for information dissemination, cultural preservation, and national integration, reaching nearly 100 percent of the population through satellite and Direct to Home services.

Established on 15th September 1959, Doordarshan marked the beginning of India’s broadcasting era. The then President, Dr Rajendra Prasad, inaugurated the first broadcast on that day. Until 1975, it was a part of All India Radio. A pivotal moment came with the separation from All India Radio on 1st April, 1976, granting Doordarshan operational autonomy to expand its reach. The introduction of colour television in 1982, coinciding with the Asian Games in Delhi, transformed Doordarshan’s visual appeal and viewership. The establishment of Prasar Bharati as an autonomous corporation on 23rd September, 1997, under the Prasar Bharati Act, 1990, further professionalized Doordarshan, integrating it with All India Radio under a unified public broadcasting framework. With a network of 35 satellite channels and 66 Studio Centers, Doordarshan serves diverse linguistic and cultural communities, by delivering content in over 24 languages and various dialects. Doordarshan also aired some of the most iconic series, such as Ramayana, Mahabharat, Hum Log, Buniyaad, Chanakya, Malgudi Days, Shaktimaan, Chitrahaar, Fauji, Circus, and Udaan, which set high standards for exceptional programming and entertainment while sustaining its essential function of raising public awareness. SHIVANG MISRA, AKASHVANI NEWS, DELHI.
