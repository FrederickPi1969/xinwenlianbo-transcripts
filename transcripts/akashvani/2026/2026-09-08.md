## Morning News

Prime Minister Narendra Modi will visit Gujarat and Maharashtra today. In Gujarat, The Prime Minister will lay the foundation stones, inaugurate and dedicate to the nation various development projects worth over 35 thousand crore rupees from Vadodara. Mr Modi will also dedicate to the nation three important sections of the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor (WDFC), marking the completion of the project. Talking to media persons at Ahmedabad airport, Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said this mega-project has now been fully completed and will be formally dedicated to the nation by the Prime Minister.

”A major highlight of the PM’s visit will be the dedication of three key sections of the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor spanning 326 route kilometers. Built at a cost exceeding ₹20,700 crore, this marks the full completion and operationalization of the Freight Corridor network. The Prime Minister will also flag off freight train services from New Sanand, New Makarpura, New Umbergaon, and New JNPT, along with a new passenger train service between Tanda Road and Vadodara, connecting remote tribal regions of Alirajpur to the national rail grid. In addition, Mr Modi will also lay the foundation stone for three railway expansion projects worth over ₹9,700 crore and three National Highway projects costing around ₹1,780 crore to ease congestion and boost connectivity across Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh. He will also launch various civic and infrastructure projects of Gujarat Government worth ₹2,600 crore, including a 110 Megawatt floating solar plant at Kadana Dam and PMAY housing units. Later, the PM will also visit Navsari to inaugurate the BJP’s new AI-enabled, high-tech office in Navsari.APARNA KHUNT, AKASHVANI NEWS, AHMEDABAD.”

Luxembourg Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Xavier Bettel has backed India’s bid for a permanent seat in the United Nations Security Council, saying the body must be reformed to reflect the realities of the present global order. In an interview with a news agency, Mr Bettel said India’s size and global importance make its inclusion in an expanded Security Council necessary. He said the UNSC could be expanded to include major countries that currently do not have a permanent role, citing India and Brazil.  Mr Bettel also criticised the existing veto system, describing it as a “relic of the Second World War” and arguing that no single country should have the power to block decisions supported by the wider international community. He proposed that a two-third majority in the Security Council should be able to override a veto, calling for a more representative and democratic decision-making process. Mr Bettel is on a three-day official visit to India.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi today inaugurated and laid the foundation stones, for various development initiatives worth over 35,000 crore rupees in Vadodara in Gujarat. The Prime Minister dedicated to the nation  three key sections of the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor marking the full completion and operationalization of the Freight Corridor network. PM Modi also flagged off freight train services from New Sanand, New Makarpura, New Umbergaon, and New JNPT, along with a new passenger train service between Tanda Road and Vadodara, connecting remote tribal regions of Alirajpur to the national rail grid.

Addressing the huge gathering, the Prime Minister emphasized that India has emerged as a global manufacturing hub, now producing its own advanced train and metro coaches. Prime Minister Modi highlighted the rapid transformation of India’s transport and logistics sector, calling the completion of over 2,800 kilometers of Dedicated Freight Corridors a historic achievement for 21st-century India and a testament to the nation’s changing work culture.

The Prime Minister pointed out that although the project was initially conceived in 2006, it suffered from years of inaction. Contrastingly, the accelerated execution over the past decade has completely transformed the national logistics ecosystem. Today the freight corridor serves as a lifeline for the rapidly developing India.

Emphasizing the operational impact, the Prime Minister noted that separating freight traffic from passenger lines has drastically improved efficiency. He underlined that this massive reduction in travel time is driving substantial fuel savings, lowering environmental impact, and establishing a faster, cleaner supply chain for the entire nation.

During the programme, Mr Modi also laid the foundation stone for three railway expansion projects worth over 9,700 crore rupees.

The Bombay High Court today refused to quash summons issued to Congress MP Rahul Gandhi in a 2019 defamation case over his remarks against Prime Minister Narendra Modi. A single bench of Justice N R Borkar dismissed Gandhi’s petition challenging a magistrate court order that initiated proceedings against him. The High Court, however, continued its 2021 direction to defer the hearing before the magistrate for six weeks, allowing Gandhi to approach the Supreme Court. The case stems from a defamation complaint over Gandhi’s 2018 remarks on the Rafale deal. His lawyers called the complaint frivolous, but the High Court found no illegality in the magistrate’s order and declined to interfere.

Today is International Literacy Day.The Day is celebrated on the 8th of September every year to remind everyone of the importance of literacy for individuals, communities and societies. This year the theme for International Literacy Day is “Literacy for people, the planet and prosperity”.

Proclaimed by UNESCO in 1966 during the 14th session of its General Conference, International Literacy Day provides an opportunity to review progress, deepen understanding of persistent and emerging challenges, and shape future literacy agendas in a rapidly changing world. To mark the International Literacy Day 2026, UNESCO, in collaboration with the Government of Mexico, is organising a global celebration on “Literacy for People, the Planet and Prosperity” in Mexico. The event will also feature the presentation of the UNESCO International Literacy Prizes.he main objectives of the global conference include, To take stock of literacy progress since 2015 and its contribution to sustainable development and peace; To celebrate progress achieved in Mexico and in Latin America and the Caribbean; To identify effective policies, as well as approaches and solutions for literacy provision, practice and monitoring across lifelong learning ecosystems; and To discuss the draft Action Plan for the implementation of the UNESCO Global Alliance for Literacy Strategy (2026-2029) among others. Literacy is a fundamental human right for all. It opens the door to the enjoyment of other human rights, greater freedoms, and global citizenship. This is Vishnu P S, for Akashvani News, Delhi.
