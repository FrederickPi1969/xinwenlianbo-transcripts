## World leaders warn against security flaw found in PHP computer programming

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on GoogleinfoA keyboard is placed in front of a displayed OpenAI logo in this illustration taken on February 21, 2023 [File: Dado Ruvic/Reuters]By Umar Farooq and ReutersPublished On 22 Oct 202622 Oct 2026Authorities in Europe, Asia and other continents have temporarily taken some government websites offline to protect against newly discovered security vulnerabilities in a programming language used to power the vast majority of sites on the internet.

On Wednesday, vulnerabilities in the PHP programming language prompted a rare public warning from the United States National Security Agency (NSA), which advised taking websites offline if they could not be updated immediately.

“Effective Immediately, all organizations using PHP for their systems are advised to be prepared for an update addressing two critical vulnerabilities,” the NSA said in a social media post.

In Germany, the Federal Ministry for Digital Transformation and Government Modernisation said it took some websites offline as a preventative measure. France’s Ministry of the Interior deactivated the official websites for local administrative offices.

Authorities in Belgium, the Netherlands, Finland and the Czech Republic also said they had shut down websites to apply a security patch to close the PHP loophole. Even Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi posted on social media to warn about the PHP risk.

Cybersecurity experts discovered the pair of vulnerabilities, dubbed PHP2Shell, with the help of artificial intelligence (AI) models.

Researchers at security firm Calif, working with OpenAI, reported the first flaw on August 11. Wiz, a security firm owned by tech giant Alphabet, reported the second vulnerability on August 24.

Wiz said the findings “rank among the most severe vulnerabilities discovered in PHP, and arguably in software history”.

The underlying flaws had been present for decades, the firm said, but proving they were exploitable required a chain of steps “at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve”.

AI tools let researchers find vulnerabilities in software by testing more attacks than human programmers can alone.

Volunteers maintain the PHP programming language, and the nonprofit PHP Foundation said on Wednesday that the flaws were among the most severe it had encountered.

Both flaws rely on accessing parts of memory without proper authorisation and exploiting that access to run malicious code. The two loopholes mean an attacker can remotely run commands on a server running old PHP code over the internet, without needing a password or legitimate account access.

The PHP Foundation also warned that AI would make such security weaknesses easier to find. It called on tech companies to mount a coordinated response to avoid major disruptions to online services.

“Tools now find bugs faster than ever, for defenders and attackers alike, and the number of reports will keep growing,” the foundation said in a statement.

## US accuses Canada of election interference ahead of midterms

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on GoogleinfoUS President Donald Trump speaks with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney at a meeting in Evian-les-Bains, France, on June 16 [File: Evelyn Hockstein/Reuters]By Tamara KhandakerPublished On 21 Oct 202621 Oct 2026The United States has accused Canadian politicians of trying to influence its midterm elections, pointing to Canada’s efforts to push back against President Donald Trump’s tariff campaign.

In a fact sheet released on Wednesday, the US embassy accused Canada of being dismissive of its allegations of election interference.

“The Canadian government brushes off concerns about potential interference in U.S. elections, even as its ministers and premiers openly describe plans to influence American politics in official documents and public appearances,” the fact sheet reads.

The two countries have been locked in a trade war since the start of Trump’s second term in 2025, with Canada responding to US tariffs with import taxes of its own.

But the US embassy identified public statements about the tariffs as being attempts to sway the November 3 midterm election.

Among the politicians singled out in the fact sheet was Canadian Industry Minister Melanie Joly. The fact sheet pointed to comments Joly made in August about responding to Trump’s tariffs.

“We’re also targeting products that will target states in the US. And so we’re being wise and strategic to put political pressure,” Joly said at the time.

The embassy also took issue with Joly’s meetings with Democratic officials in Boston and Chicago in September. When asked by a reporter what she thought might happen with regard to tariffs if Democrats won the midterms, she said, “That’s exactly why I’m meeting so many governors and potential presidential candidates.”

Joly has rejected the suggestion that her actions could amount to interference, saying Canada would never meddle in US elections. She said her meetings with US politicians, both Democratic and Republican, were about building Canada’s economy.

“Fundamentally, what I’ve done in the US is to attract investments,” she told reporters on October 14.

The embassy also pointed to Ontario Premier Doug Ford’s calls for retaliatory measures on US products, as well as his government’s advertising campaign against Trump’s tariffs, launched last year.

The campaign, which featured clips of former US President Ronald Reagan criticising tariffs in a 1987 radio address, prompted Trump to call off trade talks with Canada in October 2025.

While the embassy laid out several examples of Canadian officials trying to influence US trade policy, it didn’t explain how those efforts amounted to interference.

Other instances of alleged election interference included Liberal MP Anthony Housefather saying he had campaigned in Florida on behalf of Democratic Representative Debbie Wasserman Schultz, ahead of her primary election in August.

The fact sheet also accused individual Canadians of illegally participating in US elections, naming 11 people who had been charged or sentenced for voting between 2012 and 2024.

The sheet did not, however, present any evidence linking those citizens to a Canadian government effort to involve itself in US elections.

“All Canadians should refrain from attempting to participate in or influence U.S. elections in any way,” the US embassy’s statement said. “Americans decide America’s elections.”

The fact sheet comes a week after US Ambassador to Canada Pete Hoekstra said Washington was closely watching Canada for signs of interference ahead of the midterms.

“We’re monitoring it, we’re evaluating it and if some of it becomes election interference then America will respond to it,” Hoekstra said in a podcast interview on The Really Big Show, according to The Canadian Press.

The US and Canada have long been allies, but relations have frayed since Trump returned to office.

Not only has the Republican leader triggered a trade war with Canada, but he has also repeatedly threatened to make Canada a 51st state.

There have also been reports that separatist leaders from Alberta had sought support from Trump administration officials ahead of a failed referendum on the province’s independence this week.

Ahead of Monday’s provincial vote, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney addressed concerns about potential US interference in the referendum, saying Canada expected foreign governments to stay out of its elections.

“Our expectation is, to be absolutely clear, on foreign governments, United States included, is we don’t get ourselves involved in their elections, they don’t get themselves involved in our elections,” he told Global News.

Al Jazeera has reached out to the Prime Minister’s Office and Global Affairs Canada for comment, as well as the office of Ontario Premier Doug Ford and Industry Minister Joly.

She was tested for Ebola on October 19 after health officials learned she had recently travelled to Uganda, one of the countries affected by the current outbreak. Officials confirmed the positive result on Tuesday.

“We did three levels of tests, but all molecular. The strain was Bundibugyo,” Olajumoke Babatunde, director of the public health laboratory service at the NCDC, said on Wednesday.

The World Health Organization (WHO) also confirmed the strain, for which there is neither a vaccine nor a specific treatment.

An unnamed official told the AFP news agency that the woman is a diplomat whose condition has deteriorated into “multi-organ failure”.

Ayoade Alakija, a Nigerian physician who co-chairs a G7 committee for investment in global health, said that it had taken 12 days since the woman fell sick for her to be tested for Ebola, potentially exposing many other people to the infection.

Nigerian authorities have identified 44 contacts, most of them healthcare workers. They include the patient’s husband, a Swiss national, who lives with her in Abuja.

Officials are also tracing passengers who travelled with her from Uganda to Nigeria but do not believe she was symptomatic during the flight.

Health Minister Muhammad Ali Pate told public broadcaster NTA that the woman was in isolation and receiving treatment.

In 2014, the first Nigerian case, detected in Lagos, raised fears the virus would spread rapidly, but it was brought under control within three months, resulting in 20 infections and eight deaths.

That epidemic killed more than 11,000 people across West Africa between late 2013 and 2016.

The WHO says Nigeria is the fifth country to detect the virus in the outbreak, which this past week widened across the continent, with Kenya in the east reporting nine cases since October 6.

Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention Director-General Jean Kaseya said on X on Wednesday that the outbreak was “no longer one country’s fight”, and that he would hold a meeting of Africa’s health ministers next week to strengthen the cross-border response and review hospital capacity.

There have been more than 9,000 Ebola cases and more than 4,000 deaths there since mid-May.

The case underscores sensitivities about US involvement in Mexico and concerns about the Latin American country’s sovereignty.

Under Mexican law, the federal government must approve the activities of foreign forces on the country’s soil.

But questions emerged about whether the law was being adhered to after two US agents and two Mexican law enforcement officials were killed in a car accident on April 19.

News outlets, citing anonymous sources, reported that the US agents had been collaborating with state law enforcement in the destruction of a drug lab.

Matias Gomez Leautaud, an analyst at the Eurasia Group, a US-based risk consultancy firm, told the news service Reuters that the treason charge “seeks to set a precedent and prevent cooperation between [Mexican] states and the US government without the federal government’s coordination and approval”.

While the administration of President Claudia Sheinbaum has collaborated with the US on issues of crime and insecurity, it has opposed any effort by the US to work directly with Mexican states without federal authorisation first.

In the days after the April car crash, Sheinbaum indicated that her government was not aware that US agents had been involved in a drug operation in Chihuahua.

“Evidently, the military didn’t know there were people participating who weren’t Mexican citizens,” Sheinbaum told reporters at the time.

She called for an investigation into whether state officials had respected Mexico’s constitution and its National Security Law, which govern the involvement of foreign forces on Mexican territory.

The constitution, for example, requires Senate approval for the presence of any foreign troops in Mexico. The country has a long history of US intervention, including multiple military invasions continuing into the early 20th century.

But concerns about the US respecting Mexican sovereignty have risen since US President Donald Trump returned to office for a second term.

“We have to eradicate them,” Trump said of the cartels in March. “We have to knock the hell out of them because they’re getting worse. They’re taking over their country. The cartels are running Mexico.”

In the aftermath of the April crash, conflicting reports emerged about why the two US agents were in the country.

Instead, he said that US instructors had been present nearby for a training session about drones. They subsequently requested to join a convoy of Mexican law enforcement personnel to return to the airport, according to Jauregui.

“It is worth explaining what happened, in order to clarify the facts that are causing some confusion,” Jauregui said at the time.

Mexico’s federal Attorney General Ernestina Godoy has since accused Jauregui of permitting foreign agents to participate in operations on Mexican territory without first sharing information with federal officials.

A judge has ordered Jauregui to be held in pretrial detention as the court decides whether there is sufficient evidence to move forward with a criminal case.

Experts say the treason charge he faces is vaguely defined in Mexican law. It broadly covers acts that undermine Mexico’s sovereignty for the benefit of a foreign entity.

Convictions are rare, with the last widely recognised example coming in 1867, when Emperor Maximilian was tried by court-martial and subsequently executed for crimes against Mexico.

Jauregui is a member of the National Action Party, a conservative political movement in Mexico.  Sheinbaum, meanwhile, is a leader in the left-wing Morena party.
