“When the [first missile] explosion occurred, one of my colleagues went to the school to pick up her son and found the students frightened and gathered together in one place. We go to work and take our children to school, but there is a sense of anxiety and tension,” he told Al Jazeera.

Yemen’s Information Minister, Moammar al-Eryani, did not respond to Al Jazeera’s request for comment on criticism that air defence systems have been inadequate in protecting Aden.

Al-Shatri argued that only a small proportion of Aden’s wealthier residents could afford to stockpile food and other essential supplies in preparation for an emergency. Most residents, he said, were already struggling to feed their families.

Those factors keep them in the tier 1 market size category – one that represents the biggest media and population footprints in the sport.

The 2026 World Series’ viewership figures also pale in comparison to those of other major US sports leagues.

A Nielsen report found that an estimated 125.6 million viewers watched the National Football League’s Super Bowl LX, as the Seattle Seahawks beat the New England Patriots 29-13.

## Russia back in ice hockey world championships after sport court ruling

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on GoogleinfoThe first tournament where Russia could return is the men's world junior championship in Edmonton and Red Deer, Canada, starting on December 26 [File: Alexander Fedorov/Reuters]By APPublished On 30 Oct 202630 Oct 2026Russia must be allowed to return to the ice hockey world championships, the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) ruled, overturning an exclusion in place since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The ruling is a major win for Russia against efforts to bar it from international sport due to the war in Ukraine. Russian President Vladimir Putin is a noted hockey fan who has previously played exhibition games with former stars.

CAS overturned International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) decisions that excluded Russia from events in the 2026-27 season, including the men’s and women’s world championships. Russia also missed the Olympic tournaments this year.

Russia’s return throws into uncertainty how the men’s world championship will work next year in Germany, given that Ukraine has also qualified.

In a brief statement, CAS cited a lack of sufficient evidence to support the IIHF’s security concerns.

CAS said its ruling did not address issues such as whether Russia should be required to play without national symbols as a team of neutral athletes, as in some other Olympic sports, or other “organisational consequences”.

The IIHF expressed disappointment with the ruling and said it “maintains that its decisions were necessary to protect the safety and security of all participants and safeguard the integrity of its competitions”.

“While the IIHF respects the authority of CAS and will act in accordance with its ruling, the outcome does not remove the concerns that informed its assessments.”

Russian Sport Minister Mikhail Degtyarev wrote on the Telegram social media app that CAS “recognised the demands of the Russian Hockey Federation on key points as justified”.

The ruling is an immediate challenge for new IIHF President Franz Reindl, whose home country of Germany hosts the men’s world championship next year.

The chairman of the German ice hockey federation, Frank H Lutz, said on Friday that Germany remains opposed to letting Russia take part and that “allowing Russia in has significant security, political and financial consequences for us as hosts.”

Adding an extra team would make for uneven groups and an even longer tournament to fit in more games.

Kicking out a qualified team to make way for Russia would risk a backlash, especially if that team is Ukraine. It was last to qualify for the men’s worlds, ending a 20-year wait, and is ranked lowest among the 16 teams currently taking part.

The IIHF signalled “practical arrangements” would be decided after it has assessed the ruling.

The first tournament where Russia could return is the men’s world junior championship in Edmonton and Red Deer, Canada, starting on December 26.

Other events covered by the ruling are the women’s world championship in Canada next year and under-18 world championships in 2027 in Minnesota and the Czech Republic.

In the history of the men’s worlds, Russia’s or the Soviet Union’s 27 titles are second only to Canada’s 28. Russia last won the gold medal in 2014.

Since 2022, Russia has organised exhibition games with developmental rosters, mostly against neighbours Belarus and Kazakhstan. The IIHF has also excluded Belarus, citing the same concerns as it did for Russia. There was no mention of Belarus in the CAS ruling on Friday.

## Binance says $6.3bn stolen in biggest crypto heist in history: What we know

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on Googleinfo[Dado Ruvic/Illustration/Reuters]By Nils AdlerPublished On 30 Oct 202630 Oct 2026Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, says hackers have stolen about $6.3bn in what appears to have been the biggest crypto heist on record.

The company, which was co-founded by the Chinese-born Canadian billionaire Changpeng Zhao, suspended deposits and withdrawals on Thursday and said it would cover all losses, telling Al Jazeera in a statement that user funds were “safe”.

Two United States blockchain analysis firms have claimed the attack originated in North Korea.

So how did it happen, and what does it mean for the crypto market? Here’s what you need to know:

Blockchain analysis firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis say most of the theft unfolded over about 20 minutes, starting late on Thursday.

“An attacker moved approximately 6.36 billion USD out of Binance wallets on 10 blockchains,” TRM reported.

That dwarfs the previous record of $1.5bn, which was stolen from the exchange Bybit in February 2025.

Binance said about $3bn of that has since been frozen, including more than $2.3bn by stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle, according to the Reuters news agency.

It also warned on X that the $6.3bn figure “may continue to increase” as the company works to trace and freeze more assets.

TRM said it had assessed with “high confidence” that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking unit linked to the Lazarus Group.

According to the Council on Foreign Relations (CFR), the Lazarus Group “targets and compromises entities primarily in South Korea and South Korean interests for espionage, disruption, and destruction”.

It also conducts cyberoperations for financial gain, including targeting cryptocurrency exchanges, the CFR says.

The US Department of the Treasury sanctioned the Lazarus Group in 2019, saying it was controlled by North Korea’s primary intelligence bureau. North Korea routinely denies involvement in hacking.

TRM said the attackers ordered Binance’s wallets to empty into wallets they controlled, mirroring the Bybit hack.

Binance has been more cautious, however. “Our investigation has not attributed responsibility to any individual,” co-CEO Richard Teng said on X.

The company initially said the breach may have started with a hacked laptop belonging to one of its engineers. But on Friday, it said further investigation had uncovered “additional points of breach”.

Binance said it had found no evidence that any wallet’s private key, the digital password that controls a crypto wallet, had been leaked.

But Mark Karpeles, former head of the collapsed Tokyo exchange Mt Gox, told Reuters the attackers had probably accessed keys for at least two Binance wallets.

While North Korea denies involvement in crypto theft, it has become a much-needed source of foreign currency for Pyongyang, helping to prop up its economy and fund its nuclear programme, which is under United Nations sanctions, experts say.

In December 2022, South Korea’s spy agency estimated that North Korean hackers had stolen about 1.5 trillion won ($1.1bn) in crypto and other virtual assets over five years.

“When North Korea steals at this scale, the money funds weapons proliferation and other destabilising activity,” Ari Redbord, TRM’s global head of policy, told Al Jazeera, adding, “That makes this a global security issue, not just a crypto theft.”

Bitcoin hit a six-week low of $76,368, while Ether fell more than 3 percent to a two-month low of $2,178.

The hack comes as crypto had already been under pressure from higher US interest rates and weaker demand from exchange-traded fund buyers, analysts told Reuters.

The stolen sum is more than six times Binance’s $1bn emergency fund, the Secure Asset Fund for Users, set up in 2018 and held entirely in Bitcoin to protect users in case of a breach.

The hack has exposed one particular weak point: Large, centralised exchanges, which hold users’ assets on their behalf.

“Any assets held at a centralised exchange are only as safe as the exchange itself, and the larger the exchange’s holdings, the larger a target it becomes,” Sean Foley, an expert in crypto markets at Macquarie University in Sydney, told Al Jazeera.

Insurance offers limited protection as most crime- and cyber-insurance policies do not cover stolen cryptocurrency, and crypto-specific cover remains a niche business offered largely by specialist insurers.

Thefts are frequent. Hackers stole $2.87bn in crypto in nearly 150 attacks in 2025, according to TRM’s 2026 Crypto Crime Report.

Binance itself has faced legal trouble. Founder Changpeng Zhao pleaded guilty in 2023 to a felony linked to failing to prevent money laundering on the platform. US President Donald Trump pardoned him in 2025.

On Friday, Binance said it was coordinating with law enforcement and remained on track to resume withdrawals within 45 hours.

The mood has changed and Sanchez’s Socialists could be in trouble, analysts say, as the conservative opposition has been handed new grounds to challenge the government on competence and security.

“There are no winners in this tragedy, but it marks a new tone for the campaign where the conservative party could be more comfortable,” said Yolanda Roman, global director for public affairs at ATREVIA, referring to the Popular Party (PP) that has been leading in the polls.

PP’s leader Alberto Nunez Feijoo, the prime minister’s main rival, has demanded accountability.

“This is the moment to respect the dead, the injured and their families. That does not mean we will ignore the fact that Spaniards are asking tough questions that demand answers,” he said.

The crash has revived memories of other fatal accidents. In 2013, 80 people died in a derailment at Angrois, near Santiago de Compostela in the country’s northwest.

Spain’s high-speed rail network has long been regarded as one of the country’s flagship public services, alongside its healthcare system. It carried about 45 million passengers last year, including millions of tourists.
