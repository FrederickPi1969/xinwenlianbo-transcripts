## Colombia inaugurates embassy to Israel in Jerusalem

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on GoogleinfoColombian Foreign Minister Omar Bula Escobar and Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar open Colombia's embassy in Jerusalem, October 14 [Rafi Ben Hakoon/GPO/Handout via Reuters]By Al Jazeera Staff and AFPPublished On 14 Oct 202614 Oct 2026Colombia has opened its embassy to Israel in Jerusalem, joining a small group of countries in taking the controversial step of backing Israel’s claim to sovereignty in the contested city.

Palestinian officials swiftly denounced the inauguration on Wednesday that came as Israel is facing mounting international pressure over the genocidal war on Gaza, where three years of attacks have killed more than 74,000 people.

The move by the administration of new Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella is a major shift from his left-wing predecessor Gustavo Petro, who in 2024 severed ties with Israel over the war on Gaza.

De la Espriella, who was sworn in in August, has also restored a free trade agreement between Israel and Colombia that Petro terminated.

Colombian Minister of Foreign Affairs Omar ‌Bula Escobar said on Wednesday that relations between the two countries “should ⁠never have been broken” and that Bogota was taking “taking a concrete step towards ⁠restoring diplomatic relations”.

“Israel is a strategic partner. It always has been, and from now on, it will be an even stronger one,” he said during a ceremony that marked the embassy’s opening.

The opening made Colombia the 10th country to establish its embassy in Israel in Jerusalem – most countries keep their formal diplomatic missions in Tel Aviv.

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar welcomed the move during the ceremony. “This is an important date for the State of Israel. It’s also an important date for the city of Jerusalem,” he said while signing the Colombian embassy’s guest book.

Colombian Ambassador Tania Gilinski said Israel had always been a “strategic partner” and that moving forward it “will be so with even greater determination and strength”.

The Palestinian Authority condemned Colombia’s embassy move, calling it “a violation of international legitimacy resolutions” that undermines “the international consensus on the legal status of the holy city”.

Jordan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs also criticised the move as a “violation of international law and relevant international resolutions”.

United States President Donald Trump moved the US embassy to Israel from Tel Aviv to Jerusalem during his first term in 2018, angering Palestinians and sparking international condemnation. Previous US presidents, as well as nearly every other country, refrained from opening embassies in Jerusalem, arguing that the city’s final status should first be resolved in negotiations between Israelis and Palestinians.

The status of Jerusalem remains one of the core unresolved issues of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, with Israel claiming the undivided city as its capital while Palestinians view occupied East Jerusalem as the capital of a future Palestinian state.

Israel occupied East Jerusalem and annexed it in 1967, in a move not recognised by the vast majority of the international community.

Since Trump relocated the US embassy to Jerusalem, only a handful of small, US-aligned countries have followed suit, namely Fiji, Guatemala, Honduras, Papua New Guinea, Paraguay and Nauru. Kosovo and Somaliland, which are not member-states of the United Nations, have also opened what they consider their embassies in Jerusalem.

## Two scenarios for the Yemen war

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on GoogleinfoA fighter from Yemeni government forces fires a gun from the back of a pickup truck during clashes with Houthis near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait in Taiz province [Screengrab/AFP]The attacks by the Iran-backed Houthis on Saudi Arabia have pushed the war in Yemen to a new level. The targeting of civilian infrastructure, including airports, which has killed at least a dozen people and injured hundreds, has left no room to maintain the “red lines” that previously outlined the limits of escalation.

This is a reckless gamble by the Houthis which is not in their interest. They are aware that the retaliation will be fierce, yet they continue to attack Saudi Arabia to fulfil Iran’s agenda that seeks heightened tensions before the United States midterms.

Given the motivation to escalate, the present situation has the potential to develop into two scenarios: A major region-wide operation against the Houthis or a war of attrition.

In response to the Houthi escalation, Saudi Arabia is striving to garner maximum international support while deepening its regional and international alliances. The aim is to come up with a united front capable of taking on what has become a regional threat.

Riyadh is certainly looking to its Gulf neighbours who, themselves, are impacted by the continuing conflict in the region. There is a collective realisation that everyone is in grave danger, not just Saudi Arabia.

Therefore, Gulf states – perhaps with the exception of Oman – will support Riyadh both directly and indirectly in the fighting in Yemen.

Pakistan and Turkiye are also likely to join the front, having signed with Saudi Arabia a defence agreement known as the Mecca Pact. The pact is to be ratified by the Turkish parliament in the next few days, opening the way for Turkish military deployment in support of Saudi Arabia. Meanwhile, indirect involvement by Turkiye and Pakistan, along with logistical and intelligence support, appears to be already well under way.

A US intervention is also possible, although it seems unlikely before the US midterms, as military engagements in the Middle East remain unpopular among US voters.

A region-wide coalition can help support the counteroffensive of the forces loyal to the internationally recognised Yemeni government already under way. This could give the necessary push to retake the western coastal region, including areas beyond Bab al-Mandeb and Dhubab.

Such gains could ease pressure on Red Sea shipping and potentially shift the military balance in favour of the government. The opening of additional fronts across other Yemeni cities and governorates could stretch Houthi forces, disperse their resources, and increase the likelihood of further government advances.

Retaking Sanaa could also be on the horizon. The primary obstacle delaying the Yemeni government’s recapture of the city is internal discord among the various factions within the government itself, compounded by negative external interference that exacerbates these rifts and weakens the collective effort to confront the Houthis.

Recent measures taken by the government, specifically efforts to unify its decision-making and consolidate its military operations command, have yielded noticeable progress on the ground. However, achieving the ultimate objective of retaking Sanaa requires sustained effort and focused determination.

If government forces make substantial gains, that could force the Houthis to offer concessions in exchange for a pause in fighting. They have done that before. Their purpose would not be to seek peace but to get enough breathing room to regroup and eventually go back to fighting. Negotiators should keep that in mind when approaching talks with the Houthis.

If the Saudi-led coalition and the Yemeni government succumb to internal divisions and disagreements, this may slow progress against the Houthis. This would allow the Houthi fighters to hold on to their positions while increasing their pressure on the strategic town of Taiz and maintaining the campaign against civilian targets in Saudi Arabia.

Such a development could further complicate the conflict, increase civilian casualties and displacement, and prolong the war without producing a decisive military outcome.

This will have a significant economic impact on Saudi Arabia and complicate the implementation of its development goals, as it would have to prioritise defence spending.

The rest of the Gulf economies will also be negatively impacted because Saudi Arabia plays such a central role in the region. What is more, losing ground to the Houthis would mean that the crisis in the Strait of Hormuz would also continue, disrupting energy exports from the region.

In this scenario, there will be “no victor, no vanquished” deadlock in Yemen, but Iran would emerge as winner. A war of attrition along the Red Sea coast would provide Tehran with significant breathing room, allowing it to alleviate pressure on its own war efforts.

This would enable Iran to use the Houthi card as leverage in the intermittent negotiations it conducts with the international community, emboldening it to raise the bar regarding its conditions and demands during those talks.

This means that the only way to resolve the crisis in Yemen is decisive military action led by the Yemeni government and supported by a region-wide coalition. For that to happen, the government has to get its internal affairs and its relations with allies in order.

## How AI apps are offering free substitutes for Big Tech software

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on GoogleinfoSignage for Adobe is displayed at National Retail Federation (NRF) 2026: Retail's Big Show, in New York City, US, January 12, 2026 [File: Kylie Cooper/Reuters]By Edna MohamedPublished On 14 Oct 202614 Oct 2026As AI development rapidly expands, people are replicating paid-for desktop and mobile apps with free apps, potentially changing the future of legacy software.

Last week, a Reddit user named Brandon Thomas shared a project called “ArtCraft” – essentially a free rebuild of the Adobe Creative Suite, which includes programmes such as Photoshop and Adobe PDF. The recreated services have been named PhotoCraft and PdfCraft.

This software package usually costs individuals $779.99 per year if paid upfront or $69.99 per month on an annual contract.

As with the Adobe package, ArtCraft has announced WordCraft, DeckCraft, and GridCraft, taking on Microsoft Word, PowerPoint, and Excel. While the software engineer behind the new packages is based in the United States, the programmes are available worldwide.

Reddit user “u/ai_art_is_art”, the person behind ArtCraft, has been revealed as Atlanta, US-based software engineer Brandon Thomas.

Using AI platform Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 and Rust software, Thomas said he was able to reverse engineer Adobe’s creative suite.

Announcing his project on Reddit, Thomas said that this is not a “one-off flash in the pan that will die”.

“I’m not just some casual vibe coder. I’m a 15-year industry veteran who has written everything from six-nines payment rails that move billions of dollars of transaction volume a day to advanced robotics and optics automation. I’ve used Rust extensively for about a decade, and I know how to build and maintain large-scale Rust projects,” he said.

“Six-nines payment rails” refers to online payment infrastructure which provides 99.9999 percent operational availability, while Rust is a programming language.

Thomas said he had become fed up with being hit with cancellation fees on software products and wanted others to be able to access them for free.

He wrote on the social media and news site, Hacker News: “We’ll replace Google Search, we’ll build our own smartphones, we’ll build better infra. We’ll remove all the ads and de-enshi**ify the entire tech world.”

While Thomas denies he is “just some vibe coder”, vibe coding is at least partly what he has done.

Vibe coding is a new term in software development which refers to prompting AI tools to generate code rather than writing it manually. This means that people who do not have technical knowledge of programming can describe an idea to an AI tool and have it create it for them.

According to the IBM technology company, vibe coding is a “shift toward intent-driven software development, where AI systems assist developers by generating code from natural language instructions”.

The phrase was introduced by computer scientist and OpenAI cofounder Andrej Karpathy in February 2025, who wrote on X that AI users could “fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget that the code even exists”.

Vibe coding has become more common as AI has taken off and new apps have flooded Apple’s App Store.

The New York Times reported that Sensor Tower, a global market intelligence and digital analytics platform, estimates that new apps doubled in the first half of this year to about 560,000.

There is a legal grey area around replicating software which has already been established without infringing on intellectual property.

On Reddit, Thomas described his Adobe-similar apps as “clean-room” replacements, meaning that they were built from scratch from publicly available file-format specifications, rather than from Adobe’s proprietary source code.

However, there is still little information about how much of the code Claude wrote and how much Thomas wrote, according to Technollama, a blog written by Professor Andres Guadamuz, a reader in intellectual property law at the University of Sussex.

“We also do not know whether the claimed ‘clean-room process’ stands up to scrutiny,” he said.

The downfall of legacy software is unlikely to make apps like Adobe Creative Cloud or Microsoft completely obsolete, but it could make them far less profitable if there are free-to-use substitutes available.

## Iran targets Starlink as ‘tool of war’ with hints of more internet curbs

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on GoogleinfoThe use of satellite-based internet services and the distribution of Starlink terminals are subject to fines and imprisonment in Iran [File: Pierre Crom/Getty Images]By Maziar MotamediPublished On 14 Oct 202614 Oct 2026Tehran, Iran – Iranian authorities have pledged to continue a crackdown against satellite internet service Starlink, along with further restrictions on the global internet as the US-Israel war on Iran continues.

Elon Musk’s Starlink, which is free of the heavy filtering and restrictions imposed by Tehran, is outlawed in Iran on national security grounds. The use of satellite-based internet services and the distribution of Starlink terminals are subject to fines and imprisonment.

It is one of the many measures imposed by Iranian authorities since the United States and Israel launched strikes on the country on February 28, leading to a prolonged and economically punishing war between the countries.

Iran’s 90 million people have undergone two lengthy state-imposed global internet shutdowns in 2026 – for three weeks during nationwide protests in January and for 88 days at the start of the ongoing war.

Mohammad Amin Aghamiri, secretary of the Supreme National Cyberspace Council, described Starlink as a “tool of war” for enemies.

“It is a tool used for command and control by the enemy, for espionage and information gathering,” he said. “We have several systems for cutting off Starlink satellite terminals.”

He did not elaborate on what measures or devices were being used to block Starlink, but one was deployed during the January protests and authorities were prepared to share the technology with aligned countries.

Iran is among several countries that have banned or restricted Starlink due to national security concerns.

It has set baseline sentences of between six months and two years for using or possessing Starlink. Importing and distributing the devices carry jail terms of up to five years. If paired with national security or espionage charges, individuals can even face the death penalty.

Aghamiri has called on Iranians to use domestic platforms, supported by what the authorities call the National Information Network. He also backed a tiered-access model, which envisages protected international access for selected groups – such as traders, researchers and journalists – during periods of internet restrictions.

A Tehran-based internet and telecommunications expert, speaking on condition of anonymity, told Al Jazeera that Iranian authorities use a combination of measures to block Starlink connections.

This includes the use of multiple jammers with different configurations to disrupt services, along with spoofed GPS locations to slow new devices being set up. They can also identify Starlink-associated internet protocols if they connect to local applications and services.

Authorities also try to physically intercept terminals using patrols and drone searches, or raid locations selling the terminals.

In June, Israeli politician Naftali Bennett claimed that Israel had smuggled tens of thousands of Starlink devices into Iran when he was prime minister, between 2021 and 2022, to aid antigovernment protesters.

Since 2023, Iran has also pursued the matter at the International Telecommunication Union’s Radio Regulations Board, trying to pressure the US and Norway, which plays a role in the satellite network’s regulatory filings, to halt transmissions.

On October 4, Iran’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs summoned the Norwegian ambassador in Tehran to protest against what it described as a failure to act over unauthorised Starlink operations.

During a governance meeting at the end of September, Deputy Technology Minister Mohammad Hafez Hakami had warned authorities that Starlink could signify “the last nail in the coffin of the [cyberspace] governance” of Iran if it becomes prevalent, due to the unfettered internet access it provides.

He pointed out that the government blocked the messaging app Telegram after the cost-of-living protests in 2017-18, but had to resort to a weeklong internet shutdown for the first time during the nationwide protests in 2019. WhatsApp and Instagram were blocked after the 2022-23 protests, which preceded the near-total shutdown during the January 2026 protests.

“If this trend continues, in the next crisis we will have to cut electricity, because Starlink, as a new tool of evading state governance, is fundamentally not even dependent on our network,” Hakami said.

Starlink has allowed Iranians to send out videos of the protests abroad since 2022 despite state restrictions. Tens of thousands of terminals are believed to have been smuggled into the country since, but exact figures are unknown.

Due to the risk of a state crackdown, limited availability and financial costs, most people still have no access to Starlink and must rely on Iran’s domestic internet, which blocks most international services and thousands of websites. US sanctions and banking restrictions further impede Iranians’ access to global services.

To circumvent the many roadblocks, most Iranians use virtual private networks (VPNs) and other methods that grant access to the free internet. But those tools can reduce connection speeds, prove costly or provide unsafe connections that jeopardise user data, and become harder to come by during periods of state-imposed internet blackout.

According to the authorities, more restrictions were possible in the future if the war intensifies, after a relative decrease in violence since the June memorandum of understanding, which eventually lapsed without a permanent ceasefire, between the US and Iran.

Government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani told a news conference on Tuesday that state bodies were deliberately disrupting the internet ostensibly to protect people and national assets from cyberattacks.

“It is obvious that due to the importance of our databases and internet-based services, decision-making bodies are sometimes forced to bring down internet speeds,” she said. “We don’t deny this issue.”

The authorities claim that they have considered removing restrictions on some platforms, although no action has been taken yet.

State-linked media late on Tuesday released a policy and governance framework document that the cyberspace council’s Aghamiri said has been under consideration for nearly two years but remained confidential due to security concerns.

The document offered no timeline for reopening any platform, and media reports said implementation had been halted by wars and protests.

The document says Google Play and WhatsApp could potentially be unblocked, while YouTube and Telegram would have to negotiate with Iran and accept certain conditions determined by the authorities to be unblocked. Such steps by the social media giants are considered extremely unlikely.
