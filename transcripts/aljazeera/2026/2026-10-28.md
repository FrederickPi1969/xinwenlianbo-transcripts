## Israeli soldier visiting UK faces war crimes complaint

xwhatsapp-strokecopylinkgoogleAdd Al Jazeera on GoogleinfoThe complaint was filed by a foundation named after Palestinian girl Hind Rajab, who appears here in an undated photo before Israeli forces killed her at age five with family members as they drove in a car in Gaza City in 2024 to escape an Israeli bombardment [File: Handout/Palestine Red Crescent Society via Reuters]By Mohammad MansourPublished On 28 Oct 202628 Oct 2026The Hind Rajab Foundation has filed a criminal complaint in the United Kingdom against Lidor Dafna, an Israeli reservist believed to be visiting the country, over alleged war crimes committed during Israel’s genocidal war on Gaza.

Dafna, who served in the 603rd Combat Engineering Battalion, is accused of participating in the demolition of a flour-producing facility in May 2025 at a time when Israel’s blockade of Gaza was leading to widespread starvation.

The foundation said footage posted by Dafna on social media shows an Israeli military bulldozer demolishing wheat silos in southern Gaza.

The filing is the latest move by the Brussels-based organisation, named after Hind Rajab, a five-year-old girl killed alongside her relatives when Israeli forces fired on their vehicle in Gaza City in January 2024.

By transforming self-published digital content into evidence for criminal complaints, the foundation is testing whether legal systems in other countries may be used to hold Israeli soldiers accountable for alleged crimes.

Natacha Bracq, head of litigation at the foundation, explained the urgency of the UK filing to Al Jazeera.

“If the authorities let him leave, he [Dafna] will go back and commit more crimes. When a state waves a suspect through its airport, knowing what is documented, it is breaching its obligations to prevent and punish genocide.”

Al Jazeera has reached out to the UK’s Metropolitan Police for comment on the case but has yet to receive a response.

For the families of victims in Gaza, the pursuit of justice through international channels is a response to the failure of internal Israeli accountability mechanisms.

Speaking from Gaza, the 45-year-old grandmother and namesake of Hind dismissed internal Israeli military investigations.

“The occupation [Israel] claims that it opens criminal justice or an investigation for their soldiers, [but] we are certainly not very interested in this topic,” she stated. “They just justify their positions to the world, … but actually on the ground, I don’t think anything happens.”

The foundation’s broader goal resonates deeply with families seeking justice for children killed during the war.

“Hind is not the first child in Gaza. Hind means surely there are many crimes like hers and more,” her grandmother added. “We support anyone who speaks in the name of childhood [and always supports] the Palestinian child.”

The Hind Rajab Foundation’s methodology relies heavily on material published by Israeli soldiers themselves.

“Our starting point is simple: Israeli soldiers documented their own crimes. They’ve posted tens of thousands of photos and videos from Gaza and Lebanon on TikTok, Instagram, X and Telegram, often with their faces and names attached,” Bracq said.

This digital footprint requires rigorous processing to become potentially admissible in legal cases.

“Our collection and analysis follow the Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations. Then the real work starts: verification. We geolocate every frame against satellite imagery, matching buildings, roads, water towers, destruction patterns,” Bracq said, noting that investigators then cross-reference military sources to tie a suspect to a documented act.

The Berkeley Protocol, developed by the Human Rights Center  at the University of California in Berkeley and the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, sets out standards for collecting, verifying, analysing and preserving digital material.

Khaled al-Shouli, an international lawyer registered with the Jordanian Bar Association who represents Palestinian victims before the International Criminal Court (ICC), said social media material could be valuable evidence.

“Its value does not decrease simply because it is a post on the internet, nor does it become decisive simply because it has spread,” al-Shouli said.

He stressed, however, that answering fundamental questions about the source, time and place is essential.

“The crucial difference is the transition from proving the occurrence of the crime to proving the responsibility of a specific person for it as the appearance of a soldier in a place does not alone prove his commission of the crime,” he said.

The foundation’s complaints have prompted authorities in several countries to question Israeli military personnel.

Bracq pointed to a significant breakthrough in Belgium, where a complaint filed by the foundation led federal police to detain and question two Israeli soldiers. Both were subsequently released.

“That arrest matters beyond the two individuals involved. It demonstrated that universal jurisdiction is not a theoretical instrument but one that puts suspects in a police station within hours, and the Belgian federal prosecutor has since referred the case onward to the International Criminal Court,” she stated.

Similar actions have disrupted travel for an Israeli in North America. In Canada, former combat soldier Guy Hochman was detained and interrogated for six hours at Toronto Pearson Airport after a complaint was filed accusing him of war crimes and incitement to genocide. He later said his Canadian visa had been revoked.

“These are ruptures in a system of impunity: the moment Israelis calculate that travelling abroad carries a real legal risk,” Bracq emphasised.

Al-Shouli clarified the legal architecture supporting these moves, distinguishing between national courts and the ICC.

“The Palestinian case relies on a set of matters that fall under the category of territorial jurisdiction over crimes committed in the Palestinian territories and not on absolute universal jurisdiction,” he noted. “This distinction is important because the multiplicity of paths expands the opportunities for accountability and does not leave it hostage to one institution.”

A Hind Rajab Foundation complaint in Brazil highlighted both the potential and the limitations of filing cases against Israelis in the courts of other countries.

While Gulf countries appear determined to avoid another round of conflict – which has already restricted passage through the Strait of Hormuz, disrupted global oil and gas supplies and wreaked havoc on energy facilities and infrastructure in Gulf states – questions remain over whether a regional agreement is possible and where it would leave relations between the US and Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC).

“This veto is rooted in a racist structure that regards the Arab citizen as illegitimate and as an unwanted partner,” Honaida Ghanam, director general of the Palestinian Forum for Israeli Studies in Ramallah in the occupied West Bank, told Al Jazeera, alluding to the resistance by the majority of Israeli parties to form any government with Arab-led lists.

Both Netanyahu and Eisenkot have rejected the idea of partnering with them, citing a lack of shared values.

The numbers from exit poll projections thus far are looking clear: If Eisenkot’s bloc teams up with Arab parties, they could secure the 61 seats needed for a majority in parliament and depose Netanyahu.

According to Palestinian political analyst Ahmed Najar, the unwillingness by the opposition bloc to join forces is purely “ideological”.

“Eisenkot has imposed loyalty tests on Arab parties and ruled out relying on them even as the electoral arithmetic makes their support indispensable,” Najar told Al Jazeera.

“The government has made the lives of every Arab citizen miserable,” Jabareen was quoted by the Yedioth Ahronoth newspaper as saying.

His movement “will know how to leverage” its political strength to advance its values, he added.

Ahmad Tibi, also a Joint List leader, on Wednesday said the bloc was extending its hand to the opposition alliance to oust Netanyahu, according to Israeli media reports. It was far from clear, however, that the bloc would formally join a governing coalition.

An FEC email sent to a campaign compliance firm reportedly read, “We have a bad actor trying to breach FEC systems. As a result, we’ve had to temporarily suspend our web-facing services,” according to Reuters news agency.

The outage prevents organisations from filing the FEC’s 24 and 48-hour spending reports, which track how political groups and candidates are spending on specific races.

A status message on the FEC website showed the system was down after 10.15pm EST on Monday (2.15am GMT on Tuesday).

An update was posted on Tuesday at 8.17pm EST, reading, “The issue has been identified and a fix is being implemented.”

The FBI and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency both referred questions to the FEC.

The FEC does not play a role in administering US elections, but the reported hack comes as so-called Super PACs and dark money groups are spending record sums in the final days before the midterm elections on November 3.

Democratic FEC Commissioner Dara Lindenbaum wrote on X, “This has NOTHING to do with voter files or voter registration. Those are maintained by the states. The FEC only handles federal campaign finance findings.

The midterms are now the most expensive US elections in history, with roughly $11.21bn spent on advertising and future reservations so far, according to political advertising tracker AdImpact.

That surpasses the previous record of $11.2bn spent in the 2024 elections – including $3.2bn in the presidential race between President Donald Trump and former Vice President Kamala Harris, according to AdImpact figures.

Much of the spending is being done by super PACs – independent political committees which can accept unlimited amounts of money from wealthy individuals and corporations and spend it on campaigns, with few restrictions.

Super PACs and dark money groups have spent an average of $50m per day over the past week, according to Brendan Glavin, research director at nonpartisan campaign watchdog group OpenSecrets.

“It’s a big deal,” Glavin said. “In the last days of the election, this is the only way for us to keep track of where the money is being spent.”
