Daily Press Briefing by the Office of the Spokesperson for the Secretary-General
The following is a near-verbatim transcript of today’s briefing by Stéphane Dujarric, Spokesman for the Secretary-General.
Guest
All right, good afternoon.  I am sorry for the delay, and I know there is a lot of stuff going on.
In a short while, we will be joined by our friend David Nanopoulos, the head of the Treaty Section of the Office of Legal Affairs (OLA).  He will be here to talk to you about the annual Treaty Event taking place on the sidelines of the General Assembly.
Senior Personnel Appointment
I have a senior personnel appointment for you, which has seen some reporting about.
Today, the Secretary-General and the Director-General of the Food and Agriculture Organization [Qu Dongyu] announced their appointment of
Luke J. Lindberg of the United States as the Executive Director of the World Food Programme (WFP)
.  As you know, Luke will succeed Cindy H. McCain of the United States.  The Executive Board of WFP confirmed its concurrence with the appointment earlier today.
The Secretary-General and FAO Director-General express their deep appreciation for the work of Ms. McCain, for her leadership and dedicated service to the World Food Programme.  She led WFP through one of the most demanding periods in its history, serving as a steadfast voice for people affected by conflict, by disaster and severe hunger, and for the courageous personnel serving them on the front lines.  Both also wish to express their appreciation to Carl Skau, the Deputy Executive Director and Chief Operating Officer of WFP, who will continue to serve as Acting Executive Director until Mr. Lindberg assumes his position.
Currently serving as the Under Secretary for Trade and Foreign Agricultural Affairs at the US Department of Agriculture (USDA), Mr. Lindberg is the principal diplomat for US agriculture and brings Cabinet-level experience to the job. We congratulate him and welcome him. A lot more on his biography on the Internet.
UN General Assembly
Some numbers for you ahead of the General Assembly, because I know you are all interested in that.  So far, we expect about 73 Heads of State, 45 Heads of Government, 49 Ministers, 11 Vice-Presidents and one Crown Prince.
Alongside the mandated meetings, there are about 168 events that are scheduled during the high-level week, and so far, the Member States have submitted about 1,000 requests for bilateral meetings amongst themselves.  Obviously, the bilaterals with the Secretary-General are also being scheduled.
You will have a lot of new colleagues for the week.  We have issued 1,935 press passes for visiting journalists and an additional 1,290 press passes for official media.  We will keep updating you on numbers as they evolve.
Secretary-General — SDG Moment
This morning, the Secretary-General delivered remarks at the 2026 SDG (Sustainable Development Goals) Moment, stressing that
the Global Goals are the shared promise made by governments
to eradicate poverty and hunger, fight inequality, protect the planet and leave no one behind.
However, this promise is not being kept, he said.  Conflicts, climate crisis, growing inequality and a lack of financing, he said, are all placing sustainable development under increasing pressure.
Mr. [António] Guterres called for greater investment in education, in food security, in decent work, in healthcare and social protection, as well as firm action on human rights and gender equality.  He also urged countries to accelerate a just transition to renewable energy and to increase financing for development to build a fairer, safer and more sustainable world.
Security Council — Peacekeeping
This afternoon, at 3 p.m., the Secretary-General will speak at the Security Council on peacekeeping operations.
In his remarks, he will stress that
peacekeeping is a political instrument, deployed to support political solutions, never as a substitute for them
.  As conflicts evolve and peace agreements become increasingly rare and fragile, he will call for a reset in how peace operations are designed and supported.
The Secretary-General will emphasize the need for greater focus on peacemaking and for missions to be equipped with the resources, with the capabilities and the flexibility needed to carry out their mandates effectively. He will also stress that no organization can address today’s conflicts alone and that peacekeeping operations must work alongside host countries, regional organizations and the UN system as a whole.
Peacekeeping Ministerial Handover
On a related note, yesterday here at Headquarters, Boris Pistorius, the Federal Minister of Defence of Germany, formally handed over the hosting of the next UN Peacekeeping Ministerial to Indonesia and the Netherlands, who will jointly host it in Jakarta next year.
The UN Peacekeeping Ministerial is our flagship forum on peacekeeping bringing together Governments to pledge their political and concrete support for peacekeeping operations worldwide, to ensure that our Missions have the political support, the resources and the capabilities needed to carry out their mandates effectively.
During the handover event, Jean-Pierre Lacroix, our Under-Secretary-General for Peace Operations, thanked Germany for successfully hosting the 2025 Ministerial meeting on peacekeeping.
Data Commons
Yesterday afternoon, the Secretary-General
launched the UN System Data Commons
, a new UN80 initiative that makes publicly available data and statistics from across the UN system easier to find and connect through a single gateway — it’s quite an interesting tool for those of you interested in statistics and numbers. You can access it at
data.un.org
.  I think anybody can remember that.
Twenty-six UN system entities are participating, and some 44 million data points are already available.  You can search across institutions while tracing information back to its original source.
The purpose is simple:  to make the wealth of trusted information we produce everyday more accessible to Member States, researchers and the public, and more usable in a world in which people increasingly obtain information through digital and AI tools.
Pakistan
Turning to Pakistan:  I can tell you that the Secretary-General strongly condemns the bomb attack at a mosque in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, which reportedly killed at least 16 people, including police officers, and injured more than 50 others.
He extends his deepest condolences to the families of those killed and wishes a speedy and full recovery to those injured.
The Secretary-General underscores that attacks targeting civilians and places of worship are unacceptable and calls for those responsible to be held to account and to be brought to justice.
He expresses the solidarity of the United Nations with the people and Government of Pakistan in their efforts to address terrorism and extremism.
Yemen
Turning to Yemen, our humanitarian colleagues tell us that as fighting continues in that country and as more families are being forced to leave their homes, we and our partners are stepping up support for them.
Between 1 August and 16 September, our humanitarian partners and ourselves reached more than 11,000 displaced households with rapid response kits and other urgently needed assistance.  While this marks a significant scale-up of the response, the needs are continuing to outpace relief efforts, which face ongoing funding shortfalls and access constraints.
During the same period, our OCHA (Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs) colleagues report that more than 18,600 households were displaced across Yemen.  Sadly, that number will likely rise if the fighting continues.
Meanwhile, the Human Rights Office continues to monitor and verify civilian harm and gather testimonies from those affected.  Since hostilities escalated in August, the Human Rights office has documented 110 civilian casualties in 31 incidents, including 28 killed and 82 injured, across Taizz, Al Hudaydah and Marib.  Insecurity and access constraints continue to hamper our ability to verify the actual number as you can imagine is very likely higher.
We urge all parties, and those with influence over them, to act immediately to de-escalate, protect civilians and facilitate humanitarian access.  Every effort must be made to prevent further loss of life.
Djibouti
And across the Strait in Djibouti, people are continuing to arrive in Djibouti from Yemen after perilous journeys in small vessels.  Some describe having to leave family members behind, and reports indicate that thousands more trying to leave Yemen have been unable to flee due to insecurity and fuel shortages, that has been leaving them stranded.
In Obock in Djibouti, the new arrivals are being transferred from coastal entry points to Markazi refugee village, where they can be registered and receive support from our colleagues at UNHCR (United Nations Refugee Agency) and access national services.  We and partners are on the ground providing temporary shelter, transportation, relief items, food, water and healthcare, while supporting the Government of Djibouti and the national refugee authority with reception, with registration of refugees.
The Government, UNHCR and partners are preparing for at least 10,000 new arrivals, at a time when Djibouti already hosts some 36,000 refugees, mainly from Somalia and Ethiopia, this obviously places further pressure on an already stretched response.
The pressure is not limited to Djibouti.  Authorities in Somaliland and Puntland — areas of Somalia — have also reported increased arrivals from Yemen, including Yemeni refugees and Somali refugee returnees.
We continue urgently to call for international support to respond to the rapidly growing needs of people forced to flee their homes, both inside Yemen and across the Red Sea.  Without additional resources now, already stretched protection and assistance capacity could quickly be overwhelmed as the displacement numbers continue to rise.
Occupied Palestinian Territory
Turning to the Occupied Palestinian Territory, the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs reports that parts of a war-damaged residential building collapsed today in Gaza City, the second such incident this week, as you know. Emergency teams were able to rescue children who were underneath the building, and only minor injuries were reported.
Our humanitarian partners continue to warn that families are living amid widespread destruction, immense humanitarian needs, and ongoing restrictions on access and the entry of essential supplies.  An estimated 1.2 million people, that’s 58 per cent of the population, face shelter conditions ranging from critical to catastrophic.
And as the first rains of the season fell over Gaza today, our humanitarian partners stress that more than 65 per cent of families they’ve assessed are living in tents or makeshift shelters, with over half of those described as being in poor or very poor condition.
Once again, we urge the Israeli authorities to approve the entry of heavy equipment, engine oil, durable shelter materials and other items necessary to repair damaged buildings and help people secure safer living conditions.
Last week, in addition to the 200 households reached with rapid assistance, which we told you about yesterday, we and our partners provided nearly 6,600 families with routine shelter support, which included hundreds of new tents, as well as tarpaulins, kitchen sets, solar lights, sleeping mats and blankets.
Our humanitarian partners say more can be done to meet urgent needs once additional funding is available.
Democratic Republic of the Congo
Turning to the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where our colleagues tell us that hostilities in the eastern provinces of North and South Kivu continue to drive displacement and place further strain on already overstretched communities.
Since mid-August, intensified fighting in the two provinces forced more than 70,000 human beings to flee to Walikale territory in North Kivu — that’s what our local partners have told us.  The arrival of so many displaced people is placing additional pressure on basic services and host communities, while insecurity remains a big restricting factor on the movement of civilians making it more difficult for us to reach people in need.
Our Humanitarian Coordinator, Damien Mama, was in the town of Masisi, in North Kivu, this morning and will travel to Goma later today where he will meet with humanitarian organisations to discuss protection of civilians, access constraints and efforts to scale up assistance.
We once again call on all parties to protect civilians and ensure safe, sustained and unhindered humanitarian access so that life-saving assistance can reach those who need it the most.
Also, I wanted to flag that today we commemorate the sixty-fifth anniversary of the death of our former Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld, who, as you know, was killed in a plane crash near Ndola, Zambia, while on a mediation mission in the Congo.
As part of the commemoration ceremony, the Head of our Peacekeeping Mission in the DRC (MONUSCO), James Swan, joined representatives of the Government of Zambia, members of the diplomatic community and other guests at the crash site to honour Hammarskjöld’s enduring legacy of courage, service and active peacemaking.
Ukraine
Turning to Ukraine.  Our UN Human Rights Office report published today says sexual violence has been used against civilians and prisoners of war in Ukraine in a shocking pattern of torture, intimidation and coercion since the start of the Russian Federation’s full-scale armed attack on its neighbour more than four years ago.  According to the report, 1,000 cases of sexual violence were documented from 24 February 2022 through to the end of July this year.
Of these cases, 89 per cent were attributed to Russian authorities, including members of the armed forces, the prison service and the national security service.  The remaining 11 per cent were attributed to Ukrainian authorities.  The report notes that most of the victims of sexual violence were men, particularly in places of detention, but women and children were also affected, primarily in occupied territory of Ukraine.
Volker Türk, our High Commissioner for Human Rights says that he is deeply alarmed by the prevalence of these abhorrent acts documented in the report.
Haiti
Turning to Haiti, closer to these shores, our humanitarian colleagues warn that the nutrition situation for children continues to deteriorate in Haiti.
According to our colleagues at UNICEF, the Food and Agriculture Organization and the World Food Programme, more than a quarter of a million children between the ages of 6 to 59 months old are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition between June of this year and May of next year.  This includes nearly 94,000 children facing severe acute malnutrition, which can become life-threatening without timely treatment.
As we’ve told you several times from this podium, continued violence forces families to flee their homes, and disrupts children’s access to food, healthcare and nutrition treatment.  Some 1.5 million people are internally displaced, half of them children, while nearly half of Haiti’s population is facing food insecurity.
Our humanitarian colleagues warn that the situation might become critical between December of this year and May of next year, when 29 out of 140 municipalities are projected to reach IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 3, while the neighbourhoods of Cité Soleil and Cabaret are expected to reach Phase 4.
The nutrition sector under the Haiti Humanitarian Response Plan requires $41 million but is less than 20 per cent funded, with just $7.5 million received.  We need more money, and we need it very, very soon.
International Equal Pay Day
A couple of international days to flag.
Today is International Equal Pay Day.  Across all regions, women are paid less than men, with the gender pay gap estimated at around 20 per cent globally.
While equal pay for men and women has been widely endorsed, applying it in practice has been difficult, and progress on narrowing that gap has been slow.
World Cleanup Day
And Sunday, get your brooms, is also World Cleanup Day, and our focus this year is on food waste.
Every day, more than 1 billion meals are wasted worldwide, as around 673 million people go hungry.  The Day is a reminder of our shared responsibility to manage waste and resources more sustainably.
