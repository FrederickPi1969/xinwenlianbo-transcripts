Daily Press Briefing by the Office of the Spokesperson for the Secretary-General
The following is a near-verbatim transcript of today’s briefing by Stéphane Dujarric, Spokesman for the Secretary-General.
All right, good afternoon.  Is it Monday today?  No, Tuesday. Since I haven’t seen you yesterday, happy Monday, nonetheless.
Secretary-General’s Press Conference
Tomorrow, at 11 a.m., be in this room if you want to ask a question to the Secretary-General.  He will be here to brief you ahead of the eighty-first session of the General Assembly.
We will have the briefing live, obviously, on UN Web TV and many other platforms, but we will not take any Zoom questions, given that we are already have enough trouble taking all the questions in the room.  So, if you want to ask him questions, grab a seat.
And obviously, we will not have a briefing tomorrow.
Global Risk Report 2026
Today, we launched the 2026 Global Risk Report, which finds that geopolitical tensions and large-scale war are now seen as among the most pressing and interconnected risks of our world.
Drawing on the responses of more than 1,300 stakeholders from governments, business, civil society and the UN system, the report highlights growing concern over risks linked to artificial intelligence and emerging technologies, while warning that climate change and other environmental challenges remain among the world’s most significant threats.
However, despite a rapidly evolving risk landscape, international cooperation remains the most effective way to address these challenges and prevent today’s risks from becoming tomorrow’s crises.
The full report is available to you online should you wish to read it, and I encourage you to look at it.
Yemen
We will start in Yemen, where our Emergency Relief Coordinator, Tom Fletcher, has just released $9 million from the Central Emergency Relief Fund (CERF) to provide urgent assistance to displaced families and to the communities receiving them.  This comes in response to the humanitarian impact of the ongoing hostilities.  We are deeply concerned about the situation ongoing there.
Our OCHA (Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs) colleagues tell us that the toll on civilians continues to grow, forcing families from their homes, killing and injuring those caught in the crossfire, and already stretched humanitarian operations are coming under growing pressure.
The UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) is warning that rapidly escalating hostilities, especially on the western coast of Yemen, have displaced more than 100,000 human beings inside the country and forced thousands more to seek safety across the Bab el-Mandeb Strait in Djibouti.  And as you can imagine the dangers of that crossing.  Many families fled at short notice with very few belongings.
We and our humanitarian partners continue to support displaced families and others caught up in the hostilities in the affected areas.
The Refugee Agency is leading the inter-agency response for protection, shelter, and site coordination, working closely with humanitarian partners that continue to deliver assistance despite all of the challenges that you know about.
It has also activated its emergency response capacity inside Yemen, focusing on life-saving protection, shelter, relief items and financial assistance.
One of the biggest challenges are the constraints on movement due to insecurity because of the fighting.  Our OCHA colleagues say that access and movements along key routes are restricted or suspended, making it harder to reach many of those in dire need.  Humanitarian supplies and resources are also running low.
We call, yet again, on the parties to protect civilians, to respect international humanitarian law, to respect international law and to facilitate the safe and unhindered access of humanitarian aid so we can reach all those who need it, wherever they may be.
Saudi Arabia
And in Saudi Arabia, authorities are telling us that yesterday, 13 civilians were injured following strikes, while earlier last week, authorities also reported that strikes injured 73 civilians, including women and children.
On 10 September, an attack on the East-West Pipeline reportedly injured people and led to its precautionary closure.  As you know, the attack was among several affecting energy infrastructure.
The widening escalation is adding further pressure on already underfunded and overstretched humanitarian operations in the region, where the needs remain high, while disruptions to trade, energy and essential supplies risk compounding humanitarian needs both within and beyond the region.
