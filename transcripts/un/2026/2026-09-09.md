# Daily Press Briefing by the Office of the Spokesperson for the Secretary-General

The following is a near-verbatim transcript of today’s briefing by Stéphane Dujarric, Spokesman for the Secretary-General.

Briefings

Good afternoon.  Good afternoon to you all.  In a short while, we will be joined by Lila Pieters Yahia, who is, you know, the Resident Coordinator for Nepal.  She'll be here to brief you on the situation in Nepal following the devastating floods there and all the work of the United Nations and her team have been doing.

Tomorrow at 11 a.m., we'll have a briefing by Matthew Hollingsworth, Assistant Executive Director for Program Operations at the World Food Programme (WFP).  He'll brief you virtually from Juba on his mission to South Sudan.  And around noon, we'll have as our guest Alexander De Croo, the Administrator of the UN Development Programme (UNDP), who is briefing you virtually from Kyiv in Ukraine, where he is currently on a mission.

Secretary-General/India

Trip announcement for you on Thursday.  The Secretary-General will travel to New Delhi in India to attend the eighteenth Summit of the BRICS.  The Secretary-General has been invited to speak at the Open Session on “Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability:  Shaping the Future for Inclusive Global Growth”.  That will take place on 13 September.  In his remarks, the Secretary-General is expected to note that today's economic, demographic and geopolitical realities are not those of 1945, and that countries represented at the summit speak for that changing reality.

He's also expected to underscore that multipolarity can help bring balance to global systems and institutions, but this requires forming multilateral institutions, reforming multilateral institutions, including the Bretton Woods system, the Security Council, among others, to reflect today's realities.  He is expected to have a bilateral meeting with the Prime Minister of India, Narendra Modi, and he's also expected to have bilateral meetings with some other officials who will be attending the summit, but we'll share those details as we get them.

Peace Bell

Earlier this morning, he rang the Peace Bell at our yearly tradition at the start of each General Assembly session, and remarks delivered there.  The Secretary-General said that each year the toll of the Peace Bell reminds us of the most important and consequential job we have, building peace. In our world today, Mr. [António] Guterres said, action for peace is faltering and violence and division are tearing apart communities.  But, where Governments are failing to deliver peace, he said people are showing up in other ways.

Around the world, volunteers, advocates, civil society leaders, peacekeepers, and peacemakers are doing the heavy lifting of peace.  As world leaders prepare to gather at the United Nations during the coming weeks, the Secretary-General urges them to follow the lead of these champions of peace, adding that this is the last time he will ring the Peace Bell, but he will always stand with the champions of peace around the world.  Those remarks were shared with you.  And this afternoon he will also speak at the General Assembly at the official commemoration following the passing of the late Emir of Qatar.

Deputy Secretary-General’s Travel

Turning to Nigeria, I just want to flag that our Deputy Secretary-General is on a visit there.  Today, in Imo State, she joined the First Lady of Nigeria, Senator Oluremi Tinubu, to launch a nationwide programme on women's economic empowerment.  It will support vulnerable women across the country to build sustainable livelihoods and strengthen resilience.  She also met with the governor and other senior Government officials and visited a UNDP project in Imo Digital City, which supports efforts to expand digital skills and innovation and job opportunities.

Strait of Hormuz

Turning to the Strait of Hormuz, I can tell you the Secretary-General is deeply concerned by reports of further escalations in and around the Strait of Hormuz over the past days, including attacks against commercial vessels and threats of additional action against maritime and energy infrastructure.  Navigational rights and freedom of navigation in and around the Strait of Hormuz must be fully restored and respected in accordance with international law.

We recall the Secretary-General's appeal to all concerned to exercise utmost restraint and refrain from actions and rhetoric that could further inflame an already volatile situation.  Any further escalation risks grave consequences for civilians, regional stability, international peace and security, and the global economy, as we've been telling you.  The Secretary-General urges the parties to make full use of diplomatic channels and resolve issues through dialogue and negotiations.  The United Nations Secretariat, as we've said, is ready to support all credible efforts aimed at de-escalation and a comprehensive and durable settlement.

Lebanon

Turning to Lebanon, our humanitarian colleagues on the ground tell us that hostilities continue to affect communities across the southern part of the country.  The Secretary-General remains deeply concerned by the continued Israeli military operations in southern Lebanon, particularly in Nabatieh and surrounding areas.

As you will have seen, we issued a statement yesterday in which he condemned the mounting civilian casualties, including women and children killed, as well as injuries of others, in intensifying attacks in recent days.  He's particularly alarmed at reports of Israeli strikes affecting hospitals, as well as restrictions on humanitarian movements.  In Mansouri, which is a coastal village in South Governorate, partially in the Israel Defense Forces (IDF)-declared military area, a series of demolitions and bombardments continued this morning.

The continued destruction of home and public infrastructure risks further disrupting people's returns to their communities and setting back early recovery in areas already devastated by displacement and widespread damage.  We're also concerned about humanitarian access, particularly south of the Litani River.  As we told you late last month, access to frontline locations remains highly restricted, often requiring passage through physical barriers controlled by the IDF.

Despite these constraints, our partners have continued to reach people in need, with some 663 humanitarian movements facilitated through the humanitarian notification system since the start of the escalation on 2 March. We call again on parties to comply with their obligations under international law, including international humanitarian law.  Civilian and civilian objects must be protected at all times, and attacks directed against them are unacceptable.

As we reiterate the Secretary-General's appeal for an immediate cessation of hostilities, he calls on Israel to withdraw its forces from Lebanese territory and urges all parties to respect Lebanon's sovereignty and territorial integrity and fulfill their obligations under Security Council resolution 1701 (2006).

Occupied Palestinian Territory

Turning to the Occupied Palestinian Territory, our humanitarian partners tell us that Israeli air strikes and other attacks continue to hit populated areas, putting civilians at risk and causing damage to property.  Yesterday, in Gaza, an air strike hit a building in Al Bureij, an area near Deir al-Balah, near two UN schools that have been serving as temporary shelters for people who have been displaced, as well as a bakery supported by the UN.

No injuries have been reported, but people in the area have alerted us about roughly 100 households who've lost their shelters and belongings.  We and our humanitarian partners are assessing these needs and mobilizing rapid assistance, but teams warn that stocks of critical supplies are insufficient, both because of funding shortfalls and continued heavy restrictions on our partners' ability to replenish those stockpiles.  We reiterate that civilians and civilian objects, including shelters and humanitarian facilities, must always be respected.

Yesterday, UN-Women published a report saying that women-headed households have doubled since October 2023.  That leads to about 86,000 households, almost every fourth family.  This includes widows and women whose husbands are missing, detained, severely injured, or forcibly disappeared, or whose fate just remains unknown.  Households headed by women face greater hardship than male-headed households, including higher rates of inadequate shelter, housing damage, and displacement. For instance, 68 per cent of women-headed households lack adequate shelter, compared to 55 per cent for households headed by men.

Sudan

Turning to Sudan, where we there, too, continue to see horrific humanitarian situation.  Many people continue to face enormous challenges as insecurity and disease outbreaks continue to take a heavy toll.  Our colleagues at the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) tell us that, despite the difficult conditions on the ground, we and our partners continue to provide critical assistance, including efforts to contain cholera in Darfur and other impacted areas.  A 10-day cholera vaccination campaign to protect at least 95,000 people was launched yesterday in Tawila in North Darfur with the World Health Organization (WHO) providing support to local authorities.  According to WHO, the current cholera outbreak, which, as you may recall, was declared in June, has resulted in nearly 2,900 reported cases and 247 associated deaths in 6 states in Sudan.

Elsewhere in North Darfur, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) tells us that about 1,300 people were forced to flee their homes amid increased insecurity last week in Um Baru locality near the border with Chad.  And in Blue Nile State, IOM reports that increased insecurity forced more than 3,300 people to leave their homes from several villages in Geisan locality close to the border with Ethiopia.

Meanwhile, our humanitarian colleagues tell us that intercommunal tensions are rising in parts of the south and central Darfur states where several deadly incidents have been reported in recent days.  Once again, here as well, we call on parties to respect their obligations under international law, including international humanitarian law, to protect civilians and civilian infrastructure and to facilitate the rapid, safe, and unhindered delivery of humanitarian goods.

Central African Republic

In the Central African Republic, our humanitarian colleagues are raising concerns over deteriorating child protection and maternal health conditions in the south-east of the country.  Between February and September, girls under the age of 18 accounted for nearly 60 per cent of the deliveries recorded at healthcare facilities in Zemio, the largest town in the area.  Humanitarians warn that the true figure is likely higher as many births are happening at home.  Most health facilities in Zemio have reportedly been looted and vandalized by armed groups.

Women and girls in the area face heightened risks of sexual violence and exploitation.  Access to healthcare remains a major challenge across the country, with more than 40 per cent of the health facilities only partially accessible due to insecurity and other constraints, especially in Haut-Mbomou Prefecture.  Following serious security incidents in late June, OCHA and several partners temporarily suspended operations in Zemio.  In the upcoming days, an OCHA-led mission will seek to restore full humanitarian access to the area.

Myanmar

From Myanmar, our humanitarian colleagues tell us that intensified hostilities in Rakhine State over the past week, marked by air strikes and artillery shelling, have forced people to flee their homes and caused civilian casualties, and we condemn all and every attack on civilians.  Since [3 September], hostilities have displaced more than 1,100 people across the townships of Gwa, Ponnagyun, Rathedaung and Thandwe, with many people taking shelter in temporary locations.

Yesterday, artillery shells reportedly landed in residential areas of downtown Sittwe, including on a school compound some 250 metres from one of our offices in a residential area that is also near a displacement camp. The incident prompted the temporary suspension of classes and closure of businesses in the city.  Despite severe challenges, we, along with our humanitarian partners, continue to provide assistance in Rakhine and other conflict-impacted areas of Myanmar, in line with the 2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan.  We remind all parties to the conflict of their obligations under international law, including humanitarian law, to protect civilians and civilian infrastructure.

Ebola/Rwanda

And, sorry, just a quick note on… I should have read earlier on Rwanda and Ebola. We, with our humanitarian partners, continue to support the Government-led efforts to prepare for a potential Ebola outbreak in Rwanda, which has not happened.  We've trained nearly 200 community health officers on infection and prevention and control, early detection and risk communication.  Last week, we also assessed border crossings and health facilities to review screening detection capacities as well as staff preparedness.  We're also supporting infection-prevention and hygiene measures at the Rugerero Ebola Training Centre in the western province, and we've also trained Red Cross volunteers and ambulance drivers on transport procedures. Rwanda, as I said, has no confirmed Ebola cases but remains at high risk, given its proximity to the highest impacted areas in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

International Day to Protect Education from Attack

Today is the International Day to Protect Education from Attack, which comes as armed conflict continues to escalate around the world and attacks on education rise with it.  In his message, the Secretary-General says education is a fundamental human right but exercising that right is becoming increasingly dangerous.  He stresses that all states have an obligation to ensure the right to education for every child and the safety of schools and places of learning.  All parties to the conflict must stop and prevent attacks on schools and meet their obligations under international law and hold their perpetrators to account.

Again, as we've said, the plan is clear.  It is useful.  The question is whether all the parties involved in this conflict are willing to let it go forward. He's spoken to you about his critical role in reversing the edicts that Portugal had put in place during the Inquisition that expelled Jews from Portugal.  When he was in government, he was critical in reversing the edict and putting forward the laws that would allow descendants of those Jews to reclaim Portuguese citizenship.  And I think here at the UN, he's been very clear on calling out antisemitism.
