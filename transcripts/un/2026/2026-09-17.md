Daily Press Briefing by the Office of the Spokesperson for the Secretary-General
The following is a near-verbatim transcript of today’s briefing by Stéphane Dujarric, Spokesman for the Secretary-General.

Hate Speech
This morning, the Secretary-General spoke at an event at the Global Conference on Countering Hate Speech, saying that the
toxic message of hate speech is spreading fast and striking deep
.  It is taking on new forms, finding new pathways, and weaponizing new technologies. And this is from digital platforms to algorithms to artificial intelligence.
The Secretary-General said we must raise our collective voice and reject hatred, and we must redouble our efforts to tackle hate speech online and offline. Reining in hate speech does not mean extinguishing freedom of expression, he said, but freedom of expression must never be an excuse for causing and compounding harm.  The Secretary-General noted that the UN Strategy and Plan of Action on Hate Speech is grounded in international human rights, including the right to freedom of opinion and expression and the principles of equality and non-discrimination.
This afternoon at 4:30 p.m., the Secretary-General will
speak at the launch of the UN System Data Commons
, which creates a one-stop shop enabling easier public access to data and statistics from throughout the UN system.  It’s going to be quite a handy tool.  Twenty-six UN entities are joining forces to create a single gateway to their public data and statistics.
Almost 44 million data points are to be made available on the new platform today.  That’s a lot of data points.  […]
Peacekeeping
Just to note that our colleague Jean-Pierre Lacroix has concluded his latest travel, which included visits to the Kyrgyz Republic in China.  In the Kyrgyz Republic, he discussed peacekeeping issues with governmental authorities ahead of the country’s upcoming membership in the Security Council.  The Kyrgyz Republic has long been a longstanding contributor to peacekeeping, dating back shortly after its independence, which was 35 years ago.
Then he travelled to Beijing, where he participated in the 13
Beijing Xiangshan Forum, focused on international security and security governance. As you’ll recall, China is the second largest financial contributor to UN peacekeeping and supports the UN Peace and Development Trust Fund and currently deploys more than 1,600 peacekeepers throughout our missions.  Mr. Lacroix is now safely back in New York.
Syria — Security Council
short while ago in the Security Council
, members heard from our colleague Claudio Cordone, the Deputy Special Envoy for Syria, and they also heard from Tom Fletcher, our Emergency Relief Coordinator.  Mr. Cordone spoke about the progress being made in Syria, notably the preparations for holding future parliamentary and presidential elections in line with the new constitution, underscoring the developing cooperation with the Syrian authorities and the potential opportunities for UN electoral assistance.
For his part, Mr. Fletcher focused, obviously, on the humanitarian situation in Syria.  He said that since December 2024, more than 2 million internally displaced Syrians and 1.8 million refugees have gone home voluntarily.  He called this a historic wave of return and the highest to Syria in over two decades.
In the pursuit of the efforts to help Syrians return, rebuild, and move beyond crisis, Mr. Fletcher called on the Security Council and the international community to invest in the transition out of the humanitarian need and towards reconstruction and growth to remove the obstacles standing between recovery and development and to redouble diplomatic efforts in support of Syria’s territorial integrity.
Lebanon
And next to Syria, we have Lebanon, where our colleagues at the Office of the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) tell us that hostilities are taking a heavy toll on children as the new school year gets under way.  Nearly 100,000 children have been unable to return to school because of damage, because of destruction, because of insecurity, and displacement.  That’s what national authorities are telling us.
Damage to education facilities has been significant since the escalation began on 2 March.  According to Lebanese authorities, some 40 per cent of more than 400 schools have been attacked, including 37 that were totally destroyed and 101 severely damaged.  Displacement is also disrupting education.
Around 20 per cent of school-age children were displaced during the escalation.  Hundreds of schools have been used as collective shelters.  Today, 16,000 displaced people, including 2,500 children, remain in 164 shelters.
OCHA reiterates that children must be protected from the impact of hostilities and that schools must remain safe.  Also, that children must be able to return to school safely and without any fear.
On the ground, meanwhile, our peacekeeping colleagues in Lebanon at UNIFIL (United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon) continue to observe daily IDF (Israeli Defence Forces) activities in the Lebanese territory, in the air, and land and sea.
Peacekeepers recorded 152 violations of Lebanese airspace by the IDF over the past three days, with 309 IDF trajectories, 24 air strikes, and incursions into Lebanese territorial waters by eight Israeli defence patrol boats.
Israeli drones continue to drop explosive-filled jerrycans within UNIFIL’s area of operations, particularly near al-Mansouri in Sector West, where at least 11 explosions were recorded on Monday and Wednesday.  Yesterday, an unarmed drone originating from an IDF position south of the Blue Line crashed near a UNIFIL position in Houla.
Additional IDF drones subsequently monitored both the recovery efforts and UNIFIL’s assessment of the crash site.  Despite all of this, between Monday and Wednesday, the peacekeeping mission facilitated 17 humanitarian missions in coordination with our humanitarian colleagues.
Separately, in a preparatory meeting that is taking place today in Paris, which is co-hosted by France and Jordan, the head of the International Conference in Support of the Lebanese Armed Forces and Internal Security Forces, the officer-in-charge of the UN Special Coordinator’s Office for Lebanon, Jean Arnault, is in Paris, where he’s taking part in the meeting.
We welcome this timely initiative and underscore the importance of sustained international support to strengthen Lebanon’s security institutions.
Yemen
And in Yemen, the situation on our humanitarian colleagues on the ground tell us that the situation continues to deteriorate for people as hostilities spread to more areas in the country, taking a devastating toll on families who are already struggling.  The toll on civilians is rising, and the need for access to those who need assistance becomes ever more acute.
Since the start of September and as of yesterday, our colleagues at the UN Human Rights Office verified 55 civilian casualties in 19 incidents in Taiz, al-Hodeidah, Marib, and Lahj.  Sixteen people were killed, including 10 children, and dozens of others were wounded.  Overall, women and children account for 84 per cent of the casualties, 84 per cent.
But I want to stress that these are only verified figures.  Tragically, the actual toll is likely to be much, much higher. We, of course, condemn in the strongest possible term the killing and maiming of civilians.
Access and funding constraints continue to hamper our efforts at a humanitarian response.  Security incidents impacting humanitarian premises, supplies, and movements are, of course, hindering our operations.  The scale of these constraints is clear.
Our partners have 15,000 emergency kits ready to be distributed, but road closures mean most of them cannot get to those who need them in time.  The Mokha seaport remains closed after sustaining heavy damage, and the World Food Programme (WFP) has reported disruptions at 17 frontline distribution points.
Despite these extremely difficult conditions, we and our humanitarian partners have reached so far more than 70,000 displaced people with assistance, including rapid response kits and other very needed items.
Following the release of $9 million from the Central Emergency Response Fund, OCHA is now working to transform these funds into urgent assistance for displaced families and the communities hosting them.  But more funding is needed.  The 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan needs and response plan for Yemen is barely 20 per cent funded, meaning partners are being forced to reduce life-saving assistance at a moment when communities need it more than ever.
The current escalation is putting even more strain on already limited resources. And once again, we call on the parties to protect civilians, to respect international humanitarian law, and facilitate the unhindered delivery of humanitarian aid, wherever and whenever it is needed.
Djibouti
And on a related note, on the other side of the Red Sea from Yemen, there is Djibouti, where the government and our teams are seeking approximately $10 million to assist up to 10,000 people or 2,000 families over the next six months, following a sharp increase of arrivals from Yemen.
As of today, more than 2,800 men, women, and children have reached the Obock region after crossing the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, and you can imagine how perilous that crossing can be.  We, together with the Djibouti authorities and our humanitarian partners, are providing reception, registration, food, shelter, water, and health care and protection services.  WFP, for example, distributed 12,000 rations of high-energy biscuits, while the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) is supporting registration and logistics.
UNICEF (United Nations Children’s Fund) has also deployed personnel to be on site.  The UN Resident Coordinator, Jose Barahona, called for rapid and flexible international support during this critical period, stressing that additional resources are urgently needed to meet the growing humanitarian needs of new arrivals.
Occupied Palestinian Territory
And turning to the occupied Palestinian territory, Tom Fletcher, as you know, our Emergency Relief Coordinator, as we tell you every day, had a call yesterday with the Prime Minister of the State of Palestine, Mohammad Mustafa.
He said that through the eighty-first session of the General Assembly, he will push a global access initiative to reach 11 million people with life-saving aid, including aid to support some 2 million people in Gaza through the winter.
On the ground, the humanitarian coordinator, Ramiz Alakbarov, today allocated $2 million from the OPT Humanitarian Fund to our health partners so they can continue supporting Gaza’s health system.  Through this allocation, our partners will provide half a million dollars’ worth of fuel every month for four months to sustain dozens of critical health services with backup generators while the electricity grid continues to be non-functioning.
The allocation comes at a critical point, as funding shortages undermine efforts to sustain health care in Gaza.  This stopgap measure will keep life-saving services from being interrupted.  Our humanitarian partners stress that more sustainable solutions are urgently needed, including Israeli approvals to import solar energy equipment and restart operations at the Gaza power plant.
More funding is also required as the need for fuel and other supply services go beyond the health sector.  These supplies must be replenished on a sustainable, predictable, and regular basis. Meanwhile, we and our partners continue to provide shelter and other critical assistance to families affected by the collapse of the war-damaged building in Gaza that took place yesterday. UNICEF reiterated the call for urgent approval of heavy machinery, specialized equipment, engine oil, spare parts and fuel.
Last week, our humanitarian partners mobilized rapid local support for more than 200 households who had lost their shelters or were displaced yet again due to airstrikes, domestic fires, or other incidents.
Ukraine
And a quick note from Ukraine, where our humanitarian colleagues tell us that the last 24 hours, several civilians were killed and injured across the country.  That includes children.  This is according to what local authorities are telling our colleagues on the ground.
Overnight attacks impacted Kyiv, Odesa, and Zaporizhzhia, with further casualties reported in Sumy, Kharkiv, Kherson, Dnipro and Donetsk region.
Humanitarians continue to be impacted.  On 14 September, according to a partner, an attack damaged an aid vehicle in Sloviansk in the Donetsk region.  Our humanitarian colleagues tell us that nearly 140 incidents affecting humanitarian personnel and assets and facilities were recorded between January and July this year.
According to the UN Human Rights Office, August had the second highest number of civilian casualties this year after July.  More than 370 civilians were killed and over 2,300 were injured. Overall, the number of civilian casualties in the first eight months of the year was 55 per cent than during the same period last year.
Abyei
And a quick note from our colleagues at the peacekeeping operation in Abyei (UNISFA), who strongly condemned the outbreak of violence in the Amiet Common Market on Tuesday, which left 26 people dead and 82 injured. Following the deadly violence, the mission immediately reinforced its security presence in and around the market, deploying an additional force to try to de-escalate the situation.  It is also actively engaged with local authorities and community leaders to restore calm.
Lieutenant General Ganesh Kumar Shreshta, the head of the mission, says the peacekeepers are taking all measures to protect civilians and prevent retaliation so that the priority is to prevent further loss of life.  The mission calls on relevant authorities to work closely to conduct a swift investigation and hold the perpetrators to account.
South Africa
And from South Africa, the UN team there, led by the Resident Coordinator Nelson Muffuh, is sounding the alarm following the killing of several women within a short period of time in the East Rand region of the Gauteng province, which is in the northeast of South Africa.
We condemn the violence against women and girls in all its forms and manifestations.  Our team said there are not isolated tragedies but reflect the persistent and deadly violence faced by women and girls across South Africa.  The country has declared gender-based violence and femicide a national disaster, and our UN team on the ground is calling for action equal with the scale and gravity of the crisis.
In line with South Africa’s National Strategic Plan on Gender-Based Violence and Femicide and the UN Cooperation Framework, the UN on the ground says commitments and collaborative action must translate into tangible results, including greater accountability, access to justice, economic empowerment, backed by sustained financing, and robust monitoring.  We remain committed in supporting South Africa, meeting its international human rights obligations and national efforts to uphold human rights, safety, and well-being.  The team, of course, and we join them, extend our condolences to the families of the victims, and we stand in solidarity with the women and girls across South Africa.
Reprisals
I want to flag that the Secretary-General’s report on reprisals issued yesterday shows that people around the world continue to pay steep price for cooperating or just seeking to cooperate with the United Nations on human rights, facing persistent intimidation and reprisals amid disturbing trends in transnational repression and digital surveillance targeting civil society actors.  Continued misuse of legislation, including counter-terrorism, cybercrime, and national security laws and regulation is also a concern.
The report urges states to refrain from any acts of intimidation and reprisals, ensure accountability for violations and abuses, and protect those who engage with us in human rights investigations.
Water Resources
Also today, a report released by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) notes that rivers across the globe last year had one of the driest years in more than three decades.  The report shows a continuation of the long-term decline of the Earth’s freshwater storage and in glacier retreats trends, with serious implications for people and economies of the future.
The report points out that Asia and Africa together accounted for at least half of the world’s recorded water-related extreme events and more than 80 per cent of reported associated deaths over the past five years.
World Patient Safety Day
Today is World Patient Safety Day.  On this day, we bring together patients, families, caregivers, communities, health workers, and healthcare leaders and policymakers to show their commitments to a safer care.
Nicholas Haysom Memorial
I just want to flag, too, that at 9:30 a.m. tomorrow, our colleagues in the Peacekeeping Department and the Political Affairs Department have organized a memorial event to honour the memory of Nicholas Haysom, better known to us as Fink.
As you know, Fink was most recently head of the Peacekeeping Mission in South Sudan.  Many of you in the press knew him and appreciated him.  He was a dear colleague in the many places where he was posted, as well as his native South Africa.
You’re welcome to attend or follow it on UN Web TV.
