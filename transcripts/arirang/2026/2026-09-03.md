02:39 How far have women come in local politics in Korea? Female politicians in Korea are becoming increasingly involved in issues that directly affect residents' daily lives. Our political correspondent Ahn Sung-jin sat down with local female politicians. In Seoul, out of 25 district chiefs, only two are women: the mayors of Eunpyeong-gu and Gangdong-gu districts. They say their experiences as women have influenced how they lead and prioritize issues. Mayor of Eunpyeong-gu district Kim Me-kyung made history as the first woman to win a third term as a Seoul district chief. "Entering politics can be challenging for women, but I think more of us should take the step, especially by engaging with issues in their communities. It's meaningful that we saw the first woman leader in a gubernatorial level this year." As the first woman to serve as chair of Seoul's Urban Management Committee, she emphasizes the importance of trust between residents and the government. Her policies focus on residents' needs, including the construction of a regional recycling center for waste management and sophisticated programs for everyday life, such as support for new parents, young people, and the elderly. "I believe it's important that the local government stays close to residents and responds to problems by quickly stepping in and helping resolve disputes." Similarly, for Lee Soo-hee, the mayor of Gangdong-gu district, leadership meant taking responsibility, particularly for those whose needs might easily be overlooked. "Before entering politics, as a lawyer I worked on cases involving domestic violence and child abuse, and I think these experiences influenced the way I approach public policy, particularly when it comes to protecting people that can be overlooked." One example is Gangdong-gu's dementia family support center, the first of its kind created by a local government in the country. Gangdong-gu district is also one of Seoul's most active areas for redevelopment. Speeding up these projects with an expanded task force is a major administrative priority. For Lee Soo-hee, local politics is about solving practical problems that directly affect her constituents' lives. "Women are still relatively few in elected political positions, so they carry a strong sense of representation. I think that also gives them a greater sense of responsibility like myself." Though women's roles in politics have a way to go, many are already showing that local leadership is not measured by gender, but by the effort to understand and solve problems with experience, determination, and involvement. Ahn Sung-jin, Arirang News. 4 hours ago
