## Israeli Activist Maoz Inon Lost His Parents on Oct. 7, Decries Israel’s Ethnic Cleansing in Gaza

Israeli peace activist Maoz Inon lost both of his parents, who lived on a kibbutz just north of the Gaza border, in the Hamas attack. ## “Origins of a Genocide”: Al Jazeera’s Marwan Bishara on “Twelve Days That Shook the Middle East”

“During the Biden administration, the United States was basically complicit in genocide,” adds Bishara.

AMY GOODMAN: This is Democracy Now!, democracynow.org. I’m Amy Goodman.

Twelve Days That Shook the Middle East: Origins of a Genocide. That’s the title of a new book by the Al Jazeera senior political analyst Marwan Bishara. In the opening pages, he writes, “Twelve days were enough. It took no more than twelve days, from Netanyahu’s October 7 war speech to Biden’s October 19 rare Oval Office speech, to expose the anatomy of Israel’s war, its intent, its strategy, and the method of its madness. The genocide was not a slip; it was the result of a predetermined strategy for which there existed a blueprint, if not a premeditated decision. … Hamas had set the process in motion on October 7, it lost control of that process the very same day.”

Marwan Bishara has been with Al Jazeera for more than 20 years, has written three books and written for many publications, joining us now from Paris, France.

Marwan, congratulations on your book. Thank you for joining us. If you can talk about that quote and say — you say Hamas set the process in motion October 7th and immediately lost control of that process. What do you mean?

MARWAN BISHARA: Yes, absolutely. Clearly, the attack of October 7 was the trigger for what came after. And my argument is that we know about what came after, basically the past two or three years, from those very first 12 days after October 7. And it started with ideology. You know, genocides don’t start with bombs; they start with ideology. And in this case, the ideology has been decades in the making — a century, I would even say, in the making, in the sense of the establishment of a racist, supremacist system, regime, that had been in control of Palestinians and Palestinian life and destiny for the past 80 years, occupying them and dispossessing them and discriminating against them. This has been going on for many decades. So, that was ready, intact on the eve of October 7, October 8.

And then there was the dehumanization. Immediately after October 7, October 8, 9, 10, we saw this extraordinary campaign of dehumanization of the Palestinians as animals, Nazi-like. You cannot deal with them. They don’t understand. They are — they are primitive. They only understand the language of force and violence, and so on and so forth.

After that immediately came the hyperbole, the exaggeration of what exactly happened October 7. October 7 was bloody, and it was horrific. And your earlier guest testified to the fact that this was not easy for hundreds of Israeli civilians. But Netanyahu and his government had to go overboard, to exaggerate, to lie, to deceive about what exactly happened, and their role in it, as well, invoking the so-called Hannibal doctrine, where they themselves joined the bombing of those involved in the conflict during October 7.

MARWAN BISHARA: So, the Hannibal doctrine was something that was invoked in the early 1980s by the Israeli military, saying we cannot afford to have Israeli soldiers being captured, because then we’re going to have to negotiate their release, and that will cost us Palestinian — or, in that case, Lebanese — prisoners in Israeli jails. So, because they did not want to release Palestinian or Lebanese prisoners, they decided that if any one Israeli soldier — later on, civilian — is captured by any kidnappers, that the Israeli military then will take extraordinary measure to kill the operation, including, if it need be, to kill the soldier, the Israeli soldier, or the Israeli civilian.

So, on October 7, immediately after the war — after the operation started, in the early hours of the morning of October 7, apparently, the Israeli military invoked the Hannibal doctrine. And Israeli aircrafts, jets, you know, bombers, and tanks started carrying attacks against suspected conflict zones, whether it’s kibbutz or settlements, or whether it is convoys going back to Gaza. So, the invocation of that, of that controversial military order, clearly led to the death of perhaps hundreds of Israeli civilians and many, as well, among the Palestinians, whether it’s Hamas or any of those Palestinians who came into Gaza.

All of that, you know, taking that in consideration, what happened soon after that is the worst part of it, right? The establishment of a war cabinet, the declaration of war, the deployment of 100,000 soldiers on the border with Gaza, the recall of 350,000 reservists from the Israeli army, and setting up a strategy under the rubric of lethality, which has been developed over many years of wars in Gaza. And lethality is the one that involved mass destruction, not discriminant destruction, that involved the use of the so-called Lavender system, Gospel system, “Where’s My Daddy — Your Daddy?” system, and so on and so forth, that would actually lead to massive devastation of Gaza.

And that’s where the intention came. They’ve said it in press conferences, in war rooms. They’ve said it in public, in declarations, ministers and opposition leaders, about why there is no innocents in Gaza and why “we’re going to have to bomb all of them.” Some of them spoke about nuking them. Some of them spoke about “Nothing is going to be left standing in Gaza. We’re going to turn it into a deserted strip.” And all of these things were then repeated by the soldiers, not only as slogans, but even as songs — right? — repeated by the soldiers. So, the orders went down the hierarchy, from the government ministers to the army, and was implemented by the generals, who boasted of bombing Gaza back to the Stone Age. ## Three Years into Genocide, “The IDF Is Still Hunting Gazans”: Palestinian Journalist Akram al-Satarri

He points out that a large majority of structures in the Gaza Strip have been reduced to rubble and that Israel controls around 70% of Gaza. “The Israeli Air Force is still hovering all around different parts of the Gaza Strip, and the IDF is still hunting Gazans, disturbing the already disturbed life, spreading death and displacement once and once again.”

AMY GOODMAN: This is Democracy Now!, democracynow.org. I’m Amy Goodman.

We end today’s show in Gaza, where today’s date, October 7th, is seen as the beginning of Israel’s genocide. In the three years since the Hamas attack on Israel that killed nearly 1,200 people, Israeli forces have killed over 74,000 Palestinians. Many believe the death toll is a vast undercount. This is a displaced Palestinian from Beit Hanoun speaking today in Gaza City.

AKRAM ABU JARAD: [translated] The worst three years that have passed in our lives. There is no life, no living, no food, no basic necessities of life. We wait for water from 5:00 to 10:00. And we are living in the streets, homeless, tired, worn out. I mean, a life worse than this? We are not living, my friend. We are dead in Gaza.

AMY GOODMAN: We go now to Gaza City, where we’re joined by journalist Akram al-Satarri.

I want to thank you so much for being with us. We spoke to you a few years ago as a journalist. You then went on to work with a humanitarian aid organization for several years. Now we’re back, being able to speak to you in Gaza City. Can you describe what’s happening on the ground? We just reported that as Israelis mourn the dead at the music site in southern Israel, they could hear the booms of Israeli airstrikes on the Gaza Strip right nearby. Tell us what’s happening where you are, Akram.

AKRAM AL-SATARRI: Well, October 7th was a day when Gaza changed once and forever. The way Palestinians have been living has changed. The way Palestinians have been managing their life has changed, as well. The way Palestinians were defining their life has changed. The Palestinians now are living in a de facto that has redefined everything — their understanding of life, their way of running and managing that life, their way to access things, their way to live, their way to stay in homes, their way to live in tents. Every single aspect of the life of the people of Gaza has changed once and forever.

Around 85% of the built environment in Gaza is already destroyed. Around 74% of the land of the Gaza Strip is also controlled by Israel and the IDF. Gazans have been struggling to access water and to access food. They have been facing significant public health hazards because of the fact that they were consuming water that was not properly treated. Organizations have been struggling for the sake of helping the people of Gaza. Statements were made by international and U.N. figures, diplomats all over the world, condemning the situation that has been going on, calling for Gazans to live and lead a normal life.

Unfortunately, for the third year now, and on October 7th, Gazans are reminded by the fact that they have been living in death and fear, destruction and displacement. Another Gazan was killed a couple of hours ago in Khan Younis refugee camp, 60-years-old Gazan that was targeted by IDF. Yesterday, another Palestinian was targeted and shot at a beach camp by another drone strike, and he also lost his life. Several people were injured.

The people who are living in the tents, with no even minimum standard for living, are living in exacerbating safety and security risks. The significant proportion of them is still not reunioned with their families that are outside of the Gaza Strip. And some of them, also significant part of them, around 8,000 families, are not aware about the whereabouts of the bodies of their missed family members. Around 250 journalists were killed. As you said, 74,000 Gazans were confirmed to be dead, while the number is much higher. Around 8,000 peoples are not — 8,000 Gazans are dead, but their bodies are not discovered yet.

The Gazans who were worried about their dears were organizing mass funerals for 150 bodies, for two bodies, where the memories that were remaining from the people were their remains, their clothes, their shoes, their watches and anything simple yet can give some comfort to the ones who were not sure about the whereabouts of the bodies of their dears.

The life in Gaza has changed forever. People are still struggling. And people are today reminded, since the last night — they’re reminded that the Israeli Air Force is still hovering all around different parts of the Gaza Strip, and that the IDF is still hunting Gazans, disturbing the already disturbed life, spreading death and displacement once and once again.

AMY GOODMAN: Akram, I’m wondering if you can respond to the United Nations human rights chief Volker Türk, who’s called Gaza a death trap for journalists. You have been a journalist in Gaza for years. As you said, you lost 250 — more than 250 colleagues. What has this meant, as the Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu does not allow in international journalists, and then the Israeli military kills hundreds of Palestinian journalists?

AKRAM AL-SATARRI: The situation in Gaza is catastrophic as an overall situation when it comes to the general population, but there are some specific job-related hardships when it comes to the journalists. They were always there. They were the first responder, along with the medical teams, to cover the truth about the bombardments. And also, they were — the footage that we were providing was used by very significant committees that were fact-finding, and we were hoping that they would condemn the occupation. That made them also bear the brunt of most of the hostilities that were taking place in the Gaza Strip. That made them a target, which they were not supposed to be a target. They were targeted for doing their job.

Their job proved to be a dangerous job for the occupation, because it has been exposing some and most of the atrocities that have been taking place. They have discovered the truth. They have also — they have widespread presence worldwide, even when they are very dedicated local Palestinian journalists and the footage that have been provided — they have been provided was used and is still being used by diplomats, by INGOs, by U.N. agencies. Nicolás Jaar’s father’s family has roots in Bethlehem, Palestine. His father, Alfredo Jaar, is a renowned Chilean conceptual artist and architect.
