## “Parasitic Technology”: Could Jensen Huang/Nvidia Scandal Prompt Rethink About AI?

Nvidia’s computer chips are at the heart of the artificial intelligence boom, driving the company’s value above $4 trillion and making Huang one of the most influential business leaders in the world. His spectacular fall from the company he co-founded in 1993 comes as the U.S. stock market has become highly dependent on a handful of AI-related companies and amid intense competition between the United States and China over control of the technology, with access to Nvidia’s chips becoming a major point of contention between the two countries.

Tech critic Paris Marx notes that the AI industry’s “circular financing,” in which companies are both clients and investors for one another, has created systemic risk.

We also speak with Ramesh Srinivasan, director of the AI Futures Lab at UCLA, who says the “soap opera” of the Huang scandal has exposed “how precarious the governance of this AI truly is,” with only a handful of people and firms controlling “incredibly powerful technology that feeds off of all of us and the planet.”

AMY GOODMAN: We begin today’s show looking at the fallout from Jensen Huang’s resignation as the CEO of Nvidia, one of the world’s most powerful companies. Nvidia’s advanced AI computer chips have powered much of the artificial intelligence boom. Huang cultivated close ties to President Trump and is credited with helping to shape U.S. policies around artificial intelligence and rules around the export of advanced AI chips.

Huang’s resignation came days after reports emerged he had an affair with a Chinese businesswoman named Alice Huang — no relation — whom U.S. intelligence officials reportedly suspected of spying for Beijing and helping China obtain advanced Nvidia chips, which are banned by U.S. export controls. The Information news outlet reports Alice Huang helped route at least $7.5 billion worth of Nvidia chips to China via a Singapore-based cloud computing company called Megaspeed International. Jensen Huang confirmed he had an affair with the woman. Later, published reports revealed he also had affairs with two employees at Nvidia, including with a senior executive who reported to him.

Nvidia has announced its longtime chief financial officer, Colette Kress, would serve as interim CEO as the board searches for a permanent replacement.

On Thursday, U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche took questions about Nvidia.

REPORTER: When did the Justice Department learn, with regard to Nvidia, that Alice Huang might be a suspected Chinese asset? And there’s been concerns raised that the Commerce Department was giving favoritism to Nvidia and Megaspeed, which is Alice Huang’s company. Do you share the concerns that maybe the Commerce Department was impeding the ability to uncover the smuggling network that Alice Huang was involved in?

ATTORNEY GENERAL TODD BLANCHE: Well, your first question assumes something that I have never said and that I don’t know to be true. So, you assume that DOJ learns that she is, in fact, a spy. I’m not — nobody said that. I certainly haven’t said that. And I don’t — all I said a minute ago, and all that I think I will say, is that, of course, DOJ is investigating or looking at it. So, there’s no — there’s no conclusions reached by the Department of Justice or by me or by Director Patel around who she is or is not.

I don’t have concerns with the Commerce Department. I don’t. We have a good relationship with the Commerce Department. I’ve read some of the stories, which, you know, are sourced or not sourced, and so I don’t have any — but, no, I don’t have — the answer to your question is, no, I don’t have any — I don’t have any concerns.

So, what has occurred is this astonishing technology that reduces life to data and numbers, and reduces intelligence to pattern recognition, has been — has now been governed and run by just a few people on the planet, mainly men, and men that you’ve discussed, you know, commonly on Democracy Now!, who were there at the inauguration and so on.

That’s according to findings by the San Francisco Chronicle, which obtained internal ICE investigations into the 29 deaths that occurred in 2025. ICE’s own documents admit immigration jails failed to provide emergency medical care. In some cases, ICE jail staff even mocked or ignored people who requested help and who later died.

At least 62 immigrants have died in ICE custody since Trump’s return to office. In response to the abuse, immigrants jailed across the nation have staged hunger strikes and other peaceful actions. This week, the publication L.A. Taco reported at least 24 people detained at the Adelanto ICE jail in California’s Mojave Desert started another hunger strike — the second so far this year. Meanwhile, another hunger strike at the Northwest ICE jail in Tacoma, Washington, is also ongoing. Hunger strikers at both facilities have reportedly faced threats, including being transferred to other ICE jails across the country. Both Adelanto and Northwest are operated by the for-profit prison company GEO Group.

Here in New York, tenant advocates are demanding justice after what they described as the preventable collapse of an apartment building in Brooklyn that killed at least eight people, including two children, and injured at least 23 others Wednesday. Among the victims were two construction workers from Ecuador and Guatemala; a food delivery worker and Amazon worker, who were relatives from Guinea in West Africa; and two young cousins from Colombia.

The publication amNewYork reports the collapse may have been caused by corner-cutting by subcontractors and developers working on an adjacent construction site where crews were digging a pit for a new massive, multi-story development. In the wake of the collapse, New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani said five contractors and developers connected to the disaster are associated with about 100 other sites across New York, and that the Department of Buildings would immediately begin inspecting those sites.

The executive director of NYS Tenant Bloc, Sumathy Kumar, said in a statement that the tragedy “stresses the urgency of taking buildings out of the hands of bad landlords who are looking to make quick profits at the expense of people’s safety” and “highlights the need for dignified, high quality housing built with union labor, which is why we are fighting for social housing across the city and state.”
