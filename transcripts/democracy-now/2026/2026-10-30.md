EYAL WEIZMAN: So, one of the prohibitions of the Genocide Convention speaks about the destructions of a people’s conditions of life. A people’s life, human life, depends on the environment that sustain it. It depends on buildings, on home, depends on infrastructure. It depends on hospitals. It depends on agriculture and open areas. In the Genocide Convention, or in Raphael Lemkin, who actually framed the concept of genocide and understood that you can kill a people indirectly by destroying whatever it is that is necessary to sustain life.

The result of that, unlike the fast or hot components of genocide, where people are executed, murdered, thrown into mass graves, etc., is a genocide by attrition, is a much longer and much more extended process of genocide. And in fact, genocide by the destruction of conditions of life is typical of settler-colonial genocide. Think about the genocides undertaken in the Americas. It has taken decades or centuries to destroy entire cultures by displacing people, by destroying the environment that they depend on for culture, for sustenance.

And that is what we’re seeing happening in Gaza. It is one of the speeds of the genocides that is happening in Gaza. And I think that since the ceasefire, the so-called ceasefire, began in October 2025, the destruction of the Palestinian people by the destruction and degrading of their environment is the most pronounced means of destroying Palestinian life in the southwestern coast of Palestine, that is also known as the Gaza Strip.

They are setting a permanent architecture of control made out of all the rubble and ruins of Palestinian homes. Some of the traditional wells of the Palestinians, 200,000 Palestinians that were displaced into the Gaza Strip during 1948, were incredible Indigenous knowledge in knowing how to dig tunnels.

They were made landless in the Gaza Strip, thrown onto the sand dunes along the coast, but knew how to build a project, a subterranean project, that was made initially to bypass the siege that was imposed on Gaza. Gaza has always had a very robust trade with Egypt. I mean, in fact, the oldest trade route in the world, that the Romans called the Via Maris, runs from Alexandria through Gaza to the rest of Palestine and then the Levant. That trade route was cut by the formation of the Gaza Strip, and then the border between Palestine and Egypt. And the trade was running through the subsoil.

So, the tunnel project began as a means to alleviate the conditions of life that Israel imposed on Gaza. Israel likes to call the tunnel project “the metro,” as if every point is connected to everything else, creating an image of totality in the subsoil. Human Rights Watch said Thursday the siege could amount to the war crime of using starvation as a method of warfare. The famine declaration came as a Saudi airstrike on Taiz killed 13 people. That followed a Houthi attack on Aden’s airport that injured passengers and halted flights. Meanwhile, the Houthis are rejecting claims by the Saudi-led coalition that Saudi air defenses intercepted missiles fired toward Mecca.

Another tanker has been struck while transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian forces reportedly deployed a new tactic of strapping explosives to a small boat. It was at least the 35th attack on a ship near the strait since the start of October. It came after Connecticut Democratic Senator Chris Murphy issued a warning over his recent briefing with Pentagon officials on President Trump’s plans to launch intense new strikes on Iran after the midterm elections. Murphy spoke with the Middle East Institute.

Sen. Chris Murphy: “You’ve heard a preview that it would be a short-duration plan. I will just say this: Their plan is insane. It won’t — it won’t — it will lead to devastating escalation and the likely destruction of our commitment to the rule of law.”

In Ukraine, a Russian drone struck a crowded railroad station in the eastern city of Dnipro on Thursday, killing at least 14 people. More Russian attacks killed at least six people elsewhere in Ukraine. The strikes came after sea drones attacked a Greek container ship off the coast of Bulgaria on Wednesday, sinking the vessel. All 15 crew members were evacuated. It’s the latest in a series of drone strikes on civilian ships in the Black Sea blamed on both Ukraine and Russia. On Wednesday, Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis called for a global maritime truce during a meeting of the U.N. Security Council.

Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis: “Just yesterday, another commercial cargo vessel, owned by a Greek company, was attacked and sank in the Black Sea. We strongly condemn this attack. Targeted civilian commercial shipping is completely unacceptable. It is yet another reminder for the urgent need for a global maritime truce, ensuring that commercial vessels can navigate safely and freely, even in times of conflict.”

The Wall Street Journal reports Ukraine’s military has paused its strikes against Russian oil refineries, at President Trump’s request. The unofficial pause is expected to last through the U.S. midterm elections next week. It began last week, after President Trump called on Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky to step down and after Elon Musk temporarily limited Ukraine’s ability to use the Starlink and Starshield internet satellite networks in its aircraft and drones.

Meanwhile, Greenpeace says it’s tracking seven oil tankers carrying an estimated 288,000 tons of Russian diesel toward the U.S. This week, Senator Richard Blumenthal sent letters to shipping companies warning them that delivering Russian diesel to U.S. ports would violate federal law — despite authorization from the Trump administration. “Reverse course while there’s still time,” Senator Blumenthal wrote.

In immigration news, the number of ICE jails nationwide has more than doubled since President Trump returned to office, with his administration allocating billions of dollars for the mass detention of tens of thousands of immigrants.

In Washington state, the troubled Northwest Detention Center in Tacoma will continue to operate for another 10 years, after the for-profit prison company GEO Group signed a $1.5 billion contract with the federal government. Advocates have long reported on human rights violations, including sexual violence, as well as numerous deaths at Northwest, where immigrants have repeatedly staged hunger and labor strikes demanding their release.

Meanwhile, in Minnesota, local station KARE 11 reports the Trump administration used taxpayer funds to purchase a shuttered private prison earlier this year in the rural city of Appleton for nearly $496 million to use as an ICE jail. That amount was more than six times the cost of CoreCivic’s initial offer to sell the prison to the state of Minnesota for about $75 million. This follows an unprecedented $45 billion given to ICE to expand its detention capacity as part of Trump’s so-called One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

Meanwhile, the ACLU and a coalition of rights groups are suing the Trump administration over its disappearance of immigrants in ICE custody. The policy allows ICE to hide the whereabouts of people in detention, leaving their families and lawyers unable to locate and communicate with them.

El Salvador’s President Nayib Bukele has deployed more than 2,000 soldiers to Haiti in a move human rights advocates say could further destabilize the nation and add to rising violence. Trump has reportedly backed Bukele’s deployment of troops, with Bukele claiming his soldiers only intend to rescue four Salvadoran aviation technicians who’d been kidnapped by gangs in Haiti. Back in El Salvador, Bukele’s so-called state of exception, first declared in 2022, has led to the arbitrary arrest of tens of thousands of people, many wrongfully accused of being gang members and denied due process rights while imprisoned in torturous conditions. Bukele spoke from San Salvador Thursday.

President Nayib Bukele: “I told the gangs in Haiti, 'We wiped out the gangs in El Salvador, and we can do the same to you.' In other words, it was a warning. And our mission isn’t to wipe out the gangs in Haiti, but our mission is to rescue our four members. Now, if they don’t hand them over willingly, well, we’ll rescue them by force.”

The New York Times reports President Trump ensured Flávio Bolsonaro could flee Brazil and enter the United States after Bolsonaro’s election loss to Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. Despite his defeat, Bolsonaro has declared himself the “legitimate” leader of Brazil, claiming — without evidence — the election results were fraudulent. Trump has also falsely claimed Brazil’s election appeared to be “rigged.”

Bolsonaro faces multiple criminal investigations in Brazil, and authorities had requested he surrender his passport when he escaped to Argentina and then Ecuador before landing in Miami Wednesday on a private plane. Trump will likely grant Bolsonaro permanent residency in the U.S., according to U.S. officials who spoke to The New York Times. Flávio Bolsonaro is the son of former Brazilian far-right President Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving a 27-year sentence under house arrest for orchestrating a failed coup attempt after he lost the 2022 election to Lula.
