## Headlines for October 15, 2026

Ilan Abdul-Khaliq: “We have more than 650 patients with kidney failure who risk losing their lives, as well as infants in incubators and patients in intensive care. … These disasters we’re facing and this situation necessitate international and humanitarian intervention to find safe passages for medicines, fuel, goods and food to enter the besieged city. Earlier today, the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen claimed its forces intercepted a cruise missile in the Medina area. The Houthis have denied carrying out any attacks on Saudi Arabia today. The conflicting claims came after Saudi officials said one person was wounded by falling debris Wednesday by an intercepted Houthi ballistic missile launched toward Riyadh. A Houthi spokesperson said the group had successfully struck King Khalid International Airport, following deadly attacks there over the weekend.

Two cargo ships sank near the Strait of Hormuz Wednesday after they were struck by unknown projectiles. The Cape Dao had been badly damaged in a September attack that killed a sailor. The GFS Pride sank a day after it suffered an explosion off the coast of Oman. Crew members were safely evacuated as the massive vessel sank, littering the ocean with some of its 2,500 cargo containers. This comes amid a recent surge of attacks on civilian ships by Iran’s military in response to the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports and economic sanctions.

Meanwhile, the United Nations is urging the U.S. and Iran to agree to a plan to move fertilizer through the Strait of Hormuz to prevent a global food crisis. About one-third of the world’s fertilizer shipments normally pass through the waterway. On Wednesday, a top U.N. official warned that unless a deal is put in place ahead of the planting season, commodity prices of food staples will soar, triggering a massive food security crisis affecting parts of Asia and Africa.

In Ukraine, at least nine civilians were killed overnight and hundreds of thousands left without power after swarms of Russian drones attacked the capital Kyiv and Ukraine’s second-largest city Kharkiv. Russia’s Defense Ministry said it targeted plants supplying power and heating, as well as a data center in Kyiv. The attacks came days after President Trump unilaterally announced Russia and Ukraine had declared an “energy ceasefire” — an announcement both sides denied. This is a Kyiv resident who survived a Russian attack on her apartment complex Wednesday.

Olesya Gachechyladze: “I want to say to America and the whole world: Please help us. You said you would help us, but you’ve left us alone to face a great tragedy.”

On Wednesday, Ukrainian officials said SpaceX’s satellite internet provider Starlink had cut bandwidth to objects traveling faster than 100 miles per hour, severely limiting drones, airplanes and helicopters used by Ukraine. SpaceX CEO Elon Musk said in response that any limits to Starlink service in Ukraine are decided by the U.S. military and, ultimately, President Trump. That prompted Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky to say in an interview that he felt “betrayed” by Trump, who he said was on the wrong side of history. Zelensky also said Ukraine would resume attacks on Russian energy sites. Ukrainian drones later struck an oil refinery south of Moscow, while another attack targeted a data center in Balakovo belonging to Russia’s largest state-controlled bank.

Romanian officials say seven sailors are missing after a fire broke out on a chemical tanker in Romanian territorial waters. This follows drone attacks on at least five other cargo ships in the Black Sea that some Romanian and Bulgarian officials have linked to Russia.

In Germany, a trial got underway Wednesday for a Ukrainian military officer accused of leading a secret state-sponsored operation to blow up the Nord Stream pipelines, which were designed to deliver natural gas from Russia to Europe. The suspect, identified only as Serhii K., was a captain in a Ukrainian special forces unit at the time of the bombing in September 2022.

The U.S. is sending hundreds of soldiers to Latin America to train local forces to supposedly tackle drug trafficking. Soldiers from a 600-member battalion reportedly began deploying to a new jungle training facility in Panama. The troops are set to train with local forces in Colombia and Honduras. The Pentagon recently moved several MQ-9 Reaper drones from Africa to Colombia — and to Ecuador, where the U.S. military personnel and special forces are already operating.

The commander of U.S. Southern Command, General Francis Donovan, has said the U.S. plans to expand operations from strikes on boats to strikes on land. The U.S. has killed at least 239 people in boat strikes that legal experts have denounced as extrajudicial killings.

Costa Rica has opened a new high-security prison designed to hold more than 5,000 prisoners. It’s modeled after the notorious CECOT mega-prison in El Salvador, where prisoners are denied due process and face torture, violence, medical neglect and other serious human rights abuses. On Wednesday, Costa Rica’s right-wing populist President Laura Fernández inaugurated the High-Containment Center Against Organized Crime, or CACCO, near the capital San José.

President Laura Fernández: “An investment of more than $35 million, precisely to contain the most dangerous convicts and cut off, once and for all, the power they have held within the prisons for decades. … We are going to use a firm hand, yes, because those who commit crimes must pay the price.”

President Fernández campaigned as a tough-on-crime candidate favoring harsher criminal sentencing and mandatory prison labor for convicts. She’s a close ally of El Salvador’s far-right President Nayib Bukele and President Donald Trump.

The Irish author Sally Rooney says she has been forced to stop selling her books in the U.K. because of her support for Palestine Action, which the British government designates as a terrorist organization. In a statement to The Guardian, Rooney says that ever since she declared her support for the direct action group, she could no longer receive royalties from U.K. sales. She wrote, “The fulfilment of contracts to publish my work in the U.K. could now constitute a serious criminal offense. After a process of consultation and deliberation, and with my full agreement, my publisher Faber has therefore determined that it can no longer supply my work within the U.K.” More than 3,700 people have been arrested in the U.K. for allegedly supporting Palestine Action since the group was banned last year.

Here in the United States, a federal judge in New York has granted a preliminary injunction blocking the Trump administration from enforcing sanctions against two U.S.-based groups that support the International Criminal Court’s work: the Taxpayer Alliance Against Genocide and DAWN, Democracy for the Arab World Now. The Trump administration sought to sanction the groups after the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize was awarded to former ICC Judge Navi Pillay, who chairs the U.N.’s Independent Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, which concluded that Israel committed genocide against Palestinians in the Gaza Strip.

The New York City comptroller, Democrat Mark Levine, says the city will not be investing its pension funds in Israeli bonds, since the quality of the bonds have deteriorated amid the wars in Gaza and Iran. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani welcomed the decision.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani: “I welcome the comptroller’s decision. It is something that I’ve said in the past, that I don’t believe that our city should purchase Israel bonds. We don’t purchase the bonds of any other sovereign nation. I don’t think that we should make an exception when it comes to Israel.”
