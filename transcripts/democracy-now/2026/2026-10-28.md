## Israeli Elections to Global Movements: Tariq Ali, Rashid Khalidi on New Book “The Neck and the Sword”

Kanafani was assassinated by Israel in 1972, “murdered simply because his words were too dangerous for Israel to tolerate,” says Khalidi.

Public opinion about Israel has grown increasingly negative in the United States and across much of the world in the wake of the genocide in Gaza, and it’s “not going to shift back,” says Khalidi. “People have seen something that cannot be unseen. They’ve seen genocide. They’ve seen slaughter. And Israel has no defense.”

“Even in countries with no history of a Palestine solidarity movement, suddenly the Palestine flag emerges,” says Ali. “Why? Because their governments are trying to stop dissent on this issue, so they are defying their governments.”

ANJALI KAMAT: Israel is facing possible political gridlock after neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu nor his chief challenger, Gadi Eisenkot, won enough seats to form a new government in Tuesday’s election. The final tally could take days. The election marked the first time Israelis went to the polls since the October 7th, 2023, attack and the ensuing wars on Gaza, Lebanon and Iran. The vote drew record turnout.

With over 70% of the vote counted, Israeli — Netanyahu’s Likud party leads with the 27 seats, ahead of former military chief Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party at 22. According to Haaretz, a possible pro-Netanyahu coalition currently has 54 seats, while a possible pro-Eisenkot bloc has 49 seats.

Palestinian-led parties — the Joint list and Ra’am — have won 17 seats, their strongest showing ever. But both Netanyahu and Eisenkot have ruled out governing with the Arab parties. Millions of Palestinians living under Israeli occupation in East Jerusalem, the West Bank and the Gaza Strip are ineligible to vote.

GHASSAN KANAFANI: You don’t mean exactly peace talks. You mean capitulation, surrendering.

GHASSAN KANAFANI: Maybe to you. But to us, it’s not. To us, to liberate our country, to have dignity, to have respect, to have our mere human rights, is something as essential as life itself.

RASHID KHALIDI: He was blown up with his 17-year-old niece in his car as he was about to drive her away from his home in 1972. RASHID KHALIDI: Exactly. What Israel is doing is abominable, and people are increasingly understanding that. So you shut it down.

What Kanafani was saying in this book is writing, culture, literature are enormously important forms of resistance. And he was talking about specific writers — Mahmoud Darwish, Tawfiq Ziad and so on.
