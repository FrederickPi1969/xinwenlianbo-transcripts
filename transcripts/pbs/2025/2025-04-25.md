## Brooks and Capehart on Trump's reaction to public pressure

From dysfunction at the Department of Defense to a judge in Wisconsin being arrested by the FBI, it's shaped up to be quite the week.

Luckily, here to break it all down, I'm joined by Brooks and Capehart. That is New York Times columnist David Brooks and Jonathan Capehart, associate editor for The Washington Post.

I don't mean to connect these two in any way. They are not connected, but they did happen this week. And I just wonder, as you reflect on where they are now, what this says to you about how the administration is processing feedback and who they're listening to.

One, it strikes me as maybe something illegal, but it also strikes me as something heroic. And in times of trouble, then people are sometimes called to do civil disobedience. And in my view, when people do civil disobedience, they have to pay the price. That's part of the heroism of it, frankly.

And so you can both think that she shouldn't have legally done this and that morally protecting somebody against, maybe not even in this case, but in other cases, frankly, a predatory enforcement agency, sometimes, civil disobedience is necessary.
