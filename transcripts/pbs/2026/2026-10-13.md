## Grandma Joy and Me' follows a grandmother and grandson's journey to every national park

Eleven years ago, Brad Ryan took his then 85-year-old grandmother, Joy, camping in the Smoky Mountains after hearing that she had never seen a mountain herself. One trip to a national park turned into dozens. And eventually, what they called Grandma Joy's road trip took them to every single one, making Joy Ryan the oldest person to begin and finish visiting every U.S. national park.

We spoke with Brad and Grandma Joy back in 2022 for "PBS News Weekend."

Joy Ryan, Grandmother of Brad Ryan: Well, I have lived in the same district, in the same house for 67 years, and you just hardly imagine all the beautiful, wonderful things that you find outside. And it just made -- it's just been miraculous. And I have enjoyed every minute of it. And it'll give me something, when I get old, that I can sit and talk about.

Joy Ryan has plenty more to talk about now. She and Brad have since completed trips to all seven continents. I spoke with them after they got back from Thailand and Australia, where they traveled with Road Scholar. That's a nonprofit that organizes educational trips around the world.

Well, American Samoa is 7,000 miles from her front porch. It's a U.S. territory, but they have their own immigration system. Now my food stamps have been cut from $500 a month to $300. I would expect the most people that are politicians, $300 meal would be nothing to them. But you expect for us to raise two healthy children for $300 a month. It's no way possible that you can do that unless you have help.

## What we know about Pope Leo's lung cancer and prognosis
