## These copper vests are helping injured penguins heal faster

Finally, tonight, something to make you smile: an innovative treatment helping injured penguins to get back on their feet.

These penguins are wearing actual penguin suits, but they're more about the substance than style. Penguins are facing planetary perils, and these special vests are designed to help.

In Chile, injured penguins are swimming, eating, and shimmying in them. The cocoon of red fabric is woven with healing minerals, copper, which helps prevent bacteria and fungi from growing, and zinc, which encourages new blood vessels to form. Together, they can repair damaged tissue more quickly.

Tomas Pino Damke, Veterinary Doctor, Humboldt Conservation Center (through translator):

The copper suits were born out of the need to manage these wounds when they are in recovery. These patients generally arrive with wounds in very poor condition, where most of the time we have to perform some type of surgical management. We have to do cleaning. We have to do sutures.

And then the challenge was how we treat these wounds in an animal that is wild, that has the ability of great range of neck movement. It could remove stitches. It could disturb the wound. So we had that big problem.

In the wild, penguins face a number of manmade hazards, but fishing nets can be particularly dangerous. Penguins are considered one of the most threatened groups of seabirds on Earth, facing pressures including climate change, overfishing, and habitat loss.

Most of the penguins at this facility in Chile arrived with bad cuts and lacerations, likely from fishing nets. The vet technicians treat and wrap their wounds, then dress them in their special suits.

The suits are made by the Chilean textile company Kimba that until now made suits for animals of the four-legged variety.

Estefania Guidotti, Head of Textile Marketing, Kimba (through translator): We are working under the hypothesis that this technology could also help penguin recovery. We have tested it in dogs, cats, and mammals, but, in penguins, this is the first time.

For now, these penguins are taking their recovery one shimmy and one swim at a time. But the goal for so many of the penguins injured by fishing nets is to get them back where they belong, in the wild.

## How abortion drug restrictions make it harder to treat other conditions

For more than 25 years, the drug mifepristone has been approved for abortion in the United States. And it's been used safely by more than 7.5 million American women. But mifepristone and a similar drug have also been proven to treat common and often painful women's health conditions like uterine fibroids and endometriosis.

In the first part of our two-part series "Beyond Abortion," special correspondent Sarah Varney and producer Rachel Wellford traveled to Europe and across the U.S. to report on this treatment, as anti-abortion groups lobby the Trump administration to further restrict these drugs.

So my mother would say she had experienced the same thing too when she was growing up. And then everyone else made it seem like it was just normal.

## Trump's attacks on free press echo rhetoric used by autocrats, Jodie Ginsberg says

For more now on the decision to ban CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House, we turn to Jodie Ginsberg. She is CEO of the Committee to Protect Journalists.

And let's just start with what we heard from the president on social media this morning, defending the ban and also saying the fake news is -- quote -- "a threat to our national security and it must be stopped."

Jodie, what's your response to that claim, framing these outlets as fake news, and also the national security angle the president's stressing here?

Jodie Ginsberg, CEO, Committee to Protect Journalists: Well, unfortunately, this claim that journalists are a threat to national security is the kind of narrative we frequently hear from autocrats, from authoritarian regimes.

It's intended to stir up public distrust of the media and to perpetuate a government-led narrative. And that's what we have seen from this president is a continual drumbeat of smears against journalists and journalism, the branding of them as fake news, as enemies of the people.

And all of that is intended to sow doubt and distrust and mistrust of the media amongst the public, so that the administration can control the narrative. But, as we heard, a free press is a cornerstone of the U.S. Constitution. It is named in the Constitution and constitutionally protected, not so that we can protect journalists, but so that we can protect all of our rights to receive information about what our government, the government is doing in our name.

You mentioned in a statement from CPJ that this ban violates the First Amendment. Talk a little bit more about what aspects of constitutional law are being tested here. And now that this will make its way through the courts, as the three outlets are suing, is there existing legal precedent that tells us where this could go?

Well, of course, one of those elements is viewpoint discrimination, in that the government is not permitted to penalize news outlets on the basis of their own viewpoints, for using language that he doesn't like.

You may remember the Associated Press was also kicked out of the White House for calling the Gulf of Mexico the Gulf of Mexico, rather than the Gulf of America. And this is similar to what we're seeing now with CNN, Politico, and MS NOW, that these are being removed from the White House, barred from the White House, simply because they are news outlets whose viewpoint the president doesn't like.

So it's clear that the president, the White House are in breach of the Constitution, of the First Amendment. We do expect this to be overturned. And, of course, it is really important that we're seeing this kind of solidarity that you mentioned in the opening -- in your opening remarks because it's important that we stand up collectively and realize this is not a partisan issue.

Jodie, folks watching may wonder who don't kind of follow how we work and do our jobs every day, is the White House obligated to provide access to journalists? I mean, where is the line between managing access and punishing journalists for their coverage?

Well, the White House is obligated to make sure that it is providing a broad range of access. No one has a divine right to sit in the White House press pool, for example, but the White House does have an obligation to make sure that it is making its communications available to a wide range of news outlets.

And what you heard about the press pool is really important. Often, when the president is traveling, he's traveling on Air Force One, there's not a lot of room, or he's in private small meetings. We can't have hundreds of journalists attend those meetings.

It's important that we have these shared facilities where one news outlet with sufficient resources is able to cover for the rest and get that information. I think sometimes we forget that, when you're reading your local newspaper, for example, or listening to your local radio station or watching your local broadcaster, much of that footage is being provided by the pool.

And we saw exactly what happens when that pool coverage is removed. You could not hear the president today talking about the inauguration of his new helipad because there wasn't the quality of coverage that you would get from having a professional news outlet. Well, I think this is the important point. Freedom of expression has to exist for everybody, regardless of their viewpoints and regardless of the viewpoint of the government.

And, of course, if this is the path that the administration wants to go down, there's nothing, of course, stopping a new administration taking a similar action against news outlets that it doesn't like, which is why I think you saw all five of the -- well, all five of the networks come together, even though they don't always see eye to eye politically, to say this was not acceptable and they were not going to continue to run the press pool while CNN was banned.

So those are the kinds of concerns that we have. And, of course, the other is, if you go down this road in which you say only a Trump TV, only the coverage provided by the White House, only the coverage provided by the government is what the public gets to see, you get a government-controlled narrative.

It is vital for us as the public that we have access to a free and pluralistic media to be able to criticize the government, to be able to analyze what the government is doing, so that we can understand what decisions are being made.
