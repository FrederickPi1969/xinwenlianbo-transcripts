## Workforce Pell Grants give students new options for job training

For decades, federal Pell Grants have helped millions of students from low- and middle-income families afford college. Now, for the first time, that financial aid will also be available for short-term vocational training programs designed to help students enter the work force.

Stephanie Sy reports on the rollout of Workforce Pell, the largest expansion of the federal grant program in 50 years.

It's part of our higher education series called Rethinking College.

At Ivy Tech Community College campus in Anderson, Indiana, learning is hands-on.

Fourth intercostal space, we're going to put that right here.

From putting on an EKG to administering shots...

... to drawing blood. In just eight weeks, these students are trained to become certified clinical medical assistants, or CCMAs. They're the folks at the doctor's office who might take your vital science or get your medical history at the start of your appointment. And they're in high demand, says program chair Sara Standlee.

Sara Standlee, Ivy Tech Community College:

A lot of my students, they'll get hired before they graduate. That's how much of a need there is for medical assistants out there right now, because the fact of the matter is, is we have got a lot of people that need medical care, and we don't have enough people to be able to care for them.

But, soon, students in this course will have access to new financial support. This summer, Ivy Tech's certified clinical medical assistant program became the second in the country to be approved for a new federal grant called Workforce Pell.

President Trump signed it into law in the so-called One Big Beautiful Bill.

It expands the Pell Grant program, which first rolled out in 1973 to give students from low-income households a path to higher education and ideally a path into the middle class.

Pell Grants help fund college-style courses at least a semester or 15 weeks' long. Now, under Workforce Pell, short courses between eight to 15 weeks in length qualify.

To be eligible for funding, programs must train students for occupations defined by each state as high skill, high wage or in demand and meet federal requirements.

Workforce Pell has what we call the 70/70 rule, meaning 70 percent of our learners need to complete the program, which has not been an issue at Ivy Tech, but also 70 percent of the cohort enrolled in a Workforce Pell pathway have to be employed associated with that training six months after they graduate.

The federal government started accepting applications in July. And so far, 21 programs across six states have been approved to train future emergency medical technicians, firefighters, electrical line workers, and more. Once Workforce Pell rolls out nationwide, an estimated 190,000 students per year will receive an average grant of $2,200.

Ivy Tech expects the funding to especially benefit its adult learners, many of whom, like CCMA students Kaitlynn Creason and Terra Randolph, are juggling parenting, work, and school.

## Christina Applegate's experience highlights challenges of diagnosing MS

When actor Christina Applegate revealed earlier this week that she had been misdiagnosed with multiple sclerosis, it triggered not only an outpouring of empathy for her, but raised important questions about how often people are told they have the condition when they actually don't.

Dr. Michael Kornberg, Johns Hopkins Medicine:

So you mentioned the range of figures from various studies.

But other conditions that cause neurologic symptoms that can cause some abnormalities on a brain MRI can be confused for M.S. and lead to a misdiagnosis.

I know you're not a psychologist, but I imagine it must be just very shocking for a patient to spend many years thinking they have one thing, only to find out that that's not what has been ailing them.

So, the issue of misdiagnosis in M.S. is something that has been studied. It is a topic that we talk about in the M.S. community. And it is very distressing for people. I think Christina Applegate articulated all of this and all of those common feelings very well.
