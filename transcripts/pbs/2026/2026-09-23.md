## Xi arrives for state visit amid U.S.-China trade tensions and AI concerns

Chinese President Xi Jinping arrived in Washington this evening for his first state visit in more than a decade. And at the same time, a federal judge today heard arguments in a lawsuit brought by CNN, MS NOW and Politico challenging President Trump banning them from the White House.

Our Liz Landers is at the White House covering both stories, and she joins us now.

So, Liz, we will start with this state visit. What is the focus of it?

Yes, Geoff, President Trump is greeting leader Xi right now at Joint Base Andrews here in Washington for an arrival ceremony that has all the pomp and circumstance of this major state visit.

This is the first time that Xi has been to the White House since 2015. And President Trump visited China back in May and was not awarded this same kind of pomp, circumstance and arrival. In particular, Xi did not greet him when he landed there in China back in May. You're seeing some of those images now.

I will remind our viewers that those images are from several months ago because the White House TV pool is still not covering the president's events today. We believe that there are going to be a number of issues that will be addressed during this visit.

A senior administration official told us earlier that AI, artificial intelligence, of course, will be on the top of the list, also some of these export controls that the United States has on our computing chips and selling those to China. The possibility of reducing tariffs on some goods is apparently going to come up, and also political prisoners.

Several China watchers and experts that I have spoken with don't really think that this meeting is going to be so much about deliverables as it is about continuing to forge that personal relationship that President Trump has with leader Xi.

I spoke earlier today with Kyle Chan. He's a fellow at the Brookings institute. And he was actually talking about these export controls, saying that matters less and less for the Chinese government because they are going all out to build their own AI chips themselves.

And when it comes to artificial intelligence in particular, you heard some of that addressed from the U.N. today in Nick's story. China is not interested in a joint slowdown of AI development, according to Chan. He says that they're more worried about cyber risks, hacking companies, and they don't share, China does not share the same concerns that we have heard from U.S. AI companies and leaders recently that robots, AI may kill us all in the near future.

Geoff, one more thing I want to add. Not everybody is happy about this visit and all of the accoutrements that are being laid out for President Xi. Republican Senator Wicker, he's the chairman of the Senate Armed Services Committee, says that he would have advised against this lavish welcome to what he calls a brutal dictator.

And, Liz, there's a state dinner tomorrow evening. Who is expected to be in attendance?

Well, Geoff, as you would imagine, with all of this talk of AI and technology, there are leaders from several major business companies and AI companies that are going to be at the White House tomorrow.

A senior administration official says that Jeff Bezos will be there, as well as Elon Musk, Michael Dell, Jensen Huang of Nvidia, a chipmaker, and Sam Altman of OpenAI. The first lady, Melania Trump, said in a FOX News interview today that President Trump wanted to have the state dinner because they have a great relationship.

She said that she's been involved in every detail of the planning for the state dinner tomorrow night. And she also -- and I'd also just add that this is only the second state dinner that President Trump has now had in his second term in office, Geoff.

All right, Liz, in the minute we have left, bring us up to speed on this lawsuit involving the three news organizations that were banned from the White House by President Trump.

There was a hearing on this, this afternoon, and the judge in this case, a district judge here in Washington, said right off the bat that he was skeptical of the argument that the Trump administration, that the Trump White House is making here on due process grounds.

It sounds like the judge believes that the due process of these news organizations was violated. The government is arguing in a filing that they issued late last night that news organizations failed to maintain basic minimum standards of professionalism and decorum expected of those given access to the White House.

This has now turned into a conversation about national security. That is what the president and the administration are arguing is at stake here, that these news organizations somehow violated national security there. The judge also seemed skeptical of that, Geoff. We still do not have an answer from the judge as to whether the ban on Politico, CNN, and MS NOW will be lifted.

Liz Landers outside the West Wing at the White House for us tonight.

By Will Weissert, Ashraf Khalil, Associated Press

## At UN General Assembly, Iran's president responds to Trump's 'annihilation' threat

The president of Iran told the United Nations General Assembly his country won't bend to U.S. intimidation just a day after President Trump threatened to escalate the war from the very same stage.

On the sidelines, U.S. and Iranian officials signaled no major breakthroughs in mediated talks.

Nick Schifrin has the latest after another busy day in New York.

It is a building on U.S. soil, an organization dedicated to peace, and never before has it hosted the leader of a country at war with its host until today.

Masoud Pezeshkian, Iranian President (through interpreter):

Yesterday, the United States president described us as terrorists. We have been the victims of terrorism.

And on the same dais where President Trump yesterday delivered this threat...

Or do I annihilate the Islamic Republic, and do it quickly, never giving them a chance to kill and destroy people and countries again?

... Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian delivered his own defiance.

We will never bow our head or bend at the knee. Iran must be understood by Mr. Trump and those who seek to bully us that we are ready for dialogue and diplomacy and negotiations without accepting the language of force.

Within minutes, the sole member of the U.S. delegation had heard enough and walked out of the room.

But, inside Iran, Pezeshkian leads the faction that advocates for negotiation and economic integration with the West. He's losing that argument to hard-liners, who today threatened Iran's neighbors after they cooperated with U.S. sanctions banning Iranian airlines.

Mohsen Rezaei, Secretary of Iranian Supreme National Security Council (through interpreter): If any country joins the United States in this adventure, its airports will no longer be able to operate flights. The choice is yours.

Marco Rubio, U.S. Secretary of State: Do they want to be a country or do they want to be a revolution?

Speaking right after Pezeshkian, Secretary of State Marco Rubio called yesterday's indirect talks between Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and American mediators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner significant.

But he said Pezeshkian's speech was the pot calling the kettle black.

Slaughtering 10,000 of your own people in protests is a violation of the U.N. I hope it's a violation of the U.N. principles. I think sponsoring terrorist organizations in the region to attack your neighbors, attack energy infrastructure, attack civilian sites is a violation of the U.N. system.

So I don't know what standing Iran has to lecture anyone on human rights or the international system when they habitually violate it.

But Rubio acknowledged that another violator of the international system, Russian President Vladimir Putin, is invited to Florida in December, when President Trump hosts the G20 in his Doral resort.

We think it's an opportunity for him to engage not just with the president, but with other world leaders. We hope that's an invitation he will accept.

But, today, Kyiv was filled with the sound of Russian drone strikes. Authorities said at least two people were killed and dozens injured...

Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Ukrainian President:

De-escalation steps are important, are needed before winter, now.

... as Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned the war could escalate. And as Ukraine begins to rely on autonomous weapons, Zelenskyy warned the AI future could become the present.

As early as next year, there is already a real possibility that AI, not only people, will begin -- will begin to decide what happens on the battlefield. And we need peace before we reach that point.

Dario Amodei, CEO and Co-Founder, Anthropic: That AI could be a risk to humanity as a whole.

Today, the Security Council held its first ever U.N. General Assembly AI meeting, including warnings from American AI leaders.

No one person or company or country should be able to use the most powerful AI models to impose their world view on everyone else.

But, in this chamber, the flawed but still only international body whose resolutions are legally binding, it fell to the co-chair of the U.N.'s independent AI panel, Yoshua Bengio, to warn the world it must police AI companies and issue a clarion call for collaboration.

Yoshua Bengio, Co-Chair, U.N. Independent International Panel on AI: Today, a handful of companies and countries are acting in their self-interest by taking reckless risks with humanity's fate. Our future, all of us, our future is at stake. Decisions that affect us all should be made by us all. The choice is still ours, but we must act now to correct the trajectory.

This year's U.N. General Assembly theme is Restoring Trust, a U.N. that delivers for all. This week, that sentiment has been fleeting, as many here are worried the future feels fractured and uncertain.

For the "PBS News Hour," I'm Nick Schifrin.
