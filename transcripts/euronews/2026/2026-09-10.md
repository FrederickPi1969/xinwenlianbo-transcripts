'The days of safety are over': Russian airports face record closures

Ukraine's drone campaign forced an average of 32 airport closures a day across 32 Russian airports including Moscow and St Petersburg, with summer totals already exceeding all of 2025, as Zelenskyy warned airlines and insurers that "the days of safety in Russia's skies are over."
Russian airports temporarily closed 993 times in August — an average of 32 times a day — as Ukrainian drone operations forced repeated airspace shutdowns across the country.

Industry data shows it was the most disruptive month for Russian civil aviation since its full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in early 2022.

Figures from aviation security firm Osprey Flight Solutions state that restrictions were imposed at 32 airports in August, including Moscow and St Petersburg.

Over the summer as a whole, 2,182 closures were recorded — already exceeding the full-year total for 2025.

The Insider, an investigative Russian outlet, calculated that the August closures reached more than six times the 2025 monthly average.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned airlines and insurers on 1 September that Russian airspace "is becoming completely unsafe" for civil aviation.

"We in Ukraine do not want innocent lives to be lost. That's why we are warning our partners: the days of safety in Russia's skies are over," Zelenskyy said.

Andriy Zagorodnyuk, a former Ukrainian defence minister, said the goal was not to attack civilian aircraft but to create lasting uncertainty.

"Ukraine is not seeking to shoot down civilian aircraft, but it is making Russia's skies unsafe for flying," he said in last week's comments to Ukrainska Pravda.

The trigger for airport closures is the activation of what Russia calls the "Kover" — Russian for "carpet" — protocol, an air defence procedure designed for rare emergencies that grounds or redirects all civil aircraft when a drone threat is detected in the area.

Military analyst Mykhailo Samus, of the Centre for Army, Conversion and Disarmament Studies, said airport closures were part of a broader strategy of economic exhaustion, alongside rising insurance premiums, the withdrawal of foreign carriers and reduced domestic mobility.

Russia's Transport Minister Andrey Nikitin said no significant disruptions to civil aviation would be allowed, yet operational data contradict his statement.

In the first three days of September, an average of 86 flights per day were cancelled in Russia, according to FlightRadar24 — a level comparable to August's worst periods.

On the most severe nights around Moscow, between 77 and more than 100 flights were delayed or cancelled in a single overnight period.

Passengers have documented the disruption in detail on social media and in regional news feeds.

"We've been sitting on the plane for an hour, Vnukovo has been closed, nobody is explaining anything," one traveller wrote on Telegram.

Updated: 10/09/2026 - 11:15 GMT+2
Aerial footage shows a sinkhole where Nicolas Cage's Malibu driveway stood after coastal erosion from Hurricane Marie remnants triggered a gas leak and road closure.
… More
Aerial footage shows a large sinkhole where the driveway of a Malibu mansion once stood, after coastal erosion linked to the remnants of Hurricane Marie. The collapse opened a gaping hole in front of the property, which entertainment outlets say belongs to actor Nicolas Cage.

The driveway gave way on Tuesday morning, triggering a gas leak just before 6 a.m. SoCalGas said crews brought the leak under control hours later but shut off supply along an 85-foot stretch of the street as a precaution. Malibu officials barred access to five homes on the road while engineers assessed the damage.

Storm-driven waves and high tides battered the Southern California coast over the holiday weekend. Marie, downgraded from a hurricane to a tropical storm, churned up surf that accelerated erosion along exposed bluffs.

Cage, 62, bought the property in 2024, according to records cited by the New York Times. His representatives did not comment.

At least five people were killed and 86 remain missing after a fire erupted aboard a ferry near a popular tourist destination in the Philippine province of Palawan, the coast guard said Thursday.
A passenger ferry caught on fire overnight on Thursday off the western Philippines, killing at least five and leaving dozens of people missing.

The blaze broke out on the M/V June Aster Wednesday evening about 2.2 km offshore, the Philippine Coast Guard said.

The ferry had sailed from the capital Manila and was near its destination of Coron in Palawan province when the blaze broke out on Wednesday night.

More than 40 people have been rescued, Coast Guard spokeswoman Commodore Noemie Cayabyab said, despite challenging weather conditions. Five casualties had been brought ashore.

"Based on the data we have right now on rescued (42) and the casualties, we still have 86 people who are unaccounted for," Cayabyab told a local radio station on Thursday.

She said 134 people were on board, 117 of them passengers and 17 crew. No information was available about any foreigners on board.

"We are continuing search and rescue operations," she said, with a coast guard ship and high-speed response boats joined by a military vessel and other government boats, private volunteers and fishermen.

Passengers were helped into smaller boats taking them to shallow water, and smoke still wafted over the charred vessel by midday on Thursday.

The Philippine Coast Guard later told reporters that another person had been rescued, but their identity had yet to be confirmed.

"Just to make it clear, this is live data and may change as our search and rescue operations are ongoing," Cayabyab said in the radio interview.

"However, we have not yet been able to obtain information from the rescued individuals because we have not been able to speak with them," she said, adding that attempts to put out the blaze were ongoing.

In January, a triple-decker ferry carrying more than 340 people sank off the southern Philippines, killing at least 52 people.

The M/V Trisha Kerstin 3 sank along nearly the same route where 31 people died in 2023 after a fire broke out aboard the Lady Mary Joy 3 ferry.

A 58-year-old British tourist has been arrested in the resort town of Alanya on Turkey's central Mediterranean coast, accused of spitting on a Turkish flag and charged with insulting a state symbol.
Turkish police arrested a British tourist in the southern resort of Alanya for allegedly spitting on the Turkish flag, media reports said Wednesday.

The 58-year-old tourist argued with staff in a hotel then allegedly turned and spat on a flag hanging in the lobby, DHA and IHA news agencies reported, without specifying when it happened.

Staff reported the man to police, who detained him and took him to the Alanya courthouse where he was formally arrested on charges of insulting or degrading a symbol of the Turkish state.

Under Article 300 of the Turkish penal code, it is illegal to publicly disrespect the Turkish flag -- an offence that can carry a jail term of up to three years.

In August 2025, prosecutors opened a criminal investigation into a tourist from Bosnia and Herzegovina for performing a pole dance on a Turkish flagpole in Cappadocia which she posted on social media.

Trump strongly defended the Iran war, predicting victory and linking it directly to lower oil prices — before suggesting the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz should be renamed after him.

"Food prices and almost every other item are rapidly going down. And by the way, oil will be going down as soon as we win the war with Iran, which is taking place right now," Trump said.

But Trump appeared to acknowledge the war had taken a toll on the US economy. "We were doing great," he said, before turning to his economic advisers and telling them, "I hate to do this to you." He then explained that he had felt compelled to launch the conflict because Iran was perhaps "two, maybe three weeks away from having a nuclear weapon."

Trump also echoed other Republicans at the convention stirring up what he said was a wave of communism threatening the United States.

"Your vote will decide whether our country stumbles at the starting gate of the next 250 years or surges forward and never ever looks back," he said.

Donald Trump is betting on an extraordinary promise to shore up his party’s fortunes ahead of the November midterm elections: if Republicans keep control of the House and Senate, every American adult will receive cash.

"So if the Republicans win, you win with us and you get $5,000. It will be called the Trump dividend," Trump told Republicans in Texas, as the party struggles in polls against the Democrats.

Within an hour, Vice President JD Vance appeared to try to walk back Trump’s proposal — at least in part — by suggesting the dividend payments would not go to the wealthy. Vance suggested it could be paid for by US tariff revenues, though the suggested payment dwarfs what the US has taken in through the protectionist measures.

The promise would likely cost more than $1 trillion (€860bn) and require congressional approval, and would further exacerbate the country’s nearly $1.8 trillion (€1.55tr) annual budget deficit and concerns about inflation.

The sum would far exceed US tariff revenues even before the Supreme Court tossed much of the president’s tariff program last year.

He did not say where the money would come from, citing only that "our country is making so much money."

Doubling down on his bet that he can help Republicans defy opinion polls and retain both houses of Congress on 3 November, Trump said: "I'm asking you to pretend that I'm on the ballot."

Opinion polls overwhelmingly show that Trump's approval ratings are in the low 30s, as voters become increasingly angry about high prices and the war he launched against Iran.

Trump has frequently lamented that, during the modern era, the president’s party almost always loses seats in Congress during the midterms, and he has looked for unorthodox ways to defy the trend, including this week’s convention.
