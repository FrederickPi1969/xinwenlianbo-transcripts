In a video post shared on social media on Monday, Bolsonaro said he had been banned from leaving Brazil and that he had been "served a notice to surrender my passports."
Brazilian Federal Police (PF) have been in contact with Argentine authorities on Tuesday in an effort to find out whether Flávio Bolsonaro has left Brazil via an airport in Argentina.

PF sources quoted by the Metrópoles newspaper said the Liberal Party (PL) candidate - who was defeated by leftist President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva in Sunday's presidential election - has not passed through any official Brazilian border checkpoint in recent hours.

However, the same sources stressed that it would be possible for him to cross the border without being subjected to checks.

It comes after Argentine outlet Infobae reported that the son of former Brazilian president Jair Bolsonaro had arrived in the Argentine town of Puerto Iguazú at 2 am on Tuesday, using his national identity card. He was reportedly preparing to continue his journey to the United States on a private flight.

According to Infobae, the aircraft was a Gulfstream G400, registration LV-JQW, owned by the company Baires Fly SA, which requested customs clearance for four passengers and three crew members.

Since early Tuesday morning, Bolsonaro's allies have been trying to contact him, without success, according to Letícia Casado, a columnist for the UOL news website.

Rogério Marinho, Bolsonaro's general campaign coordinator, has denied that Bolsonaro has left Brazil.

On Friday, two days before the second round of the presidential election, Bolsonaro's brother Eduardo argued that he should leave Brazil if he were defeated. Eduardo has been living in the US since 2025, after being sentenced to prison by Brazil's Supreme Federal Court for coercion in the case involving his father Jair's attempts to overturn the results of the 2022 election.

The rumours come after Supreme Federal Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes ordered that Bolsonaro hand over his passports to the PF.

Bolsonaro agreed with his legal team that he would comply with the order and that he would deliver the documents to the authorities on Tuesday morning, but this has apparently yet to happen.

US President Donald Trump said on Monday that the Brazilian election looked "rigged," without providing evidence.

Speaking to the media, Trump said the vote "looked terrible" and "like another rigged election."

"I didn't take sides," Trump told reporters when asked for his thoughts. "Elections have to be indisputable, and it looked very bad to me."

Bolsonaro initially refused to recognise Lula's victory, saying it was "impossible" to acknowledge the result and that the election should have been annulled. He later said he would "organise the opposition."

In a video post shared on social media on Monday, he added that he had been banned from leaving Brazil and had been "served a notice to surrender my passports."

Hundreds of glowing lanterns floated into the night sky above Bagan's ancient pagodas. Thousands gathered to celebrate Thadingyut, Myanmar's Festival of Lights marking the end of Buddhist Lent.
… More
Families released paper lanterns over the centuries-old temples, while devotees travelled from across the country to pray, make offerings and take part in the three-day celebrations, held from 25 to 27 October.

For the first time, Bagan hosted an official Sky Lantern Festival, organised by the military authorities' tourism department. Around 1,000 lanterns were released each evening above the UNESCO World Heritage site to attract visitors.

Also known as the Festival of Lights, Thadingyut marks the end of Buddhist Lent, a three-month period of religious observance during the rainy season.

The festival commemorates the Buddha's return from the celestial realm after teaching his mother, traditionally celebrated by lighting candles and lanterns. It is also an occasion for family reunions and honouring elders.

The Jordanian armed forces launched a wave of strikes against southern Syria on Monday evening, targeting what it said were "hideouts of terrorist organisations."

"RJAF fighter jets destroyed a number of terrorist locations, killing and wounding several terrorists and destroying their equipment," the military said in a statement, adding that the operation had been carried out "in retaliation" for a recent attack on a Greek Orthodox church in Karak that reportedly killed nine people.

Government spokesperson Mohammad Al-Momani said the operation had resulted in the deaths of more than 20 IS operatives as well as the destruction of a "major" weapons depot. He said the strikes had been conducted following intelligence on "imminent terrorist threats."

The Church of the Holy Sepulchre, known to Orthodox Christians as the Church of the Resurrection, stands in the Christian Quarter of Jerusalem's Old City. It is one of the most important shrines in Christianity, as it is believed to have been built on what was thought to be the site of the crucifixion, burial and resurrection of Jesus Christ. It was commissioned by the Roman emperor Constantine the Great, and was built in the fourth century.

The first church was consecrated in 335 AD. Over the course of its history, the building was destroyed and rebuilt several times. It suffered particularly heavy damage in 1009 under the Fatimid caliph al-Hakim. In the following decades the church was partially restored.

After the Crusaders captured Jerusalem in 1099, extensive alterations were carried out. The church's present appearance is therefore the result of numerous phases of construction and restoration.

For a long time hardly any structural changes, renovation work or excavations were permitted, because several Christian denominations share the church and each feared that changes could weaken their rights and influence. Now the different parties have reached agreement. Since 2016 the Church of the Holy Sepulchre has been undergoing restoration, for the first time in more than 200 years.

In 2022 a comprehensive restoration of the church floor was initially planned. As work progressed, it turned into an extensive excavation project. Researchers discovered, beneath the church, an abandoned stone quarry, agricultural plots, a garden and rock-cut tombs.

The garden is of particular significance, as the Gospel of John mentions a garden at the place of Jesus's crucifixion. According to the Bible, the crucifixion took place close to the city limits of Jerusalem. The Letter to the Hebrews notes that Jesus suffered outside the city gate. This, too, is consistent with the archaeologists' findings.

Lead archaeologist Francesca Romana Stasolla of Rome's "La Sapienza" university interprets the finds as evidence of a burial landscape. The excavations suggest that cultivated land once lay between Golgotha, known as Calvary in the Bible's new testament, and the tomb, according to Stasolla.

The archaeologists also found traces of olive trees and vines, dated to around 33 AD, the period in which Jesus's death is usually placed.

According to Stasolla, the tombs carved into the rock also point to an earlier burial site. The Gospels of Matthew, Mark and Luke describe Jesus's tomb as a burial chamber hewn from rock, so this detail fits as well.

The finds do not provide scientific proof that Jesus was buried underneath the site of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. There is no inscription to that effect, nor can it be shown that the tomb chamber was unused or belonged to Joseph of Arimathea, who, according to the Gospels, laid Jesus in his new rock-cut tomb. The plant remains also cannot yet be dated precisely to the time of the crucifixion.

The excavations therefore do not settle the question of Jesus's actual burial place. They do, however, show that the site venerated by Christians for centuries has geographical and archaeological features that correspond to the descriptions in the New Testament.

With no party expected to win an outright majority, weeks of coalition talks could follow a vote being closely watched in European capitals.
Israel’s election is on a knife-edge as voters head to the polls today, with the final surveys showing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu neck and neck with his main centrist challenger, Gadi Eisenkot.

It is the country’s first general election since the Hamas-led attack of 7 October 2023. Some 7.3 million people are eligible to vote, including hundreds of thousands casting a ballot for the first time, while tens of thousands of Israelis living abroad are thought to have flown home to take part.

Netanyahu, 77, is Israel’s longest-serving prime minister, having held the post on and off for more than 19 years. He has campaigned on his security record and his close relationship with US President Donald Trump, arguing he is best placed to keep the country safe.

Eisenkot, 66, a former military chief and political newcomer, leads the Yashar party and an informal bloc stretching from the right to the centre-left. He has pledged an independent state inquiry into the security failures surrounding the 7 October attack, something the current government has resisted.

Voters at polling stations reflected the divide. “He’s a man who’s not afraid to take action,” said 85-year-old Zipporah Bennett, explaining her support for Netanyahu. In Jerusalem, 65-year-old Alexandra said: “I want change in our country.”

Arab-majority parties, which represent around 21% of the population, could prove kingmakers if the result is as tight as expected, though neither leading candidate has publicly said he would partner with them.

The vote is being closely watched in Europe. The EU is Israel’s largest trading partner, but relations have come under strain over the war in Gaza. In April, Spain, Ireland and Slovenia renewed a push to suspend the EU-Israel Association Agreement at a meeting of foreign ministers, but Germany and Italy blocked the move. alqudsWikipedia

Analysts caution that replacing Netanyahu would not necessarily mean a break with the policies driving European criticism, pointing out that Eisenkot has opposed Palestinian statehood. He has, however, promised to crack down on violent settlers. Al Jazeera

Polls close at 22:00 local time (21:00 CET). Final results could take days, and no party is expected to come close to the 61 seats needed for a majority in the 120-member Knesset, meaning coalition negotiations could last weeks or even months.

In Tel Aviv, families affected by the 7 October 2023 attacks observed a moment of silence as polling stations were opening across Israel for the general election.
… More
Families affected by the Hamas-led attacks of 7 October 2023 gathered in Tel Aviv on Tuesday to honour relatives killed that day, observing a minute's silence before joining together in song beside a white installation bearing the date “7.10.23”.

Holding photographs of their loved ones, participants included Yona Adam, whose daughter Mapal was killed at the Nova music festival, and Einav Zangauker, mother of former hostage Matan Zangauker.

The commemoration took place as Israelis headed to the polls on Tuesday, 27 October, for their first general election since the attacks, voting to elect the 26th Knesset.

In Kiryat Arba, an Israeli settlement near Hebron in the occupied West Bank, voters queued outside polling stations. Far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir also cast his ballot in the nationalist-right stronghold.

In Umm al-Fahm, an Arab-majority city in northern Israel, election officials prepared ballot papers at al-Aqwas school before voters entered to cast their votes. Similar scenes unfolded at polling stations in Jerusalem.

Exit polls are expected at 21:00 CET on Tuesday, although it could take weeks before a new government is formed.

Announcing last Friday that she had brokered the ceasefire, Melania Trump said civilian officials from both Russia and Ukraine would visit residents in their homes and "determine the level of assistance they require".
A localised ceasefire brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky went into effect early on Tuesday.

The local pause in fighting started at 3 am to allow an aid mission into Oleshky in southern Ukraine later this week.

According to her office, Melania Trump led talks with civilian negotiators from Russia and Ukraine after meeting with UN officials who briefed her on the situation in the town.

The aid mission is planned ahead of a new round of US-brokered talks between Russia and Ukraine expected next month aimed at ending the war after more than 4½ years of fighting.

Up to 2,000 people are stranded in Oleshky with dwindling food and medicine. They are unable to leave because of explosive mines and drone attacks in the area, according to the UN.

Russian and Ukrainian negotiators as well as the militaries of the two countries confirmed in separate statements that the ceasefire had begun.

Announcing last Friday that she had brokered the ceasefire, Melania Trump said civilian officials from both Russia and Ukraine would visit residents in their homes and "determine the level of assistance they require".

"Residents who wish to evacuate will receive coordinated support and transportation to their preferred destination. I anticipate some will want to go to Russia, and some to Ukraine," she said.

Newsmax, a right-wing US cable network, has said it had been granted "exclusive television access" to accompany observers entering the city on Thursday.

Minutes after 3 am, multiple ballistic missile explosions shook Kyiv and air alerts sounded in multiple parts of the country.

A Russian strike damaged a major bridge spanning the Dnipro River in Kyiv, the city's mayor said.

"A strike hit the Southern Bridge. Metro infrastructure on the bridge was also damaged," Mayor Vitaly Klitschko wrote on Telegram, with city officials adding that train services across the bridge had been suspended.

Late Monday, the capital also came under attack, when Russian ballistic missiles damaged the grounds of a university in Kyiv, killing one person and leaving 10 injured, city officials said.

Moscow's latest pummelling has damaged medical facilities in the capital, Klitschko said, adding that a "blast wave shattered the windows in (an) intensive care unit".

"At another medical facility in the same district, windows were also shattered in two wards. Neither patients nor medical staff were wounded," he said on social media.

In another attack, "a building in a non-residential area was also hit. According to preliminary reports, there is one fatality near one of the supermarkets in the area," Klitschko said.

A strike near a dormitory belonging to one of Kyiv's universities has shattered windows in the building, wounding a 20-year-old woman, Klitschko said.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said "Russian ballistic missiles struck the grounds of a university in Kyiv and the area around it –- effectively the campus, Kyoto Park, the area near a metro station, and residential buildings."

"The missiles hit very crowded areas, and there is not a single military facility nearby. This was a strike directly on the city, on its infrastructure," he said on social media.

In Kharkiv, Ukraine's second-largest city, authorities said attacks late Monday killed two people and injured four.

“It is important that the world sees that Russia is showing nothing but a desire to kill and continue waging war,” Zelenskyy wrote in an online post shortly afterward.
