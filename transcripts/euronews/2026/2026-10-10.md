The latest attack follows just two days after three Saudi citizens were killed in Houthi-claimed attack on Riyadh's international airport on Thursday.
The Riyadh airport was attacked once again on Saturday, in what is reported to be a missile strike by Yemen’s Houthis, according to a diplomatic source.

"Dozens of people were injured, most of them Saudi nationals," a source at a hospital in the Saudi capital said, adding that at least five of the injured were treated in an intensive care unit.

The attack comes two days after three people were killed in attacks on Riyadh's international airport on Thursday.

While Saudi authorities have not confirmed a new attack or evacuation of the airport, several witnesses said people at Riyadh's international airport were being evacuated on Saturday, as officials called on passengers to check their flight status.

One witness reported hearing an explosion while waiting to board, while another witness said they saw a strike that had wounded people.

"I was on gate 403" at the domestic terminal, one witness said. "I saw 401 hit, I saw injured in front of my eyes. Everyone was running."

The Canadian, German and Spanish embassies in Saudi Arabia warned citizens to avoid the airpot following reports of the latest attack.

"Kuwait Airways announces that flight KU775 from Kuwait to Riyadh and flight KU776 from Riyadh to Kuwait have been cancelled today, Saturday, October 10, 2026, due to the closure of the airport as a result of the security situation," read the statement posted online".

The report said Abbas had issued a decree calling for presidential and legislative elections to take place on 11 September 2027, with the presidency blaming "security, logistical and on-the-ground obstacles to establishing the conditions necessary for elections."

Wafa later reported that the Executive Committee of the Palestine Liberation Organisation - the official representative of the Palestinian people - had approved postponing the election, saying conditions had "changed fundamentally" since the elections were announced.

The EU's top diplomat, Kaja Kallas, said earlier this week that the elections "would offer a long-overdue opportunity to renew the legitimacy of Palestinian institutions."

"Palestinians have the right to choose their representatives in elections that are credible, inclusive and transparent," she added.

In September, Dubravka Šuica, the EU Commissioner for the Mediterranean, also said she "welcomed the decision to hold elections before the end of the year."

"The elections will be an important democratic moment," she wrote in a post on social media.

"Ensuring a fair, credible and inclusive electoral process is essential to strengthening Palestinian institutions," Šuica added.

On Friday, Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar accused Abbas of "fooling the international community by canceling once again promised elections" as rumours of the postponement circulated.

Legislative elections were last held in the Palestinian territories in 2006, when the Hamas Islamist movement emerged victorious after defeating the Fatah party.

Updated: 10/10/2026 - 12:11 GMT+2
Panama was hit by a powerful 7.7 earthquake, a 6.6 aftershock and many tremors, damaging buildings and prompting evacuations but no deaths reported.
… More
A series of strong earthquakes hit Panama on Friday, 9 October, causing damage to buildings and triggering evacuations. Residents rushed into the streets of Panama City, while tremors were also reported in Colombia.

In Penonomé, part of a cathedral collapsed, including one of its spires, while footage showed extensive damage to a nearby hotel. Residents in Herrera province also reported cracks and damage to their homes.

Tocumen International Airport briefly suspended operations before reopening, while the Panama Canal continued operating normally.

According to the US Geological Survey, the first earthquake struck at a depth of 10 kilometres, around 10 kilometres southwest of Pitaloza Arriba in Herrera province. Tsunami warnings were issued for Panama and several neighbouring countries.

Panama's president said no deaths had been reported in the immediate aftermath, as emergency teams continued assessing the damage.

The country lies along the Pacific Ring of Fire, a vast zone of seismic and volcanic activity where most of the world's earthquakes occur.

"I know nothing about this woman, and neither does anyone else! In fact, to the best of my knowledge, no one ever heard of her other than the fact that she, very obviously, hates Israel," he wrote, adding: "I don’t know what the woman has done."

He went on to claim he "should have won 8 Nobel Peace Prizes" for having "settled 8 wars" and said he would soon "settle, or win, two more," pointing to the wars in Iran and Ukraine.

Trump listed the conflicts he said he had "settled" as Cambodia and Thailand, Kosovo and Serbia, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Rwanda, Pakistan and India, Israel and Iran, Egypt and Ethiopia, Armenia and Azerbaijan, and Israel and Hamas.

Becoming increasingly irate, Trump continued to detail his perceived achievements before renewing his attack on Pillay, who he described as a "liberal person who works at the United Nations."

"The Nobel 'Peace' Prize has been forever discredited by a handful of radical ideologues who are anti-democracy, and enemies of civilization!" he concluded.

The post came after the president had already lambasted the award decision while speaking at a campaign rally in Syracuse, New York, on Friday, where he branded it as "disgraceful and embarrassing."

Born in Durban on South Africa's east coast, Pillay would go on to become the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights in 2008, a post she held until 2014.

Earlier in her career, she worked as a defence attorney for anti-apartheid activists, helping establish rights for prisoners on Robben Island.

She then served as acting judge on the South African High Court and as a judge on the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda.

She was also appointed as a judge on the International Criminal Court in 2003, working within the Appeals Chamber until 2008.

In 2025, whilst serving as the chair of the Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem, and Israel, Pillay accused Israel of genocide in Gaza.

Learning she had won the Peace Prize on Friday, Pillay appeared unable to resist taking a playful jab at Trump.

"If you clap a little bit more, I may share this prize with Trump," she said, in an apparent nod to last year's winner, Venezuelan opposition leader Maria Corina Machado, presenting her award to the American president.

It comes as US Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced on Friday that the US would be sanctioning the International Criminal Court, which Washington has repeatedly accused of "politicised actions" and of "targeting Israel."

"We will ban transactions with this rogue court, cutting off their resources and crippling their ability to operate against us," Rubio said in a video address shared on social media.

Updated: 10/10/2026 - 0:27 GMT+2
Clashes broke out between police and protesters in Brussels on Friday, 9 October, as thousands marched against the Belgian government's planned spending cuts. Police estimated that 30,000 people took part in the demonstration, organised by trade unions amid growing opposition to austerity measures.
… More
The unions fear the government's proposed savings package will hit workers, pensioners and low-income households. They oppose potential changes to wage indexation, VAT and other measures affecting purchasing power, arguing that wealthier people should contribute more.

Students also joined the march to protest against education cuts, rising fees and reforms they fear could make university less affordable.

The demonstration began with crowds carrying placards, playing music and dancing through the capital. But violence later erupted near Brussels-Midi railway station, where some protesters threw objects and fireworks at police.

Officers responded with water cannon and tear gas, while some demonstrators hurled gas canisters back at them. Police deployed additional units equipped with tear gas launchers, supported by a helicopter.

Further unrest was reported near Brussels-Central station. Police said the clashes took place on the sidelines and were separate from the main trade-union march.

Police initially reported 21 people detained, including 13 minors, but the number rose to more than 100 by Friday evening, with around 30 judicial arrests and 80 administrative detentions. No confirmed casualty figures were available, despite clashes, fires and property damage. Police said the main trade-union march remained peaceful.
