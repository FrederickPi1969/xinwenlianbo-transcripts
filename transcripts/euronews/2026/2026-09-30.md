Qatar and UAE back Ethiopian government as fighting spreads beyond Tigray

Fighting has displaced an estimated 150,000 people, drawing responses from Qatar and the UAE as Ethiopia and its neighbours trade accusations.
Qatar has condemned what it called the military escalation against Ethiopia’s federal forces, while the United Arab Emirates has voiced support for the Ethiopian government amid renewed fighting in the country’s north.

Qatar’s Foreign Ministry said the fighting threatened Ethiopia’s unity and sovereignty and the stability of the wider Horn of Africa.

It called for an immediate end to the fighting, protection for civilians and respect for the Pretoria Peace Agreement between Tigray and Ethiopia.

The Qatari statement did not name the Tigray People’s Liberation Front (TPLF) or the new alliance of armed groups fighting the federal government. Qatar said its “full support” was for efforts to reduce tensions and resolve disputes through dialogue.

The UAE condemned what it described as attacks by the TPLF and its armed allies on federal forces, as well as the seizure of airports in Tigray and the spread of fighting into neighbouring Afar and Amhara.

UAE Minister of State Sheikh Shakhboot bin Nahyan Al Nahyan said his country stood in “full solidarity with the government and people” of Ethiopia. He also rejected attempts to change the country’s constitutional order through force.

The statements followed the most serious fighting in northern Ethiopia since the agreement ended the 2020–2022 war, in which hundreds of thousands of people were killed.

Tigrayan forces seized the airports in Mekelle, Axum and Shire last week, prompting Ethiopian Airlines to suspend flights to the region.

The TPLF says it is fighting defensively after federal forces attacked Tigray using drones, artillery and ground troops. The federal government accuses the group of launching a planned offensive.

Tigrayan forces captured Erebti in Afar over the weekend. On Monday, pro-government forces said they had taken control of Alamata, an important supply town around 120 kilometres south of Tigray’s capital, Mekelle.

A drone also struck the headquarters of Tigrai TV in Mekelle on Sunday. Journalists said they had left the building after hearing an earlier explosion. No casualties were reported, and the federal government did not comment on the strike.

Médecins Sans Frontières said the number of wounded people treated at hospitals in Korem and Alamata rose to more than 420 between 22-25 September. More than 300 wounded people arrived at Suhul Hospital last week.

The Afar Pastoralist Development Association estimates that around 150,000 people have been displaced by the fighting.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was “gravely concerned” by the seizure of the three airports and the spread of hostilities beyond Tigray.

He urged all sides to stop the fighting, protect civilians, allow aid groups to reach those in need and return to talks under the peace agreement.

Ethiopia’s army chief, Field Marshal Birhanu Jula, has accused Eritrea, Sudan and Egypt of supporting armed groups fighting the government. He did not provide evidence for the accusations.

Dozens of activists gathered outside OpenAI's developer conference at San Francisco's Fort Mason Center on Tuesday, marching, chanting and holding signs and banners.

Protesters called on the company to end contracts with the US military and Immigration and Customs Enforcement, while demanding greater regulation of artificial intelligence.

FlyDubai flight FZ1073 to Israel made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia as passengers describe harrowing scenes of a fight between the pilots, with one of them stabbed. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said a pilot "attempted to crash the plane."
A commercial plane traveling from the United Arab Emirates to Israel was forced to make an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia on Wednesday after a fight between the pilots with one of them being stabbed, as Israeli media published passengers testimonies who said that a pilot tried to crash the plane.

In his first statement on the incident, Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu said that “one of the pilots stabbed the other and apparently attempted to crash the plane, with everyone on board."

In a video statement, Netanyahu said he spoke with one of the passengers who told him that “somehow, he managed to break into the cockpit with one of the crew members, and together they subdued the pilot who had committed the stabbing while he was attempting to sabotage the aircraft's systems" and that "another flight crew member entered the cockpit and managed to stabilize the plane."

"I salute these heroes who demonstrated resourcefulness and extraordinary courage. They saved many lives and averted a terrible disaster," the Israeli prime minister said, adding that almost all of the passengers on the flight were Israelis.

He also announced that “the pilot who committed the stabbing has been arrested and is currently being interrogated by Saudi authorities."

The Israeli prime minister did not comment on a possible terror link, while Israel's security agencies continue their investigations.

The plane carrying nearly 180 passengers landed safely in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, a civilian and military base in the kingdom, but the passengers described harrowing scenes onboard of blood in the cockpit and erratic flying movement.

Around two and a half hours after departing from Dubai, the FlyDubai plane transmitted a general emergency signal followed by a 7500 possible unlawful interference code, according to flight tracking Flightradar24 portal.

Flightradar24 showed the Boeing 737 abruptly descending from 33,000 feet to 17,000 feet within 30 seconds, before making an erratic circle close to the Jordanian border and eventually landing in Tabuk about an hour later, prompting Israeli fighter jets to scramble.

The aircraft’s climb and descent as well as its reported high speed are consistent with a struggle for controls.

Israeli media quoted passengers and Israeli officials stating that a fight broke out in the cockpit between the two pilots and one of them was stabbed, while passengers subdued the other pilot.

Two off-duty pilots who were reportedly among the passengers on the flight reportedly took control of the plane and landed it safely, according to multiple Israeli media reports.

Passengers told Israel’s Channel 12 by phone said they heard shouts of the altercation between the pilots and the plane dipped steeply before landing in Saudi Arabia.

Another passenger told Israel’s Channel 15 that when the cockpit door was opened, passengers saw blood inside and they intervened to stop the fight between the pilots. According to the same passenger, one of the pilots was inured with a folding knife.

The passenger described to Channel 15 the chaotic scenes onboard, with passengers shouting in fear for their lives as the plane engaged into its emergency landing.

Channel 12 Israel interviewed another passenger who said her husband helped “neutralize the terrorist” onboard as he “tried to crash the plane,” but was subdued by the passengers. “There were stabbings and a lot of blood,” the passengers said.

The powerful sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un rejected on Wednesday South Korean findings that the North planted land mines that injured three South Korean soldiers last week and threatened to retaliate if the South fires on North Korean troops fortifying the border.

The incident could further inflame tensions between the two Koreas, which have no working military hotlines or other emergency communications channels as Pyongyang hardens its stance towards Seoul while expanding its military nuclear programme.

Three South Korean soldiers were injured in two explosions on 21 September near the military demarcation line in the western section of the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), the heavily fortified border buffer zone between the two sides.

The statement by Kim Yo-jong came after South Korea’s military announced a preliminary assessment on Monday that the blasts were caused by North Korean mines following a joint inspection of the site with the US-led United Nations Command.

Kim Yo-jong, who is considered the second most powerful figure in Pyongyang, called the South Korean assessment a “conspiratorial farce,” accusing Seoul of making groundless claims to create a pretext for provocation against the North.

North Korea in recent years has added mines, anti-tank barriers and other front-line fortifications, raising concerns in the South about the growing presence of North Korean soldiers near the border.

Kim criticised the South for broadcasting warnings and firing warning shots at North Korean troops conducting front-line fortification work, insisting “our soldiers have never crossed the border.”

“If the (South Korean) military gangsters fire at our soldiers carrying on the permanent project for the southern border, not warning shots, our border units will take immediate and merciless retaliatory action,” she said.

The recent explosions, which took place about 10 metres south of the military demarcation line, came during an operation to establish a patrol route through a densely vegetated area.

The route was intended to allow future inspections with the UN Command after South Korea since last year detected “terrain changes” that may have resulted from North Korean minelaying.

The DMZ is a 248-kilometre strip and one of the world’s most heavily fortified borders, with an estimated 2 million land mines, barbed-wire fences, tank traps and combat troops on both sides.

In August 2015, another spate of land mine blasts that maimed two South Korean soldiers and sharply raised tensions between the Koreas.

The Koreas then traded threats, staged weapons demonstrations and resumed border propaganda broadcasts before days of marathon talks eventually pulled them back from the brink.

North Korea issued a vague expression of regret over the soldiers’ injuries at the time but has never clearly accepted responsibility.

The UN Command said on Wednesday that North Korea had violated the armistice agreement that ended fighting in the 1950-53 Korean War.

The agreement prohibits both sides from committing any hostile act “within, from, or against” the DMZ and bans military or civilian personnel from crossing the military demarcation line without authorisation from the armistice commission.

Mansouri is only the second woman in the Arab world to hold the office of prime minister. Her established political career has seen her serve as mayor of Marrakesh and member of parliament previously.
Morocco's King Mohammed VI named Urban Planning Minister Fatima Zahra Mansouri as the country's prime minister on Tuesday, making the 50-year-old attorney the first woman to hold the office in the Muslim kingdom.

Mansouri, who until her nomination also was the mayor of Marrakech, was tasked with forming a new government, the Royal Palace said in a statement. The announcement came four days after results of parliamentary elections were announced, with Mansouri’s centrist Authenticity and Modernity Party, or PAM, scoring the highest votes.

Mansouri, the second woman to serve as prime minister in the Arab world after Najla Bouden in Tunisia, is expected to build a coalition in the coming days to form a government. Morocco’s electoral system makes it difficult for a single party to win an absolute majority.

Her government is expected to make billions of dollars in investments linked to the 2030 FIFA World Cup, while seeking to deliver on promises to fight inflation, raise wages and create jobs.

The Moroccan Constitution gives the king, who is the country’s highest authority, the power to appoint the prime minister, but he must choose from the party that wins the most seats in parliamentary elections.

That was a change successfully pushed by Arab Spring protesters in 2011. Before that, the king could name the prime minister without any such restriction.

Mansouri joined PAM upon its creation in 2008 by Fouad Ali el-Himma, a childhood friend and close adviser to the king.

A law graduate from the University of Montpellier in France, Mansouri became one of the party’s top leaders in 2024. She twice served as mayor of Marrakech and was a member of parliament.

Mansouri is the daughter of the “pasha of Marrakech” Abderrahmane el-Mansouri, a senior local official who was also Morocco’s ambassador to the United Arab Emirates, headed Marrakech’s bar association and was a major landowner.

She faced accusations of conflict of interest after a group of unidentified hackers claimed she had used her position for personal gain regarding land transactions in Marrakech. Mansouri denied the allegations, called them defamatory and took legal action.

One million of the 15.8 million registered voters in last week’s election voted for the Authenticity and Modernity Party, marking its first win since its creation. The election was the first since the Ceuta migration crisis last July and deadly youth-led protests last year.

After the royal nomination, the focus shifts to the coalition that will form the next government.

PAM has expressed its willingness to work with all parties, but some, including the moderate Islamist Justice and Development party and left-wing Federation of Democratic Left, have announced no plans of such collaboration, insisting they will remain in the opposition.
