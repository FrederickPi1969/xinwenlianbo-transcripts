Updated: 1 November 2026 - 18:41 GMT+1
France faces a third weekend of mass youth protests over living costs, education and public services, with clashes erupting in Paris.
… More
France saw a third straight weekend of protests on Saturday, 31 October, as young people and their supporters took to the streets over the rising cost of living, education and public services.

In Paris, the march ended in clashes near Porte de Charenton, where demonstrators threw paving stones and a Molotov cocktail at police. Officers responded with tear gas and baton charges, while a nearby car was set on fire.

Earlier, students had denounced what they described as the government's dismissive attitude towards young people and defended their right to protest peacefully.

The French Interior Ministry estimated that 320,000 people took part in rallies nationwide, while youth organisations put the turnout at nearly 500,000. In Paris, the estimates stood at 70,000 and 200,000 respectively.

Around 70,000 police officers and gendarmes were deployed across the country, with march routes in the capital redirected away from central areas and government buildings.

What began as a protest over underfunded schools and teacher shortages has since grown into a broader movement over purchasing power and young people's prospects.

Switzerland is renewing direct talks with North Korea as tensions rise on the peninsula. Seoul hopes the Swiss office will help it engage with Pyongyang.
Switzerland has formally reopened its diplomatic office in North Korea as Ignazio Cassis became the first Swiss foreign minister to visit the country since 2003.

Cassis visited Pyongyang on 29 and 30 October before travelling to Seoul. In a statement issued on Saturday, the Swiss foreign ministry said the trip aimed to restore direct political dialogue with North Korea as it looks to maintain contact with both sides of the divided peninsula.

The office restarted operations on 19 October. It falls under the Swiss embassy in Beijing, which remains responsible for diplomatic and consular relations with North Korea. Switzerland’s previous office in Pyongyang handled humanitarian cooperation and closed in 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The two countries have maintained diplomatic relations since 1974, but regular political consultations stopped after 2019.

Bern said the office would allow Switzerland to deal directly with North Korean authorities and follow developments in the region.

During his visit, Cassis met with North Korean Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui and Jo Yong Won, secretary of the ruling Workers’ Party’s Central Committee.

His discussions with Choe covered restoring political dialogue, security on the Korean Peninsula and wider security challenges in Europe and Asia.

Speaking to Swiss news agency Keystone-ATS on Saturday, Cassis said he had offered Switzerland’s help in facilitating dialogue to ease tensions on the peninsula. He said the proposal appeared to have been well received.

North Korea’s state news agency KCNA said on Saturday that the two ministers discussed diplomatic cooperation and international issues in a "friendly atmosphere." It did not provide a detailed account of the discussions.

Asked by Euronews whether the office could support wider European dialogue with Pyongyang, a Swiss foreign ministry spokesperson said the department had nothing to add to Saturday’s statement.

Cassis also made a trip to Seoul on 30 and 31 October, meeting National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac and senior foreign ministry official Jeong Yeon-du.

He briefed Jeong on his Pyongyang talks. Jeong expressed hope that the Swiss office would help South Korea engage with the North, according to Seoul’s foreign ministry.

The visit came amid renewed military tensions. North Korea fired around 10 short-range ballistic missiles towards the sea on Thursday, according to South Korea’s military, a day after Seoul renewed its call for military talks to prevent accidental border clashes.

In a separate statement on Sunday, South Korea’s foreign ministry said Foreign Minister Cho Hyun had urged support for dialogue between the two Koreas at a G20 meeting in Atlanta.

Switzerland has helped monitor the armistice that halted fighting in the Korean War since 1953 through the Neutral Nations Supervisory Commission, based at Panmunjom. Cassis also met the head of the Swiss delegation to discuss the security situation.

Updated: 1 November 2026 - 14:37 GMT+1
Police remained on the streets of Frankfurt on Sunday after rioting and looting broke out following youth demonstrations, leaving shopfronts smashed, fires burning and several people injured.
… More
Police remained on duty in central Frankfurt on Sunday, 1 November, after riots broke out following a demonstration the previous day. Footage shows smashed shop windows, looted stores, burning barricades and debris scattered across the streets. Police used water cannon to extinguish fires and clear obstacles from the roads.

Several protesters and police officers were injured, while 30 people were detained. The situation had calmed by early Sunday morning after police cordoned off parts of the city centre.

Hesse state premier Boris Rhein condemned the violence, saying he was shocked by the events and that those responsible would face consequences.

Violence was also reported in Cologne, where police used water cannon to disperse crowds, and in Berlin, where protesters attacked officers with bottles and pyrotechnics.

Celebrities gathered in New York for Heidi Klum’s 25th annual Halloween party, with the host transforming herself into a gruesome severed hand and guests arriving in an array of elaborate costumes.
… More
Celebrities gathered in New York on Saturday, 31 October, for the 25th anniversary of Heidi Klum’s famous Halloween party, with guests showing off an array of elaborate and unusual costumes.

Klum once again made headlines with an elaborate transformation, appearing as a giant, gruesome severed hand. The costume featured dangling ligaments and tendons, with only her eyes and mouth visible, while blood appeared to drip from her mouth. She was accompanied by helpers dressed in blood-stained hazmat suits.

The idea came to Klum last year, when she looked at her left hand and wondered whether she could fit her entire body inside a giant version of it.

Among the guests was actor Kyle MacLachlan, who brought back his evil Cliff Vandercave costume from the 1994 film The Flintstones. He arrived with his wife, Desiree Gruber, and their son, Callum Lyon MacLachlan.

Iran ramps up its threats and does not exclude pre-emptive military action, but its officials have nevertheless noted positive steps in mediated negotiations with the US ahead of the decisive midterm elections. The Gulf countries remain "braced for the worst."
Iranian Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei has issued a warning to the world that “any level or type of threat must be met with an immediate, impactful, and geographically unrestricted response."

Khamenei's threat comes as urgent diplomatic efforts continue to avoid a new all-out Iran war, which would have devastating consequences for the Middle East and beyond, as Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Sunday that Tehran hopes "that there is rationality on the other side to prefer diplomacy to other paths," as "drawing on the experiences of the past two wars, we are much stronger than before."

The supreme leader met with Iran’s top military commanders on Saturday, presenting them with directives to prepare for potential new US-Israeli military action, according to the official IRNA news agency.

It follows warnings from the US that its forces could conduct a short but intensive military campaign against Iran after the US midterm elections next week.

The threat of a "geographically unrestricted response" means Iran could look to target Europe as well as Turkey and the South Caucasus in response to a renewed attack.

The description of “any level or type of threat” could indicate that Iran may act pre-eemptively, before a US-Israeli military strike.

State media said that Khamenei - who hasn’t been seen publicly since the Iran war began with US and Israeli strikes in late February - also called for an updated list of potential US and Israeli targets, to include “all kinds of physical and non-physical assets."

"Should Iran’s enemies resume military or terrorist attacks, it will be the Armed Forces of the Islamic Republic and the Iranian nation that will determine when and under what conditions those attacks will end," he said.

Iranian state media reported that in a meeting on Saturday, the commander of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Major General Ahmad Vahidi, together with the Iranian army commander and his chief of staff, briefed the supreme leader on Iran’s military readiness and told him that Iran’s weapons production has increased “seven-fold”.

The commanders report directly to Khamenei and have previously overruled the civilian Iranian leadership fuelling constant speculations as to who makes the decisions in Tehran.

Tehran's threats were reiterated on Sunday by Iranian army spokesperson Mohammad Akraminia who told Iranian state media that “should a new war erupt, the geography of the conflict will not be confined to the West Asia region,” that "any potential future war could prove significantly longer than past conflicts.”

The Institute for the Study of War (ISW), a Washington-based think tank, estimated that Khamenei’s threats were “likely part of an Iranian effort to deter the United States and Israel from striking Iran.”

While multiple speculations are swirling in the Gulf about the imminence of the war breaking out again versus the state of negotiations, with purported lists of demands from both sides being circulated by medias or whether US negotiators will meet the Iranian ones directly or not, the Gulf nations are bearing the brunt of the months-long state of war, with severe consequences on their economies.

While both the US and Iran have repeatedly pushed previous standoffs to the limit in their attempt to pressure each other into concessions, the Gulf countries continue hoping that a last-minute diplomatic breakthrough of some kind can lead to a peaceful resolution and avert another round of war, especially after the US warned the Gulf that it could launch a new attack on Iran.

UAE presidential adviser Anwar Gargash told a conference in Bahrain on Sunday that the Gulf states remain "braced for the worst," while hoping that mediation efforts, also led by Qatar, will prevail.

He warned that the Gulf has been blocked in a prolonged state of “no war, no peace,” and that if Washington and Tehran’s “positions are not bridged, then we have to worry about the worst.”

The UAE diplomat also decried the Gulf countries’ lack of unity in action as a bloc, stating that “we have not collectively risen to the challenge” and that rebuilding trust with Iran would take an "extremely long time."

While Tehran continues its threats and defiant rhetoric, its foreign minister and its security chief have signalled positive steps in their mediated negotiations with the US via Qatar.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi announced on Saturday evening that Tehran could agree to reopen the Strait of Hormuz in just two days, instead of the previous timeline of seven days.

He said that Iranian diplomats were in Qatar for talks after a Qatari delegation mediated discussions in Tehran over the last few days.

Araghchi last month proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz within a week if the US agreed to lift its blockade of Iranian ports, release frozen Iranian assets and waive sanctions on Iranian oil sales, among other conditions. That was rejected.

In an interview with Qatar-based broadcaster Al Jazeera, Araghchi said that Iran was not planning to meet with US officials but called recent US efforts toward “broader dialogue” a positive step.

The statement comes after a confrontation outside the Constitutional Tribunal building on Friday night, broadcast live on television, that has pushed a decade-old dispute into the wider European political debate.
The European Commission said on Saturday it was closely monitoring the crisis surrounding Poland’s Constitutional Tribunal, following a confrontation outside the court building on Friday night that was broadcast live on television. The incident has brought renewed attention to a dispute that has been ongoing for more than a decade.

“It is for the Polish authorities to ensure that the Constitutional Tribunal can function in accordance with the Polish constitution and in respect of the relevant case law of the Court of Justice of the European Union,” a Commission spokesperson told Euronews on Saturday.

“The appointment of eight new members of the Constitutional Tribunal lies within the constitutional power of the Sejm, Poland’s parliament,” the spokesperson added.

The Commission spokesperson also pointed to a ruling by the European Court of Human Rights, which on 5 May 2026 issued interim measures requiring the competent Polish authorities “to refrain from hindering the taking up and exercise of judicial duties by judges appointed by the Sejm in March 2026”.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk addressed Poles in a televised speech on Saturday evening, in his first major response to the crisis. He described Friday’s events as “scandalous incidents” and said he hoped such scenes would never be repeated in Poland.

Restoring calm could not mean accepting lawlessness or violence, Tusk said, as he vowed to restore conditions for independent justice and rule of law.

“A democratic state has to be strong and capable of enforcing justice,” he added, vowing that the rule of law would prevail over violence.

The crisis escalated on Friday night when President Karol Nawrocki attempted to enter the Tribunal building amid a deadlock over the appointment of new judges.

Police surrounded the building as the confrontation unfolded live on television, highlighting the growing stand-off between the president and the government over the composition of the country’s top court.

The Tusk government says it is pushing ahead with long-needed reforms to restore the rule of law after years of what it describes as interference in the judiciary by the Law and Justice (PiS) party. The opposition accuses Tusk’s government of attempting a coup.

The co-chairs of the European Conservatives and Reformists (ECR) group on Saturday condemned the Polish government’s actions, describing them as a violation of the rule of law, and backed President Nawrocki.

“The Tusk government’s actions constitute a clear and grave violation of the rule of law. We condemn this attack on judicial independence and Poland’s constitutional safeguards. No government stands above the law,” ECR co-chair Nicola Procaccini said.

He added that European institutions “must not avert their eyes” and said respect for national sovereignty and judicial independence must remain a matter of principle, “unaffected by political alliances”.

The Tribunal’s legitimacy has been contested for more than a decade. The dispute dates back to 2015, when the then-governing Civic Platform and the opposition Law and Justice party clashed over the appointment of five judges.

It has since developed into a wider conflict over judicial independence, the Tribunal’s composition and the authority of its rulings.
