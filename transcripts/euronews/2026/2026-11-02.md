Updated: 2 November 2026 - 19:40 GMT+1
The UK celebrates 140 years of the motor car as pre-1905 vehicles parade through London's historic streets before heading to Brighton.
… More
Britain's motoring history took to the streets of London on Sunday, 1 November, as veteran cars took part in the annual London to Brighton Veteran Car Run.

The 97km journey from Hyde Park to the south coast saw some vehicles travelling at just 12 to 20 mph. The event marked 140 years since Karl Benz introduced his Patent-Motorwagen, widely regarded as the first production car.

One vehicle even broke down on The Mall, highlighting the mechanical challenges of driving these early models.

For participants, the run is as much about the experience as the destination. Driving a car from the early days of motoring requires patience and a good understanding of its mechanics.

Shaun Harborne, at the wheel of a 1901 Renault Type D, described the vehicles as fragile, with their suspension, brakes, clutch and gearbox all requiring careful handling.

Karl-Heinz Jungbeck, who travelled from Munich in a 1903 Lacoste & Battmann, said the event offers a taste of the adventure motorists would have experienced more than a century ago.

Updated: 2 November 2026 - 17:26 GMT+1
Ukraine’s Skyfall says its Stargate drone has evacuated two wounded soldiers from the front line, flying 15 km each way at up to 105 km/h.
… More
Ukraine has started using a drone designed to lift wounded soldiers off the battlefield, according to its manufacturer, Skyfall.

The company says the “Stargate” has already carried two soldiers out of the front line, and that both are now recovering.

The drone can reach 105 kilometres per hour and fly 15 kilometres each way with a person on board, a 30-kilometre round trip.

A spokesperson describes it as the first aerial casualty evacuation drone, developed over roughly a year after requests from the front.

Heavy mining and enemy drones overhead, he explains, often make conventional methods impossible, while unmanned ground vehicles struggle with rivers and rough terrain.

Updated: 2 November 2026 - 15:27 GMT+1
Supermarkets in several European countries have reported cyberattacks targeting refrigeration systems, causing food to spoil and forcing some stores to close off their fridges and freezers.
… More
Supermarket refrigeration systems have been hit by a suspected cyberattack across several European countries, leaving fridges and freezers running at dangerously high temperatures.

Stores in Belgium, Sweden, the Netherlands, Estonia, Luxembourg and Ireland have reported problems, with hundreds of thousands of euros worth of food potentially affected.

The incident appears to have taken place overnight between Sunday and Monday. In Belgium, dozens of stores from chains including Delhaize, Carrefour and Intermarché were reportedly affected, alongside an IKEA store near Mons.

Belgium's Centre for Cybersecurity confirmed it was aware of an incident targeting supermarket refrigeration systems but did not provide further details.

Updated: 2 November 2026 - 14:33 GMT+1
China's Mount Tai hosts a robot challenge, with nearly 90 teams tackling 3,100 steps and a 600-metre climb on an ancient mountain path.
… More
China put its latest robotics technology to the test on Monday, 2 November, as nearly 90 teams gathered for the CMG World Robot Mount Tai Challenge in Tai'an, Shandong Province.

Humanoid, quadruped and exoskeleton robots tackled a 3.9km stretch of ancient mountain path, climbing 3,100 stone steps and gaining 600 metres in elevation. The route featured steep slopes, long stairways and sharp bends.

As spectators watched, the machines made their way up the mountain, with several reaching the finish line.

The challenge turned one of China's best-known mountain routes into a testing ground for robotics.

Uneven stone steps and steep inclines tested the machines' ability to maintain their balance and negotiate difficult terrain. Some quadruped robots also carried food supplies along the track.

Updated: 2 November 2026 - 14:20 GMT+1
Millions of Americans are heading to the polls on Tuesday, 3 November, for the 2026 US midterm elections. The vote will determine which party controls Congress for the next two years and how much power President Donald Trump will have to advance his political agenda.

Midterm elections take place halfway through a US president's four-year term. They give voters an opportunity to elect new members of Congress and decide which party will control the legislative branch of government.

Unlike presidential elections, Americans are not voting for a president. Donald Trump will remain in the White House regardless of the outcome.

Instead, voters will elect all 435 members of the House of Representatives and 35 members of the 100-seat Senate. Elections for governors in 36 states are also taking place, alongside thousands of state and local contests.

Polling stations close at different times across the country, depending on the state. The first results are expected after polls close on the East Coast, while voting continues further west.

For European viewers, the first results should start arriving during the night between Tuesday and Wednesday.

The biggest question is whether Republicans can retain control of both chambers of Congress.

Republicans currently hold narrow majorities in the House and Senate. Democrats need a net gain of four seats in each chamber to take control.

If Republicans retain their majorities, the president will have a clearer path to passing legislation and advancing his domestic agenda.

Republicans are also defending seats in other competitive states, while Democrats face the challenge of protecting some of their own vulnerable seats.

The House battle is different. All 435 seats are contested, with control potentially coming down to a small number of closely fought congressional districts.

The midterms are also widely seen as a referendum on the president's first two years in office.

Voters' concerns about the cost of living, the economy, immigration and foreign policy could shape the outcome.

The results will offer an indication of public support for Trump's policies and help determine the political landscape ahead of the 2028 presidential election.

Advocacy groups have warned that the removal of former allies and their families could endanger their lives.
Afghanistan's Taliban authorities voiced concern on Monday over reported plans by Washington to deport scores of Afghan immigrants to Pakistan with the aim of their eventual return to their home country.

More than 150 Afghan nationals in the United States would be deported via Pakistan to Afghanistan, including relatives of those who assisted US forces before their 2021 withdrawal, according to US media reports and an advocacy group.

"The Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan expresses its serious concern over reports that the United States intends to deport some Afghan refugees to Pakistan who have not yet been able to obtain legal residency documents in that country," foreign ministry spokesperson Abdul Qahar Balkhi said in a statement.

Advocacy groups have warned that the removal of former allies and their families could endanger their lives.

After regaining power, Taliban authorities announced a general amnesty for Afghans who worked with Western-backed forces and governments and have repeated promises this year that their former foes could safely return home.

However, the United Nations has recorded reports of extrajudicial killings, detentions and abuses.

President Donald Trump's administration last year issued travel bans for a number of countries, including Afghanistan, as part of a broad immigration crackdown.

His administration sought to end deportation protections for Afghans, some of whom were granted special immigrant visas following the 2021 takeover by the Taliban.

Among those expected to be deported are relatives of Afghans who assisted the US military in the country prior to the fall of Kabul, according to AfghanEvac, a group seeking to help former Afghan allies.

While the United States cannot arrange deportations directly to Afghanistan, anonymous US officials told The New York Times that Pakistan will oversee their expulsion to Afghanistan.

In recent years, Germany and Turkey have deported Afghan nationals directly to Afghanistan, despite concerns from human rights groups over the risks facing returnees.

Balkhi said Taliban authorities urged "the United States of America to adopt a responsible and measured approach in this regard and to take any decisions and measures regarding the fate of Afghan citizens with clear mutual understanding, necessary coordination, and consideration of the rights and legitimate demands of the individuals concerned".

"The Afghan side is ready to interact in this regard in a practical and mutually understanding manner," he added.

Pakistan's government earlier this year ordered law enforcement nationwide to arrest Afghan citizens without visas as part of a years-long repatriation policy that has pushed back millions of Afghans who fled throughout decades of war.

Relations between Pakistan and Afghanistan have been fraught since the Taliban authorities took power and worsened after deadly border clashes last October that led to near-total closure of the border.

Iran says it can reopen Hormuz in two days if US meets conditions

Foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said the timeline shortened from the seven days first proposed in September was “another indication of Iran’s good-faith approach” but that “everything depends” on Washington.
Iran said on Monday it was ready to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days if the US accepted its conditions, having proposed a seven-day timeline when it first put the idea to Washington through Qatar in September.

"The possibility of implementing this plan in two days is another indication of Iran's good-faith approach," foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said.

"We have stated that, if Iran's demands are met, it would be possible to restore security to the Strait of Hormuz and resume the negotiating process within a short period," he added.

Baghaei said that an exchange of messages was ongoing with the US via mediators and that "everything depends" on the US side accepting Iran's demands. "Only then can we discuss the subsequent stages," he explained.

Tehran's conditions include ending the war on all fronts, unfreezing Iranian assets abroad, lifting sanctions on its oil sector, and ending the US naval blockade of Iranian ports.

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi set out further requirements in an Al Jazeera interview released on Sunday, saying Washington must also accept a new transit route through the strait agreed between Iran and Oman, reduce US troop numbers in the region, and remove sanctions imposed since August.

Washington has rolled out a fresh wave of measures from August onwards, which it described as the "economic asphyxiation" of Iran.

Since the Iran war broke out in late February, Tehran has claimed it maintained control over Hormuz, regularly targeting vessels that attempt to cross without its authorisation. Meanwhile, the US imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports.

The diplomatic activity comes as both Washington and Tel Aviv have intensified military threats. On Saturday, Trump said he still had to "take care" of one or two targets, including Pickaxe mountain in central Iran, a site Washington suspects houses nuclear infrastructure, which Tehran denies.

Baghaei warned at Monday's briefing that "any act of aggression against Iran, any military attack against Iran, by whatever means, will be met with a powerful, immediate and comprehensive response from us."

Iran's deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi travelled to Doha on Monday to help draft a preliminary agreement, with Qatar acting as mediator.

China said it was sending its special envoy Zhai Jun to Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia to press all sides to halt the fighting. President Xi Jinping said the Middle East and Gulf must not become an arena for great-power competition. Beijing and Muscat jointly called on Washington and Tehran to return to the "Islamabad memorandum of understanding," Chinese state media reported.

Officials in Tehran continued to set red lines. Deputy parliament speaker Ali Nikzad said Iran would not enter nuclear negotiations under pressure, and that previous talks had produced only broken promises.

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said on Saturday that the US would need to meet Iran's conditions before talks could begin. Rezaei said he had received a letter from US Vice President JD Vance but had not yet replied.

US military command CENTCOM rejected Iranian claims that its strikes had targeted only military objectives. CENTCOM said on X that Iran had deliberately hit residential areas, civilian centres and civilian sailors in international waters, and that its attacks had killed at least 20 people.

Anwar Gargash, adviser to the UAE president, told the Manama Dialogue conference in Bahrain that Abu Dhabi did not want a prolonged crisis with Iran but acknowledged that trust had been lost. "This damage was not caused by us. Rather, following Iran's attack on us, trust was damaged," he said.

The diplomatic activity falls ahead of US midterm elections on Tuesday, in which economic pressures linked to the Iran war have increased the likelihood of Republican losses in Congress.

The announcement of results on Wednesday coincides with the anniversary of the 1979 seizure of the US embassy in Tehran, during which 52 US diplomats were held for over a year. Tehran-organised demonstrations against the US are expected across the country, while billboards depicting Trump behind prison bars have been erected in Tehran in recent days.

Trump said on Sunday evening that he and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had held "very good" talks the previous day and discussed Iran, without elaborating. Netanyahu's office has not commented on the meeting.

Since Israeli voters went to the polls last Tuesday, Netanyahu has spoken publicly only about the vote. Results remain inconclusive, with preliminary assessments suggesting he may not secure a coalition, although no alternative government has taken shape.

Updated: 2 November 2026 - 10:12 GMT+1
Serbia marks two years since the Novi Sad station collapse, as students in power vow justice for the 16 victims and accountability for alleged corruption.
… More
Serbia marked the second anniversary of the deadly collapse at Novi Sad railway station, as thousands of students and supporters marched through the rain to honour the 16 people killed.

The silent procession ended outside the station, where participants laid wreaths and placed red hearts bearing the victims' names. Others lit candles in their memory. On 1 November 2024, a concrete canopy above the station entrance collapsed in the northern Serbian city, killing 16 people.

The tragedy sparked a student-led movement calling for accountability and an investigation into alleged corruption and negligence during the station's renovation. Thirteen people, including a former government minister, were charged, but no trial has begun.

Sunday's commemoration came just a week after the movement's Student List won Serbia's snap parliamentary election on 25 October, defeating the bloc led by Aleksandar Vučić. Preliminary results gave the students 140 of the 250 seats in parliament.

For those gathered in Novi Sad, the election result has raised hopes that the case will finally progress and those responsible will be held to account.

The crash occurred near Cresthaven, a neighbourhood in Palm Beach County that is less than 11 kilometres from Trump's resort home Mar-a-Lago.
A Learjet plummeted into a neighbourhood in southern Florida miles from US President Donald Trump's resort home on Sunday, killing all nine people on board, officials said.

Several unverified clips posted to social media depicting the deadly crash showed an aircraft nearly vertical slamming at high speed into the ground, creating a large fireball and leaving a flaming crater near a busy roadside.

"Today's plane crash in Palm Beach County, Florida is a horrific tragedy," Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said in a statement, confirming that all nine people on the aircraft were killed.

Duffy said the plane, a Learjet 60, had departed from Austin, Texas and was headed for the Bahamas.

The crash occurred near Cresthaven, a neighbourhood in Palm Beach County that is less than 11 kilometres from Trump's resort home Mar-a-Lago.

"Obviously this is a cataclysmic plane crash," Florida Governor Ron DeSantis told reporters near the scene.

"It looked like a missile going down," he said, adding there were no reports of anyone on the ground being injured.

Trump had been at Mar-a-Lago on Sunday but departed from the nearby and newly renamed President Donald J. Trump International Airport shortly after he boarded Air Force One at 11:55 am (1755 CET), about an hour before the Learjet crash.

The plane came down near the entrance to a mobile home park, Palm Beach County Commissioner Joel Flores said, noting about 600 residents were evacuated and relocated to shelters.

"The FAA and National Transportation Safety Board will investigate" the crash, the Federal Aviation Administration said in a statement.

Storms had been reported in the area. Trump carried an umbrella as he boarded Air Force One.

In an audio recording posted to air traffic website liveatc.net, someone in the cockpit can be heard reporting "moderate turbulence" and saying the crew was "managing the ice."

The person said the plane was "picking up precipitation" and needed "to climb" shortly before saying: "Mayday, mayday, we're descending 10,000 feet per minute."

Seconds later, the person said they were "declaring an emergency" as the plane descended 20,000 feet per minute.

"We're going to crash into the ground," the person said, shortly before communications stopped.

US President Donald Trump wrapped up his campaign in Iowa and Kansas with a final push to rally support, as Democrats seek to regain control of Congress.
US President Donald Trump round up his midterm campaign efforts on Sunday, holding rallies in Iowa and Kansas, while divisions emerged among Democrats in Michigan, threatening to undermine their effort to retake the Senate.

The president was in Salina, Kansas, and Dubuque, Iowa, where Republicans are facing strong competition from Democratic candidates in both states.

“We're going to elect ‘America first’ Republicans up and down the line,” he said near the start of a speech that lasted two hours.

Trump later travelled to Kansas, where Air Force One flew over an enthusiastic crowd before landing at Salina airport for an outdoor rally.

Once again, the president delivered a lengthy speech, lasting nearly 90 minutes, during which hundreds of attendees left before he had finished speaking.

“This is our last stop. We’re closing it down tonight," Trump said, who has been rallying across the country since the start of October. The president has held more than two dozen rallies in 19 states.

With more than 54 million votes already cast, Tuesday's elections are considered a referendum of Trump's second term in the White House.

Trump's approval rating has fallen to a low point in his second term, reflecting growing public discontent over his domestic decisions, sweeping tariffs and his decision to launch the war in Iran.

A shift in strategy is evident. In 2024, the Republican party largely focused on flipping states that had voted for Democrat Joe Biden in 2020. This year, however, Trump has concentrated much of his campaign efforts on states where he has dominated before.

On the ballot are all 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 seats in the Senate. Republicans currently hold a narrow 219-214 majority in the House and a 53-47 majority in the Senate.

Polls suggest Democrats are well positioned to regain control in the House of Representatives, while also have a viable chance at securing majority in the Senate.

Winning the House would put Democrats in charge of the federal budget, would give them the ability to block legislation and would give them greater authority to investigate the Trump administration.

Meanwhile in Michigan, Democratic congresswoman Haley Stevens broke with her party by publicly endorsing Republican candidate Mike Rogers rather than supporting her party's nominee.

Stevens lost the Democratic Senate primary to progressive candidate Abdul El-Sayed and announced she would be backing Rogers in the Senate race.

"This is a very close election, we all know that, but Abdul made very clear that I was not welcome in his campaign," she told reporters when announcing her endorsement.

Trump praised Stevens for endorsing Rogers and welcomed her to move to the Republican Party. “I don’t know Haley, I’ve never met Haley, but Haley said what few people are willing to say,” he said.

At his rallies, the US president also continued his attacks on El-Sayed, using Islamophobic rhetoric and branding him a "jihadist".

"That's another important election for us. We don't want to have a jihadist, we don't want to have a... can you imagine, we'll have to put him through metal detectors in order to seat," Trump said.

El-Sayed fired back that if he is to go through a metal detector, Trump should sit for a lie detector.

"It's simple. I've got nothing to hide, you've got so much to hide," he added, raising questions about the Jeffrey Epstein controversy and disputed claims surrounding the 2020 presidential election.

El-Sayed, whose national profile has been growing, has been a vocal critic of the US alliance with Israel and has called for an end to US arms sales to the country.

Mercedes driver Kimi Antonelli made history and become the youngest Formula 1 world champion. The 20-year-old driver finished third in the Mexico City Grand Prix race behind Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton.
Kimi Antonelli became Formula One's youngest world champion as he drove his Mercedes to a 16th podium finish at the Mexico Grand Prix on Sunday.

The 20-year-old Italian driver finished in third place behind Red Bull's Max Verstappen and Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton.

“I’m still trying to realise and understand what just happened, when you work so hard and then you finally achieve it is truly special,” Antonelli said.

The Italian driver was joined by his parents and younger sister. "All the emotions come, to be able to witness this moment with my family is a moment I will always remember".

Antonelli is the first Italian to win the world champion title since Alberto Ascari in 1953. At just 20 years old, he became the youngest-ever world champion, breaking Sebastian Vettel's previous record, set when he won the title aged 23 in 2010.

"It’s been an incredible season, and to share this podium with Max and Lewis is truly incredible. And looking forward to some more battles in the future," Antonelli said.

The Mercedes driver secured the title despite a difficult start to the race weekend, as Antonelli fell ill ahead of the race weekend and was forced to miss Thursday’s media day.

Meanwhile, four-time world champion Max Verstappen claimed his sixth victory at the Mexico City Grand Prix.

“The ‘Max-ico’ Grand Prix will be a great name to call it,” Verstappen said. “To be honest I love the city and the fans, but I have no idea why I have won it six years, it just happened. It always comes together for me here".

Lewis Hamilton, who had secured pole position on Saturday, lost his position while battling for the lead with his teammate Charles Leclerc on the fourth lap.

"I think we had the pace to win today, but it wasn't meant to be and we'll take it offline and figure out what we can do in the background," said a visibly irritated Hamilton.

Leclerc finished fourth while McLaren's outgoing world champion Lando Norris came in fifth position. Antonelli's teammate, George Russell, finished sixth.

After this weekend's race, F1 will travel to Brazil before heading to Las Vegas. The final races of the season are scheduled to take place in Qatar and Abu Dhabi, although it remains unclear whether both will go ahead as planned amid ongoing regional instability.
