Updated: 09/09/2026 - 11:41 GMT+2
Women’s rights activists gather outside France’s Justice Ministry as lawmakers begin examining a proposed law on sexual violence.
… More
Several hundred women’s rights activists and supporters gathered outside France’s Justice Ministry in Paris as lawmakers began examining a proposed comprehensive law on sexual violence.

Protesters chanted, held purple banners and called on authorities to ensure the legislation is adopted and backed by sufficient funding and resources.

Placards at the demonstration included messages calling for greater protection for children and an end to sexual violence.

The rally also drew political figures, including presidential candidates Marine Tondelier and Raphaël Glucksmann.

Organisers say the proposed law should cover prevention, victim support, justice and follow-up for both victims and perpetrators.

Updated: 09/09/2026 - 11:30 GMT+2
Decorated cows descend from Alpine pastures into Gstaad for Switzerland's Züglete festival, drawing crowds, music and visitors from around the world.
… More
Decorated cows made their way from the Alpine pastures into Gstaad on Saturday for the annual Züglete festival in Switzerland. Families dressed their animals with floral crowns before leading them down the mountain and through the busy town centre.

The event marks the traditional move from the summer pastures to their winter farms. Crowds gathered along Gstaad's promenade to watch several herds pass, while cowbells and alphorns added to the day's celebrations.

The festival also drew visitors from abroad. Two tourists from Melbourne said they had built their European holiday around seeing the Züglete after discovering the event online. The cows will continue towards their winter homes, with the journey reversed in spring when they return to the Alps.

US CENTCOM said crews were warned to abandon ship before its strikes after the IRGC fired missiles at a US Navy warship twice in two days. Tehran launched missile strikes on US targets in Jordan and warned tankers near Kuwait and Bahrain to evacuate.
US forces destroyed five Iranian oil tankers on Tuesday after the Revolutionary Guards fired ballistic missiles at a US Navy warship twice in two days, as Iran launched missile strikes on US targets in Jordan.

US Central Command (CENTCOM) said the crews of all five vessels — the Kivik, Charminar, Horizon 1, Riesco and Derya — were warned to abandon ship before the strikes.

Iranian state-affiliated news agency confirmed one oil tanker was struck near Jask and another tanker four miles off Kharg Island. Iran's state broadcaster has reported that a commercial vessel was also attacked in the coastal waters of Jask county.

According to Tasnim, citing local sources, the attack on the tanker near Kharg caused no casualties.

Iran launched missiles at US targets in Jordan following the tanker strikes, according to Iranian state-run television.

"Given the malign actions of the terrorist US military in targeting several oil tankers of the Islamic Republic of Iran, we warn all tanker crews in the area of the ports of Kuwait and Bahrain, which host these terrorists and are partners in their aggression, to leave their vessels immediately, whether at anchorage or at the quays, as they will be targeted," IRGC said.

"The US military has ordered Iranian oil tankers to evacuate so that it can strike them; therefore I declare that any attack on Iranian oil tankers will result in US bases in the region being targeted by the armed forces of the Islamic Republic of Iran," Major General Abdollahi said in a statement.

Jordan's military said its air defence systems intercepted 18 ballistic missiles launched toward its territory and that two others landed in uninhabited areas. No casualties were reported.

Jordan hosts several US military installations and has been a repeated target of Iranian strikes since the war began on 28 February.

Iran also said it had captured a US unmanned underwater vessel at the entrance to the Strait of Hormuz before dawn, broadcasting images of what it described as the captured drone on state television. Iran had claimed to have struck a US Navy sea drone on Sunday.

The Trump administration separately announced new sanctions on more than two dozen Iranian commercial and private airlines and foreign cargo providers, in a further effort to isolate Tehran from its remaining trading partners.

The exchange marks one of the sharpest escalations since the framework deal the two sides signed in June broke down the following month.

Both sides have been attempting to assert control over the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway through which roughly one-fifth of the world's traded oil passed before the war began.

The US has been enforcing a naval blockade of Iranian ports while Tehran has sought to impose its own transit regime on commercial shipping through the strait.

US Republicans open an extraordinary midterm convention in Dallas later on Wednesday betting that putting Donald Trump at the centre of their campaign can rescue a party facing grim election forecasts, despite the president's deeply negative approval ratings.

The two-day gathering, dubbed "Trumpapalooza," is designed to persuade the Republican leader's supporters that he is effectively on the ballot on 3 November, when Republicans risk losing the House of Representatives and face a fierce fight to retain the Senate.

"So what I really want you to do is pretend, please, that I'm on the ballot," Trump said recently at a rally in South Carolina. "Just come and vote. It's so important."

Decision Desk HQ projects Democrats to win the House 230-205 and the Senate to finish 51-49 in their favour, revising its 50-50 prediction as the outlook continues to worsen for Republicans.

Trump, whose approval rating stood at just 33% in recent polling, will speak on both convention nights at the American Airlines Centre.

His challenge is to mobilise voters personally loyal to him without further alienating independents already souring on his presidency amid inflation woes, gasoline prices topping $4 (€3.40) a gallon and the continuing war with Iran.

"Many Trump supporters only vote when he's on the ballot," Matthew Green, a politics professor at the Catholic University of America, told the AFP news agency.

"They're not diehard Republican voters; they're diehard Trump voters. So, in that respect, it makes some sense to feature Trump in the hopes of getting those folks to turn out in November."

But Green was sceptical that asking those supporters to imagine Trump on their ballots could overcome his broader weakness.

"When you're in the low- to mid-30s, that's traditionally associated with a very bad midterm for your party," he said of the 80-year-old's approval ratings.

The gathering is the first Republican national convention held specifically for a midterm election, although Democrats experimented with similar meetings in the 1970s and 1980s.

Instead, organisers have constructed what amounts to a two-night television rally touting Trump's record and Republican candidates.

More than 100 House members and candidates are expected, with over 50 speakers or presentations scheduled each night.

Trump delivers Wednesday's keynote and returns for Thursday's finale after Vice President JD Vance speaks. Cabinet members, congressional leaders and candidates in closely watched House and Senate races are also on the programme.

Republicans plan to highlight tax cuts, immigration enforcement, manufacturing and prescription drug prices, while depicting Democrats as dangerously far to the left.

Some Republicans, however, are privately hoping Trump sticks to that script rather than the personal grievances and off-message subjects that regularly dominate his appearances.

Thai authorities said on Wednesday they are investigating a Bangkok clinic where a Nigerian-born British businessman and influencer underwent a penis enlargement procedure that led to his death.

A coroner in London ruled last month that the 6 March death of 43-year-old Igho Ubiribo in the Thai capital was caused by the hyaluronic acid injection he received hours earlier.

The case has reignited debate about medical tourism, with Thailand a leading destination for visitors from abroad seeking healthcare, including cosmetic procedures.

Ubiribo, reportedly known as "Tiny" and "Ego", had 185,000 followers on Instagram, regularly posting pictures with his wife Danielle enjoying a luxury lifestyle.

Thailand's health ministry ordered an investigation into his death and officials visited the central Bangkok clinic where Ubiribo was treated on Monday.

Authorities are looking into whether it holds a valid licence and followed health and safety protocols, a health ministry official told the AFP news agency.

He was having a massage after receiving the filler when he complained of chest pains and collapsed, according to an August report from the UK coroner, which formally determines cause of death.

He was taken by ambulance to hospital, suspected of having a pulmonary embolism, but lost consciousness before he could have a CT scan.

Medics tried to resuscitate him for over 100 minutes but he was declared dead around 1:00 am.

"Post-mortem examination and toxicology determined the cause of death to be due to the hyaluronic acid injection," the report said.

Senior Bangkok police official Utit Soodjai told AFP his officers were also investigating.

"When it comes to medical procedures, we advocate for the highest level of caution," Thai health minister Pattana Promphat told reporters.

The probe was intended "to ensure fairness for everyone and to reassure both local and international patients that they are receiving safe, high-standard and valuable care in Thailand", he added in a statement.

According to the health ministry, the clinic's managers said Ubiribo had been a regular patient since 2024 and had undergone five penile enlargement injections.

The first four procedures took place in 2024 and 2025, while on his fifth visit on 5 March he received 40 millilitres of filler.

The doctor had recommended splitting the procedure into two sessions, but Ubiribo requested that the entire amount be injected in one session, clinic management told investigators.
