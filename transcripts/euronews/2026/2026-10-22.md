Gulf nations brace for possible return to all-out war between US and Iran

The volatility around the Israeli and US midterm elections is fueling fears of renewed US-Israeli strikes on Iran in a short and intensive military campaign to push Tehran into concessions, with Iran vowing a devastating response against the Gulf nations.
The Gulf countries and the wider Middle East are bracing for a possible resumption of hostilities in the Iran war amid multiple simultaneous warnings and media reports of increasing intensity and detail, coupled with a massive US military build-up in the Gulf and Israeli and Iranian armies placed on high alert, raising fears of an imminent escalation with devastating consequences for the region.

Against the backdrop of growing speculation over the last weeks that the US and Israel may launch a new attack against Iran before or after the mid-term elections in the US and next week’s elections in Israel, a new US Department of State travel alert issued on Wednesday evening was relayed by news agencies and wider media as an urgent signal as to the imminence of war restarting, with the Gulf nations bearing the brunt of a potentially larger conflict.

The US Department of State announced that it ordered non-essential US government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia as “the hostilities have the potential to escalate rapidly beyond that immediate area.”

Several news outlets reported that families of embassy staff in the United Arab Emirates, Jordan, Qatar, and Bahrain were also ordered to leave, quoting multiple diplomatic sources.

“The US Department of State issued an ordered departure for non-emergency U.S. government employees and their family members in Saudi Arabia only,” as a result of the growing Houthi attacks, Pigott said in a post on X.

The US Saudi warning follows a Middle East-wide red alert issued by Washington on Monday to US citizens in 12 countries across the Middle East, warning their citizens in Israel, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Lebanon, Jordan, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, the UAE, Yemen and Iran, citing risks of potential attacks on US interests.

With days before Israel’s close elections in which prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu could lose power, and ahead of next month’s US midterm elections in which war-linked high prices have sparked voter anger, there are growing concerns and speculations that US-Israeli attacks on Iran could resume.

The US has been rebuilding a massive strike force in the Gulf by sending more military assets to the region, including a third aircraft carrier and thousands of troops, to join the largest US naval and air force in the Middle East since the invasion of Iraq in 2003.

Iran has been warning for weeks that it has reorganised its defence capabilities and that any attack would be matched with what they called a devastating response against US interests and civilian infrastructure across the region, which would be far worse than their previous attacks on the Gulf nations and longer in time too.

Iranian officials and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) are preparing for war, having declared their “100% certainty” that the US and Israel will attack them again.

According to reports in Iran, Tehran activated its so-called Mosaic Defence doctrine by which Iran would operate in a decentralised way, by giving local commanders a list of targets to keep fighting even if the central command is destroyed.

In parallel with the US Department of State alert on Wednesday, the New York Times and other media reported that the US and Israel have developed specific plans for new strikes on Iran after the US midterm elections.

US President Donald Trump declared recently that any considerations for a new attack on Iran would be made after the US midterm elections.

According to the New York Times report, which quoted US officials, targets would likely include Iran’s energy infrastructure, nuclear facilities, and arsenals of missiles and drones, with the objective of pushing Iran into a deal to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

US Secretary of Defence Pete Hegseth met Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu earlier this week on an unannounced trip in Israel to discuss “the threats in the region.”

Speaking at an IDF officer graduation ceremony on Wednesday night, Netanyahu said that the Tehran regime has to be finally overthrown.

The Israeli premier justified this by stating that Iran's Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei "decided to follow in his father's footsteps (to attempt) once again to develop the bombs of death."’

The younger Khamenei, who has been selected as the new supreme leader following his father Ali Khamenei's killing in the US-Israeli strikes on Tehran in the opening salvo of the war on 28 February, is yet to be seen in public amid continued reports questioning his health condition.

Earlier this week, US media reported that dozens of Israeli fighter jets and refuelling aircraft reportedly flew towards the Iranian border before turning back, calling the sorties a military exercise and a "show of force," according to the Times of Israel newspaper.

According to media reports, US officials have questioned Israel's fighter jet deployment, believing the purpose of the exercise was to test Iranian air defence units and provoke them to open fire.

In parallel, Israeli media reported that Israeli security officials warned the government that an attack on Iran could be perceived as political interference in the election next week.

Updated: 22/10/2026 - 13:11 GMT+2
At least nine people were killed in Russian attacks across Ukraine over the past 24 hours, according to Ukrainian authorities, as overnight missile and drone strikes sparked fires in Kyiv and the Kharkiv region.
… More
In the Kharkiv region, a separate wave of Russian attacks killed one person and injured 15 others, including three children, according to regional governor Oleh Syniehubov. In the city of Kharkiv, drones damaged houses, a shop, cars and an office building, while a strike on a lorry park set four lorries, a trailer and a car on fire.

In the capital, one person was killed and 22 others injured, including four children, as ballistic missiles and drones struck six districts. Fires broke out in residential and non-residential buildings, while homes, vehicles and infrastructure were damaged. In the Dniprovskyi district, one person was killed and six others, including a child, were injured.

Night-time footage shows firefighters tackling blazes from elevated platforms, while images taken after the strikes reveal damaged buildings and burnt-out vehicles. Kyiv’s mayor said debris hit homes and a car service station, with fears that people could be trapped under rubble.

Beyond Kyiv and Kharkiv, where one person was killed in each city, Russian attacks also claimed lives in Sumy, Zaporizhzhia and Donetsk, bringing the nationwide death toll to at least nine, according to Ukrainian authorities.

The attacks came hours after Moscow and Kyiv agreed in principle to resume US-brokered peace talks. Rescue teams worked through the morning to extinguish fires and assess the damage.

Updated: 22/10/2026 - 13:00 GMT+2
Supermarket shelves are running empty in parts of southern Italy as truck drivers protest against soaring fuel prices, disrupting deliveries of food and other essential goods.

In Naples, trucks parked near the port on Monday as protesters blocked the loading and unloading of goods at supermarket distribution centres, bringing deliveries to a halt, particularly those of fresh food.

Shortages of fresh pasta, packaged cheeses, meat and fruit and vegetables have been reported in Sicily, with panic buying by shoppers adding to the pressure on supplies. Partially empty shelves have also been seen in Calabria and Puglia.

The disruption comes as transport operators demand support to cope with rising diesel prices, which are driving up the cost of their business.

Updated: 22/10/2026 - 15:29 GMT+2
Two men plunged into raging floodwaters in Queensland, Australia, to save a driver from a sinking four-wheel-drive on Thursday. The dramatic rescue ended in an emotional embrace.
… More
Australian Broadcasting Corporation reporter Will Murray was covering the floods when a vehicle stalled after entering deep water on Buchanan Road. As it began to sink, Murray handed his phone to camera operator Liz Pickering, called emergency services and waded into the floodwater. Another bystander quickly joined him.

The pair first tried to open the nearest door, then climbed onto the vehicle and considered smashing the rear window. Murray eventually forced open the rear passenger-side door, allowing the driver to escape before the ute disappeared beneath the water.

Footage captured the dramatic rescue, followed by an emotional moment as the relieved driver embraced one of the men who helped save him. ABC camera operator Liz Pickering said the two men “undoubtedly saved that man’s life”.

No serious injuries were reported. About 170 millimetres of rain had fallen in Morayfield, where severe flooding forced road closures and left the shopping centre largely inaccessible. Queensland authorities warned motorists not to drive or swim through floodwaters, warning that vehicles can quickly become trapped or swept away.

Refusing to cross, unable to escape: Seafarers recount Hormuz dangers to Euronews

Euronews spoke to a crew member of a cargo vessel anchored off Pakistan for weeks and an Iranian maritime manager about dangers in the vital waterway — where traffic has fallen 90% since the war began and at least 95 seafarers in commercial roles have died.
The Al Ruwais, a 314-metre liquefied natural gas carrier flying a Bahamian flag, has been sitting adrift off the coast of Pakistan for weeks.

Fearing for its safety, its 34-member crew does not want to test the waters and attempt to cross the Strait of Hormuz, despite demands from the ship's owners.

"What strikes me as strange is that this company is willing to risk the ship just to pass through the Strait of Hormuz," an electrician aboard the UAE-owned vessel told Euronews.

"If it were up to the company, we'd go through, but for that to happen, the entire crew would have to agree. And they don't. I am waiting to leave by boat for Pakistan, where I will board another vessel."

Under maritime regulations, the right to refuse transit into a designated warlike area is individual, meaning that one sailor objecting is enough to stop a vessel from moving into a danger zone.

On ships whose crews do agree to cross, companies have offered captains up to $100,000 (€89,000) per month plus $50,000 (€44,500) risk bonuses, while ordinary crew wages have risen four to six times.

"On other ships, they give as much as $10,000 (€8,900), but not everyone gets the same amount, and it depends a lot on the position and base salary," the sailor aboard Al Ruwais told Euronews.

"It's only fair that everyone gets the same amount, otherwise, it would mean that the captain's life is worth more than that of the deckhand."

A vital waterway for the world's energy trade, the Straight of Hormuz has been under two concurrent naval blockades since the outbreak of the Iran war following US-Israeli strikes in late February.

The southern corridor near Oman is patrolled by US forces, while the northern route passes through Iranian-controlled waters. The IRGC Navy has stated that vessels transiting routes it considers unauthorised face what it described as "punitive action".

Traffic through the strait has collapsed as a result. More than 125 vessels passed daily before the war. That figure has fallen to fewer than 10 to 12, a drop of more than 90%, according to ship-tracking firm Kpler.

Drone attacks, sea mines and strikes from unknown projectiles have driven the decline, with UK Maritime Trade Operations recording multiple incidents involving fires, sinkings and power failures aboard tankers and LNG carriers.

Ships that do attempt the crossing have taken to turning off satellite AIS transponders, extinguishing running lights at night and shutting down radio equipment to avoid detection.

The dangers are somewhat different for Iranian sailors. Hamed, a 50-year-old former captain who now runs a maritime management company in Iran, told Euronews that since March of last year the war has resulted in the deaths of some 95 seafarers in commercial, fishing, passenger, tugboat and offshore platform support roles.

Military vessels have suffered heavy damage and ships undergoing repairs in Bandar Abbas have been struck, he said, although precise casualty figures have not been officially released.

"Nearly 60% of our ships are sanctioned and forbidden from calling at foreign ports such as India, Pakistan, the UAE, Oman, Kuwait, and Iraq," Hamed said. "Even Pakistan's maritime borders, where Iranian vessels used to travel, have now been closed to us."

Around 40% of southern Iranian seafarers have refused sea assignments, he said, with many shifting to the Caspian Sea to handle increased grain imports from the north. Wages in the south have been raised by 30 to 100%.

Sailors now earn between 50 and 80 million tomans (€165 to €265) per month, and captains between $1,200 and $4,000 (€1,100 to €3,700).

US forces have boarded Iranian commercial vessels roughly 15 times since the war began, Hamed said, arriving by helicopter and speedboat to inspect cargo for explosives, with no guarantees of their safety.

“The US has provided no safe-passage guarantees for Iranian commercial ships," Hamed said. "Every week we witness aerial or naval drone attacks on Iranian vessels."

He estimated that roughly 35,000 Iranian seafarers across approximately 2,000 vessels continue operating in southern waters to deliver wheat, corn and medicine to ordinary Iranians.

If the situation deteriorates further, he warned, authorities could be forced to declare a complete shutdown and evacuate coastal facilities.

Government agencies and companies around the world shut down websites Wednesday after two critical flaws came to light involving PHP, a programming language that is the basis for a large share of the internet.
Critical security flaws have been discovered in PHP, a widely used programming language that helps power around 70% of the world’s websites.

Developers released emergency fixes on Wednesday, while cybersecurity authorities urged organisations to update affected systems immediately or take them offline until they could be secured.

The warning prompted governments, companies and major online services in several countries to temporarily shut down websites and other systems as a precaution.

The vulnerabilities were uncovered with the help of artificial intelligence. Security firm Calif identified one of the flaws in August during an OpenAI-funded audit of PHP, while Google-owned cybersecurity company Wiz independently discovered and reported both vulnerabilities. OpenAI said its GPT-5.6 Sol model was used in the Calif research.

The PHP security team rated both vulnerabilities 9.2 out of 10 for severity. Details were meant to remain confidential while developers prepared fixes, but information about the flaws emerged publicly before the planned release, intensifying the race to secure vulnerable systems.

Security researchers initially reported little evidence of widespread exploitation before the vulnerabilities became public. However, attempted attacks have since been detected, increasing concern that unpatched systems could be targeted.

The Wikimedia Foundation, which operates Wikipedia, said it deliberately took a number of supporting services offline while security updates were installed and warned users that some disruption could occur.

Amazon Threat Intelligence also detected attempts to exploit the vulnerabilities, including activity before their public disclosure. Security researchers have since reported attempts to target vulnerable systems in the wild.

France’s Interior Ministry temporarily took prefecture websites offline while updates were applied, while Germany’s federal IT provider ITZBund shut down several government websites. German officials said the move was precautionary and that no security incidents had been identified.

Government and public-service websites were also taken offline in countries including the Netherlands and Finland, as authorities worked to secure their systems.

In the UK, the National Cyber Security Centre urged organisations running PHP to identify vulnerable systems, monitor them for suspicious activity and install security updates as soon as possible.

In Ukraine, authorities say those killed include a man in Sumy, a woman in Kharkiv, three people in Zaporizhzhia and two in Donetsk.

In the capital, Kyiv, the mayor Vitali Klitschko posted on social media that strikes had wounded 15 people in the capital, eight of whom had been taken to hospital.

In another post that strikes had damaged metro infrastructure at the Pivdenny Bridge, which officials said had restricted traffic between parts of the city.

Ukraine's Airforce had posted warnings on Telegram throughout the early hours of "ballistic missiles towards Kyiv" and reports of fighter jets, and instructed residents to stay in shelters.

On Thursday morning, the airforce said it had shot down an 181 Russian UAVs overnight, adding there were still strike drones in the air.

Prime Minister Mark Carney said the Trump tariffs make both US and Canadian industries weaker and reduce choice for households in both countries.
Canada has announced a $6.2 billion (€5.7 billion) package to protect its steel and auto industries from US tariffs, as Prime Minister Mark Carney warned that Washington has permanently altered a trade relationship built over decades.

Carney and Ontario Premier Doug Ford jointly unveiled the plan on Wednesday, directing $3.7 billion (€3.4 billion) to steelmakers and $1.8 billion (€1.65 billion) to automakers over five years, with $1.1 billion (€1 billion) of the auto funding coming from the federal government in Ottawa.

The announcement came the same day US-owned Cleveland-Cliffs confirmed layoffs at its Hamilton, Ontario, plant, underlining the human cost of the trade dispute.

“US tariffs have upended a trade bargain that’s existed for decades,” Carney said. “They’ve raised costs for American families while putting Ontario workers and businesses at a disadvantage.”

The damage to the auto sector is already visible in the trade figures, as Canadian auto exports to the US have fallen more than 10 percent, while US exports to Canada have dropped more than 20 percent, a decline Carney said hurts both sides of the border.

“Tariffs make both our industries weaker and reduce choice for our families,” he stressed.

Carney confirmed he spoke at length with President Trump on Wednesday, covering trade, Iran and the Middle East.

He signalled no breakthrough but said Canada remains ready to negotiate. Talks collapsed in August after Trump imposed 50% tariffs on around $20 billion (€18.4 billion) in Canadian goods, prompting Canadian retaliation.

For his part, Ford was direct about what the dispute has done to bilateral ties. “No matter what happens in the midterms, no matter who sits in the White House, we’re not going back to the way things were,” he said.

Carney has set a target of doubling Canadian exports to non-US markets within a decade, acknowledging that an integrated North American auto industry may no longer be Washington’s priority.

Meanwhile, the day took a sharper diplomatic turn when the US Embassy issued a statement accusing Canadian politicians of seeking to influence next month’s American midterm elections.

The embassy named Industry Minister Mélanie Joly for discussing retaliatory tariffs targeting Republican states and Ford for running a US television ad featuring former President Ronald Reagan criticising tariffs, the ad that prompted Trump to walk away from trade talks in October 2025.

“The implicit assumption that Canada could significantly alter the outcome of the midterms is absurd,” said Daniel Béland, political science professor at McGill University.
