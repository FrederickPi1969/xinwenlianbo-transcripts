Updated: 15/10/2026 - 16:12 GMT+2
Visitors to Frieze London in the UK are becoming part of the artworks themselves, climbing a giant interactive spider, entering virtual worlds and taking part in installations that turn spectators into participants.
… More
At the fair, Benjamin Lallier's Mother Needham invites visitors to toss coins and make a wish, while Yuli Serfaty's Who's A Good Girl explores the relationship between gender and technology. Greek artist Theo Triantafyllidis presents Feral Metaverse (Spider), a giant climbable sculpture that doubles as a multiplayer game. Up to six visitors can enter a shared virtual world, where they must work together to create shapes such as human towers and wheels.

But behind the playful installations, Frieze remains a major commercial event. Art critic Tabish Khan says a struggling market is prompting galleries to favour more conventional displays, with paintings taking priority over elaborate installations.

Other highlights include Swiss artist Miriam Cahn's self-portraits exploring ageing, presented by Galerie Jocelyn Wolff, and a selection of contemporary Indian art from the Fitzwilliam Museum. Together, the works reflect a fair balancing artistic experimentation with the pressures of a challenging art market.

A school roof collapsed in Peshawar, northwestern Pakistan, on Thursday 15 October, killing at least 32 pupils and a teacher. Officials said the roof was structurally weak and had been overloaded by construction work. The school’s owner was arrested and an investigation launched.
… More
Rescue teams rushed to the scene, where classrooms were left strewn with rubble, school bags, books and children's shoes. Injured pupils were taken by ambulance to Lady Reading Hospital, where medical staff treated survivors as distressed relatives gathered outside. Images from the school show abandoned belongings scattered across the floors, offering a stark glimpse of the devastation left behind by the collapse.

Peshawar’s police chief said the roof had been weakened by thebuilding’s poor structural condition and the weight of construction work. Heavy rain may also have played a role, but the exact cause remains under investigation.

Questions have emerged over whether the tragedy could have been prevented. A father who lost two children told AFP that pupils had reportedly warned the school administration earlier that morning that the building was showing signs of collapse. His account raises concerns about whether warning signs were overlooked before the roof gave way.

At the hospital in Peshawar, ambulances continued to bring in casualties as families waited for news of their loved ones. The circumstances surrounding the collapse and the reported warnings have yet to be independently established.

Azerbaijan engineers rare summit with in-conflict Middle East and Asian leaders

Israel and Iran have met for a rare “confidence-building measures” summit in Baku, with Azerbaijan saying it serves as “a connecting hub in the Eurasian space.”
For the first time since the outbreak of the Iran war, top delegations from Middle Eastern and Asian countries engaged in ongoing political and military conflicts met in Baku on Thursday for the Conference on Interaction and Confidence-Building Measures in Asia (CICA), hosted by Azerbaijan.

Azerbaijan’s President Ilham Aliyev has brought together Israel's President Isaac Herzog, Iran’s deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi, Turkey’s Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, Russia’s Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov, Qatar’s Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al-Thani, UAE Minister of State for Foreign Affairs Khalifa bin Shaheen Al Marar and a Palestinian delegation led by Foreign Minister Varsen Aghabekian Shahin together with regional and Asian leaders, including China's Foreign Minister Wang Yi, for the seventh edition of the CICA summit.

In his opening speech, Aliyev declared that “geographically situated at the intersection of Asia and Europe, Azerbaijan plays an increasingly important role as a connecting hub in the Eurasian space.”

“Our country’s cooperation with many states in bilateral and multilateral formats across the political, economic, transport, energy and humanitarian fields, in turn, strengthens the security system in our region,” Azerbaijan’s president said, explaining the importance of the agreement of the countries present to participate in the summit alongside each other during tumultuous times.

The CICA is defined as “a forum for promoting peace, security and cooperation through dialogue and mutual trust,” as well as “confidence-building measures and opportunities to strengthen cooperation across Asia,” while the agenda focuses on “regional security, transport links, digital cooperation and sustainable growth.”

As the summit kicked off on Thursday, the presidents of Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan joined heads of state and governments from China, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam, India and Sri Lanka.

In a surprising move, Israel’s President Isaac Herzog travelled to Baku on Wednesday for the summit and met with Azerbaijan’s leader, whom he called a friend.

In multiple social media posts, Herzog called Azerbaijan “an important partner of Israel in our region,” and said the visit “aims to further strengthen the strong Israel-Azerbaijan ties in the areas of security, energy, and trade." His office said he would also “connect with the local Jewish community.”

In a post about the talks, Herzog hailed the “excellent” relations and said that under Aliyev’s leadership “Azerbaijan is a country that has earned deep respect, reliability, and influence in our region.”

“The excellent relations between Israel and Azerbaijan are based on mutual respect, shared interests, and the deep historical ties that exist between our peoples. In our meeting today, we discussed ways to further deepen our strategic relations in many areas for the prosperity of both our countries," Herzog said in his post on X.

"We also discussed the recent events in the region, as well as our shared commitment to ensuring stability and security in our region,” he added.

Azerbaijan’s presidency posted a video of the meeting on social media, together with a brief statement that the two leaders held talks.

Moreover, Israel’s president appeared to have travelled to Baku onboard an Azerbaijan Airlines commercial flight, as shown by photos and videos of his arrival, reportedly to be able to cross through Turkish airspace.

In another development, Israel’s president held what he called “an excellent meeting” with Bahrain’s foreign minister Dr Abdul Latif bin Rashid Al Zayani.

“We discussed the friendship and ties between Israel and Bahrain, and the importance of the Abraham Accords, led by the United States, in strengthening security, stability, and prosperity in the Middle East,” Herzog said in a post on X.

“Bahrain is an important country in the Middle East, and I conveyed my warmest wishes to King Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa and the Bahraini people," he added.

Since the beginning of the Iran war in late February, Bahrain has been heavily bombed by Iran, and the two countries have had icy relations for a long time.

Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) said on Wednesday it arrested the Swedish citizen in the Sebezh district of Pskov region, near the borders with Belarus and Latvia.

He is accused of entering Russia under cover of joining the Russian military while intending to carry out "sabotage and terrorist attacks" on military sites.

The FSB said it established that from February to June the detainee served as commander of a drone section in the 24th separate assault battalion of Ukraine's Aidar unit — a volunteer formation that emerged during Russia's initial invasion of Ukraine in 2014 which the Kremlin has designated a "terrorist organisation," a common domestic classification aimed at Ukrainian units.

He had allegedly admitted to serving in the unit. The FSB said it holds documents confirming his participation in combat against Russian forces, and that he faces charges of illegal border crossing, organising the activities of a terrorist organisation and serving as a mercenary. His name has not been disclosed.

Sweden's government has not commented. Moscow's claims could not be independently verified.

The second arrest — that of Yuki Imai, a journalist for Japan's Hokkaido Shimbun newspaper — took place on Sakhalin Island as he was preparing to leave the country, the FSB said.

He is accused of gathering information on Russia's defence capabilities at the direction of Japanese intelligence services.

Japan summoned Russia's ambassador in Tokyo Nikolai Nozdrev on Wednesday. Kimitake Nakamura, head of the foreign ministry's European affairs department, lodged a protest both over the arrest and Moscow's decision to announce it without first informing the Japanese government.

The Committee to Protect Journalists called for his immediate release, warning that journalists "must not be used as a bargaining chip in broader geopolitical disputes."

The organisation estimates that at least 30 journalists are currently held in Russia in connection with their work.

The arrests followed Medvedev's post on Telegram on 9 October calling for Russia to increase its "exchange pool" of foreign nationals it uses to negotiate the release of its citizens deemed useful to the regime.

Writing after the arrest in Montenegro of Mikhail Zvinchuk, a Kremlin propagandist and founder of Rybar — a Telegram channel close to Russia's military and security services — Medvedev said Moscow might need to detain more Western nationals to secure the release of Russians held abroad.

"Now, quite likely, we will have to fill up the exchange pool with European and American idiots in order to pull our own people out," he wrote.

"There is no other way: if you live with wolves, you have to howl like a wolf." Zvinchuk has since been returned to Russia.

Russia has for years detained foreign nationals on charges including espionage and drug smuggling, a practice its critics describe as the taking of hostages for exchange.

The most recent large-scale swap took place in August 2024, when 26 people were freed in a multinational exchange: Russia released 16 detainees, and Western countries handed back 10.

Among those Russia released were Wall Street Journal journalist Evan Gershkovich, sentenced to 16 years in a high-security prison on espionage charges, and former US Marine Paul Whelan, convicted on the same charges and given the same sentence.

Germany obtained the release of Rico Krieger, who had been sentenced to death in Belarus, and Patrick Schabel, detained at Moscow's Sheremetyevo airport over cannabis-infused gummy sweets.

Russia received back, among others, Vadim Krasikov, who was serving a life sentence in Germany for the assassination of Georgian national Zelimkhan Khangoshvili in a Berlin park in 2019, Artem and Anna Dulcev, accused of spying in Slovenia under the guise of South American art dealers, and Pablo González aka Pavel Rubtsov, a Russian-born Spanish journalist who had been held in Poland on espionage charges since February 2022.

China and North Korea opened a new four-lane road bridge on Thursday, 15 October, ending a 12-year delay and creating a major new route for cross-border trade.
… More
The 3,026-metre, four-lane New Yalu River Bridge links the Chinese border city of Dandong with Sinuiju in North Korea. Hundreds of people gathered in Dandong on Thursday to watch as lorries began crossing in both directions, some decorated with Chinese and North Korean flags and flowers.

Completed in 2014, the bridge had stood unused for 12 years because connecting roads and customs facilities on the North Korean side were not finished. Its official opening marks a significant step in efforts to revive trade between the two countries after years of strained relations and pandemic-era border restrictions.

The crossing is expected to ease freight transport and reduce reliance on the ageing Sino-Korean Friendship Bridge nearby, which has carried much of the cross-border traffic since 1943. Lim Eul-chul, a North Korea expert at Kyungnam University, estimated that the new crossing could triple or quadruple cargo traffic, particularly shipments of heavy machinery and construction materials.

China remains North Korea's largest trading partner and a crucial economic ally. Residents in Dandong hope the new link will bring more business and improve living standards across the border, although North Korea has yet to resume issuing tourist visas to Chinese visitors.

The Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality said some areas had the highest amount of rain since 1985.
Istanbul was battling to recover from flooding caused by what officials said on Thursday was the heaviest rainfall in the city since records began more than 80 years ago, as the downpour continued.

The rain began lashing Turkey's largest city in earnest on Wednesday morning and intensified throughout the day, causing chaos and flash flooding, with footage showing cars being dragged away by the fast-flowing waters.

"We are talking here about the highest level of precipitation in Istanbul since records began in 1942," Istanbul governor Davut Gul said.

Two people died in the floods, one of them a 14-year-old boy who was electrocuted when he stepped into a puddle in the Balat area. He was rushed to hospital but medical personnel were unable to save him, officials said.

On Wednesday evening, the governor said schools would be shut across Istanbul until Monday, urging people to stay home "unless absolutely necessary" and advising those living in basement properties "to spend the night on safer, higher floors".

He advised residents to stay away from "stream beds, underpasses and other areas at risk of flooding".

Dramatic footage posted online and collated by DHA news agency showed a line of parked cars in the northern Sariyer district being pushed into the torrent of muddy water, where they were swept away.

Another showed people being hauled from the raging waters to safety through a first-floor window, while elsewhere two people with umbrellas were seen stepping into the shovel of a large mechanical digger, which took them away.

Forty-three flights bound for Istanbul Airport on the Black Sea had to be diverted, as did three flights heading for the city's Sabiha Gökçen airport on the Asian side, which also temporarily suspended take-offs due to strong winds, the transport ministry said in a statement carried by state news agency Anadolu.

Turkey's AFAD disaster agency has set up a crisis desk to manage incidents caused by the heavy rainfall, which by nightfall Wednesday had risen to 1,277 flooding incidents, 15 landslides, 47 people stranded and two roofs blown off.

Roof and building collapses are common across Pakistan, mainly because of poor safety standards and shoddy construction materials in the South Asian nation of more than 240 million people.
At least 32 pupils and a teacher were killed on Thursday after a roof collapsed at a school in the northwest Pakistani city of Peshawar, a hospital official said.

Asim Khan from Peshawar's Lady Reading Hospital confirmed the toll to the AFP news agency, adding that over 30 people were injured, with six believed to be in a critical condition.

A spokesperson for the emergency rescue service in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province said the schoolchildren were crushed and trapped after the roof gave way.

"When the Rescue 1122 emergency response teams arrived at the scene, they found that one roof had collapsed on to another, causing the classroom below to be crushed and trapping children inside," spokesperson Bilal Ahmad Faizi said.

"There are concerns that more children may still be trapped beneath the rubble," he said, adding that 16 ambulances, two disaster response vehicles and more than 60 personnel were deployed to the site.

Images released by the emergency services showed rescue workers in high-vis vests scrambling to move debris, rubble and dirt.

Resident Sher Ahmed said he blamed "substandard construction materials" for the tragedy, and called for a probe.

"With profound grief and sorrow, I urge the authorities to conduct a thorough investigation and take strict action against anyone found responsible so that such tragedies can be prevented in the future," he said.

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif in a statement expressed "deep sorrow and grief over the loss of precious lives".

"The Prime Minister extends his condolences to the bereaved families and prays for them to have the strength and patience to bear this irreparable loss," the statement said.

"In this hour of grief, the entire nation shares the sorrow of the affected families," he said in a statement.

Roof and building collapses are common across Pakistan, mainly because of poor safety standards and shoddy construction materials in the South Asian nation of more than 240 million people.

In June this year more than a dozen children were killed in a roof collapse at a tutoring centre in the eastern city of Lahore.

In 2025, 27 people were killed and 10 injured when a five-storey building collapsed in the impoverished area of Lyari in the southern city of Karachi.

New four-lane bridge between China and North Korea opens after a decade of delay

A new four-lane road bridge linking China and North Korea finally opened on Thursday, 12 years late, enabling the Asian giant and its isolated nuclear-armed neighbour to substantially step up cross-border trade.

China is North Korea's largest trading partner and a vital diplomatic and political backer, although relations have not always been smooth with the ruling Kim dynasty.

Hundreds of people gathered on Thursday in the northern Chinese border town of Dandong, taking selfies in front of the 3,026-metre New Yalu River vehicle bridge before it opened.

Trucks bearing the nations' flags began moving in both directions late in the morning, with some of the North Korean vehicles decorated with flowers.

"It's great," one woman who came to watch told the AFP news agency in the town dotted with Chinese as well as Korean shops and restaurants, where residents were relieved the bridge had finally open.

"It's already been over 10 years. It's certainly going to be good for business," she said, without wishing to be named, accompanied by her friend and her dog.

"I hope the standard of living in North Korea can rise to the level we have here," Dandong local Yu Shuyan, who before the pandemic had visited North Korea, said.

Chinese Premier Li Qiang said that the bridge symbolised Beijing's friendship with Pyongyang and that relations were "entering a new stage and opening a new chapter".

"Thanks to the joint efforts of both sides, the successful opening of the bridge will not only strongly boost economic and trade cooperation between the two countries, but will also further facilitate people-to-people exchanges and tighten the bonds of affection between the two peoples," Li said by video link at the opening ceremony, China’s Xinhua news agency reported.

The structure was completed in 2014 but its opening was delayed due to a lack of connecting roads and customs facilities on the North Korean side, South Korea's Yonhap news agency reported.

North Korea also sealed its borders during the COVID-19 pandemic and train journeys between the East Asian neighbours only restarted in March after a six-year halt.

But the new structure will supersede the "extremely narrow" rail-and-road Sino-Korean Friendship Bridge downstream on the Yalu River, said Lim Eul-chul, a North Korea expert at Kyungnam University.

"Previously, transporting heavy cargo such as industrial machinery, raw materials, construction materials and energy-related supplies required extreme caution because of safety concerns surrounding the ageing bridge," Lim told AFP.

"The new crossing will make such shipments much easier and more efficient," he said, calling it a "groundbreaking development" that will substantially increase cargo traffic.

The new bridge's launch came about a month after North Korea and Russia opened a road bridge as part of their efforts to cement relations.

China has long been North Korea’s biggest trading partner and economic pipeline. But in recent years, North Korean leader Kim Jong-un has prioritised Moscow, sending thousands of troops and conventional weapons to support Russian President Vladimir Putin’s war in Ukraine, apparently in exchange for military and economic assistance.

But Kim has recently drawn closer to China with the apparent intent to improve his country's economy and build a front against the United States.

China, for its part, wants to restore its unique influence over North Korea. Last year, about 98% of North Korea’s external trade was with China.

North Korea has not yet resumed issuing tourism visas to Chinese citizens, who once made up the bulk of its overseas visitors.

South Korea's unification ministry said that Seoul is "closely monitoring" the situation regarding the new bridge.
