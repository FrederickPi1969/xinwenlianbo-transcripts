Although the US midterm elections result appears to have reduced for now the prospect of a new full-scale US and Israeli attack on Iran, the fundamental US objective of stopping any Iranian nuclear programme is generating an intense debate in the Iranian society and its power circles.
Iran’s nuclear doctrine is generating a heated debate in the Islamic Republic’s parliament and power circles as the United States and Israel designated Iran’s capability to develop a nuclear weapon as the primary objective of their war on Iran.

Kamran Ghazanfari, a member of Iran's parliament, told Euronews on Friday that building nuclear weapons is far more essential for Iran that neutralising what he called “"US and Israeli propaganda against the Islamic Republic."

"When America and the Zionist regime have repeatedly threatened us with the use of nuclear weapons, we cannot be indifferent to this issue, as it is a threat that targets all the people of Iran and our very existence,” Ghazanfari said, adding that “they are enemies and have shown that they will not refrain from any crime so we cannot say 'God willing they will not use nuclear weapons against us'. We must confront them,” the Iranian lawmaker said.

He called on Iran’s leadership that “we must review the nuclear doctrine of the Islamic Republic so that the enemy knows that if it seeks a nuclear attack on Iran, it will face a reciprocal response from Iran,” adding that “just as since the Second World War until now none of the countries that possess nuclear weapons have used these weapons against each other because they fear that the other side will carry out such an attack against them, the Islamic Republic must also create deterrent power."

Ghazanfari does not see the lack of nuclear submarines and bombers capable of carrying nuclear weapons as undermining the Islamic Republic's nuclear deterrence, stating that missiles are also a solution to carry nuclear warheads.

But a former Iranian lawmaker, Heshmatollah Falahatpisheh, told Euronews that even if Iran had several nuclear warheads it would still lack nuclear deterrence, stating that "I find it unlikely that nuclear deterrence is part of our country's strategy because nuclear deterrence has three components: having an atomic bomb, a deterrent nuclear balance, and the ability to deliver the final blow. Those who think nuclear deterrence is created simply by having an atomic bomb are mistaken."

In a telephone call with Euronews, he said that "I really do not know whether the country has an atomic bomb or not, but, as far as I was aware, Iran's nuclear programme was peaceful, and even its possible military dimensions were closed by the JCPOA so that Iran's case would be removed from Chapter Seven of the UN Charter.”

“In my view Iran's military strategy is a conventional strategy, and this strategy has been fairly effective. I also find it unlikely that what Iran’s Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei meant when he said that Iran has certain things was an atomic bomb," Falahatpisheh said.

Kamran Ghazanfari, however, believes that when the secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council says that "we have something", he is referring not to an ordinary weapon but to a "special weapon".

He said that Iran is trying to formally withdraw from the Treaty of the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT), the landmark international treaty whose objective is to prevent the spread of nuclear weapons and weapons technology.

"I infer that this change of doctrine has in practice already happened. In parliament we are also pursuing withdrawal from the NPT, and last year some members of the presiding board of parliament blocked it, but MPs are still pressing for this plan to be put on the open floor, although a series of obstruction efforts are still preventing the plan from reaching the floor, Ghazanfari said.

But Heshmatollah Falahatpisheh considers the withdrawal from the NPT to be laying the groundwork for future wars against Iran, stating that "Iran's relations with the NPT have in practice been destroyed and whether Iran remains in the NPT or not depends on the nature of relations between Iran and America.”

“The discussion of Iran leaving the NPT does not help Iran's national interests because leaving the NPT amounts to hiding part of Iran's nuclear programme,” Falahatpisheh told Euronews, adding that Iran’s “Supreme National Security Council has not agreed to the MPs' triple-urgency bill to withdraw from the NPT, otherwise this plan would have been put before an open session of parliament."

Meanwhile on Thursday, Iran’s Assembly of Experts also called for examining the issue of Iran's withdrawal from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.

In a statement, the assembly, whose main task is oversight of the performance of the leadership, said that "examining the issue of withdrawal from the NPT, reviewing the country's defence doctrine and creating the necessary deterrence has become an unavoidable necessity and has turned into a public and national demand."

Finally, in announcing Iran’s latest position on the NPT membership, Iran's Foreign Ministry stressed that the Islamic Republic still remains committed to the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons.

Azerbaijan intensifies its European engagement at the EPC Summit
Leaders gathered in Dublin, Ireland, for a summit of the European Political Community (EPC).

Azerbaijan added additional thrust to its engagement with Europe announcing more energy to the EU, a strategic partnership with the UK, additional agreements with Ukraine, a dozen bilateral meetings with European leaders and a new meeting with Armenia to advance the historic peace process.
Azerbaijan is set to increase and diversify its energy supplies to Europe, beyond the current 10 EU member states, Azerbaijan’s president Ilham Aliyev announced on Thursday during the 9th European Political Community Summit in Dublin.

Aliyev held talks with European Council President Antonio Costa during the EPC summit to discuss the long-term energy contracts “in the context of continuing successful cooperation between Azerbaijan and the European Union in gas supplies,” an Azerbaijani presidency statement said, while Costa declared that “the resumption of negotiations on a new EU-Azerbaijan agreement is good news for our citizens.” The EU-Azerbaijan Strategic Partnership in the Field of Energy was established in 2022.

Commenting on the meeting, Costa emphasized the two-way benefits the EU-Azerbaijan cooperation declaring that “this has been an important year for EU-Azerbaijan relations,” adding that “with peace between Azerbaijan and Armenia now a reality, the EU is also investing to ensure that people across the region benefit.”

Aliyev said that Azerbaijan increased its gas supplies to the European Union from 8 billion cubic meters to 13 billion cubic meters, which accounts for nearly half of Azerbaijan’s exports, and that with the discovery of new fields, the South Caucasus country plans to increase gas supplies further.

The cooperation is now also focusing on renewable energy, a strategic national objective for Azerbaijan, with Aliyev highlighting the agreement reached between Azerbaijan, Georgia, Turkey and Bulgaria on exporting renewable energy to the European markets.

Azerbaijan’s president said that the gas pipeline stretching from Baku to Italy, passing through the territories of seven countries and supplying 10 European Union member states via interconnectors in Bulgaria, was currently operating at full capacity, and that Baku intends to continue expanding its transported volumes.

Costa and Aliyev also said that the European Union’s assessment of the Nakhchivan railway project would be completed soon and that they discussed the developments of transport links and connectivity within the global Middle Corridor project connecting Asia to Europe through the South Caucasus and Central Asia towards the Black Sea, a fast-developing alternative to the Strait of Hormuz crisis for the global supply lines.

Costa invited Aliyev to Brussels to continue the discussions on EU-Azerbaijan’s cooperation given the South Caucasus country’s role in Europe’s energy supply and security.

Azerbaijan’s president Ilham Aliyev and British Prime Minister Andy Burnham signed a Joint Declaration on Strategic Partnership between Azerbaijan and the United Kingdom, during the EPC Summit in Dublin.

With the UK being the largest European investor in Azerbaijan’s economy, historically driven by BP's Caspian operations, the new strategic framework focuses on defence and security cooperation, expanding trade and investment, renewable energy, infrastructure as well as artificial intelligence, advanced technology and others.

British officials said relations have now reached a historic milestone and both leaders committed to expanding mutual trade, defence cooperation, and private-sector investment ties under the new accord.

With Russia watching on, the UK and Azerbaijan agreed to “strengthening cooperation in defence and security in support of regional peace and stability” (...) “across a broad spectrum of security priorities, in response to evolving security challenges and threats.”

The two sides also agreed to “support the development of the Middle Corridor, alongside transport, logistics and infrastructure projects that facilitate cross-border trade and investment.”

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy held a “detailed discussion” with President Aliyev during the EPC summit, focusing on emergency support for Ukraine's battered energy grid ahead of the harsh winter and post-conflict reconstruction strategies.

Zelenskyy confirmed that contracts were signed under the so-called “Drone Deal,” an ongoing bilateral drone agreement, saying that “we are working to scale up orders,” adding that “relations between our countries are developing.”

Zelenskyy also thanked Aliyev for Azerbaijan's humanitarian assistance as officials confirmed an ongoing €1.35 million emergency grid support package from Baku, comprising heavy electrical equipment and generators.

Total Azerbaijani humanitarian aid to Ukraine has now reached €48 million, focusing on civilian energy infrastructure and medical rehabilitation.

Ukraine’s president also noted that Kyiv is closely analysing Azerbaijan's post-conflict reconstruction across Karabakh and the East Zangezur regions, as the Ukrainian government views Baku's massive infrastructural overhaul as a viable blueprint for its own eventual post-war recovery.

The two leaders also discussed expanding economic and trade relations, including private-sector economic cooperation, with Zelenskyy saying that “we value the fact that companies are staying and continuing to work for our people, often despite Russia’s vile attacks.”

Building on their historic peace process, Azerbaijan’s president Ilham Aliyev held new talks with Armenian prime minister Nikol Pashinyan on advancing their peace consolidation, border delimitations, water cooperation and interconnection of electricity grids as well as more confidence-building measures.

The two leaders also discussed the progress of the Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity (TRIPP) connectivity project, including the planning of its railway and highway components.

Since November 2025, a total of 105,000 tonnes of cargo have been transported to Armenia through the territory of Azerbaijan.

President Aliyev met with Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis for talks on upgrading the Southern Gas Corridor's infrastructure in the context of Azerbaijani-Greek bilateral relations, anchored by the Trans Adriatic Pipeline (TAP).

Aliyev also held talks with Moldovan President Maia Sandu on energy cooperation and on strengthening Moldova’s energy security as well as economic and trade ties with a particular focus on the agricultural sector.

Azerbaijan’s president met his Romanian counterpart Nicusor Dan for the first time to discuss energy and connectivity projects, focusing on linking the Caspian and the Black Seas.

How a new ‘green energy corridor’ from the Caspian Sea could help Eastern Europe kick fossil fuels
Azerbaijan, Turkey, Georgia and Bulgaria have signed a Strategic Partnership Agreement in Clean Energy at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya

The partnership signed at the COP31 climate summit aims to transform Azerbaijan into an interregional electricity hub.
Azerbaijan has put 'Green Energy Corridors' at the top of its agenda at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya.

The goal is to physically integrate renewable grids across multiple countries and establish a direct electricity transit route from the Caspian Sea to European markets.

The country has formed a new alliance with Türkiye, Georgia and Bulgaria, who all signed the multi-state Strategic Partnership Agreement in Clean Energy.

Moving beyond broad climate pledges, discussions at the Azerbaijan Pavilion highlighted the proposed green energy routes and strategic Azerbaijan-Türkiye energy interconnection as critical drivers of cross-border infrastructure.

By linking the South Caucasus and Eastern Europe, the four nations intend to diversify transmission routes and ensure long-term regional energy security.

Formalised on the sidelines of the Antalya climate talks, the agreement turns years of conceptual planning into binding infrastructure commitments.

The core objective is to develop a high-capacity transmission network that exports wind and solar energy generated in the Caspian basin directly to Eastern and Central Europe.

Azerbaijan's Energy Minister Parviz Shahbazov, Türkiye's Minister of Energy and Natural Resources Alparslan Bayraktar, Georgia's Vice Prime Minister, Minister of Economy and Sustainable Development Mariam Kvrivishvili, and Bulgaria's Minister of Energy Iva Petrova signed the document on 10 November 2026.

To achieve their goals, the four nations are establishing a synchronised grid framework that bypasses traditional logistical bottlenecks.

The infrastructure will rely on expanding existing overland high-voltage lines through the South Caucasus and Türkiye.

A crucial component of the project involves the parallel enhancement of fibre-optic communications along the transit route, ensuring secure, uninterrupted transmission and modern grid management.

The partnership directly addresses Eastern Europe's urgent macroeconomic mandate to diversify energy imports and reduce historical reliance on legacy fossil fuel corridors.

Integrating the South Caucasus power network directly with Bulgaria establishes a vital new energy artery into the broader European Union grid.

Establishing a dedicated Green Energy Corridor will provide European consumer markets with a stable, alternative supply of decarbonised electricity.

Distributing the immense financial and technical burdens of grid modernisation across four distinct national economies will create a highly structured environment.

Multilateral alignment lowers logistical risks for international investors, private equity firms, and development banks expected to fund the upcoming construction phases.

Throughout the COP31 summit, the Azerbaijan Pavilion functioned as a central hub for detailing the logistical and financial scope of these cross-border projects.

Presentations highlighted the 'Corridor of Dialogue' concept, framing the strategic Azerbaijan-Türkiye energy interconnection as the foundational backbone for the wider four-nation initiative.

Massive investments in domestic renewable generation, particularly onshore and offshore wind projects in the Caspian Sea, will require robust, export-oriented infrastructure to be economically viable.

Aside from the principal signing, the Azerbaijani Ministry of Energy conducted a rapid series of parallel diplomatic engagements to solidify the broader grid agenda.

Minister Shahbazov held intensive bilateral meetings with international renewable giants including Vestas, ACWA Power and NKT to discuss the parallel development of power generation, energy storage and cross-border grids.

Framing the Antalya pact as a continuation of Baku's historical energy strategy, Shahbazov stated that the ministry is actively turning previous COP grid agendas into concrete projects physically connecting producer nations with consumer markets.

Researchers found that dementia risk increases with the number of cigarettes smoked per day, rising by 16% for those who smoke 10 a day and by 34% for those who smoke 20, compared with people who have never smoked.
A scientific review involving about 2.8 million people found that smoking is associated with an increased risk of dementia, Alzheimer's disease and vascular dementia, with the risk rising as the number of cigarettes smoked per day increases.

According to the study, which was led by researchers from Curtin University in Australia and published in the journal "eClinicalMedicine", smokers face a 31% higher risk of developing dementia compared with people who have never smoked.

The researchers observed a link between the number of cigarettes smoked per day and the likelihood of developing dementia. People who smoke 10 cigarettes a day face a 16% higher risk, while the risk rises to 34% among those who smoke 20 cigarettes a day, compared with people who have never smoked.

Lead researcher Mario Serfo said the findings show the need to pay attention to the impact of smoking on brain health, alongside its well-known risks to the heart and lungs and the likelihood of developing cancer.

He added that the relationship between smoking and dementia becomes stronger as the number of cigarettes consumed increases.

The study showed that people who had quit smoking do not face a higher risk of developing dementia compared with those who have never smoked, and that their risk is lower than that of people who continue to smoke.

However, the researchers explained that the study's findings do not prove a direct causal relationship between smoking and dementia, although they add to the scientific evidence suggesting that quitting smoking may help reduce the risk of developing the disease.

Blossom Stephan, a co-author of the study, called for brain health to be included in discussions about the risks of smoking and the health benefits of giving it up.

The researchers stressed the need to expand tobacco control campaigns so they also warn about the impact of smoking on brain function and cognitive abilities, in addition to its well-known risks to the heart and lungs and the likelihood of developing cancer.

In this context, they recommended including warnings about brain health on cigarette packs and incorporating them into smoking cessation programmes to raise awareness of the health consequences of tobacco consumption.

The researchers called for further studies to determine whether the timing of quitting smoking and the length of abstinence affect the level of dementia risk.

Chris Van Hollen, who has support from some of the more vocal progressives within the Democratic party, is the first to openly challenge long-term Democratic Senate leader Chuck Schumer's bid.
US Democratic Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland said Thursday that he will run for Senate Majority Leader in the next Congress, setting up a challenge to Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer after the party swept to power in the midterm elections.

The announcement from the progressive senator sets up an internal party clash as long-term Democratic Senate leader Schumer, 75, has faced calls from some in his party to step aside for new leadership.

Van Hollen, who has support from some of the more vocal progressives within the party, is the first senator to openly challenge Schumer.

Van Hollen said in a statement that he is running because “the American people called for change”.

“It’s now on us to meet this moment, and to do that we need new leadership,” Van Hollen said. “This is not about any one person — it’s about answering the call of the American people. That is why I’m running for Senate Majority Leader.”

Left-wing independent Senator Bernie Sanders said this week that he would not support Schumer in continuing his role.

Schumer said he welcomed discussion about the future of the party, but insisted: "I have the votes to continue serving as the Democratic Leader."

"I look forward to leading our new Democratic majority and delivering for the American people," he wrote on X.

While Schumer is the favorite to win, Van Hollen’s run exposes a rift in the party that opened in the spring of 2025, when Schumer was put on the defensive since he sided with Republicans on a spending vote.

Even as Schumer has redeemed himself within his caucus since then — leading them through a government shutdown last year and orchestrating huge wins in the election — many in the Democratic base are agitating for generational change.

Van Hollen, 67, is not that much younger than Schumer, 75. But he has positioned himself as a new face in the party. He has also said he is considering a run for president.

It will be the first contested election for Democratic leader in decades. The question of whether to reelect Schumer has already divided the caucus and many senators will not say publicly how they will vote.

Newly elected US lawmakers gathered on Capitol Hill on Thursday 12 November for a traditional group photograph ahead of the opening of the 120th Congress. The incoming House members are attending orientation following the November midterm elections.
… More
Footage captures newly elected members of the US House of Representatives ascending the Capitol steps, mingling in clusters and exchanging warm greetings, some punctuated by enthusiastic high-fives. Drawn from Illinois, Iowa, Michigan, New York, Virginia and beyond, the freshman cohort gathered for a mandatory orientation programme designed to introduce first-time lawmakers to parliamentary protocol, their impending duties and life on Capitol Hill.

Their arrival in Washington follows November’s midterm elections, which yielded sweeping gains for the Democrats alongside a markedly smaller intake of Republicans. This transitional phase sees incoming politicians crossing paths with departing incumbents clearing their desks, signalling the formal handover to the next legislature.

Over the coming weeks, novices will immerse themselves in legislative mechanics and institutional operations before the 120th Congress convenes on 3 January 2027, when members will take the oath of office and formally assume their mandates.
