A rare double waterspout formed over the Italian island of Capri on Saturday, stretching across its iconic Faraglioni rock formations.

The two swirling columns emerged from heavy clouds hovering over the island, creating a striking natural spectacle.

Waterspouts are relatively common over the Mediterranean in late summer and autumn, when cooler air moves over warmer waters.

Capri is one of Italy's most popular tourist destinations, attracting an estimated three million visitors each year. The Faraglioni rock formations are among the island's best-known attractions, although many tourism businesses close during the winter months, leaving the island largely to its residents.

Egypt is seeking closer ties with Libya’s western government after years of backing its eastern rival. Talks in Tripoli covered energy projects and Egypt’s objections to Libya’s maritime deal with Turkey.
Egypt reopened its historic embassy building in the Libyan capital, Tripoli, on Saturday during a visit by Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty for talks on trade, energy and a disputed maritime boundary.

Libya’s acting foreign minister, Taher al-Baour, joined Abdelatty at the ceremony. Egypt closed the building in January 2014 after gunmen kidnapped five embassy staff. Its diplomatic mission resumed work in Tripoli in May 2021.

Cairo has long backed Khalifa Haftar, the military commander whose forces dominate eastern Libya, where a rival government is based. In Tripoli, Abdelatty met Abdulhamid Dbeibah, the prime minister of Libya’s internationally recognised Government of National Unity.

The two discussed ways to make trade easier. Dbeibah praised the role Egyptian companies and workers have played in rebuilding Libya, according to Egypt’s foreign ministry.

Abdelatty also met Libya’s acting oil minister at the reopened embassy. He called for faster progress on gas pipelines between the two countries and on plans to refine Libyan crude in Egypt for export. The Libyan minister said he would travel to Cairo for further talks, Libya’s state news agency reported on Sunday.

In his talks with Dbeibah, Abdelatty raised Egypt’s objections to a 2019 agreement between Turkey and Libya’s then government in Tripoli. He said Egypt’s concerns should be taken into account when maritime boundaries are drawn, according to his ministry. Dbeibah told Egyptian broadcaster Al-Qahera News that officials from both countries would discuss the maps and their respective claims at their next meeting.

The agreement draws a boundary between Turkish and Libyan maritime zones in the eastern Mediterranean, where several countries claim rights to explore for oil and gas. Greece says the boundary ignores the rights of Greek islands, including Crete, which lies between the Turkish and Libyan coasts. Egypt and Cyprus also oppose the deal.

Libya’s eastern-based parliament ratified the agreement on 20 October after opposing it for years. The EU said the next day that the deal infringed on other countries’ rights and could have no legal effect on them. Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan defended it as “an entirely legitimate agreement, both from a legal and a political perspective”.

Egypt has announced a new natural gas discovery in the Mediterranean Sea, with initial estimates pointing to around 200 billion cubic feet in the reservoir of the exploratory well "Nasim-1X".

Cairo hopes the discovery will help support domestic gas production and reduce reliance on imports.

The Egyptian Ministry of Petroleum and Mineral Resources said on Friday that drilling and appraisal work at the well, located in the Taiba concession area, had revealed a gas-bearing layer with a net thickness of around 15 metres, featuring distinct geological and petrophysical characteristics.

Technical and reservoir studies are still under way to determine the volumes that can be produced, assess possible daily production rates and evaluate the commercial viability of the discovery.

Egypt plans to connect the well to existing production facilities via a pipeline about 22 kilometres long, linking it to the Abu Madi - El Gamil pipeline. This is intended to make use of the available infrastructure and shorten the time needed to begin production.

Italian company Eni is operating the Taiba concession in partnership with Australian company Woodside, while Egyptian firm Petrobel carried out the drilling work on behalf of the partners.

The Ministry of Petroleum attributed the positive results to higher investment and exploration activity, noting an improved investment climate and progress in settling overdue financial receivables owed to foreign companies working in the energy sector.

Egypt is seeking to increase its domestic natural gas production after a decline in output in recent years led to greater dependence on gas imports to meet market demand.

Cairo is relying on the development of new discoveries and faster exploration work to strengthen local supplies, amid strong gas demand from the electricity and industrial sectors.

The US military said on Saturday that its forces had "disabled" a commercial vessel in the Gulf of Oman after its civilian crew "ignored repeated warnings" and made a number of attempts to "violate" the naval blockade of Iran.

"A U.S. military aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of St. Kitts-flagged M/V Huda, disabling the ship after CENTCOM forces directed the crew to abandon targeted spaces. The cargo vessel tried to depart regional waters after visiting an Iranian port," US Central Command (CENTCOM) said in a statement, adding that it was continuing to "strictly enforce" the blockade against Iran.

According to the military command, since July, US forces have redirected 147 commercial vessels that complied with the blockade, while disabling five merchant ships that refused.

"Additionally, CENTCOM has previously destroyed another 10 Iranian commercial oil tankers that were part of the Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps’ multibillion-dollar shadow network," it added.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) agency said late on Saturday that it had received a report of an incident involving a bulk carrier and military forces in the Gulf of Oman, around 18 nautical miles east of Khor Fakkan on the east coast of the United Arab Emirates.

The UAE National Guard announced early on Sunday that 22 people had been rescued from a cargo ship in the gulf.

At the same time, Iran's Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) navy claimed in a statement that two oil tankers, one to the east of the Strait of Hormuz and the other sailing in the strategic waterway, had caught fire after hitting mines.

"These two tankers, through multiple violations and by switching off their navigation and positioning systems, intended to transit the Strait of Hormuz via an unauthorised route. After hitting a mine, they exploded and are burning," the IRGC navy said in a statement.

The Guards also accused CENTCOM's commander of "fantasy" and warned that any ship or vessel that cooperates with the US military, "disobeys Iranian regulations for the Strait of Hormuz and enters the unauthorised area, will inevitably hit a mine."

Tensions between Tehran and Washington over the Strait of Hormuz have flared once again in recent days following the end of the US midterm elections.

On Friday night, reports emerged of explosions on Qeshm Island, and the Tasnim news agency reported that two missiles had struck the area.

A few hours later, US President Donald Trump confirmed that US forces had targeted an Iranian air defence system.

Iranian news agencies reported on Saturday night that further explosions had been heard in Qeshm, Sirik, Jask and Bandar Abbas.

"President Isaac Herzog has decided to task Gadi Eisenkot, chairman of the ‘Yashar’ party, with forming a government," a statement from the president's office released on Saturday evening said.
