Updated: 18/10/2026 - 13:05 GMT+2
Paris woke up to widespread damage on Sunday after violence overshadowed Saturday’s protests over the cost of living. Streets were littered with debris, burnt bicycles and damaged vehicles, while shops across the city had smashed windows and signs of looting.

Businesses including Nike, Sephora and Burger King were among those left damaged, and windows at the Paris Opera ticket office were also shattered. Cleaning crews worked to clear the streets as workers began repairing shopfronts.

Central Lyon was left littered with debris, shattered shop windows and looted stores on Sunday morning after violence erupted during Saturday’s cost-of-living protests.

The unrest came amid nationwide demonstrations over rising prices, with France’s presidential election scheduled for next year.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said a "serious incident creating a major fire" had occurred at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta.
An incident at a Canadian military base has resulted in a number of deaths and injuries, Canada's defence minister announced late on Saturday.

"I'm saddened to share that there have been both fatalities and injuries as a result of an incident on the base," David McGuinty said in a statement, identifying the location as Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said a "serious incident creating a major fire" had occurred at the site.

"Our thoughts are with all the brave women and men affected. We are working closely with the Government of Alberta and local authorities," he wrote in a post on social media.

According to the Canadian government, Wainwright is one of the "busiest" army bases in the country and is home to the Canadian Manoeuvre Training Centre as well as the 3rd Canadian Division Training Centre.

Wainwright is around 210 kilometres east of Edmonton - the capital of Alberta - and 110 kilometres southwest of Lloydminster.
