Around 100 clowns and circus fans fill the historic centre of Mariana, Brazil, for an international gathering celebrating circus, music and colour
… More
Around 100 professional clowns took to the streets of Mariana in Brazil during the International Clown Gathering, joined by circus enthusiasts in colourful costumes and face paint.

The event brings together circus artists and audiences to celebrate the art form and introduce it to younger generations. Music, costumes and make-up fill the streets as participants perform and interact with crowds. Organisers say it also gives regional artists a chance to showcase their work.

Held in the historic centre of Mariana, in the state of Minas Gerais, the gathering aims to keep circus traditions alive while drawing new audiences to the art. For participants, the joy and colour of the circus are at the heart of the celebration.

Khalifa and 11 others face death sentences in a major synthetic drug trafficking case, with appeals and further legal challenges expected.
An Egyptian television presenter faces execution after a Cairo court sentenced her to death for financing and coordinating a synthetic drug network, but her lawyer says they will appeal, and legal experts say the case is far from over.

Sarah Khalifa, known for hosting the crime programme Mission Impossible on private channel Al-Mehwar and for her events business, was sentenced on Saturday alongside 11 others in a major synthetic drug manufacturing and trafficking case.

Nine defendants received life sentences and seven were acquitted. Those sentenced to death were also fined 500,000 Egyptian pounds (€8,000).

Khalifa's brother Mohamed was among those sentenced to death, on charges of manufacturing and testing drugs.

Khalifa's lawyer Mohamed El-Gendy said the defence would appeal and pursue all available legal avenues.

A higher criminal court can reconsider the evidence and each defendant's role, upholding, reducing, or overturning sentences. A further challenge before Egypt's Court of Cassation is also possible.

Before Saturday's ruling, the court had referred Khalifa and 12 other defendants to Egypt's Grand Mufti — a procedural requirement before a criminal court can impose the death penalty. The Mufti's opinion is advisory and not binding.

Egyptian media initially described the case as involving synthetic hashish, but forensic evidence presented during the trial identified the substances as synthetic cannabinoids, laboratory-made chemicals that mimic some of the effects of cannabis on the brain but can be significantly more potent and unpredictable.

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime describes MDMB-4en-PINACA as a potent synthetic cannabinoid placed under international control in 2021 following a World Health Organization recommendation citing severe adverse effects and deaths.

Synthetic cannabinoids can cause psychosis, seizures, heart and kidney problems, and in severe cases, death.

The committee said the substance was not specifically listed on Egypt's controlled narcotics schedule, but concluded it fell under Egyptian law because its chemical structure and effects were similar to already-listed substances.

The defence challenged that interpretation and the court accepted the committee's findings.

The case began in April 2025 when Egypt's anti-narcotics authorities said they had uncovered a network importing raw materials used to manufacture synthetic drugs.

Police initially raided two Cairo apartments and recovered around 200 kilograms of drugs, raw materials and equipment.

As the investigation widened, prosecutors said the total seized exceeded 750 kilograms. The investigation lasted some 40 days before prosecutors obtained arrest warrants.

Al-Masry Al-Youm newspaper, citing judicial and security sources, reported that investigators alleged raw materials were sourced abroad, concealed inside cosmetics containers and luggage on flights.

Updated: 07/09/2026 - 10:59 GMT+2
An Amazon cargo plane overran the runway at Miami International Airport, hit vehicles and caught fire. Five died, five were injured. FAA and NTSB investigate.
… More
An Amazon cargo plane overran the runway at Miami International Airport on Sunday, hitting several vehicles and catching fire.

The aircraft, Prime Air Flight 7598 from Puerto Rico, came to rest near the northwest end of the airport. US authorities said five people died and five were injured. The Federal Aviation Administration has opened an investigation.

Miami-Dade Fire Rescue said crews arrived to find the plane ablaze, with heavy flames and smoke from a complex engine fire. Around 200 emergency personnel responded. All runways and taxiways were closed and a ground stop was issued while the disabled aircraft was assessed.

Officials said there was no wider threat to public safety.The FAA said the Boeing 767-300 cargo aircraft had departed from Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport in San Juan and overran the runway around 2 p.m. local time.

In Egypt, a court sentenced 12 people, including popular TV presenter Sarah Khalifa, to death in a major drug manufacturing and trafficking case.
In Egypt, well-known television presenter Sarah Khalifa has been sentenced to death along with 11 others in a major case involving drug manufacture and trafficking.

According to reports carried in the state-run press on Saturday, the 39-year-old Khalifa was known for her programme Mission Impossible, which covered security and crime issues.

State-affiliated newspaper Al-Ahram reported that a court in Cairo convicted Khalifa and the 11 other defendants of offences including "forming an organised criminal group to procure substances used in drug manufacture for the purpose of trafficking, and possessing and transporting unlicensed firearms and ammunition", and handed them death sentences.

The verdict was announced after consultation with Egypt's Grand Mufti for a religious opinion, in line with the procedures laid down in Egyptian law for capital cases. The court's ruling can be appealed.

State media outlet Akhbar El-Yom said that Khalifa, who also owns a cosmetic clinic and an events company, and the other defendants were accused of "importing raw materials used in production from abroad to manufacture synthetic drugs and trade in them".

El-Ahram said law enforcement officers seized more than 750 kilogrammes of drugs and raw materials during the raids, as well as two flats used as laboratories and numerous unlicensed firearms and rounds of ammunition.

The court also sentenced Khalifa and some of the defendants to five years in prison each in a separate case over the abduction and assault of a young man.

In Egypt, the death penalty is applied in cases of premeditated murder, terrorism, certain sexual assault offences and drug trafficking. According to figures from the Egyptian Rights and Freedoms Commission, 541 death sentences were handed down in the country in 2025.

Nepal held a day of mourning on Monday to remember the victims of the devastating 26 August floods, with government offices lowering flags and family members carrying out mourning rituals.

Monday is the 13th day since the flooding that has killed more than 1,300 people in Nepal and Tibet and left some 5,000 missing including foreigners, according to Nepal’s disaster management agency.

In Hindu-majority Nepal, mourning periods last 13 days and close family members stay at home and relatives visit to pay respect. On the 13th day, rituals are held to pray for the departed souls.

Several people whose family members are still missing have performed symbolic funerals with mourning rituals. In Hindu tradition, cremation is an important final rite and for some families, performing the ceremony even without the bodies of their loved ones provides a sense of spiritual release.

In Kathmandu, family members gathered near the offices of the prime minister and key ministries to demand the government speed up the search and rescue for the hundreds of people who were working on hydropower plants and were either swept by the rushing Bhotekhoshi river buried by the debris.

Rescuers pulled out a Chinese man from a power plant on Saturday and two Nepalese were rescued from the plant on Friday.

Rescue efforts have increasingly focused on 12 hydropower projects, where about 900 workers are missing and roughly 500 are believed to be trapped in various tunnels, authorities said.

The disaster posed an unprecedented challenge, Australian rescue expert Arnold Dix said on Monday, with multiple search operations underway in hydropower tunnels.

"We are facing something I have never faced before," said Dix, president of the International Tunnelling and Underground Space Association, in a post on social media.

"Not one rescue, but multiple potential rescues, unfolding at the same time, in different places, each with its own dangers, uncertainties, and lives that may still be saved."

Rescue efforts continue "with the same tempo we had on day one," Nepal Army spokesman Raja Ram Basnet told the AFP news agency on Monday.

"Our focus is on search and rescue at three levels - on ground, by air, and in the tunnels. We are making sure that no one is still stranded."

The rescue of three workers from deep inside tunnels at the weekend buoyed hopes of finding other survivors.

Specialised rescue teams from India, China, South Korea and the United States have been searching through debris, damaged structures and tunnels at the projects, hoping to find survivors.

"We are measured not only by the lives we save, but by the lives we refuse to abandon," said Dix, a key adviser during the Indian rescue of 41 men trapped for 17 days inside the collapsed Silkyara tunnel in Uttarakhand state in 2023.

Indonesia has more than 120 active volcanoes and frequently experiences eruptions due to its location along the Pacific “Ring of Fire.”
Indonesia kept several airports closed and shifted schools to remote learning on Monday though a volcanic eruption that halted 2,300 flights and coated areas with ash was subsiding.

The continuous, 25-hour eruption of Anak Krakatau that produced a fountain of lava and a boom heard 700 kilometres away ended on Sunday, the country's Geological Agency said.

But it remained active on Monday with moderate, intermittent eruptions, dark ash emissions and small, localised earthquakes, the agency said in a statement.

AirNav Indonesia said tests showed volcanic ash present in the airspace around Jakarta and several other cities, prompting authorities to close eight airports until 6 pm on Monday, including the four commercial airports managed by state-owned operator InJourney Airports: Jakarta's international airports of Soekarno-Hatta and Halim Perdanakusuma, Husein Sastranegara in West Java and Radin Inten in Lampung on the southern tip of Sumatra.

The closure disrupted about 2,300 flights and affected 268,539 passengers at the four airports managed by InJourney Airports, the company said.

The eruption’s impact extended beyond air travel. The government authorised schools in areas affected by volcanic ash, including in Jakarta, West Java, Banten and Lampung provinces, to temporarily move to online learning at home to reduce students’ exposure to ash.

“The probability of additional eruptions in the coming days remains high,” said Lana Saria, head of the Geology Agency.

“The main hazards include volcanic bombs, heavy ashfall and possible lava flows if sustained eruptive activity resumes," she said.

Anak Krakatau is a volcanic island in the Sunda Strait between Indonesia's main Java and Sumatra islands. Its name means “child of Krakatau,” and the volcano is the offspring of the infamous Krakatau, whose monumental 1883 eruption triggered a period of global cooling.

An eruption of Anak Krakatau in 2018 caused a tsunami that killed at least 430 people in Sumatra and Java.

Researchers believe volcanic activity triggered an underwater landslide and caused a large chunk of Anak Krakatau’s southwest slope to collapse and displace a large volume of water.

Indonesia has more than 120 active volcanoes and frequently experiences eruptions due to its location along the Pacific “Ring of Fire,” the ring of seismic faults around the Pacific Ocean where most global seismic activity occurs.
