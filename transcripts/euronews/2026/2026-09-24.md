In his opening remarks before the formal talks at the White House, Xi told Trump that he is “ready to work with you to steer the giant ship of China-US relationship on a steady course toward the future,” and that the two countries “must hold the line of no conflict and no confrontation.”

The Chinese leader underlined that China and the United States are the two superpowers that “facing the changes in our world, in our times, and of historical significance today”, they “shoulder a historic responsibility of advancing human development and progress”.

“We can certainly find the right path for two great countries to get along on this planet we both call home_,”_ Xi said, adding that “through candid, in-depth, and continued dialogue, our two countries can understand each other better, seek common ground, while shelving differences and build up mutual trust.”

Xi’s highly anticipated visit to Washington started with the two leaders proclaiming their long relationship, but the two global economic rivals were gearing up to discuss the key thorny issues in their formal talks.

“From the time of my first election in 2016, President Xi and I have forged a friendship, a truly great friendship, actually, built on mutual respect and the vital interests of our people,” Trump said, while also touching on cooperation during World War 2.

But moving on, Trump said the US and China were working towards “more balanced” trade relations and that their talks would focus on foreign affairs, trade, financial cooperation, security and artificial intelligence which they identified as a major topic.

In turn, Xi said he hopes “Chinese companies are treated fairly here in the United States” while China would welcome US companies there and that “competition should be a healthy one and should be kept within bounds.”

China has resisted the Trump administration’s urging and economic threats to use its considerable leverage to convince Iran to agree to US terms to end the Iran war, even if China which is far more dependent on Gulf oil than the US.

China condemned the US and Israeli war on Iran and Xi previously expressed concerns that the conflict signalled a return to what he then called “the law of the jungle”.

Xi is expected to press Trump again, in the same way as he did when the two leaders met in Beijing in May, to permanently end arms sales to Taiwan as China has made clear that it views Taiwan as the most sensitive issue in the US-China relationship.

Back in May, Trump described arms sales to Taiwan as a “very good negotiating chip” with China.

On trade, China is pushing for the US to loosen export restrictions on cutting-edge advanced chips.

The two sides agreed on Wednesday to extend by two months a one-year trade truce that was set to expire on November 10 as they work to find a more durable agreement.

“It should be a race of catching up with one another, not a wrestle in which one either wins or loses,” Xi said.

Xi also referred to AI in his opening comments, saying that the two countries had a responsibility to ensure that humans always operate AI.

“We have both the capability and responsibility to develop and manage AI for good and ensure that a development of AI is always under human control and serves the well being of the people,” the Chinese leader said.

Saida Mirziyoyeva, head of Uzbekistan’s Presidential Administration, used her UN address to focus on regional cooperation, Afghanistan and reform of the organisation. She also covered domestic reforms, AI, human rights and environmental challenges.
Uzbekistan called for a more effective United Nations, presenting closer cooperation in Central Asia as an example of how dialogue can ease regional tensions.

Saida Mirziyoyeva, who has headed Uzbekistan’s Presidential Administration since June 2025, delivered the country’s address to the 81st session of the General Assembly in New York.

She framed Tashkent’s foreign-policy approach around “openness, good-neighbourliness and dialogue”, adding that Uzbekistan stood for “peace, cooperation and respect for human dignity”.

Uzbekistan backed an expansion of the UN Security Council and reiterated its candidacy for a non-permanent seat for 2035–2036.

The country is also due to chair the Non-Aligned Movement from 2027 to 2029, which Tashkent sees as another platform for international dialogue.

The speech pointed to changes in relations among the five Central Asian states, where disputes over borders and water resources have complicated ties for decades.

“The situation and atmosphere in the region have completely changed,” Mirziyoyeva said, referring to closer cooperation between neighbouring governments.

On Afghanistan, she argued that viewing the country solely as a security threat would not solve its problems and that the Afghan population should not be left to deal with them alone.

At the first EU-Central Asia summit in Samarkand in April 2025, European and Central Asian leaders expressed shared concern about security threats and possible spillover from Afghanistan into both Central Asia and Europe.

According to the figures presented, the economy grew from $50bn (€44bn) to $147bn (€129bn), while foreign investment exceeded $150bn (€131bn).

Mirziyoyeva put the poverty rate at 4% and said almost 10mn people had moved out of poverty.

She cited wider access to preschool and higher education, as well as improvements in health indicators, as part of the government’s reform record.

On artificial intelligence, she argued that technological development should remain linked to “human rights, security, equal opportunities and responsibility”.

Technology, she added, should improve people’s lives rather than create new forms of dependence.

Environmental issues centred on the Aral Sea, whose dramatic shrinkage remains one of Central Asia’s most serious ecological problems.

Mirziyoyeva described the disaster as the result of decisions taken over decades, pointing to water-saving technologies and efforts to restore degraded land.

West Bank herders move milk by donkey through a deep trench at night, saying new access restrictions and lack of roads block routes to fields.
… More
Herders in the West Bank use donkeys to carry milk churns through an earthen trench before loading them onto a vehicle.

The journey takes place at night, with farmers helping the animals over the steep earthworks.

According to a farmer, access to the area has been restricted, making it difficult to transport milk and reach grazing land. He says there are no roads and that the trench has cut off their usual route, limiting the movement of people and vehicles.

They work at night because they fear being stopped or arrested if they enter the area during the day.

Repaired after a deadly collision with New York’s Brooklyn Bridge, the Mexican tall ship Cuauhtémoc returns to San Francisco for a four-day stop.
… More
The Mexican Navy tall ship Cuauhtémoc sails beneath San Francisco's Golden Gate Bridge before docking along the Embarcadero.

The visit marks its return to the city after 17 years. It is part of an international training voyage along the US West Coast, following a stop in Honolulu. The crew will spend four days there before continuing to San Diego.

The Cuauhtémoc was involved in a fatal collision with New York's Brooklyn Bridge last year, which killed two sailors and injured 19 others.

Repaired after the accident, it is now back at sea. Visitors can board the vessel for tours while it is docked.

Pyongyang, which supports Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, has helped staff and arm its forces. The UN says up to 25,000 North Koreans assemble drones in Russia, with most of their pay seized by Kim Jong-un's regime.
According to a report by the UN Security Council's Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team (MSMT), between 15,000 and 30,000 North Korean workers were present in Russia at the end of 2025.

Up to 1,000 of them were employed at strategically important facilities, including defence plants, steelworks and shipbuilding capacities.

The Ukrainian outlet "Ukrainska Pravda" also confirmed that up to 25,000 North Korean citizens are working on drone production in the Alabuga special economic zone in Tatarstan, where Russian versions of Iran's Shahed drone are assembled.

Radio Liberty cites similar data, noting that North Koreans are registered as "students" in order to bypass UN sanctions that prohibit the use of DPRK labour abroad.

In 2025 Russia issued more than 36,400 visas to North Korean citizens, of which 35,800 were student visas.

Workers from North Korea are now reportedly spread across Russia, from traditional employment regions in the Far East (Vladivostok, Khabarovsk and Sakhalin) to newer destinations such as Moscow, Saint Petersburg and Irkutsk.

Tatarstan has become the main centre of their employment, in particular the Alabuga special economic zone, which features in most investigations as a key hub for assembling strike drones.

It is here, according to Current Time and Radio Liberty, that North Korean workers staff assembly lines producing drones used by Russia to attack Ukrainian cities and infrastructure.

Although UN Security Council sanctions have banned the export of North Korean labour since 2017, Moscow and Pyongyang have built an entire ecosystem for circumventing these restrictions, the MSMT report notes.

North Koreans are being registered en masse on student and tourist visas, which allows their real status to be concealed and their work at Russian companies to be legalised.

According to reports, Russian legislation was amended to create legal loopholes that enabled "students" to receive income officially.

The report also describes cases in which Russian universities were bribed to arrange fictitious study programmes, as well as the role of intermediaries from third countries, including Belarus, which help organise entry and distribute the workforce.

This points to a systemic scheme that allows Pyongyang to earn millions of dollars and enables Russia to plug labour shortages in strategic industries.

According to MSMT, the working conditions of North Korean labourers in Russia remain extremely harsh and are fully controlled by the Pyongyang authorities.

The average annual income of such workers is around $7,500 (€6,593), but almost all of this is seized by Pyongyang.

The report notes that in 2025 alone North Korea received up to 800 million dollars from the labour of its citizens abroad. These funds, the report stresses, are channelled into financing the country's nuclear and missile programme.

Long queues at Turkey’s Gurbulak border with Iran turn a 24‑hour route into a week, leaving truck drivers and their families struggling.
… More
Hundreds of trucks wait at the Gurbulak border crossing in Turkey's eastern Agri province, with drivers standing beside their vehicles as they face long waits to enter Iran.

Truckers say the delays have sharply reduced the number of journeys they can make. One driver says a route that once took 24 hours now takes a week, while rising fuel costs have further cut profits.

Another says he now makes only one journey every 40 days or two months, leaving his family dependent on his income.

The queues have grown as Iran relies more heavily on overland trade following disruption to maritime routes.

A religious service at the historic Gandzasar monastery has offered a rare insight into Karabakh’s Christian heritage, as historic religious disputes linger while Azerbaijan and Armenia work toward lasting peace.
With peace returning to the region, Azerbaijan’s Udi Christians continue their centuries-old practice of pilgrimage and prayer at Karabakh’s historic Christian sites, as Azerbaijan showcases the preservation of religious sites whose historical identity remains contested.

For local communities and officials, these sites mean more than religious practice, as they project the safeguarding of Christian identity in the region.

As part of a government programme to explore the region’s Christian heritage and religious traditions, Euronews and other media outlets met a congregation of Udi Christians for their religious service at the ancient monastery of Gandzasar, near Vangli village in Azerbaijan’s Aghdara district, and talked to representatives involved in the preservation and maintenance of the site.

Prince Hasan-Jalal Dawla built the monastery’s main church between 1216 and 1238. Azerbaijan identifies it with the Caucasian Albanian Christian tradition, while Armenia identifies it with the Armenian Apostolic one, as competing interpretations continue to challenge the historic peace process between the two countries.

The Udi people are an indigenous, predominantly Christian ethnic group in Azerbaijan, primarily based in the village of Nij.

"Today, thanks to the acquisition of independence, we are in the monastery built by our holy fathers. We are proud of our history," said Danakari Rafiq Arami, Deputy Chairman of the Alban-Udi Christian Community, emphasising the emotional weight of holding a service in the native Udi language.

Azerbaijan’s Udi community make pilgrimages to historic Christian sites associated with their heritage, including other monasteries in the Karabakh region, such as Khudavang, Ağoğlan and Amaras, which Azerbaijan identifies as historic Christian sites in Karabakh in its preservation programme.

Khudavang is located in the Kalbajar district, while Ağoğlan Monastery is in the Lachin district and Amaras Monastery is in the Khojavend district.

Christianity has a long history on the territory of present-day Azerbaijan, which prides itself on the peaceful coexistence of Muslims, Christians and Jews. UNESCO’s documentation on the Historic Centre of Sheki states that Christianity was introduced in the region as early as the 1st century CE.

One of the principal sites associated with this history is the church at Kish, near Sheki. The wider Udi Christian legacy is also represented in Nij, in the Gabala district, where the Udi community continues to practise Christianity.

The media visit comes shortly after a diplomatic delegation visited Gandzasar and Khudavang monasteries on 12 September.

Representatives of the diplomatic corps accredited in Azerbaijan were briefed on the history and architecture of the sites, as well as Azerbaijan’s claims about alterations to the monuments, which ignited a row with Armenia over the sites’ heritage and identity.

EU Special Representative for the South Caucasus Magdalena Grono, who took part in the diplomatic visit, said there were still "deeply divergent views and narratives" concerning the origins of some sites.

Grono stressed the importance of addressing unresolved historical disputes as part of the broader reconciliation process.

Trump justified the ban in a social media post on Friday, accusing the outlets of publishing "fake news" and warning that other news organizations could face similar treatment.
CNN, MS NOW and Politico say their reporters were once again denied entry to the White House by the Secret Service on Thursday, despite a court ruling ordering their access to be restored.

In response, the three media outlets have requested an urgent court hearing, alleging that the White House has defied the judge's order.

Earlier in the day, a federal judge had ordered the White House to reinstate press access for the three outlets after their reporters' credentials were revoked.

Judge Timothy Kelly ruled the bans violated basic due-process protections, since the outlets weren't given notice or a chance to challenge the decision before losing access.

CNN, MS NOW and Politico filed a joint lawsuit asking for a temporary restraining order against the ban on the grounds that it violates the First Amendment of the US Constitution, which upholds the right to a free press.

Citing established legal principle, Kelly noted that the US government cannot strip someone of a constitutionally protected interest without first affording them an opportunity to state their case.

"The ‘general rule’ is that individuals must receive notice and an opportunity to be heard before the Government deprives them’ of a constitutionally protected interest," Kelly wrote in his judgment seen by CNN.

The ruling requires immediate reinstatement of the affected outlets' access to the White House premises.

Trump announced the ban in a Friday social media post, accusing the outlets of "FAKE NEWS" reporting and warning other media organizations could follow, but the administration didn't spell out any process for appealing the decision until the following Wednesday — days after the reporters' passes were confiscated.

In court, Justice Department lawyers argued the bans were justified on national security grounds, pointing to stories including one on plans to build a bunker beneath the East Wing, but the outlets' attorney countered that national security had not been mentioned when the ban was first announced and appeared to have been introduced only after the lawsuit was filed.

Kelly's ruling noted that the East Wing story the administration cited had in fact been published months ago and current CNN senior white house reporter Betsy Klein's press pass had been renewed after that, undermining the timeline of the government's argument.

The judge's decision came after nearly 50 news organizations, including the major broadcast networks in the US, had rallied in support of the banned outlets, with some halting White House pool coverage in solidarity.

"The public has a vital interest in receiving accurate, independent information about its government. No administration should restrict a news organization because it objects to its reporting," ABC, CBS, CNN, Fox and NBC said.

The decision was published just hours after Trump greeted his Chinese counterpart, Xi Jinping on Wednesday, for his three-day state visit, as their top officials agreed to prolong a trade truce until January.

In a grand welcome, US President Donald Trump greeted his Chinese counterpart, Xi Jinping, on the tarmac of Joint Base Andrews, just outside of Washington, D.C, on Wednesday, for his three-day state visit, as their top officials agreed to prolong a trade truce until January.

The truce, which includes both countries agreeing to scale back tariffs and not impose new trade restrictions, was announced by Trump's Treasury Secretary, Bessent, during a Fox News Channel interview, “I don’t know whether a bigger deal can be done. I don’t know whether we will just roll the current deal,” Bessent said.

However, upcoming international summits in China in November and Florida in December could give the two sides new opportunities to discuss the matter, the Treasury Secretary added.

In a statement upon Xi's arrival in the US, accompanied by his wife, Peng Liyuan, the leader wrote that his country and the US “should be partners, not rivals. Achieving the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation and making America great again can go hand in hand and the two countries can contribute to each other’s success, thereby benefiting the wider world.”

In comments released by Chinese state media Xi also said that he was looking "forward to in-depth exchanges with President Trump on major issues concerning our bilateral relations and the global situation,".

Trade alongside artificial intelligence, Trump's war with Iran and China's position on Taiwan have been among the biggest issues between the two superpowers, but this visit is expected to be more about show than substance.

Meanwhile Xi's welcoming has caused backlash among some of the Republicans, Sen. Roger Wicker, chairman of the Senate Armed Services Committee, criticised the “lavish welcome.” saying during a speech on Tuesday in the Senate that Trump should not have invited the Chinese leader “based on all of the troubling issues we have with President Xi and the Chinese Communist Party.”

As Secretary of State, Marco Rubio said that the US and China need to maintain their relationship at the highest level since the countries are "the two largest economies in the world” and “probably the two most powerful militaries in the world.”

Xi's first state visit to the US in more than a decade is expected to be marked by a formal welcome banquet, another flyover, talks in the Oval Office and a state banquet at the White House on Thursday.

Before wrapping up on Friday after tea at the White House and a visit to the National Archives, home the US Declaration of Independance.
