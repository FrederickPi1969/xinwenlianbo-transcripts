Updated: 28 October 2026 - 16:54 GMT+1
At least 68 people have been killed and dozens more injured after two high-speed trains collided near Urda, in central Spain’s Toledo province, on Wednesday, 28 October. Authorities warned the toll could rise further as rescuers searched the wreckage.
… More
Footage released by Spain’s Military Emergency Unit shows rescue personnel searching the debris, while Civil Guard officers and firefighters assist at the scene. Around 100 UME personnel were deployed, with sniffer dogs and specialist units working through twisted metal.

The injured were taken to hospitals in Toledo, where ambulances arrived throughout the morning and police cordoned off entrances. Regional officials said 58 people required hospital treatment, about 50 of them seriously.

King Felipe VI sent condolences to the victims and their families, while Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez expressed his solidarity and support.

The crash occurred shortly before 9am on the Madrid–Seville line, at kilometre 118 between Mora, Orgaz and Urda. A Renfe AVE train carrying 397 passengers struck the rear of a privately operated Iryo train, which had stopped after a reported signal fault, knocking two carriages off the tracks. The Iryo service was carrying 147 people, including a baby.

The disaster is Spain’s second major rail accident this year, after a January crash in southern Spain killed 45 people.

Trump is seeking to lower energy costs ahead of next week's midterm elections, as rising oil prices linked to the US war with Iran fuel voter frustration.
US President Donald Trump announced on Tuesday plans to significantly ease restrictions on methane emissions from oil and gas producers, targeting regulations aimed at limiting the release of the potent greenhouse gas.

The regulations were introduced under former Democratic President Joe Biden's administration.

"Tonight, I'm officially terminating Biden's expensive and unnecessary methane regulations to save consumers and energy producers $45 billion," Trump said at the rally in Lubbock, Texas.

Trump told a cheering crowd that the move would "unlock vast quantities of Texas natural gas." Texas is the largest fossil fuel-producing state in the United States.

"Americans must be able to afford to heat their homes in the winter and fill up their car and drive to work," said EPA administrator Lee Zeldin. "They can't do that when the people producing the energy are weighed down by unnecessary burdens."

Methane, the main component of natural gas, is the second-largest contributor to global warming after carbon dioxide, accounting for roughly a third of the rise in global temperatures since pre-industrial times, or around 0.5°C.

Although methane traps significantly more heat than carbon dioxide, it remains in the atmosphere for just seven to 12 years, compared with hundreds of years for CO₂.

According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), the United States is the world's largest source of methane emissions from oil and gas production, followed by Russia.

Updated: 28 October 2026 - 14:38 GMT+1
At least 10 people died when a high-speed train struck another near Toledo, Spain. Two carriages derailed, leaving rescuers searching through the wreckage as authorities warned the death toll could rise.
… More
The crash occurred between Urda and Los Yébenes, in Toledo province, when a train travelling from Madrid towards Seville struck the rear of another that had stopped on the same track. Two carriages were knocked off the rails, one of them torn apart, with twisted metal and debris scattered down a gravel embankment. Aerial footage shows the damaged trains surrounded by firefighters, police and rescue teams in a flat, rural landscape. Castilla-La Mancha government spokeswoman Esther Padilla confirmed 10 deaths but warned that the toll was likely to rise.

The collision involved a RENFE train carrying 397 passengers and an IRYO service with 147 people on board. The cause remains unknown. Emergency workers concentrated on reaching victims in the two worst-damaged carriages, while injured passengers were taken to nearby hospitals. A military emergency unit was deployed, and rail services between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia were suspended.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez cancelled his engagements to focus on the disaster. It was Spain's second major rail tragedy of 2026, following a January collision in southern Spain that killed 45 people. That earlier accident had already raised concerns about safety on the country's extensive high-speed rail network.

A US official, speaking on condition of anonymity due to visa privacy laws, said “the State Department will issue visas to Flavio Bolsonaro and his family and allow them entry into the United States.”

Bolsonaro's arrival in the United States follows conflicting reports about his movements after leaving Brazil despite a court order banning him from travelling abroad.

Argentine authorities said on Wednesday that the 45-year-old had entered Argentina "with valid travel documents and no legal impediment."

The country's migration agency said Bolsonaro travelled with his wife and two daughters aboard "a private flight operated by the company Baires Fly S.A. bound for the city of Guayaquil, Ecuador."

The same plane Argentine athorities said was carrying Bolsonaro then made a brief stop in Ecuador before reportedly flying to an unknown destination.

Earlier, US media outlets CNN and The Washington Post had reported that Bolsonaro arrived in the United States on Tuesday, citing anonymous sources. His reported arrival location was not disclosed.

Bolsonaro, who received tacit support from US President Donald Trump, is under investigation in Brazil for alleged corruption, money laundering and tax evasion.

He was ordered to surrender his passport and is expected to lose his parliamentary immunity following his election defeat.

Bolsonaro has maintained close ties with US President Donald Trump, whom he visited at the White House in May.

Trump, who did not formally endorse Bolsonaro in the runoff against Lula, said on Wednesday that he would welcome him to the United States, describing him and his family as "fine people".

South Africa's state-owned transport company Transnet says it has tested the world's longest freight train, measuring 7.6 kilometres and made up of 696 wagons.

The train completed an 860-kilometre journey from Saldanha Bay on the country's west coast to an inland iron ore mine on 18 October. The wagons were empty during the test run.

Electric and diesel locomotives positioned along the train provided the power needed to move the massive convoy.

Transnet says the train surpasses the previous record set in 2001 by a 4.5-mile-long freight train travelling from an iron ore mine to a port in Western Australia.

The company presented the journey as a milestone for South Africa's rail freight network, although the record claim comes from Transnet itself.

Cyclone Arnab strengthened into a “very severe” cyclonic storm late Tuesday with gusting winds and heavy rain reported in several coastal areas.
Ferocious winds and torrential rain lashed India's southeastern coast as Cyclone Arnab made landfall on Wednesday, forcing nearly 100,000 people to evacuate their homes in two states, authorities said.

Arnab hit Andhra Pradesh as a "very severe cyclonic storm", with winds of 125-135 kilometres per hour and gusts reaching 150 kph, the India Meteorological Department (IMD) said.

"Very severe cyclonic storm Arnab is continuing to interact with the land surface," the IMD said in a statement, along the coasts between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam.

Authorities ordered thousands to move inland or take shelter in the states of Andhra Pradesh and Odisha ahead of the storm, while neighbouring Sri Lanka and Nepal also issued weather warnings.

"Protecting every life is our foremost priority," Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister Nara Chandrababu Naidu said as the storm approached the coast.

As many as 50,000 people have been told to move to safe areas in Andhra Pradesh, and 48,000 in Odisha.

India's weather bureau warned of "total destruction" of thatched houses, localised flooding, the uprooting of power and communications poles, and "widespread damage" to standing crops.

The state’s disaster management authority said more than 12 million people live along the cyclone’s expected path.

Authorities set up over 2,000 cyclone shelters across 20 districts, while emergency alerts were being sent to the mobile phones of millions of residents, said Prakhar Jain, head of the Andra Pradesh state disaster management authority.

More than 30,000 fishing boats were secured along the coast, while nearly 1,100 medical teams were deployed across affected areas to provide emergency assistance, he added.

Supporters of Gadi Eisenkot celebrated in Tel Aviv as exit polls gave Israel's opposition bloc a narrow lead over Benjamin Netanyahu's camp, but no outright parliamentary majority.
… More
As the projections appeared on giant screens, Eisenkot’s supporters broke into chants of “Bibi, go home!” and “Who’s coming? The next prime minister!”

The polls put the anti-Netanyahu bloc on 52–53 seats against about 50 for the prime minister’s allies, leaving both short of the 61 needed for a majority in the 120-seat Knesset.

Likud remains the largest single party with 24–25 seats, while Yashar is projected second on 21–23. Eisenkot declared victory and said the results gave him the first right to form a government.

Elsewhere in Tel Aviv, activists from the Pink Front gathered at Habima Square, waving their trademark pink heart flags, wading into the reflecting pool and toasting with sparkling wine as the first results came in.

Among those at the Yashar rally was Ohad Ben Ami, a former hostage held by Hamas and an honorary candidate on the party’s list, who criticised the government’s handling of negotiations and argued that the captives had been abandoned.

The projections remain preliminary, and weeks of coalition negotiations are expected before a prime minister is confirmed.

Updated: 28 October 2026 - 0:12 GMT+1
Demonstrators gathered outside Portugal's parliament to protest against the housing crisis and police violence during recent camp clearances. The president has called for dialogue while defending police actions.
… More
Keys, cardboard houses and red carnations filled the air outside Portugal's parliament on Tuesday evening as thousands of demonstrators rallied in Lisbon over the housing crisis and alleged police violence.

Protesters sang “É a nossa morada” (“This is where we live”) and chanted against fascism, while banners called for housing rights and condemned the treatment of activists. Singer Marisa Liz joined the gathering.

The demonstration followed police operations to clear protest camps in Lisbon and Porto, during which officers used batons and made dozens of arrests. Activists claimed 60 people were injured, while police reported seven minor injuries.

President António José Seguro criticised the violence and urged dialogue, saying he would await the results of investigations. Police defended their actions as proportionate.

Demonstrators want the government to abandon plans to make evictions easier and lift rent caps on new rental agreements.
