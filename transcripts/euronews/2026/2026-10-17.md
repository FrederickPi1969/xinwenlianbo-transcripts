Updated: 17/10/2026 - 23:01 GMT+2
A fire engulfed the historic town hall in Limoges, central France, after a group set the 143-year-old building alight amid unrest following nationwide cost-of-living protests.

Local prefect Maurice Barate said the roof had collapsed onto the lower floors, except for the west wing, and that around two-thirds of the building’s exterior had already disappeared.

The blaze followed demonstrations that escalated into violence in several French cities on Saturday. The extent of the damage to the town hall was still being assessed.

Updated: 17/10/2026 - 18:29 GMT+2
Protesters broke into Marseille’s town hall on Saturday during nationwide demonstrations over rising prices in France, clashing with police as tensions escalated in the southern city.

According to AFP journalists at the scene, demonstrators forced their way through the building’s doors, overturned a bust of Marianne, a symbol of the French Republic, and damaged furniture inside the entrance hall. Police later intervened to clear the building.

Earlier, masked protesters had also attacked the façade of a municipal police station, setting fire to rubbish bins and throwing smoke devices inside.

A branch of the tourist office in Lyon was vandalised and set on fire during nationwide protests over the cost of living.

Zelenskyy's deputy chief of staff told Euronews earlier this week that the Ukrainian leader had been invited to Budapest on 23 October, to join events commemorating the 70th anniversary of the 1956 Hungarian Revolution.
Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar said he did not expect Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to visit Budapest later this month, despite having extended an invitation to Kyiv.

More than 100 politicians have been invited to state ceremonies on 23 October to mark the 70th anniversary of the 1956 Hungarian Revolution.

However, in an interview with 444.hu published on Saturday, Magyar said he thought Zelenskyy's attendance was unlikely, even though Russian President Vladimir Putin had not been invited.

It comes after Magyar and Zelenskyy said at the EU summit in Brussels on Friday that they could meet again later this year.

"Either I will travel to Kyiv and from there we will go together to Beregszász, or the president will come to Budapest and from there we will go to Beregszász," Magyar said.

In post on X following their Brussels meeting, the Hungarian PM said he had expressed his "full solidarity" with Ukraine as it continues to battle Russia’s ongoing invasion.

He added that the pair had agreed to "continue talks at ministerial level, with a special focus on improving the situation of minorities in both countries" and that they believed it was "in both countries’ interests to build a new foundation for our political and economic relations."

Zelenskyy's deputy chief of staff had told Euronews earlier this week that the Ukrainian leader had been invited to Budapest for the 23 October commemorations amid efforts to reset relations between Kyiv and Budapest after years of diplomatic disputes that have repeatedly obstructed Ukraine's path towards EU membership.

"We have received an invitation. On 23 October, an event will be held to mark the 70th anniversary of the Hungarian Revolution," Zhovkva said. "The President of Ukraine has received an invitation to attend. We shall see."

Super tanker ablaze after striking mine in Strait of Hormuz, IRGC says

A super tanker has caught fire after striking a sea mine in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said on Saturday.

In a statement shared via Iranian state media, the IRGC's navy said a tanker carrying more than two million barrels of oil struck a mine whilst "attempting to pass through an unauthorised route" in the Strait, causing a "massive explosion" and a "fierce fire." It is unclear exactly where the incident took place.

The IRGC navy accused the tanker of having turned off its positioning and radar systems in an effort to evade detection.

"The Strait of Hormuz is closed, and the consequence of defying Iranian regulations in the Strait of Hormuz is burning in the fire of trust in the terrorist US army," the IRGC navy added in its statement.

The Strait of Hormuz is a crucial gas and oil transit chokepoint that has been at the heart of tensions between Washington and Tehran since the Iran war broke out in late February.

Iran effectively cut off the passage, which usually carries around 25% of the world’s seaborne oil trade, following US and Israeli strikes on the country, prompting the US to impose a naval blockade on Iranian ports.

The US military has repeatedly pushed back on claims that the IRGC has control of the Strait, saying commercial vessels have continued to transit the region "despite unwarranted IRGC aggression."

Admiral Brad Cooper, the commander of US Central Command (CENTCOM), said in an update on Friday that US forces were working with regional allies and partners in the shipping industry to help protect vessels navigating the Strait.

"Let me be clear, the Strait of Hormuz is controlled by the United States and our regional partners, not Iran," he said. "It remains open for business."
