The institution, founded in 1935 by an Italian priest, houses more than 400 people with disabilities on a 50 hectare site. Known as the "city of charity", the centre has suffered the consequences of cuts to health and social services, as well as funding difficulties for specialised care and therapy programmes.

During his tour, Leo XIV hugged residents in wheelchairs, talked with them and gave them his blessing. The arrival of the Pope brought some workers and residents to tears. Addressing them, he said that their lives are a blessing and not a burden for society.

In the centre's chapel, the Holy Father defended the dignity of all people and called for the importance of solidarity to be restored in the face of the pursuit of economic success and the cult of appearances. "The progress of a nation is also measured by how it welcomes all people with disabilities and particular needs," he said.

The message carries particular weight in a context of tension between the Argentine government and organisations working in the field of disability. Last year Congress overturned Milei's veto of a law that provided for an increase in funds for this sector. However, the executive has resisted implementing it, arguing that it could put at risk the fiscal balance achieved.

As a consequence, care centres such as Cottolengo have outstanding debts amounting to hundreds of thousands of euros. In addition, the government's adjustment measures have affected specialised therapy programmes that for decades have been fundamental for many of the approximately five million people with disabilities living in Argentina.

The papal visit also comes just a few days before the Senate is due to study a draft bill that seeks to reserve funds for disability benefits in next year's budget. Milei opposes this initiative.

Kuyen Rolon Rey, a Cottolengo volunteer, highlighted how important it is that Leo XIV chose to start his tour of Argentina precisely in this establishment. "The people here deserve this grace," he said. He also praised the fact that the Pontiff chose a place that is small in size, but big in the love it represents.

The visit to the care centre opened a day in which Leo XIV was scheduled to meet at the Catholic University of Argentina with business representatives, trade union leaders and representatives of popular movements.

The meeting is part of the message of Catholic social teaching that the Pope is developing during his trip: putting the weakest and most vulnerable people at the centre of economic decisions. This line also revives an integral part of the legacy of Pope Francis, who was Argentine by birth.

On Sunday, during his first speech in the country and in Milei's presence, Leo XIV had already recalled one of his predecessor's recurring ideas: money must be at the service of people and not the other way round. "The economy must serve the person, not the person the economy," he said.

Those expected to take part in Monday's meeting include representatives of national manufacturers, affected by the arrival of low-priced imported products after Milei removed trade restrictions that had been in force for years.

Members of the main Argentine trade union confederation were also invited, whose representatives conveyed to the Vatican in September their concern about job losses and falling wages.

Since coming to power at the end of 2023, Milei has driven a broad austerity programme that has turned him into one of the leading figures in the international conservative backlash against the liberal establishment.

His government has defended cuts to disability programmes as part of a reform process aimed at eliminating fraud and unnecessary spending in the federal administration.

Pope Leo XIV was welcomed by crowds in Claypole, near Buenos Aires, as he visited the Don Orione care home. The pontiff greeted the faithful and unveiled a plaque during his 12-day South American tour.
… More
Standing in the popemobile, Pope Leo XIV waved to crowds lining the route before stepping down to greet people waiting near the entrance. Footage shows him walking past the faithful, acknowledging those who had come to see him, then pausing in the chapel before the relic of St Luigi Orione's heart.

At the Pequeño Cottolengo Don Orione in Claypole, in Almirante Brown partido some 30km south of Buenos Aires, the pope unveiled a commemorative plaque and toured part of the 50-hectare site, which houses more than 400 people with disabilities in 16 homes. Welcomed by Bishop Jorge Rubén Lugones of Lomas de Zamora and the centre's director, Brother Jorge David Silanes, he told residents their lives "are not a burden for society, they are a blessing", and said a nation's progress is measured by how it welcomes people with disabilities.

The visit forms part of his 12-day tour of Uruguay, Argentina and Peru. Argentina is the homeland of his predecessor, Pope Francis, and had not hosted a reigning pontiff since John Paul II in 1987. Leo XIV arrived in Buenos Aires on Sunday 8 November, welcomed by President Javier Milei at Jorge Newbery. The choice of Don Orione, founded in 1935 as a "city of charity", turned attention on care services at a moment when disability funding is politically contested.

The IIHF has moved Norway, ranked 10th, to Division 1A to free a place for Russia at the tournament in Alberta, after the Court of Arbitration for Sport ordered Russian teams reinstated. The change is not yet confirmed, and Russia has not been guaranteed Canadian visas.
Norway's junior ice hockey team has been dropped from the World Junior Championship in Canada to make room for Russia, following a Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) ruling ordering Russian teams to be reinstated in international competition.

Norway, which ranked 10th, has been moved to Division 1A to free a place for Russia at the tournament, which runs from 26 December to 5 January in Alberta. Tickets for the event, including for Norway's matches, are already on sale.

The International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) has yet to formally confirm the change. Norway remained listed as a participant on the IIHF website on Monday.

Russia's participation is also uncertain. The Russian team has not been guaranteed visas to enter Canada, which could restore Norway's place.

"The situation is still unclear," Norwegian Ice Hockey Federation general secretary Petter Salsten said in a statement.

"That gives us hope that the question of our participation in the tournament in Canada has not yet been finally settled."

Salsten said the federation disagrees with the CAS ruling but is obliged to comply. He added that the situation "clearly illustrates all the practical difficulties and immediate consequences for everyone involved if countries are immediately reinstated in full."

The IIHF has flagged serious problems with scheduling, participant safety and tournament logistics that Russia's potential return would create.

Six countries, including Norway, have pledged to boycott matches against Russia for as long as the war in Ukraine continues. Several long-standing corporate sponsors of ice hockey have also said they will withdraw support if Russia is allowed to compete.

The CAS ruling covers five world championships in 2027, including the senior men's tournament scheduled for May in Germany.

Ukraine, which qualified for the senior men's championship for the first time since 2007, could be displaced if Russia is reinstated, as the lowest-ranked team would have to give way.

The German government said on Friday that holding the championship in Germany with Russia participating was "virtually impossible," following consultations with the IIHF, the German Ice Hockey Federation (DEB) and the German Olympic Sports Confederation (DOSB).

DEB chief executive Frank Lutz has said Germany may consider relinquishing its hosting rights.

Russia has been suspended from international ice hockey following its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in early 2022. At its last world championship in 2021, the Russian team finished fourth.

Leningrad Governor Alexander Drozdenko said air defences shot down 113 drones targeting the region, but debris from one struck the roof of a multi-storey building in Vsevolozhsk, killing a resident.

KINEF, the largest refinery in northwest Russia and second largest in the country behind the Omsk facility, is owned by the Surgutneftegaz group and processes roughly 18 million tonnes of crude oil a year, about 7% of Russia's total refining capacity.

The plant produces petrol, diesel, aviation kerosene, fuel oil, bitumen and petrochemical products, and supplies aviation fuel to St Petersburg's Pulkovo airport. Its design capacity is 20-21 million tonnes annually.

The refinery has been targeted multiple times by Ukrainian forces since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022. Surgutneftegaz was placed under EU, US and UK sanctions in 2025.

Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces shot down 430 Ukrainian drones across 14 regions and occupied Crimea overnight. Euronews could not independently verify these claims.

Rosneft announced on Monday it was capping petrol and diesel sales at its filling stations. The restrictions took effect in Karelia, Buryatia, Zabaykalsky Krai, and the Novosibirsk, Arkhangelsk and Penza regions, according to Russian media.

In cities, customers are limited to 30 litres of petrol per transaction; on motorways, the cap is 50 litres. In Krasnoyarsk Krai, separate limits apply: 30 litres of AI-95, 50 litres of AI-92 and 100 litres of diesel per customer.

Authorities in Leningrad region also recommended filling stations cap petrol sales at 30 litres and diesel at 50 litres per transaction.

Russian forces launched 317 Shahed drones since Monday evening, including 105 jet-powered variants and Banderol/Dan-T, Gerbera and decoy drones, Ukraine's air force said. Of these, 276 were shot down. Impacts were recorded in 31 locations and falling debris in nine more.

A drone strike on a food warehouse in Odesa on Monday morning killed three people and left one in serious condition.

Two people were killed and seven injured in strikes on Dnipropetrovsk region. Nine people, including children, were injured in attacks on Kharkiv region overnight on Tuesday.

Six people, including one child, were wounded in strikes on Zaporizhzhia and the surrounding area.

Seven people were injured in a drone strike on Kremenchuk in Poltava region, among them a 16-year-old. A rehabilitation centre for children with disabilities was destroyed in the attack.

In Sumy, three power workers were injured on Monday morning when a Russian strike hit their vehicle.

Russian forces struck rescue teams in Kamyshivakha three times with drones as they cleared rubble from a Russian air strike on 2 November that killed 55 people. Up to 19 may still be trapped under the debris. Police have identified 30 of the dead.

The British comedian was found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault on Thursday following a four-week trial.
British comedian Russell Brand was sentenced to 10 years and six months for rape and sexual assault on Monday.

“About 20 years ago, Russell Brand, you were becoming famous,” Judge Joel Bennathan said. “You were clever — and in many people’s eyes, funny and charismatic. You had also become a sexual predator.”

Following a four-week trial at Southwark Crown Court, Brand was unanimously convicted by a jury on Thursday of four sexual offences, one count of rape and three counts of sexual assaults, involving four women between 2005 and 2009 in different locations.

In addition, Brand was cleared of two counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault. However, he remains under investigation after London police said it was looking into rape allegations made by two other women.

The British actor-comedian who turned into a conservative, religious online guru claimed he was innocent and that all his encounters were consensual.

“Mr. Brand abused the influence and power that came with his fame," Prosecutor Riel Karmy-Jones said. “He used it to facilitate an access to women without which the offending could not have taken place. It also fed his own view that he could commit such offenses with impunity.”

His wife Laura Gallacher, with whom he has three children, slumped in her seat in the public gallery of London's Southwark Crown Court as the total sentence of 10 years and six months was announced.

Brand told his wife, who has supported him in court throughout the case, "we'll be OK" as he was led down to the cells.

Speaking in court out of view to preserve her identity, one of the women he was convicted of abusing said the assault left her in shock and made her feel like "damaged goods."

“For many years as his star was rising it was impossible to escape seeing him all over the media, being celebrated for his disgusting behaviour against women like it was all a joke," she said. “There were many times I felt visibly sick at the sight of him parading around thinking he can get away with anything."

“I felt that if I reported him, I would not be believed,” she said in a statement. “His fame, wealth and influence created a power imbalance which made me feel powerless.”

Iran labels ships transiting the strait's southern route under US naval guidance as violating its rules.

On Saturday, CENTCOM reported an attack on a commercial ship in the Gulf of Oman, saying it had repeatedly attempted to breach the US naval blockade against Iran.

Iran’s IRGC said two oil tankers caught fire after striking mines while crossing the strait.

Trump subsequently said the force was operating at full capacity alongside what he described as the largest naval blockade in history.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Iran’s demands were not only unacceptable but in some cases “overblown, ridiculous and really unrealistic.”

He said he could report no progress in talks, that steps taken so far had not been very constructive, and that US actions in the Strait of Hormuz were defensive.

In 27 Russian regions, there is a generalised HIV epidemic. This was reported by the Central Research Institute of Epidemiology of Rospotrebnadzor. Scientists use this term for the stage when the human immunodeficiency virus spreads in the general population rather than only among risk groups.

And although, according to the Central Research Institute for the Prevention and Control of AIDS, 45,000 new HIV cases were detected in Russia in 2025, which is 13.4% lower than in 2024, last year a record was set for the number of HIV and hepatitis infections in medical institutions, according to statistics compiled by the specialist portal Medvestnik.

These are the highest figures in the past 15 years. Clusters of viral hepatitis C and mixed HIV plus hepatitis infections predominate, said Yelena Igonina, head of the Department for the Epidemiological Wellbeing of the Population at Rospotrebnadzor. In 2025, eight clusters of infection were recorded in healthcare facilities in Russia, with 104 patients infected.

According to Rospotrebnadzor, investigations continued in 2026 into 11 clusters of infection in the territory of ten regions of Russia, where the spread of HIV and hepatitis in medical organisations is suspected. In Moscow, an investigation is underway into a cluster in which 22 cases of viral hepatitis C have been identified.

One of the largest confirmed clusters in the past two years was the infection of 82 people in a sanatorium in Kabardino-Balkaria. While receiving medical care, 76 patients contracted viral hepatitis C and six were found to have mixed infections. In oncology hospitals in Kamchatka Territory and Buryatia, 147 and 42 patients, respectively, were infected with hepatitis. Another three hepatitis C clusters were identified in dialysis centres in the Samara and Kursk regions, where 26 people were affected.

Experts describe violations of sanitary and epidemiological rules as the main cause of the infections, including the use of single-use catheters or glucometers on several patients. In such situations, the source is usually patients with undiagnosed infections.

Yelena Igonina notes that these "systemic and gross violations" have become entrenched in today’s Russia. Although simultaneous testing for hepatitis C antibodies and RNA covers blood, component, organ and tissue donors well, testing for RNA among other categories that should be tested, namely dialysis, haematology and transplant patients, contacts in hepatitis clusters, children under one born to mothers with HIV and patients with immunodeficiency, is significantly lagging behind.

In addition, for the first time in six years, two cases of HIV infection via transfusion of blood components were identified in 2026, the Central Research Institute of Epidemiology of Rospotrebnadzor added.

When hospitals receive large numbers of people with undiagnosed infections, international HIV consultant Aleksei Verter told Current Time, this means that "the infectious process in the country is latent, hidden" and a great many people do not know they are infected.

Despite a decline in HIV incidence and mortality in Russia in recent years, the number of people infected continues to grow, Medvestnik’s authors note. Statistics show that for each death there are one and a half new infections, that is, the number of people "living with HIV continues to grow constantly", explains Natalya Ladnaya.

The decline was preceded by a long period of rising incidence, but in the past three years rates of morbidity and mortality among people with HIV have been falling and, according to her preliminary data, this trend will continue in 2026. At the same time, the expert is convinced that there is no reason to rest on one’s laurels, since "the dynamics can change rapidly".

In recent years, the number of deaths from HIV in Russia has been between 30,000 and 34,000 per year. This is an extremely high figure, stresses the head of the research department for the prevention and control of AIDS at the Central Research Institute of Rospotrebnadzor. "Among all the countries in the world there are only 15 where more than 15,000 deaths of people with HIV are recorded, including Russia." "The highest HIV incidence rates per 100,000 population in the European region are in Russia, Ukraine and North Macedonia", says Central Research Institute of Epidemiology director and Rospotrebnadzor academician Vasily Akimkin.

HIV infection has become the leading cause of death among Russians of working age. Among men aged 40 to 49, more than 3% are affected by the virus, clarifies Natalya Ladnaya, while the average age of those infected in Russia has increased over the past 15 years from 32 to 44. It is also noteworthy that two-thirds of new HIV transmissions occur through heterosexual contact.

More than 1.232 million people with a confirmed HIV diagnosis now live in Russia, which is 0.8% of the country’s population, Akimkin adds. Rospotrebnadzor has warned that if current approaches to prevention and treatment remain unchanged, the number of people living with HIV in Russia could reach 1.47 million by 2030, according to the agency’s mathematical model. Under this scenario, the annual number of new cases will be between 31,000 and 51,000, and the number of deaths included in the model could rise to 51,000 a year, which is linked, among other things, to population ageing.

In the past seven years, the main causes of death among children with HIV have also changed. An increase in mortality in this group of patients was recorded in 2025 in all federal districts except the Central and Ural districts.

According to an analysis by several Russian media outlets, Rospotrebnadzor and the Health Ministry calculate figures differently. Rospotrebnadzor’s data are more complete, as they are based on laboratory notifications of every positive immunoblot. The Health Ministry’s data are based on the number of people who have been registered for regular follow-up care. As a result, the overall gap between the total number of people with a confirmed diagnosis, 1.23 million according to Rospotrebnadzor, and those actually under medical supervision, 846,500 according to the Health Ministry, amounts to 385,555 people.

Only half of people living with HIV in Russia had a suppressed viral load by the end of 2025, said Natalya Ladnaya at the conference "Epidemiological Surveillance of HIV Infection", as quoted by Medvestnik. In practice, this means that at least 600,000 people with HIV "continue to play an active part in transmitting the virus", the expert concludes.

Viral load suppression occurred last year in 77.9% of people with HIV who were receiving antiretroviral therapy (ART), and "so far it has not been possible to exceed this level", she says, while WHO standards for keeping the epidemic under control are 95-95-95, that is, 95% of people with HIV know their status, 95% of them receive ART and 95% of those on therapy achieve suppression of HIV replication.

As for access to medical care for people living with HIV in Russia, 72% of those infected were under follow-up last year. Despite extensive HIV testing, which has increased by 75% over the past decade and reached 54 million tests last year, effectiveness is declining. In the view of the director of the Central Research Institute of Epidemiology of Rospotrebnadzor, approaches need to be adjusted by increasing the share of people at the highest risk of infection, who are insufficiently covered by testing. Their share has now almost halved, from 5.8% in 2014 to 3% in 2023.

But the effectiveness of the work is declining, meaning there are more tests, but testing is increasingly carried out among people with a lower probability of having HIV.

The fact that the burden of HIV infection is not decreasing even as incidence falls is reflected in healthcare spending, stresses Natalya Ladnaya. In 2025, spending related to HIV infection was estimated at 279 billion roubles.

In the expert’s view, there is no reason to expect the situation to change; on the contrary, the spread of the virus may intensify among traditionally vulnerable population groups and among people living in regions bordering Ukraine that are characterised by high levels of migration. According to Rospotrebnadzor, incidence and mortality may also rise in the Far Eastern, Southern and North Caucasian federal districts.

Bloggers have also spoken about the spread of viruses, including HIV, among those fighting against Ukraine. According to Anastasia Kashevarova, the Russian authorities should "seriously think about it, there is a threat of an epidemic of socially dangerous diseases", since hepatitis, HIV and tuberculosis are currently spreading in the army and to frontline regions, but "will then spread further into the interior of Russia".

The Insider previously reported that after Russia’s occupation of parts of Donetsk and Luhansk regions, patients with HIV faced shortages of life-saving medicines. The Russian Health Ministry did not include them in centralised drug procurement, and some patients were given medicines past their expiry date.

According to observers, the fact that the healthcare budget is shrinking and all funds are going to the war helps explain, among other things, shortages of sterile consumables in state hospitals.

US President Donald Trump said he would possibly meet with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China to discuss "denuclearisation" and how to end the Iran war.
Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping might meet in China for trilateral talks next week to discuss the ending of the Iran war.

Trump told reporters he expected to meet Russian and Chinese leaders at an Asia-Pacific summit in Shenzhen, China, scheduled for November 18-19.

"Well, I'm going to be going to China," Trump said on Sunday, "and one of the meetings I think will be with President Putin and President Xi, and that'll be interesting."

He added: "We'll talk a lot of things, including denuclearisation, but we'll be talking about a lot of things. We're trying to end the war in Iran."

If it happens, it would be historic, although the three leaders are unlikely to discuss anything substantive because their interests are at odds. This one-of-a-kind trilateral meeting has been acknowledged by the Kremlin on 21 October, saying it was "being prepared."

This followed a Wall Street Journal report that Putin wanted the three-way talks to broker a peace deal in Ukraine that would be favourable to Moscow, which invaded Ukraine in 2022.

"I think it will happen. The question is the format and the content," said Sun Yun, director of the China program at the Stimson Center. "China wants the image but not the costs."

In the US, Trump is often criticised for having a penchant for authoritarian leaders like those of China and Russia, among others.

"Putin has the most to gain from a leaders’ trilateral, and the Russians are clearly behind the push for a meeting, which would be a diplomatic coup," said Danny Russel, a former senior State Department official who served in former President Barack Obama’s administration.

Trump, with his "crush on authoritarian strongmen," would love the meeting, though he clearly has been warned by his advisers to steer clear of Ukraine as a topic of the discussions, said Russel.

Trump is expected to travel to Shenzhen to meet Xi for the third time this year, amid a fragile trade truce set to expire in January. Beijing is hosting this year’s leaders' summit of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation, a 21-member grouping that includes the US and Russia.

His willingness to meet with Putin also shows the desire to end Russia's isolation that was triggered by its invasion.

The meeting may amount to no more than a photo opportunity that would allow Putin to be seen next to US and Chinese leaders, said Craig Singleton, senior director of the China program at the Foundation for Defence of Democracies.

"But these photo opportunities have diminishing diplomatic returns," Singleton said. "The spectacle may be historic without producing anything particularly consequential."

Jensen Huang, CEO of US tech giant Nvidia, is being investigated by the company's board after reports that Huang was the “target of a foreign intelligence effort.” Huang admitted to having an intimate relationship with a business executive who is alleged to have been spying on behalf of China.
US tech giant Nvidia is opening a probe into its CEO, Jensen Huang, after reports that Huang was the “target of a foreign intelligence effort,” the company's board of directors announced on Sunday.

A report by The New York Times said that the CEO had admitted to the board that he had been "unfaithful" with a business executive who, according to US intelligence, was gathering information for China.

Nvidia is one of the global leaders in the development of artificial intelligence. Its chips power many of the data centres and computing systems used to train and run AI models. Its advanced chips have also been targeted by US export restrictions to prevent China from getting the latest semiconductor technology.

In a letter addressed to Nvidia employees, Huang said that he took "full responsibility" for his actions, while admitting to being unfaithful to his wife and apologising to his family and colleagues. However, he did not mention the name of the person involved or her reported ties to Beijing.

“Leading Nvidia is the privilege of my life,” the 63-year-old wrote. “My responsibility is to serve you and be worthy of your trust. I have fallen short of that responsibility.”

China has consistantly rejected the spying allegations, with foreign ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun telling a regular briefing on Monday that "the narrative of a so-called Chinese spy is entirely baseless vilification and smearing."

The board leading the company, which is one of the most valuable in the world, says it has "no reason to believe" that either national security or Nvidia's secrets were compromised, but that a thorough independent investigation would be carried out. The board added that Huang "fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it."

In his letter to employees, Jensen Huang said, “I love our country and our company. I am committed to protecting Nvidia and supporting America’s national security.”

George Chen, partner and chair of digital practice at The Asia Group, told AFP that "it would be premature to conclude that Jensen poses a threat to US national security before the facts are fully established."

"The key questions are whether he was aware of any espionage activities and whether any confidential or strategically sensitive information was compromised."

Last month, The New York Times reported that US intelligence agencies had discovered that Huang had developed close ties with Chinese citizen and chip buyer Alice Huang, and that she was directly sharing information with Beijing.

Alice Huang is a top executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company that runs data centres across Southeast Asia and has become Nvidia’s largest customer in the region.

Meanwhile, US President Donald Trump was asked on Sunday whether he should have acted on the spying information sooner. However, Trump praised Huang, saying, “I think he’s an honorable guy. I think he probably has an excuse for it. He’s done an amazing job for our country.”

Trump is heading to the Asia-Pacific summit in China's Shenzhen next week and told reporters Huang is invited, saying: "I don't know but I'll certainly take him."
