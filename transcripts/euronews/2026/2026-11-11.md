The island nation's highest court overturned a 1986 law criminalising consensual sexual relations between adults of the same sex.
A decade-long legal battle led by LGBT+ Trinidadian activists successfully ended as Trinidad and Tobago's highest court overturned a long-running law criminalising gay sex.

The UK-based final court of appeal for British dependencies and overseas territories issued a unanimous decision Tuesday that the 1986 Sexual Offences Act could not be used to prosecute private, consensual sexual relations between adults of the same sex.

Seen as a landmark ruling for LGBT+ rights in the Caribbean, this law had previously carried sentences of two years for "serious indecency" and five years for "buggery".

"What I'm going to be doing in the coming months is very targeted activism about how the Caribbean, not just Trinidad and Tobago, deals with endemic homophobia," said LGBT+ Trinidadian activist Jason Jones after the verdict was read.

Freedom Law Chambers, the law firm that represented Jones, said in a statement that the judgement represented a "phenomenal victory for gay rights, equal dignity and respect for private and family life in Trinidad and Tobago."

The Judicial Committee of the Privy Council, which issued the decision, said constitutional rights must be interpreted in a way that makes them "practical and effective in the present day, not in the past."

The judgement was welcomed by UN Human Rights chief Volker Turk, who called it a significant milestone for rights and equality.

"I hope the judgment will help remove a longstanding source of stigma, exclusion, barriers and violence experienced by Trinidadians and Tobagonians based on sexual orientation and gender identity," Turk said.

Heavy rain, rough seas and strong winds hit Costa da Caparica in Portugal, with coastal facilities closed and streets left waterlogged.
… More
Powerful Atlantic waves crashed against the shoreline at Costa da Caparica, south of Lisbon, on Wednesday, November 11, as Storm Charles brought heavy rain and strong winds to Portugal.

The coastal resort saw waterlogged streets, overturned electric scooters and deserted seafront terraces, while a warning sign marked the closure of a pier.

Portugal’s weather agency issued red warnings for persistent heavy rain and strong winds in parts of the country. Waves along the Lisbon and Setúbal coastlines were forecast to reach up to seven metres, with maximum heights potentially reaching 13 metres.

Authorities urged residents to stay away from beaches and exposed coastal areas due to the risk of flooding and dangerous sea conditions.

EU Ambassador to Qatar Antonino Maggiore tells Euronews that Europe plans to strengthen defence cooperation with Qatar and the Gulf countries, as "every crisis brings opportunities," while also deepening political and economic cooperation with the region.
The European Union and Qatar are deepening their defence cooperation, and "security is fundamental in the current geopolitical context and it's a mutual interest," the EU's new ambassador to Qatar Antonino Maggiore told Euronews in Doha.

Maggiore described the EU's defence cooperation thrust in Qatar and the Gulf countries as following two main objectives: connecting private defence industries through a new platform and presenting the EU's common defence industry policies to Qatar and the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries.

In a wide-ranging interview with Euronews at the start of his mandate in Qatar, Maggiore said that the EU and Qatar are organising a seminar on defence technology and artificial intelligence in Doha on 3 December, to connect private-to-private industries, "to create a platform for our defence industry to work together with the Qataris counterpart and possibly also from the GCC."

Maggiore said the EU wants to build on existing defence partnerships between Qatar and individual European countries, including France, Italy, Spain and Germany.

The seminar will also give the European Commission an opportunity to outline its plans towards the common European defence industry and discuss possible future partnerships with countries outside the bloc.

"There is a debate ongoing in the European Union on how, especially with the new multi-annual financial framework, which is our budget, how we can explore the possibility to strengthen partnership with third-party countries, also as EU, in the defence sector," Maggiore said.

The defence talks follow an EU-GCC summit scheduled for 24 October that was postponed due to ongoing tensions in the Gulf.

Maggiore acknowledged the setback but said both sides remained committed to strengthening relations.

"It's a missed opportunity that we have to postpone, but there is no postponement of the objectives and the urgency of strengthening the relations," he said. "There is a reciprocal wish of each other, the European Union, Qatar, the GCC."

The ambassador pointed to a recent visit to Doha by EU Special Representative for the Gulf Luigi Di Maio as evidence that diplomatic engagement was continuing unabated.

Maggiore praised Qatar's role in mediating between the United States and Iran, saying the EU wanted to support Doha's efforts to ease regional tensions.

He highlighted a recent telephone conversation between the European Union's High Representative for Foreign Affairs Kaja Kallas and Qatar's Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani.

The ambassador also addressed efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, where shipping restrictions have disrupted global trade, saying the EU and Qatar shared the same position on freedom of navigation through the waterway.

"No fees, no tolls for the freedom of navigation," Maggiore said, referring to the EU's position.

"Member states currently have different positions on the crisis in Gaza, and of course this is a challenge for us," he said.

He added that the EU remained committed to humanitarian assistance and diplomatic efforts to end the crisis.

He confirmed that European Commissioner for Transport Apostolos Tzitzikostas would visit Doha for the upcoming Doha Forum in December to discuss connectivity with Qatar and other Gulf partners.

"Connectivity is a pillar, I think, where we can foster and boost our economic ties," he said.

The EU is also working on an initiative called Connect EU, aimed at bringing European businesses together with Qatari companies.

Maggiore identified two priorities he hopes to advance over the next year: visa liberalisation for Qatari citizens and negotiations on a strategic partnership agreement.

He said making travel easier would help strengthen ties between people in Europe and Qatar.

The proposed strategic partnership agreement would provide a broader framework for cooperation, something Maggiore hopes to see progress on within the next year.

"We do need a framework," he said, stressing the political will on both sides to strengthen the partnership.

Russia’s last openly critical newspaper Novaya Gazeta shuts its Moscow office

The paper said security services and censors forced it to stop operating after they evicted it, shut down its donation system, blocked its printing, and revoked its media registration.
Novaya Gazeta, Russia's last remaining newspaper openly critical of the Kremlin and Moscow's ongoing all-out war in Ukraine, has closed after authorities stripped it of its media registration and severed its ability to operate, the newspaper said.

In a statement on 30 October, the paper said security services and censors had "forced the editorial team to cease its activities."

The newsroom was ordered to vacate its premises, its reader donation system was shut down, its online resources were blocked, its printing arrangements were terminated, and state censors at printing facilities refused to allow the paper to publish, before its media registration was revoked.

"Elections and elected bodies of power have in effect been abolished, civil society institutions destroyed, freedom of conscience and human dignity trampled underfoot," the newspaper said in its farewell statement.

Editor-in-chief Sergei Sokolov held a final editorial meeting attended by staff, founder and Nobel laureate Dmitry Muratov and journalists from other outlets.

Staff packed their belongings and took down from the walls the portraits of murdered colleagues, among them Anna Politkovskaya, Yuri Shchekochikhin and Natalya Estemirova.

Muratov described repression in Russia as "overwhelming," saying that by 2021, before the full-scale invasion of Ukraine, hundreds of people had already been convicted under relevant statutes.

"This is because the country is not run by parliament, or by local administrations or governors, but by the FSB of the Russian Federation," he said.

In his farewell address to staff, Muratov made several requests: to support Russians opposed to the war, to push for the release of political prisoners, to seek a humanitarian corridor for villages in the former area of Oleshky, and to work to stop Russian attacks on Ukrainian cities.

"The light, heat and water in cities in Ukraine could be wiped off the face of the Earth. These cities will be poured into sacks like dust. This madness, this slaughter must be stopped," he said.

Muratov also said more than 400,000 of the newspaper's articles had been archived and were being republished worldwide.

"We will go on living, and I am sure that this country, which is going through such hard times, will sooner or later come to its senses. As soon as the first signs of this appear, Novaya Gazeta will return to our readers and colleagues," Sokolov said in his address to those gathered outside the building.

Reporters Without Borders, Amnesty International and the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights condemned Russia's closure of the newspaper.

The Kremlin dismissed its significance, with spokesman Dmitry Peskov stating that "some media outlets cease to exist and new ones take their place," which he called a "normal process".

Novaya Gazeta was founded in 1993 and became known for its coverage of the Chechen wars and its investigations into human rights abuses and corruption.

Six of its journalists were murdered. Russian authorities had designated it a "foreign agent", but the paper continued to publish until its final closure.

After the journalists left, a new sign appeared on the wall alongside the newspaper's nameplate: "Novaya Gazeta lived here."

A second claim brought under a Florida consumer protection law was thrown out, halving the original $10bn lawsuit to $5bn, but the US president has still hailed the ruling as “A Huge Victory”.
A US judge has rejected the BBC's motion to dismiss Donald Trump’s defamation lawsuit against the broadcaster allowing the case to proceed to trial.

The US president's claim is based on the way the BBC edited a speech he gave on 6 January 2021 in its Panorama documentary.

On Tuesday, US District Judge Jeffrey T. Kuntz denied the British broadcaster’s attempt to have the defamation claim dismissed. He did, however, throw out a second claim, which Trump had brought under Florida’s Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act.

Each claim was for at least $5bn (€4.48bn), meaning Kuntz's ruling halves the lawsuit from $10 billion (€8.97bn) to at least $5 billion.

The case centres on a BBC documentary that spliced together two separate parts of the speech. The edit made it appear that Trump had explicitly urged his supporters to attack the US Capitol in Washington. Trump accuses the broadcaster of deceptively editing the programme to make it look as though he was inciting violence.

In his 40-page ruling, Kuntz wrote: “Whether President Trump can prove these claims and establish he sustained damages at summary judgment or trial is left for another day.”

“Given the time disparity between the two statements, the distance itself tends toward a plausible inference that BBC knew or should have known that the segment they were creating would not and could not have been a truthful statement,” Kuntz wrote.

“This was not trimming around the edges but creating something different than what was stated in the speech.”

A BBC spokesperson said: “We are surprised by this decision but respect the Court’s authority and note the case will now proceed to the next stage. We remain confident in our position and will continue to respond through the proper legal process."

“We will continue fighting against, and defeating, all of those who try to deceive the American People!” he wrote.

Trump has also claimed the BBC had violated Florida law, specifically the Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act, but the judge ruled that the documentary did not constitute commercial speech.

The BBC has apologised for the edit, which led to the resignation of director general Tim Davie and head of news Deborah Turness. However, it denies that the programme caused Trump any real harm.

The ruling follows a setback for Trump last month, when a magistrate judge ordered him to give a deposition by 11 December. The BBC had accused him of avoiding its efforts to obtain sworn testimony from him.

The court said that if Trump fails to attend, he could face sanctions, including the possible dismissal of his lawsuit.

An Ottoman-style military band welcomes international delegates to COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, with traditional marches and lively rhythms.
… More
An Ottoman-style marching band brought a historic musical tradition to the heart of the global climate debate on Wednesday, 11 November, welcoming international delegates to the COP31 venue in Antalya, Turkey. Dressed in traditional military uniforms, members of the Turkish Ministry of National Defence's Mehter Band performed rousing marches as participants arrived for the leaders' summit. Some foreign visitors clapped along, while others began dancing to the rhythms, capturing the moment on their mobile phones.The performance added a distinctly Turkish touch to the international gathering.

The performance took place on the third day of COP31, ahead of the Antalya Leaders' Summit on 11 and 12 November, hosted by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan. The Mehter tradition is associated with the military bands of the Ottoman Empire, whose powerful percussion and wind instruments made their music a distinctive part of ceremonial occasions. The contemporary band, which operates under Turkey's Ministry of National Defence, has also performed at major international events, including the 2026 NATO summit. Against the backdrop of negotiations on climate change, the performance offered visiting delegations a glimpse of Turkey's musical heritage.

The FBI says the case is of “the utmost concern” to Washington and its allies, while army counterintelligence chiefs warn that insider threats are “real and persistent”.
A 41-year-old American soldier has been arrested near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia, in an operation British counter-terrorism police carried out with help from US military and law enforcement agencies.

The serviceman was detained on Tuesday under national security legislation and taken to a police station in London, where he remains in custody, the Metropolitan Police said.

“This significant arrest and investigation is a result of close collaboration between UK and US authorities,” said Vicki Evans, Senior National Coordinator for Counter Terrorism Policing.

Officers said the case was “not linked to any other ongoing Counter Terrorism Policing investigations.”

FBI director Kash Patel thanked British police and intelligence services for their work on the case.

“This important arrest involves allegations of espionage, which is of the utmost concern to the United States and our allies,” Patel said in the police statement.

Sean Stinchon, commanding general of US Army Counterintelligence Command, said the alleged conduct of a “senior soldier, entrusted with sensitive information” was a “sobering reminder that insider threats are real and persistent”.

The Pentagon referred questions to the US Army, which did not immediately respond to requests for more details.

The arrest is the latest in a series of espionage cases in the UK linked to Moscow. Britain has been one of Kyiv’s strongest backers since Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

In September, a British man appeared in court in London accused of preparing to sabotage drone manufacturing sites for Russian military intelligence.

Relations between London and Moscow were strained well before the war. In 2006, former Russian spy Alexander Litvinenko was poisoned in London, and in 2018 there was an attempt to poison double agent Sergei Skripal in Salisbury.

US Defence chief Hegseth defended operations he led in Yemen, Iran and against alleged drug smugglers, stating, "I stand behind every one of these operations. I was proud to execute them, and I would do it again tomorrow," while portraying the impeachment effort as an attack on US troops.
US Democrats suffered a blow on Tuesday as Republicans in the US House of Representatives blocked a move to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth over the war waged on Iran.

The House failed to secure the votes needed to send the impeachment resolution to the Judiciary Committee, with a vote of 211 to 204, handing President Donald Trump a victory just days after his party's disastrous midterm election defeat. Democrats and some Republicans had been hoping to hold a vote on Wednesday, which would have seen Hegseth face formal charges.

The resolution was formally proposed by Trump critic, Kentucky Republican Thomas Massie, who was the only member of his party to oppose the procedural manoeuvre, according to US media.

Republicans currently hold a majority in the House, but this will end in January, when Democrats are expected to take control of both chambers of Congress.

Trump's party's midterm defeat comes amid widespread discontent among the US public over the country's war on Iran, as well as soaring gas prices and the rising cost of living.

Trump took to his social media platform, TRUTH Social, to express his strong support for Hegseth, describing him as a "Great American Patriot" who had been exonerated.

Meanwhile, earlier on Tuesday, Hegseth called the attempt to impeach him a "farce" while defending the military operations he has carried out under Trump, as well as the war on Iran.

During a Pentagon press conference, he said, "It's a farce, and it's a stunt, and everybody knows it," branding the accusations as "slander" and "sabotage".

Following Hegseth's television appearance, Trump once again showed his support through a TRUTH Social post, writing, "Pete Hegseth gets an 'A' PLUS, PLUS, PLUS."

Vice President JD Vance also commented, saying that Hegseth had "obviously not" committed impeachable offences.

The eight articles of impeachment brought against Hegseth by Massie include several allegations concerning the handling of the Iran war, including waging war without congressional authorisation and abuse of power as Pentagon chief.

They also include allegations surrounding strikes carried out against alleged drug-smuggling boats in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific, which Massie described as "extrajudicial killings", as well as the 2025 conflict with Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis.

Massie also accused Hegseth of suppressing free speech after he attempted to punish Democratic Senator Mark Kelly over a video in which Kelly urged military and intelligence personnel to stand up against illegal orders.

Democrats will be able to revive impeachment efforts once they take control of Congress in January. At the same time, Massie will be leaving Congress after losing his Republican primary to a Trump-backed challenger.
