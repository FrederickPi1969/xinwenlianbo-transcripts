Thailand is a “geopolitically safe zone” “in a world where we see the rules based international order breaking down,” Thailand’s Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister tells Euronews in a wide-ranging interview in Bangkok.
Thailand’s Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sihasak Phuangketkeow told Euronews that as “we live in a world where we see the rules-based international order breaking down,” “we need to restore multilateralism, because nobody can go it alone.”

In a wide-ranging interview for Euronews’ new World Conversation global programme, the veteran top Thai diplomat, with over 40 years of foreign ministry service, outlined how Thailand has managed to secure a balanced approach with the United States and China at a time when the “the main global contestation is between the two superpowers whom both are friends of Thailand.”

“We ask our friends not to force us to take sides,” Sihasak explained, adding that “not taking sides does not mean being neutral as sometimes neutrality does not serve your interest.” He pointed to growing Chinese investment in electric vehicles and the US role in regional security.

A market of 700 million people, Thailand is “a geopolitically safe zone,” the deputy prime minister said in Bangkok, and therefore its strategic objective is to maintain peace and stability. In this context, he emphasized the crucial role played by the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) alliance for pan-Asian stability, adding that Thailand was ready to rebuild ties with Cambodia and defended a gradual return to regional talks with Myanmar.

The gunman first shot and killed a person in a gym, then continued firing after reaching the street. According to police, he then barricaded himself in a building.
At least one person has been killed and four others injured in a shooting near Logan Circle, a popular neighbourhood within walking distance of the White House in Washington, DC, according to police in the US capital.

The suspected gunman has barricaded himself on the top floor of a nearby residential building. His identity and motive are not yet known.

According to US media reports, an armed man entered the Vida Fitness gym, shot a customer in the back and then ran out of the building. The gym was evacuated, and more shots were fired in the street before people scattered.

Nearby public schools have been placed on alert. For now, pupils are not being allowed to leave, and no one is being allowed in.

Police have sealed off the area and asked people to take cover. A SWAT team and other officers have also been sent to the scene.

One eyewitness, who was in the gym when the shooting started at around 8am local time, said there were about 50 people inside.

“Within seconds, a man fell to the ground and blood started pouring out from under him,” he said. “One of the gym customers rushed over to help him. The man died instantly. He had been shot in the neck.”

He said he and others ran out of the gym. He believed two shots were fired inside the gym and a further five or six outside.

The area has seen violent crime before. In the early hours of a Sunday in August 2025, a group of teenagers attacked a former staff member of the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), the cost-cutting body set up under President Donald Trump, and tried to steal the car belonging to him and a woman he was with.

An Israeli airstrike hit Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis, southern Gaza, early on Thursday 12 November, killing six people, including a doctor, and injuring at least 27 others, according to the World Health Organization (WHO).
… More
The strike devastated the hospital's surgical department, with damage also reported to operating theatres, recovery facilities and the intensive care unit. Footage shows extensive destruction inside the hospital, with shattered ceilings, twisted metal and rubble scattered across medical equipment and beds. Rescue workers searched through the debris, while grieving relatives gathered outside to pray over the bodies of those killed.

Gaza's Health Ministry identified one of the victims as general surgeon Rizk Abu Shaira. The Israeli military acknowledged carrying out the strike, saying it had targeted a senior Hamas commander and other militants allegedly operating from the hospital, but provided no evidence to substantiate its claims.

The attack struck one of the few remaining functioning hospitals in Gaza, where years of war have devastated healthcare services and left medical facilities struggling to meet overwhelming needs. According to the WHO, 1,056 attacks on healthcare facilities and personnel have been documented in Gaza since October 2023, resulting in 1,010 deaths and 1,725 injuries. The organisation warned that approaching winter conditions, including heavy rain, flooding and cold temperatures, could further worsen the humanitarian crisis, particularly as water and sanitation systems remain severely damaged.

Israel has repeatedly accused Hamas of using hospitals for military purposes, allegations the group denies. Under international humanitarian law, hospitals and medical personnel are protected, although facilities can lose specific protections if used to commit acts harmful to the enemy, subject to strict conditions. The WHO reiterated its call for unhindered humanitarian access and the protection of medical facilities.

A Russian serviceman was killed when his car exploded in Saint Petersburg on Thursday. Investigators have opened a murder inquiry, while local media report that another person was injured.
… More
The explosion occurred at about 7 am in the Shushary area of the city's Pushkinsky district, on Kolpino Highway, leaving the vehicle badly damaged. A passing bus also had its windows shattered by the blast.

Russian news outlet Fontanka reported that the victim was a colonel serving as chief navigator of the 6th Air and Air Defence Army. Russian authorities have not officially identified the serviceman or confirmed the cause of the explosion. The Investigative Committee has opened a murder investigation, with forensic experts examining the scene.

The blast comes more than four years after Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. Several Russian military officials have since been killed in attacks on Russian territory, some of which have been attributed to Kyiv, although Ukrainian authorities rarely claim responsibility. Investigators have not established any connection between Thursday's explosion and the war.

Updated: 12 November 2026 - 15:52 GMT+1
Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, is a Chinese businesswoman who has been at the centre of allegations involving a suspected Chinese intelligence operation targeting Nvidia and its former CEO, Jensen Huang.

US intelligence agencies believed she had been recruited by Beijing to gather information on one of the world's most valuable technology companies. Alice Huang and Jensen Huang share a surname but are not related.

She previously held a senior position at Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based data centre company that rents computing power to businesses developing and running AI systems. Megaspeed described itself as a key business partner of Nvidia's cloud computing operations in the Asia-Pacific region.

Photographs from a technology industry event in 2024 showed the two sitting together. In May 2025, they also attended a dinner hosted by Jensen Huang in Taipei during the Computex technology expo.

Video footage from the restaurant showed Jensen Huang putting his arm around Alice Huang as she kissed him on the cheek. They were later seen leaving in the same car.

Jensen Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and apologised to his family and colleagues. Nvidia's board subsequently launched an independent investigation into the matter.

On Thursday, November 12, Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO and board member after 33 years leading the company. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress was appointed interim CEO.

Separately, Singapore's authorities confirmed that Alice Huang had been investigated over possible offences involving the purchase and export of servers through Singapore. According to a study, the end of the world was supposed to come on 13 November. It sounds dramatic and would be quite urgent – if the study were not already "outdated". Still, it is fascinating.
The so-called Doomsday study is already 66 years old, but it is probably the best-known study about the end of the world. In 1960 researchers used a mathematical formula to calculate the end of humankind for Friday 13 November 2026. The article with the provocative title "Doomsday: Friday, 13 November, A.D. 2026" was written by Austrian-American physicist Heinz von Foerster and his research team and published in the prestigious journal Science (source in German).

The researchers analysed historical data on the world population over the past two millennia and fitted a growth curve to it. Their projection was that if the then current growth rate of humans on Earth were extrapolated unchanged into the future, the curve would reach an infinite value in November 2026.

So by tomorrow, Friday, people would have multiplied to infinity. The mathematicians' logical conclusion was that on this day humanity would, purely in mathematical terms, "crush" itself through overpopulation.

The study caused a worldwide stir at the time, and that was exactly the intention. By pinning the date to a Friday the 13th, Heinz von Foerster mainly wanted to draw attention to the issue of overpopulation, which was being intensely debated then. Until the early 1990s the curve matched the actual figures remarkably well. After that global population growth slowed significantly, which is why current projections tend to expect stabilisation at around 10 billion people.

The world population is still growing, but more and more slowly. At first the number of people increased sharply because fewer were dying. Later families around the world began having fewer children. The fastest growth was in 1963, at more than two per cent per year. By 2020 this growth had fallen to below one per cent.

According to estimates by the United Nations (UN), the number of people on Earth will peak in the mid-2080s at about 10.3 billion. After that the world population will slowly start to decline again.

As early as 1910 there was widespread panic about Halley's Comet. Newspapers warned of poisonous gases in its tail that could wipe out all life. Some traders exploited this fear and sold gas masks, protective pills or "comet umbrellas". But the comet passed and nothing happened.

Later there were repeated dates like this. A US radio preacher predicted Judgement Day for 21 May 2011. On 21 December 2012 a cycle of the Mayan calendar ended, which many also interpreted as the end of the world.

Even science has sometimes been wrong. In 1972 the study "Limits to Growth" warned that we would soon run out of vital raw materials. But people found new sources and solutions, and many commodities even became cheaper.

The problem with such predictions is always the same: past trends are simply projected into the future. This overlooks the fact that people respond to problems and adapt. That also applies to 13 November 2026. Humanity has developed differently from what was calculated 66 years ago.

So if you are now thinking you can finally tell your boss what you really think because everything will be over on Friday anyway, you might want to think again.

An 11-month-old boy has been rescued alive after spending about 24 hours buried underground in a dog crate in the US state of South Carolina, where police say he was left by his father.

Koah Sharp was found on Tuesday in woodland on a neighbour’s property, crying and covered in dirt, his grandfather John Comer told the Associated Press news agency on Wednesday.

His margin of fewer than 5,000 votes caps a midterm wave that has handed Democrats control of both chambers of Congress.
Democrat Scott Colom has won the US Senate race in Mississippi, becoming the first Black senator elected from the southern US state in more than 140 years, US networks projected on Wednesday.

Colom, a 43-year-old district attorney, defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith. It was perhaps the most remarkable seat flip of a midterm election in which Democrats won back the majority in both the Senate and the House of Representatives.

CNN, NBC and CBS called the race on Wednesday, eight days after election night. With about 99 per cent of the vote counted, Colom led Hyde-Smith by 49.2 per cent to 48.7 per cent, a margin of 4,585 votes.

“Mississippi, our prayers have often changed the course of history,” Colom told a jubilant crowd of supporters in Jackson, the state capital, on Wednesday night. “God didn’t want us to get it the easy way. He wanted us to get it the Mississippi way.”

In a prayerful, solemn speech, he paid tribute to Mississippi’s long tradition of Black activism and faith in progress.

“While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters,” she said. “I want to congratulate Scott Colom and wish him well as he takes on the tremendous responsibility of representing our great state.”

The result in one of the most solidly Republican states deepens the setback for US President Donald Trump. Voters across the country turned away from his Republican Party, even in states that had not sent a Democrat to Washington in decades.

Democrats will now hold 55 seats in the 100-member Senate and Republicans 44. One race, in Alaska, has yet to be called.

Including Mississippi, Democrats flipped eight Republican-held Senate seats, with breakthroughs in South Carolina, North Carolina, Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Texas and Maine. Several of those seats had not changed hands for decades.

Control of both chambers of Congress gives Democrats the power to block legislation, investigate the administration and stop Trump’s appointments. The defeat also leaves the 80-year-old president as a lame duck for the final two years of his term.

The race went down to the wire, surprising many observers who had doubted how far the Democratic wave would reach. The wave was driven by mounting anger over Trump’s hardline immigration policy, the war in Iran and the rising cost of living.

“Blue Mississippi is here,” Chuck Schumer, the top Democrat in the Senate, wrote on X after the projections came in.

“Scott Colom is the only person who could’ve gotten this done. I knew it from the moment I met him.”

The first Black senator ever elected in the United States, Hiram Revels, was from Mississippi. He served during the Reconstruction era that followed the American Civil War.

In his speech, Colom described how Revels, a minister by training, used his powerful oratory to win the backing of other politicians for his run for the Senate.

“That’s an example of the power of prayer, and through our history, there are so many people that lifted this state up through the power of prayer in very dark times,” Colom said.

“It’s local people that have faith that Mississippi can make progress, and it’s local people like the people out here who had faith that we could win this election,” he added.

The festivities continued despite persistent rain and grey skies, with Cologne Cathedral partly obscured by mist.

Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang is soon to be ousted by the company's board amid accusations of a second affair, according to US media on Wednesday. The news comes following reports earlier this week of Huang having an affair with a woman accused of spying for China.

The Wall Street Journal, quoting people close to the matter, reported that the board was preparing to meet to come up with a decison.

Meanwhile, the New York Times said on Wednesday, that video evidence shows the 63-year-old executive being affectionate with Nvidia's vice president of health care, Kimberly Powell, marking a second extramarital affair.

Powell, 48, has been a prominent figure in Nvidia’s efforts to expand chip technology in higher education and healthcare. Speaking at a conference in San Jose, California, bystanders reportedly saw them "being affectionate at the hotel", The Times reported.

The report of a second affair follows Huang admitting to having a relationship with Alice Huang, a senior executive at Singapore-based data centre company Megaspeed.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Jensen Huang and Alice Huang were photographed together in Taipei in 2024 and 2025.

According to reports, US intelligence circulated a memo last spring alleging that Alice Huang was an operative for China's Ministry of State Security.

In a note sent to Nvidia employees on Sunday, the company's CEO acknowledged having a relationship with Alice Huang, admitting, "I was unfaithful to my wife."

That same day, Nvidia's board of directors announced that it had initiated "an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort."

"I support the investigation and will cooperate fully. I love our country and our company," Huang wrote in his statement. Meanwhile, members of Congress have reportedly called on him to deliver a closed-door briefing about his interactions with Alice Huang, according to The Times.

As the artificial intelligence boom has sent Nvidia's stock to unprecedented heights, Huang has increasingly appeared alongside US President Donald Trump at White House events. Just last month, he received the National Medal of Science from the Republican president.

Huang has also been among the world's most influential technology executives, alongside Elon Musk, Jeff Bezos and Mark Zuckerberg, who pledged to support industry self-regulation of artificial intelligence amid growing concerns over its potential risks. Trump, however, has rejected calls for government oversight and dismissed warnings that AI could pose an existential threat to humanity.

The historic SS United States, once the world's fastest ocean liner, was deliberately sunk in the Gulf of Mexico off Florida on Wednesday, November 11.

The vessel was taken beneath the water as part of a $13.5 million project to turn it into the world's largest artificial reef, attracting divers and fishing enthusiasts while creating a habitat for marine life.

The controlled sinking took several hours, longer than organisers had initially expected. Crews had filled 128 fuel and lubricant tanks with water to help the ship settle evenly on the ocean floor. By around 9 pm, the giant vessel tilted to one side before finally disappearing beneath the surface.

At least eight people have died after a three-story building in Brooklyn collapsed at the edge of a construction site, New York City’s mayor said Thursday, as firefighters kept digging through the rubble to find others who may be trapped beneath the wreckage.

Some 250 firefighters and police officers tackled the rubble with pickaxes and saws to pull people covered in dust from beneath piles of twisted wood and brick.

During a press conference, officials said that at least 17 people had been hospitalised, four in critical condition. While it is unclear whether people might still be trapped, New York City Fire Department officials stated that search and rescue operations would continue through the night.

“This has been an incredibly challenging and fast-moving situation, with precarious wreckage to navigate and dangerous voids beneath the surface,” Mayor Zohran Mamdani said. “This type of rescue is exactly what the FDNY trains for.”

According to New York City’s Buildings Commissioner, Ahmed Tigani, the building fell when construction workers were digging an empty lot next door for a new building. However, the cause of the collapse is under investigation.

The collapsed building, which housed apartments above ground-level businesses, was reduced to a massive mound of debris at the bottom of the construction pit. The neighbouring structure, which shared a wall with the building, was left standing but suffered extensive damage. Much of its exterior wall had been ripped away, leaving the interiors exposed, including a bedroom and closets with clothes still hanging inside.

One of the survivors was a woman who was pulled from the rubble by firefighters late in the afternoon after a hole had been dug through the debris. Meanwhile, more people continued to be pulled from the rubble as search and rescue operations proceeded, including seven children, although officials did not specify whether any of them were among the dead.

FDNY Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore said the rescue teams, who were digging by hand and using drones to carry out operations, were performing “dangerous and perilous” work. “Every move is choreographed,” she said.

One resident of the collapsed building, Anderson Velez, recounted that he was at work when his wife phoned and said that their two children, aged 2 and 7, were among those trapped in the rubble, the New York Post reported.

“She called me when the building collapsed while she was inside with rubble on top of her. She was screaming for someone to help get my son out and then the call cut off,” he said.
