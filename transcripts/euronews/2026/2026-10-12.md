Updated: 12/10/2026 - 19:46 GMT+2
Spain's National Day on Monday, 12 October, was marked by demonstrations in Barcelona in support of Spanish unity, while far-right leader Santiago Abascal visited Ceuta, the Spanish enclave on the North African coast.
… More
In Barcelona, about 2,000 demonstrators waved Spanish and Ceuta flags at a rally supporting Spain’s territorial integrity, under the slogan “Catalonia with Ceuta, one Spain”. Meanwhile, Vox leader Santiago Abascal travelled to Ceuta, where he laid a wreath in honour of those who died for Spain. He had chosen to mark Spain’s National Day in the North African enclave rather than attend the military parade in Madrid.

His visit came amid renewed focus on migration and Ceuta’s disputed status: Morocco claims sovereignty over the Spanish-administered territory. More than 70,000 people crossed from Morocco into Ceuta on 30 and 31 July; most returned within days, but thousands remained, putting pressure on local resources.

On Sunday, police, backed by soldiers, cleared the last major migrant encampment at El Guano. More than 350 officers took part in the operation, two days before King Felipe VI and Queen Letizia were due to visit Ceuta. The royal couple are also scheduled to travel to Melilla on 14 October, their first visit to the two enclaves since 2007.

Updated: 12/10/2026 - 15:52 GMT+2
Fire tore through Paris’s oldest covered food market in the Marais, leaving traders devastated. An investigation is under way and the market may stay shut for months.
… More
A major fire tore through Paris’s oldest covered food market, the Marché des Enfants Rouges, in the early hours of Monday, 12 October, destroying its stalls and causing part of its metal structure to collapse.

The blaze broke out at around 4.15am in the historic Marais district. Firefighters prevented it from spreading to neighbouring buildings. One firefighter suffered a minor medical episode, but no other casualties were reported. Distraught traders gathered outside to assess the damage.

Paris Mayor Emmanuel Grégoire said the cause remained unknown and an investigation would follow. Nearby buildings were being checked for structural damage, while the city sought temporary premises for affected businesses. The market could remain closed for months.

Founded in 1615 under Louis XIII, the landmark takes its name from a former orphanage whose children wore red clothing. Listed as a historic monument since 1982, it housed food shops, restaurants and florists, and ranked fifth in Food & Wine magazine’s 2026 list of the world’s best food markets.

German industrial giants, including Siemens Energy, are exploring opportunities to support Azerbaijan's green energy transition, as the country aims to handle 30 million tonnes of transit cargo by 2030.
German energy and infrastructure heavyweights are exploring new opportunities in Azerbaijan. Their plans cover both gas supplies and renewable energy, at a time when Europe seeks to reduce its dependence on Russian fossil fuels.

Speaking at the German-Azerbaijani Connectivity Conference 2026, which was hosted by the German-Azerbaijani Chamber of Commerce (AHK Azerbaijan) in Baku, European corporate leaders outlined concrete investment plans ranging from fossil fuels to renewable energy networks along the Middle Corridor.

The gathering brought together more than 250 participants, including over 35 guests from Germany and Azerbaijani policymakers.

Nargis Wieck, Executive Director of AHK Azerbaijan, stressed the need to build on strong bilateral trade ties to encourage concrete investments.

Wieck told Euronews that one of the key questions was how to “deepen our economic cooperation through concrete projects”.

She also said that part of the chamber’s role involves “helping German companies become part of Azerbaijan’s economic ecosystem, and Azerbaijani companies integrate more closely into German and European value chains”.

German Ambassador Dirk Lölke noted that after President Ilham Aliyev’s recent visit to Berlin, regional connectivity could become the "second pillar" of bilateral economic relations, alongside energy cooperation.

On the energy front, Siemens Energy said it was ready to provide critical technology and expertise for Azerbaijan’s renewable energy sector and the planned Caspian-Black Sea-Europe Green Energy Corridor.

Frederik Doye, Senior Vice President for Europe at Siemens Energy, emphasised that Azerbaijan’s vast offshore wind potential positions the country as a vital energy link connecting Europe, the South Caucasus, and Central Asia.

Azerbaijan plans to develop 8 GW of wind and solar projects by 2032, according to Deputy Minister of Energy Elnur Soltanov.

He said that the priority was to develop the partnership in a number of areas, including "renewable energy, energy connectivity, energy efficiency and the broader energy transition".

"Our goal is to connect this potential to European markets through several complementary routes - the Caspian-Black Sea-Europe Green Energy Corridor, the Central Asia-Azerbaijan Corridor, and westward links to Türkiye and Europe via Georgia and Nakhchivan," he added.

Beyond generation, German technology providers are setting their sights on power grid modernisation, battery energy storage systems (BESS), and electric vehicle charging infrastructure.

Julia Egel, Head of Stakeholder Engagement at AHK Azerbaijan, noted that German firms are perfectly positioned to support Azerbaijan's mandate to boost renewable power generation. The country's stated target is to increase the share of renewables in its installed electricity generation capacity to 30% by 2030.

Egel also announced an upcoming trade mission of German energy companies to Azerbaijan, with plans for targeted business-to-business meetings and potential German pavilions at next year's Baku Energy Week.

Alongside renewable energy projects, German companies are looking to secure gas supplies. German gas importer VNG AG said it was interested in a long-term supply agreement with the State Oil Company of Azerbaijan (SOCAR) lasting 15 to 20 years.

Deadly attacks on Riyadh’s airport have brought fresh calls for joint Gulf defence, as Qatar warned the wider conflict was damaging global energy supplies.
A senior UAE official called for stronger Gulf security and defence cooperation on Monday as regional governments backed Saudi Arabia following Houthi missile attacks that killed 14 people at Riyadh’s international airport over the weekend.

Anwar Gargash, diplomatic adviser to the UAE president, said attacks on Saudi Arabia undermined the security of the Emirates and every other Gulf state.

Writing on X, he warned that “we are all targeted” and said failures in collective defence had encouraged attacks on the region.

“Gulf security is a shared responsibility that allows no room for complacency or delay,” he said.

Saturday’s attack on King Khalid International Airport killed 13 people and wounded more than 300. The death toll rose from 12 after an injured Indian citizen died on Sunday, according to authorities.

A separate attack on Sunday killed a Pakistani national and injured 11, Saudi authorities said. Yemen's Houthis claimed responsibility for both attacks.

Turkey, Egypt and Pakistan joined Saudi Arabia in a statement on Sunday condemning the attacks and supporting the kingdom’s right to defend itself.

The three backed Saudi measures to counter the attacks and prevent their recurrence, provided they complied with the UN Charter and international law, and demanded an immediate, unconditional end to Houthi attacks and held the group responsible for their consequences.

France also said on Sunday it would continue supporting the Gulf kingdom in defending its territory and infrastructure. Its foreign ministry said a French woman was among those wounded in Saturday’s attack.

Iran’s foreign ministry said on Monday it remained ready to help mediate between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis. Iranian foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei said the Iranian and Saudi foreign ministers had discussed developments by telephone on Sunday.

Qatar’s Energy Minister Saad Sherida al-Kaabi warned that the wider regional conflict had caused “by far, the world’s worst energy crisis”, affecting the flow of around 20% of global oil and gas supplies.

Speaking at the International Energy Forum’s ministerial meeting in Riyadh on Sunday, he expressed solidarity with Saudi Arabia and offered condolences to the families of those killed.

Al-Kaabi, who also heads QatarEnergy, said attacks on energy facilities across the region had caused tens of billions of dollars in damage and severe volatility in global markets.

He urged the forum to examine ways to protect energy infrastructure during conflict and safeguard supply flows.

“Energy security must unite us, not divide us. It must be protected, not weaponized,” he said.

Saudi Arabia’s General Authority of Civil Aviation extended the suspension of operations at Riyadh airport on Monday as it continued checks on the safety and readiness of its facilities. It did not announce when it would reopen.

The White House calls the adverts, reportedly paid for with $20 million of Homeland Security funds, public service announcements, but they have drawn criticism from both parties.
A US federal judge has ordered Donald Trump’s administration to stop running taxpayer-funded television adverts promoting the president, weeks before November’s midterm elections.

US District Judge Jennifer Rochon issued a temporary restraining order on Sunday barring the administration from making or airing the ads for 14 days. The administration has used $20 million (€17.7m) of Department of Homeland Security funds to pay for them, according to US media reports.

"Defendants shall not enter into, renew, or expand any agreement authorizing the development, creation, dissemination, placement, or airing of the challenged advertisements," Rochon said in the court order.

The plaintiffs, represented by the nonprofit legal group Democracy Forward, argued the adverts "exceed the purposes authorized by Congress and violate restrictions on publicity and propaganda".

"We are pleased the court acted swiftly to prevent more taxpayer dollars from being used for such blatantly unlawful political propaganda," they said in a statement.

The White House has defended the spending, describing the adverts as public service announcements.

The first, broadcast in late September, featured a song saying "Love Me" as Trump declared "America will never be a communist country." Another showed black-and-white footage of Trump walking down a hallway as he warned of a "final battle", with an onscreen message reading: "Paid for by the US Government."

The campaign has drawn criticism from both parties. Democratic Senator John Hickenlooper called the adverts "illegal", while some senior Republicans voiced reservations.

Trump, who is not on the ballot but is campaigning to help Republicans keep control of Congress, said last week that he and his campaign would pay for the adverts. The White House later told AFP the post "was not referring to reimbursement."

The rising tensions come as US President Donald Trump's recent overtures to Pyongyang have raised concerns that Seoul, an ally of Washington, could be sidelined in potential nuclear talks involving the North.
