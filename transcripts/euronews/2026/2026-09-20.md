It comes after North Korea this week rejected a resolution by the UN nuclear watchdog against its nuclear programme.

A spokesperson for the country's Foreign Ministry said the International Atomic Energy Agency's resolution had "no effect on the DPRK's position as a nuclear weapons state."

In August, Trump told reporters that North Korean leader Kim Jong-un had "57 very powerful nuclear weapons" as he once again touted the pair's relationship.

"You know what, the fact that I get along with him, that's a good thing not a bad thing," Trump said, adding that Pyongyang's nuclear arsenal should "never" have been allowed to happen.

Trump announced at the time that he also intended to meet with Kim later this year, although he gave no further details on the plan - which would be their first meeting since Trump's first term in the White House.

On Friday, South Korean President Lee Jae Myung said he was "urging" Trump to maintain dialogue with Pyongyang, adding that Seoul was working to facilitate talks between the North and the US.

"I cannot say for certain how likely it is, but it is also clear that we must make every possible effort to turn that possibility into reality," he said of the potential for a meeting between US and North Korean officials.

However, hopes for an imminent meeting may have been doused slightly after Kim's powerful sister, Kim Yo Jong, criticised recent military drills in the Western Pacific, which she labelled a "US-led war rehearsal."

The US, South Korea and Japan wrapped up Exercise Freedom Edge 26, which US Pacific Command said advanced "trilateral readiness across the Indo-Pacific," on 11 September.

"Our will to defend our sovereignty will be expressed in clearer action and there will be never ever change in the course of intensifying the nuclear war deterrence," Kim Yo Jong added in a statement shared by North Korea's KCNA state news agency on Friday.

The White House Correspondents’ Association, led by Fox News’ Jacqui Heinrich called on the Trump administration to immediately restore access to the representatives of the media organisations it barred, arguing that the move sets a dangerous precedent for free speech.
Reporters from three major news organisations were denied access to the White House on Saturday, a day after US President Donald Trump said he was barring them because of reporting he found to be “fake news.”

The media outlets, CNN, MS NOW and Politico, have previously been targeted by the Republican president, particularly CNN, who Trump kicks his antics up a notch, regularly mocking their reporters and lambasting their authenticity.

The actions were a clear escalation of Trump’s long-running efforts — in the courts and through administrative action — to restrict news coverage by journalists he finds objectionable, and the latest test of First Amendment protections in the United States.

White House reporters from the two cable networks — Betsy Klein of CNN and Akayla Gardner of MS NOW — both went live to report they’d been turned away, their press badges deactivated, when arriving for their jobs at the White House.

Soon after, Politico said its White House reporter Cheyenne Haslett was also denied entry and had her badge confiscated. All three news outlets hinted at legal action.

“We will vigorously defend our First Amendment rights,” Politico Editor-in-Chief Jonathan Greenberger said in a statement to the Politico newsroom. A CNN statement said the network “will not be deflected from our duty to hold the government and other public bodies to account."

And MS NOW said it “intends to take any and all steps necessary to defend our First Amendment rights and the essential role of independent journalism in our democracy.”

In his second term, Trump and his administration have routinely punished certain media outlets, both in the courtroom and through regulatory action.

Trump has also lashed out at individual reporters in person or over social media, sometimes in strikingly personal terms — insulting them in briefings, or in his recent speech at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner, where he called CNN’s Kailtan Collins “very dumb and unhappy”.

The White House Correspondents’ Association President Jacqui Heinrich called on the administration to immediately restore the three outlets' access.

“The implications extend beyond these organisations: a standard used to exclude one news outlet because of its coverage could be applied to any outlet in the future,” she said.

“The American people, through a free and independent press, must be able to scrutinise those elected to power, regardless of whether government officials view it favourably.”

The move follows the president’s decision last year to bar Associated Press reporters from the Oval Office, Air Force One and other events in retaliation for the news outlet’s decision not to follow his lead in changing the name of the Gulf of Mexico.

The AP said that it would note, when appropriate, that Trump had ordered the water body, which lies partially in Mexican and other waters, renamed the “Gulf of America.”

Since returning to office, Trump has pursued other legal action against a variety of different outlets, including The New York Times, The Wall Street Journal and the BBC.

His administration is also involved in a long-simmering confrontation involving ABC over renewal of its broadcast licenses.

The US has moved to clamp down on drug-smuggling routes under US President Donald Trump, who has pledged to "wage war" against drug cartels.
The US military said its forces struck a suspected drug-smuggling boat in the Caribbean on Saturday, killing four people.

"Joint Task Force Western Hemisphere executed a lethal, kinetic strike on a go-fast vessel operating along established narco-trafficking routes in the Caribbean," US Southern Command (SOUTHCOM) said in a statement shared on social media.

It added that "confirmed intelligence revealed the vessel's active involvement in narco-trafficking."

The post also contained video footage of what appeared to be the strike, showing a moving boat followed by a large explosion.

The US has moved to clamp down on drug-smuggling routes under US President Donald Trump, who has pledged to "wage war" against drug cartels. The president says cartels threaten the US' national security and foreign policy and bring "devastating consequences" to communities across the country.

As part of this push, the US launched operation "Southern Spear," which aims to "disrupt the flow of narcotics in the Western Hemisphere" through a campaign of air strikes on suspected drug-smuggling boats in the Caribbean Sea and eastern Pacific.

Trump has also spearheaded a new security coalition including nations from across Central and South America - dubbed the "Shield of the Americas" - in a bid to combat cartels.

The English singer-songwriter opened his show sharing his opinions on Gaza, noting that while 7 October attacks were horrific, the Israeli response which killed countless women and children was “catastrophic” and “disproportionate”.
Ed Sheeran opened a concert on Saturday with comments about Gaza after controversy swirled around his tour and supporting acts withdrew in solidarity with opener Macklemore, who was dropped after making pro-Palestinian comments onstage at an earlier show.

Sheeran began the concert alone on stage with no opener, but was joined by other musicians later in the show.

Sheeran said he wants his music and shows to be about unity and humanity, not politics. But he said that while the attacks in Israel of 7 October, 2023, were horrific, what is happening in Gaza “catastrophic and unjustifiable” and slammed it as “disproportionate”.

“I don’t want to disappoint fans, and I’ve never wanted to be an activist musician, but this has put me in the middle of an important and passionate argument about freedom of speech and the most complex political issue on the planet,” said Sheeran during his performance at Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia.

The comments before a nearly full stadium came after a tumultuous week that began with rapper Macklemore, Sheeran's original US opener, being dropped in response to saying “Free Palestine” during a 5 September tour stop in New Jersey.

Sheeran has said he was not responsible for the decision, which came at the urging of New England Patriots owner Robert Kraft. His company owns a stadium where Sheeran’s tour was scheduled to play on Friday. Within hours of Sheeran’s statement Tuesday, his supporting acts withdrew.

Sheeran has performed many songs solo during his Loop Tour concerts, but was joined for several songs at each stop by the Irish folk band Beoga before they dropped out in support of Macklemore.

Macklemore was replaced by Danish band Lukas Graham and Irish singer Aaron Rowe before they backed out, and Finneas, Billie Eilish’s brother and musical collaborator, was due to tour with Sheeran in South America later this year before he too dropped out this week.

Before Saturday's show, a small group of pro-Palestinian demonstrators convened outside the stadium.

Hillary Steinberg, 34, said she got concert tickets a year ago but wound up getting a refund and attended the demonstration instead, after she was disappointed that Sheeran refused to take a stand to defend Macklemore or empathise with the plight of the Palestinian people.

“I was, until this week, a big Ed Sheeran fan,” Steinberg said. “I think all art is political, and all music is political, and so this choice — even though he was saying that he wasn’t complicit — is a political choice.”

Diane Savitz, a 53-year-old from Stroudsburg, Pennsylvania, got tickets months ago and attended. She said she is a big Sheeran fan, doesn't think he did anything wrong and a concert is not the place for politics.

“A concert should be for people enjoying the music. Music should bring people together,” Savitz said. “Everyone has their right to freedom of speech, but not every platform is the place for it.”

The Grammy winning artist was adamant earlier this week that he wouldn't be drawn into discussion of the war in Gaza, arguing that his fans don’t come to his shows to hear about these issues. But, as the controversy grew, and ticket prices and sales declined, he decided to address the issue head on.

Sheeran told the crowd in Philadelphia that everyone is welcome at his concerts, and they can have opposing points of view while still singing his songs, which are about “love, hope and unity.”

“This happens in so many places that I perform around the world, but it should never happen in the free world,” Sheeran said.

Embassies in Bahrain, Israel, Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE said the "military conflict has the potential to escalate rapidly" and that the security environment remained "complex."

The alerts also advised people to "be aware of potential flight cancellations, airspace closures, and travel disruptions."

It comes after the Houthi rebels on Saturday said they had carried out two operations using ballistic and cruise missiles as well as drones to target Saudi Arabia in response to Saudi attacks.

"The first targeted sensitive sites in the Saudi capital, Riyadh, and the second targeted Aramco facilities in Yanbu," the group claimed.

A spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition forces - which were formed in 2015 to back Yemen's internationally recognised government - later confirmed that the Royal Saudi Air Defense Forces had intercepted a ballistic missile fired towards Riyadh. The spokesperson added that the group had also targeted the cities of Baish, Taif, Farasan, and Yanbu.

On Saturday, residents of Riyadh reported seeing flames and smoke at the capital's King Khalid International Airport.

Video footage circulating on social media appeared to show a towering column of smoke rising from the airport, which flight tracking site Flightradar24 had said was experiencing "major problems."

Yemen's Houthi rebels, who declared a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia in July, have been launching increasing attacks on Saudi cities and shipping, further heightening tensions in the Middle East.

Saudi Arabia has hit back with its own strikes on the group - which has seized vast stretches of territory in Yemen this month in a lightning offensive - but has failed to stop its advance.

In a press briefing on Friday, Stéphane Dujarric, a spokesperson for the UN Secretary-General, addressed the situation in Yemen.

"Since hostilities escalated in August, the Human Rights office has documented 110 civilian casualties in 31 incidents, including 28 killed and 82 injured, across Taizz, Al Hudaydah and Marib. Insecurity and access constraints continue to hamper our ability to verify the actual number as you can imagine is very likely higher," Dujarric said, according to a transcript shared by the UN.

"We urge all parties, and those with influence over them, to act immediately to de-escalate, protect civilians and facilitate humanitarian access. Every effort must be made to prevent further loss of life," he added.
