Around 40 vintage and classic cars have taken to the streets of Almería for the 36th edition of an annual rally. From a rare 1905 Vermorel to a DeLorean, the event celebrates more than a century of motoring history.
… More
Among the highlights was a rare 1905 Vermorel belonging to local collector Pepe Moreno, a vehicle normally displayed in a museum in nearby El Ejido.

A century-old Franklin also drew attention, alongside an elegant early 1950s Chevrolet, a late 1920s Nash, vintage Rolls-Royce and Mercedes-Benz models, and a DeLorean DMC-12, the futuristic sports car immortalised by the Back to the Future films.

Spectators watched as owners started their historic engines and drove through the city centre, offering a glimpse of motoring history spanning several generations. For the owners, the rally is as much about sharing their passion as preserving these remarkable machines.

Running from 5 to 9 November, the rally brings together enthusiasts from across Spain for a journey through the landscapes and villages of Almería province, including stops in Enix, Terque and Alsodux. The route also takes participants to Fort Bravo, the famous Western film sets in the Tabernas desert, combining automotive heritage with a tour of southern Spain's cinematic scenery.

Markle Sr's arrest follows an investigation launched after his son, Thomas Markle Jr, reportedly discovered boxes containing hundreds or potentially thousands of images at his father's former home in the Philippines.
Thomas Markle Sr, the estranged father of the Duchess of Sussex Meghan Markle, was arrested outside Tampa, Florida, on Friday morning, US media have reported.

The specific federal charges and further details of the arrest were not immediately made public, with the Daily Mail and New York Post's initial reports of his arrest backed up by news agency Associated Press.

The arrest follows an investigation launched after Markle's son, Thomas Markle Jr, reportedly discovered boxes containing hundreds or potentially thousands of images at his father's former home in Cebu City, in the Philippines.

Markle Jr alerted Philippine police and contacted the US Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), prompting authorities in both countries to examine the allegations.

Philippine authorities have reported recovering around 7,500 photographs and one video depicting underage children from electronic devices linked to the investigation.

Interior and Local Government Secretary Jonvic Remulla said some of the children appeared to be as young as 10 or 11.

According to Philippine police, an initial forensic examination identified suspected child sexual abuse and exploitation material on several storage devices. Investigators have stressed, however, that the findings do not yet establish who owned, produced or distributed the material.

The FBI has been cooperating with Philippine authorities as investigators seek to establish the ownership of the devices and determine whether further individuals were involved.

Markle, 82, has previously denied possessing child sexual abuse images and rejected the allegations against him.

The investigation has also expanded beyond the Philippines, with reports of a separate cache of material allegedly linked to Markle being examined in Mexico.

Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, recently broke her silence over the allegations, saying she was "deeply distressed" by the reports and expressing concern for any children who may have been harmed.

In a statement, she also revealed that a letter containing what she described as "troubling and disturbing allegations" about her father had been sent to London's Metropolitan Police in 2018 and subsequently shared with her.

She also addressed her long-standing estrangement from her father, saying her decision to prevent him from having contact with her children, Prince Archie and Princess Lilibet, was intended to protect them.

The Duchess married Prince Harry at Windsor Castle in May 2018. Her father, a retired television lighting and photography director, did not attend the wedding after a highly publicised family dispute.

The investigation took a further turn on Thursday after Philippine authorities named Thomas Markle Jr as a person of interest in the inquiry.

Remulla said investigators were examining information suggesting that underage girls may have visited Markle Jr's home in Cebu. He stressed that the 60-year-old was not being treated as a suspect.

Markle Jr was the person who initially reported the alleged images to authorities. His possible involvement is being investigated separately from the discovery of the material, according to reports.

The development has added another layer of complexity to the case, which now involves investigators in the Philippines, the United States and potentially Mexico.

The arrest brings renewed attention to the long-running family rift between Meghan and her father.

Markle Sr returned to the United States earlier this year after suffering a stroke and undergoing the amputation of his left leg while living in the Philippines.

His relationship with Meghan deteriorated in the run-up to her 2018 wedding to then-Prince Harry. Prince Harry stepped back from his role as a senior working member of the British royal family in 2020, giving up his official royal duties alongside Meghan.

Iran’s Security Council chief, Mohsen Rezaei, went to Moscow to deliver a personal message from Ayatollah Khamenei and discuss building a pipeline to import Russian gas.
Iran’s Supreme National Security Council Secretary met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday, where he delivered a personal message from Iran’s Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei regarding the Iran war and discussed what both sides called “strengthening ties”.

Iran’s top security official described the meeting as an "extremely important exchange of views," which covered "the post-war situation and regional security.”

Russia has been a long-standing supporter of Iran, purportedly supplying Tehran with intelligence and equipment during the war. Tehran has previously backed Russia in its all-out invasion of Ukraine, and provided its Shahed drone technology.

Moscow has since cloned it into its domestically-made Geran UAVs, which it commonly uses in its daily strikes on infrastructure and civilian targets in Ukraine.

The meeting, which was not announced in advance, follows the Russian president's meeting with his Iranian counterpart Masoud Pezeshkian in Moscow during a summit of the Gas Exporting Countries Forum last week.

The content of Khamenei’s message to Putin was not made public, Iranian state broadcaster IRIB reported.

"The construction of gas pipelines from Russia to Iran was discussed" during the meeting with Putin, Rezaei stated.

He explained that the project aims to help Tehran "address its gas shortages, while also facilitating the sale of Russian gas to neighbouring countries."

Although Iran is a major producer of natural gas, it has long suffered from shortages of the fuel, a crisis that has only intensified since the onset of the Iran war in late February.

According to Tehran, during the most intense phase of the war — which lasted nearly 40 days — the US and Israel launched strikes against critical Iranian infrastructure, including gas processing plants.

Iran responded by attacking what it said were US military facilities and other interests in the broader Middle East region. Regional governments, witnesses on the ground and Euronews journalists reported the strikes also hit civilian and infrastructure targets.

Both sides also imposed concurrent blockades of the Strait of Hormuz, a key waterway for the world's gas and oil trade.

In early October, Putin stated that Russia is prepared to act as a mediator to help end the war. Moscow has not acted as an intermediary in the talks. Pakistan and Qatar have so far served as the main mediators.

Rezaei noted that Putin emphasised the need for regional security mechanisms and asserted that "any interference by external powers" in resolving the situation in the Middle East is unacceptable.

Russia has delayed, deflected to Kyiv or otherwise rejected all attempts to broker an end to its ongoing war in Ukraine, now well into its fifth year.

According to Rezaei, the meeting also addressed progress on key bilateral infrastructure projects. "The Supreme National Security Council has approved the executive agreement for the construction of the Rasht–Astara railway, with Russian participation," he said, as cited by the Iranian news agency IRNA.

"The Minister of Roads and Urban Development will soon sign this contract on behalf of the Islamic Republic of Iran," Rezaei added.

Spanning just over 160 kilometres, this section represents the final missing link in the western route of the International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC), which is intended to provide a direct connection between Russia and Iranian ports on the Persian Gulf.

Press reports citing the semi-official Iranian news agency Fars indicated that Russia had finalised preparations to provide Iran with a $1 billion loan, though the transfer of funds has been delayed.

According to Fars, this $1 billion is intended to be the first instalment of a planned $20 billion Russian loan to Iran.

Some experts have linked the delay in disbursing these funds specifically to the stalled progress of the Rasht–Astara railway project.

"We are prepared to provide the necessary credit resources," Putin stated during a meeting of the Valdai Discussion Club on 1 October.

"The question is for our Iranian friends to complete their part of the work at this stage."

After the meeting, the Russian Foreign Ministry issued a warning travel advisory telling Russians to avoid visiting Iran and the Middle East while the risk of renewed war persists.

The Russian Foreign Ministry had previously advised Russian citizens against travelling to Persian Gulf countries for tourism — specifically in February, right before the US and Israel launched their attacks on Iran in the opening salvo of the war.

What makes a city attractive to investors and the people who live there? From affordable housing and urban planning to smart technologies and changing lifestyles, Azerbaijan’s first International Real Estate Exhibition put the future of the built environment in focus.
Azerbaijan hosted its first international real estate exhibition in Baku this week, drawing developers and investors from across the region as the country pursues large-scale urban expansion and reconstruction.

AIREX 2026 examined the country’s property market and the challenges facing a sector increasingly shaped by technology, sustainability and changing urban needs.

AIREX organiser Riad Salayev said the aim was to attract “more key players from the region” and “more international participants and investors” to the exhibition.

The exhibition comes as Azerbaijan continues to invest in urban development. Baku is undergoing major changes, while reconstruction in Karabakh is creating demand for new homes and public infrastructure.

For new neighbourhoods to work, housing must go hand in hand with schools, healthcare, transport and other essential services.

Chief of Staff of Azerbaijan’s State Housing Development Agency (MIDA) Jamshid Amirov said the agency had launched 20 projects to help citizens buy homes on preferential terms.

“Seven projects are in Baku, three in Karabakh and ten in other regions,” Amirov told Euronews.

Amirov said 11 affordable housing projects have been completed so far, including five in Baku and six in the regions.

“We are currently implementing three additional projects,” he said, with “three more residential complexes in the design phase”.

Elchin Gadimov, Chief Commercial Officer of PASHA Real Estate Group and CEO of International Projects, said his sector was moving towards “capital efficiency, customer experience, international demand and long-term asset value”.

Gadimov explained that with advancements in AI, developers can now use a simple photograph to visualise a proposed project without expensive rendering software or specialised equipment.

What once required “significant time, technical expertise and financial resources” can now be explored “in minutes”, helping teams test ideas earlier.

International architecture practice Chapman Taylor has worked on master-planning projects in Shusha and Jabrayil, as well as districts in Aghdam and Fuzuli. Its work in Fuzuli also includes a hospital and a vocational school.

Chapman Taylor Group Board Director Marcelina Zielinska said that successful urban development required more than designing individual buildings.

Planners must consider “business, the economy, location, climate and community”, Zielinska told Euronews.

Alikhan Aliyev, Director of Kazakhstan-based BI Group in Azerbaijan, also highlighted the importance of residents' experience.

The company’s priorities, he said, include “architecture, comfort, safety and residents’ quality of life”.

The exhibition also offered a glimpse of how developers from other markets view Azerbaijan and the wider region.

Tarik Imam, founder of Kinglike Luxury, which operates in Georgia and Türkiye, described Baku as “an emerging destination for international real estate investment”, pointing to its “visionary developments” and “strong growth potential”.

Its Chief Commercial Officer, Lina Buziashvili, said AIREX offered an opportunity to “strengthen regional partnerships” and “connect with investors”.

She also highlighted the potential for partnerships that create “long-term value across the region”.

The Maldives provided a different example of how property development can extend beyond residential construction. Meekail Haleem, Acting Head of the Hospitality Section at the state-owned Housing Development Corporation, presented plans for a cruise terminal and waterfront commercial district in Hulhumalé, as well as the proposed tourism destination Kudagiri Falhu.

The projects are intended to broaden the country’s tourism and commercial offer. Haleem said the aim was to “diversify the Maldives’ tourism and commercial offerings”, with joint ventures and international partnerships helping to bring the developments forward.

Digital tools are also changing how residential buildings are managed, from resident services to the systems that keep buildings operating.

At Sea Breeze in Azerbaijan, Digital Residence is developing a model that combines AI-powered services with intelligent building management.

The IMF expects Kazakhstan’s economy to grow by 4.9% in 2027, up from 4.4% this year. Kazakhstan is forecast to grow faster than any other oil exporter in the IMF’s Middle East and Central Asia group in 2026.
Kazakhstan’s economic growth is expected to accelerate to 4.9% in 2027, up from a projected 4.4% this year, according to the International Monetary Fund’s (IMF) latest World Economic Outlook, released in October.

The 2026 forecast marks a slowdown from the 6.5% growth recorded in 2025. Nevertheless, Kazakhstan is projected to post the strongest economic growth among oil-exporting countries in the IMF’s Middle East and Central Asia group this year. The region’s economy is projected to contract by 0.3% in 2026 before growing by 6.3% in 2027.

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has hit Middle Eastern oil exporters hardest. The IMF expects Bahrain, Iraq, Kuwait and Qatar to suffer double-digit contractions this year, followed by double-digit growth in 2027 as production and exports return to normal.

Meanwhile, the report says commodity prices, remittances and ongoing reforms will continue to support growth in the Caucasus and Central Asia.

Uzbekistan’s economy is forecast to grow by 7.8% in 2026, before slowing to 6.5% in 2027. Kyrgyzstan is projected to record growth of 9% this year and 7% next year, while Tajikistan’s economy is expected to expand by 7.3% and 6.8%, respectively. Turkmenistan is forecast to see growth of 2.4% in 2026, edging up to 2.5% in 2027.

The IMF identifies geopolitics and technology as two major forces shaping the global economic outlook, with the US-Iran conflict posing a significant challenge to energy markets.

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz in March disrupted the transportation of around 20% of the world's oil supply, equivalent to approximately 20 million barrels a day.

The disruption prompted oil producers outside the Persian Gulf, namely Brazil, Kazakhstan, the United States and Venezuela, to increase output to compensate for the shortfall. Some Gulf producers redirected supplies through alternative routes, while the International Energy Agency (IEA) and China released emergency reserves.

The IMF's baseline scenario assumes that the Strait of Hormuz will reopen in March 2027, with a broader return to pre-war conditions expected by October of that year.

Russia's war in Ukraine has also added to disruptions in global oil supplies. Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy infrastructure, both onshore and at sea, have affected shipments of Kazakh crude transported through the Caspian Pipeline Consortium (CPC).

Despite the negative impact of geopolitical shocks on the global economy, investment in artificial intelligence (AI) and related technologies has provided some support to economic activity.

The IMF points to the growing demand for advanced technology hardware, including semiconductors, as a potential opportunity for commodity-exporting economies such as Kazakhstan. These countries are positioned earlier in the global supply chain, supplying raw materials needed for the production of advanced technology components, much of which takes place in economies such as South Korea and Taiwan.

An Azerbaijani delegation led by its energy minister travelled to Ukraine to sign a 2026-2027 Action Plan. Bilateral talks focused on emergency winter grid equipment, joint manufacturing, and expanding European transit via the Trans-Balkan route.
Azerbaijan and Ukraine have formalised a major expansion of their economic and energy cooperation, concluding the 14th meeting of their Joint Intergovernmental Commission in the western Ukrainian city of Uzhhorod.

The bilateral talks mark the first time the commission has convened on Ukrainian territory since the beginning of the full-scale Russian invasion.

Co-chaired by Azerbaijani Energy Minister Parviz Shahbazov and Ukrainian First Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Energy Denys Shmyhal, the delegations signed a formal Protocol and adopted a comprehensive Action Plan for 2026-2027.

The strategic agreements cover 14 distinct sectors, with a strong focus on energy infrastructure resilience and cross-border commercial ties amid severe wartime logistical hurdles.

Shahbazov confirmed in a post on X that the two sides "discussed prospects for further strengthening cooperation in trade-economic, investment, energy, transport, technology and innovation," noting that the newly adopted action plan will guide the period ahead.

A critical focus of the Uzhhorod summit was the physical protection and stabilisation of Ukraine's national energy grid ahead of the winter months.

First Deputy Prime Minister Shmyhal and Shahbazov toured a western Ukrainian energy facility to inspect "second-level protection" systems designed specifically to defend critical power infrastructure against ongoing Russian military attacks.

During the site visit, officials confirmed the delivery of the latest Azerbaijani emergency equipment shipment, valued at $1.47 million (€1.32 million).

This strategic dispatch includes nine industrial transformers, 186 kilometres of high-voltage cables, generators, and 12 kV electrical distribution gear required for rapid grid repairs.

"We express our gratitude to Azerbaijan for its constant and substantial assistance in recovery efforts following Russian strikes," Shmyhal stated on X following the inspection.

"We are particularly grateful for transformers, generators, and everything else energy workers need."

This recent package brings Baku’s total humanitarian, rehabilitation, and reconstruction assistance to Ukraine to $52 million (€47 million) since the outbreak of the war.

Beyond immediate emergency aid, the two nations are strategically aligning their long-term energy transit routes to ensure uninterrupted cross-border trade.

During the accompanying Azerbaijan-Ukraine Business Forum, discussions shifted toward deep structural integration and the joint utilisation of existing regional infrastructure.

The Ukrainian government actively pushed for the coordinated use of the Trans-Balkan route alongside its vast underground gas storage facilities and established oil pipeline networks. Both delegations also reviewed broader macro-economic strategies to develop the Caspian-Black Sea-Europe Green Energy Corridor.

Expanding these specific transit routes allows both states to maintain reliable energy supplies while offering alternative logistics options for European markets currently navigating highly restricted supply chains.

Despite severe logistical constraints and transport bottlenecks, economic activity between Baku and Kyiv remains robust.

Official data released during the commission session indicates that bilateral trade turnover increased by 10.5% over the first eight months of 2026, driven directly by a 17% rise in Azerbaijani exports to Ukraine.

To anchor these commercial ties, both governments are actively pivoting toward industrial localisation and technological partnerships to rebuild Ukraine's heavy industrial base.

"Ukraine is interested in joint production, localisation in Ukraine, and technological partnerships," Shmyhal noted on X, extending a direct invitation to Azerbaijani firms to invest in distributed energy projects and logistics.

In response, the Azerbaijani delegation pitched the Alat Free Economic Zone as a primary tax-free logistics hub for joint enterprise-level collaboration.

The summit officially concluded with a targeted site visit to the Khust Industrial Park, where officials inspected a wind turbine manufacturing facility and the Azerbaijani-owned ATEF enterprise to assess direct opportunities for joint production.
