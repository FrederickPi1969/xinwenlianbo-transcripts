Trump tied the decision to criticism from left wing politicians, while US forces struck Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz.
US President Donald Trump said on Tuesday that Washington would stop giving Israel around $4 billion a year in military aid from 2027. He made the announcement as polls opened across the United States for the midterm elections.

Speaking by phone to Fox News, Trump said Israel no longer needed the money, but that the US would keep selling arms to its closest Middle East ally. He said Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had come to him with the idea, and that they had been discussing it for a couple of years.

He also tied the decision to the campaign. He said the aid had become too easy a target for critics such as Abdul El-Sayed, a Democrat and outspoken critic of Israel who is running for the Senate in Michigan.

Unlike France, Spain, Italy and many other European countries, the US has no electoral silence period banning campaigning or political comment on polling day. The main restrictions are state laws on campaigning near polling stations, so politicians, the president included, are free to give interviews while voting is under way.

Netanyahu’s office did not immediately comment. Gadi Eisenkot, his main rival, said it was dangerous that Israel’s defence establishment had learned of the change through the media, and called for the prime minister to be replaced.

The current 10-year, $38 billion agreement was signed in 2016 under Barack Obama and runs until 2028.

The announcement came as tensions rose again in the Gulf. A US official said American forces had carried out defensive strikes on Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz that were using surface-to-air missiles. Iranian state television reported explosions on Qeshm Island.

Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf, Iran’s parliament speaker and chief negotiator, said Tehran had delivered a “firm and rational response” to Washington and that it was now up to the US to prevent renewed war. He told parliament Iran remained ready “with its finger on the trigger”, but did not say what the response was.

Portugal's housing protesters celebrated in Lisbon on 2 November after a court ruled that activist Renata Cambra could set up a tent in Rossio Square, where police had cleared demonstrators over the weekend.

The decision overturned the automatic 00:30 curfew used by officers to demand that protesters leave, recognising the tent as a symbolic part of the demonstration rather than an illegal occupation of public space.

The Acampada movement is campaigning against Portugal's housing crisis and the laws governing relations between landlords and tenants.

Cambra says house prices have risen sharply, making decent accommodation increasingly difficult to find. She also points to the impact of tourism, short-term rentals and luxury properties on housing costs.

Democrats look all but certain to take the US House. The real fight is for the Senate, where a handful of races in states Republicans never expected to defend will decide who controls Washington.
Polling stations are opening across the United States in a midterm election that, a year ago, Republicans expected to survive with their Senate majority intact. That expectation has been reduced to a hope.

Start with the House of Representatives, where the question is no longer whether Democrats win, but by how much. Prediction markets give them a 95% chance of taking control. The Cook Political Report now rates 222 seats as at least leaning Democratic, four more than the 218 needed for a majority, against 195 for Republicans, with 18 pure toss-ups. A good night for Democrats could see them controlling as many as 230 seats. A great night means the Senate and a blue wave.

Republicans hold 53 of the Senate's 100 seats. To take control, Democrats need a net gain of four. Three is not enough: in a 50-50 Senate, Vice President JD Vance casts the tie-breaking vote, just as Kamala Harris did a record 33 times as vice president; however, Senator John Fetterman, the Democrat from Pennsylvania, could muddle certain partisan votes in a 50-50 Senate.

Prediction markets now give Democrats roughly a three-in-four chance of winning the Senate. Cook rates six races as toss-ups, all of them held by Republicans.

The battleground covers 12 seats. Democrats are defending three: Michigan, Minnesota and New Hampshire. Republicans are defending nine: Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Nebraska, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Texas.

Two of the three Democratic seats look safe. In Minnesota, Peggy Flanagan leads Michele Tafoya by 5.2 points in the RealClearPolitics average. In New Hampshire, Chris Pappas leads former Senator John E. Sununu by eight points in the same average. Michigan is another matter, but we will come back to it.

North Carolina is a swing state, but it leans Republican: it has voted for the Republican presidential candidate in every election since 1980 bar one. With Senator Thom Tillis retiring, it should have been a fair fight.

Instead, national Republicans all but walked away. The Senate Leadership Fund, the party's main Senate super PAC, had planned to spend $71 million backing its candidate, Michael Whatley. It cancelled its remaining ads in early October, after spending more than $30 million. Former Governor Roy Cooper now leads Whatley by eight points in polls from both Quantus Insights and Trafalgar Group, two Republican-leaning pollsters.

Barring an upset, North Carolina will be the Democrats' first flip. That leaves three more to find.

Democrats placed one of their biggest bets in Ohio. Sherrod Brown, who lost his seat two years ago, is running against Republican Jon Husted, who was appointed to JD Vance's old Senate seat in 2025. Brown leads by 2.8 points in the RealClearPolitics average, in a state Trump won by double digits.

Texas would be the bigger upset. The state has not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1988. Yet Democrat James Talarico leads Republican Ken Paxton 49% to 47% in the latest Texas Southern University poll—in line with other recent surveys. Still, this is within the margin of error and is effectively a tie. Texas probably isn’t turning blue. Republicans lead every other statewide race in the University of Houston's Hobby School poll, from governor down to railroad commissioner. What Talarico could benefit from is ticket-splitting: voters backing a Republican for governor, attorney general or Congress, then crossing over to the Democrat for the Senate. Republican Governor Greg Abbott wins 93% of Republicans in the Hobby poll. Paxton wins 88%.

Part of the problem for Paxton is Talarico, a disciplined young candidate who has run a strong campaign. The other part of the problem is Paxton. The embattled attorney general, impeached by his own party in the Texas House in 2023 and acquitted by the state Senate, is viewed unfavourably by 57% of likely voters and favourably by just 42%, according to Texas Southern University. Trump backed him over four-term Senator John Cornyn in the primary. If Paxton loses, it will be a wound Republicans partly inflicted on themselves.

Alaska votes Republican for president, but it has a long independent streak. It sent Democrat Mary Peltola to the House in 2022. This time, Peltola is challenging Senator Dan Sullivan, and she leads 48% to 46% in the latest New York Times/Siena poll. Again, within the margin of error.

Then came the other Dan Sullivan. The New York Times reported last week that Democratic operatives, including senior Peltola aides and officials at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, recruited Daniel J. Sullivan, a retired teacher, to run as a spoiler and confuse voters. Ballot-stacking is generally legal, but the optics are terrible. Peltola denies involvement. Governor Mike Dunleavy has ordered a criminal investigation and the Justice Department sued, unsuccessfully, to stop Alaska certifying the result if Sullivan loses.

A trick meant to take votes from Sullivan could end up costing Peltola the race, or the legitimacy of her win. Expect President Trump to aggressively attack the result should Peltola win.

Kansas has not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1932. This year, the Democrats are running Adam Hamilton, the senior pastor of the largest Methodist congregation in the country, against Senator Roger Marshall. Hamilton leads by 4.1 points in the RealClearPolitics average.

Even the Washington Post's pollsters, whose own survey had Hamilton 10 points ahead, caution that Democrats were far more eager to respond than Republicans. But in a state Trump won by double digits, a single-digit race is a warning.

The rest of the map In Maine, Troy Jackson leads Senator Susan Collins by 3.6 points in the RealClearPolitics average—but never underestimate Senator Collins, who has a history of pulling off electoral upsets. Iowa is a coin flip, with Josh Turek at 48% and Ashley Hinson at 47% in the New York Times/Siena poll. In South Carolina, Senator Darline Graham, appointed in July after the death of her brother Lindsey, leads Democrat Annie Andrews by 2.6 points in the RealClearPolitics average. Nebraska offers Republicans the most breathing room, with Senator Pete Ricketts leading independent Dan Osborn by six points in the latest Times/Siena poll.

If Republicans are to hold the Senate, Michigan may be where they do it. It is the only Democratic-held seat in RealClearPolitics' toss-up column, in a state that voted Trump, Biden, Trump. With Senator Gary Peters retiring, a Republican win would force Democrats to find five flips.

The Democratic nominee is Abdul El-Sayed, a progressive former public health official backed by Bernie Sanders and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, whom Republicans tie to the Democratic Socialists of America. A staunch critic of Israel, he has pledged to oppose further US aid to the country. His platform still includes Medicare for All and abolishing ICE, but on the stump, he has traded the left-wing agenda for a populist pitch.

Some in his own party have been harsher. On Sunday, Congresswoman Haley Stevens, whom El-Sayed narrowly beat in the Democratic primary, endorsed Mike Rogers, the Republican candidate, after being disinvited from a joint campaign event. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called it "a despicable betrayal". Senator John Fetterman, a fellow Democrat, took Stevens' side, saying El-Sayed has "the worst case of little man syndrome I've ever seen". El-Sayed still leads by 5.3 points in the RealClearPolitics average, but data from Trafalgar Group has him just one point ahead. A Republican win in Michigan would be a major upset, but the path to it is narrower than El-Sayed's.

Turkey and South Korea are expanding nuclear, defence and trade ties. Their latest agreement explores funding for nuclear projects, while Ankara weighs potential partners for its planned Sinop plant.
South Korea and Turkey signed an agreement on Tuesday to explore financing for Turkish energy projects, as Seoul seeks a role in building the country’s planned second nuclear plant at Sinop on the Black Sea coast.

The memorandum of understanding was signed by the Export-Import Bank of Korea and Turkey Nuclear Energy Inc following talks between South Korean President Lee Jae Myung and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan in Seoul.

The two organisations will examine possible funding arrangements and set up a working group to exchange information, Turkey’s energy ministry said. Its announcement did not specify a funding amount.

Lee said cooperation on the plant was progressing “smoothly”, with site assessments and a joint working group already under way. He said the new financing agreement would support South Korean companies seeking to take part in Turkish nuclear projects.

The latest agreement builds on a nuclear cooperation memorandum signed in November 2025 between Turkey Nuclear Energy Inc. and South Korea’s state-run Korea Electric Power Corporation, known as KEPCO.

In an interview with Yonhap published on Monday, Erdoğan described South Korea as a potential partner for Sinop. He said preliminary feasibility studies had been carried out with KEPCO and that technical and economic assessments were continuing.

Ankara would consider financing arrangements, electricity generation costs and benefits to the Turkish economy before making a final decision, he said.

“We are taking into account our contacts with other countries and examining all possible choices in light of our national interests,” Erdoğan told Yonhap.

The two leaders also agreed to expand joint defence production and technology cooperation, and explore opportunities for companies from both countries to work together in overseas markets.

That would build on existing cooperation on Turkey’s Altay main battle tank programme, South Korea’s presidential office said.

Separate energy and mining agreements cover renewable energy, hydrogen and cooperation on critical minerals. The countries will establish an energy transition forum to meet annually and review joint projects, Turkey’s energy ministry said.

Erdoğan said bilateral trade exceeded $11 billion (€9.8bn) last year and that the leaders discussed how to reach their existing $15 billion (€13.4bn) target.

South Korea’s presidential office said the talks also addressed trade imbalances, import restrictions and customs delays. The countries agreed to raise their joint free trade agreement committee to ministerial level and signed a separate investment cooperation memorandum.

Erdoğan’s visit is his first to South Korea since 2018. The countries established diplomatic relations in 1957 and became strategic partners in 2012.

Flash floods sweep across the United States Southwest, destroying homes, stranding residents and forcing hundreds to evacuate.
… More
Aerial images showed roads, neighbourhoods and fields submerged across the southwestern United States on Monday, 2 November, after days of heavy rain triggered severe flash floods.

In Colorado, cars, motorhomes and homes were swept away in Arboles, while in Camp Verde, Arizona, residents scrambled to safety as water rose around an RV park. Hundreds of people were forced to evacuate, and rescue teams carried out dozens of operations, including helicopter rescues. At least one person died, while police suspect a second death was linked to the flooding.

The floods affected communities across the Four Corners region, where Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico and Utah meet. In Durango, Colorado, around 200 residents joined clean-up efforts on Monday, clearing mud and debris from homes and streets.

The San Juan River reached 16.81 feet (5.1 metres), flooding parts of the town before its level began to fall.

The heavy rainfall also brought some relief to reservoirs in a region enduring a prolonged drought.

Qatar said Tuesday it saw hope for an interim deal as mediators continue to exchange proposals, despite fresh US strikes and threats to shipping through Hormuz continue to complicate efforts to end the Iran war.
There was still hope for a deal between the US and Iran, Qatar said on Tuesday as mediators exchanged proposals in real time after talks in Tehran.

“If there were no hope, there would be no negotiations,” Qatar’s foreign ministry spokesperson Majed Al Ansari said at a weekly briefing in Doha.

A Qatari delegation met Iranian officials for more than three hours on Monday before returning to Doha that evening, and Washington and Tehran were now reviewing proposals, Al Ansari said.

Meanwhile, Qatar was working towards an interim agreement intended to build trust and allow both sides to demonstrate they would carry out their commitments.

“Confidence is built through the process, not specific actions,” Al Ansari said, adding that an interim deal could be a first step towards lasting peace.

The ongoing war began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February, resulting in separate US and Iranian blockades of the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial route for oil and gas exports.

Al Ansari also said freedom of navigation was “non-negotiable,” condemning threats to ships belonging to Qatar or other countries.

He warned that attacks on shipping and other military escalation threatened the whole region and would affect the negotiations.

Despite overnight US strikes on Iran’s Qeshm island in the Strait of Hormuz, Al Ansari said Doha was assessing the talks by the official responses to its proposals, rather than public threats and statements.

According to Al Ansari, Qatar has continued to work closely with Pakistan and coordinate with its Gulf neighbours to prevent further escalation.

Iranian officials have repeatedly said Tehran's conditions must be met before Hormuz could reopen or nuclear talks could begin.

Investigators in Turkey probing a multi-billion-dollar investment fund scandal have uncovered a "comprehensive criminal network" that infiltrated the highest levels of financial markets, Justice Minister Akin Gürlek said on Tuesday.

His remarks came days after police detained the Istanbul stock exchange boss as part of a sprawling probe that began in mid-September into allegations of widespread market manipulation affecting the savings of nearly half a million investors.

The scandal has dominated the headlines for weeks, raising uncomfortable questions about who might have profited and putting pressure on President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's ruling AK Party (AKP), with one of its top cadres resigning following allegations about her investment activities.

"The structure is not limited to a simple stock market scam, there is a comprehensive criminal organisation involved," Gürlek told the private channel NTV.

"Although the investigation initially began with market actors, it has now revealed a criminal organisation that extends into the upper levels of the financial bureaucracy," he said.

"Those within the mechanism that controls the money rendered the oversight mechanisms ineffective by establishing a system of bribery," he added.

By forming "a cartel", they sought "to siphon off liquidity from the market by manipulating certain shares and to take control of the market", Gürlek added.

Investigators were looking into 433 suspects and probing "all links within the structure, which extends to the very foundations of the financial system and the upper echelons of the capital markets".

So far, 112 suspects had been jailed pending trial, while another 186 had been granted conditional release, and 38 others were still being sought, Gürlek said.

Among them were "political figures linked to criminal organisations within our fund market", he said, without giving further details.

On Saturday, Korkmaz Enes Ergun, head of the Borsa Istanbul stock exchange, was detained on suspicion of "membership in an organisation established to commit crimes" and accepting bribes, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported.

Two weeks ago, the head of Turkey's SPK capital markets regulator was sacked by presidential decree just days after prosecutors began investigating his predecessor in connection with the affair.

At the same time, the deputy finance minister also resigned after his son-in-law was jailed pending trial in the fund scandal.

The government has called for the voluntary return of all monies obtained through market manipulation, with Gürlek telling NTV "approximately 30 billion lira (€536 million) in cash, real estates and shares" had so far been collected, while vowing to pursue any monies transferred abroad.

Gürlek said members of the network engaged in "close and systematic collaboration" and also claimed there was "extensive collusion between those carrying out the stock market manipulation and the media".

Although he did not elaborate further, police have so far detained five journalists, charging them with spreading misinformation about the scandal, several of whom have been jailed pending trial.

Gürlek stressed a key priority was to ensure the return of investors' money, with Erdoğan's office saying on Tuesday that by 30 October, payments totalling 97.3 billion lira had been made to nearly 250,000 investors in a post on X.

The Asian Football Confederation cited “exceptional circumstances” and travel disruptions in moving the tournament from January 2027 to late December, after Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia forced the Dakar Rally and the WTA Finals to be cancelled or relocated.
The 2027 Asian Cup football tournament in Saudi Arabia will be postponed from January to December due to "exceptional circumstances" in the region, the continent's governing body said on Tuesday.

A number of sports events in Saudi Arabia have been cancelled or postponed in recent weeks following Houthi attacks on the country.

The Asian Cup was originally scheduled to take place from 7 January to 5 February next year.

"The organising committee for AFC Asian Cup approved to reschedule the flagship national team tournament in light of the current exceptional circumstances arising from developments in the region," the Asian Football Confederation said in a statement.

Local organisers insisted last month that the Asian Cup was "on schedule", despite growing fears for the tournament's fate because of the security situation in Saudi Arabia and the wider Middle East.

The Saudi cities of Jeddah, Riyadh and Al Khobar are set to stage the month-long competition.

The decision to move the tournament back almost a year came after "extensive discussions and close consultation" between Saudi officials and the AFC, the Kuala Lumpur-based governing body said.

"Exceptional circumstances have been created for the organisation and delivery of the competition as originally scheduled, making the postponement necessary in the interest of all participating teams, officials, spectators and other stakeholders," it said.

Yemen's Houthis have targeted Saudi infrastructure, including airports, since the war in Yemen resumed in July following a years-long truce. The Houthis are fighting a Saudi-led coalition supporting Yemen's internationally recognised government.

An exhibition tennis tournament due to feature Novak Đoković, Carlos Alcaraz and Alexander Zverev was postponed.

The WTA Finals were also scheduled to be held in Riyadh but will instead take place at Indian Wells in the United States.

The AFC faced growing pressure from Asian football associations to postpone the tournament due to fears for player and fan safety.

Following the announcement of the postponement, Yasser al-Misehal, chairman of the local organising committee, vowed that Saudi Arabia was "fully ready".

"The tournament will move to the new dates, and our preparations will continue as planned," he told the Saudi Press Agency.

"We're working closely with the AFC and all our partners to make sure the tournament runs smoothly and meets the high standards expected of Saudi Arabia and the Asian Cup."

The organisation representing South Korean players was among those that had expressed severe reservations about the competition going ahead early next year.

"The ongoing military tensions and security instability in the Middle East are issues that cannot be taken lightly," Lee Keun-ho, president of the Korea Professional Footballers Association, said last month, according to Yonhap News Agency.

Lee added that "no tournament's value can take precedence over the lives and safety of players and football fans."

Parliament speaker Ghalibaf said “the ball is in America’s court to prevent war” as Iran reached its highest defensive readiness and Iranian outlets reported Tehran had updated its target bank to include US support bases in Europe under a “response without geographical limits”.
Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned on Tuesday that any US strategic error would open "a new battlefield in the war," hours after a US Air Force jet reportedly struck an IRGC missile system on Qeshm Island.

Ghalibaf told parliament Iran was at its highest level of defensive readiness, and that responsibility for preventing further escalation rested with Washington. "The ball is in America's court to prevent war," he said.

The remarks came after residents of Qeshm Island, Hengam and Bandar Abbas reported successive explosions and tremors overnight, according to Iranian news agencies IRNA and Fars.

US media outlets reported that a US Air Force jet had struck an IRGC surface-to-air missile launch system on the island after a missile had been fired from Qeshm at a US aircraft in the Strait of Hormuz.

The operation was described as limited and aimed at protecting commercial shipping. CENTCOM issued no formal statement confirming or denying the accounts, and no Iranian military or government official formally confirmed a direct attack on the island.

The IRGC said separately that its electronic warfare systems had downed two US MQM-170 reconnaissance drones south of Qeshm Island and destroyed a US MQ-9 drone east of the Strait of Hormuz.

Ghalibaf said late Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, as commander-in-chief, had defined Iran's military strategy for confronting a possible attack.

According to Ghalibaf, senior military commanders and Mojtaba Khamenei — the new supreme leader in direct control of the IRGC — met last week and reportedly decided on "a response without geographical limits" and Tehran's right to determine when any conflict ends.

Former foreign minister and parliamentarian Manouchehr Mottaki said on Sunday he interpreted that formulation to mean US military bases in European countries, including Britain, Italy, Portugal and Germany, could be drawn into any conflict. "If our war is to break out, it will no longer be a regional war, it will be extraregional," he said.

Tehran-based Hamshahri newspaper reported on Tuesday that Iran had updated its "target bank," examining a scenario in which US support bases and infrastructure in Europe would be targeted under the "response without geographical limits" strategy.

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi expressed support for Gulf state mediation in a post on X. "We have no hostility with the Gulf Cooperation Council countries, we value the positive role played by Qatar and Oman, and we continue dialogue with Saudi Arabia," he said.

Vehicles were swept away by powerful floodwaters on the Greek island of Rhodes early on Monday, 2 November, after heavy rain hit the eastern village of Archangelos.

Cars and trucks were left partly submerged as water rushed through the streets, carrying rubbish bins and debris. Homes were also flooded, with Archangelos reporting the worst damage, according to local media.

Dozens of homes and businesses were affected, and the full extent of the damage was still being assessed.

Although the rain had eased by Monday morning, meteorologists warned that further showers and thunderstorms were possible, potentially bringing more disruption to the island.

A 2024 United Nations report said proceeds from North Korean cyberattacks had "reportedly helped to fund the country's development of weapons of mass destruction".
Pyongyang has blasted the FBI's allegations that it was behind the theft of $6 billion (€5.3 billion) from cryptocurrency platform Binance, as it test-fired strategic cruise missiles in a new display of its weapons capabilities.

The missile launch came on the heels of a public rebuttal from Kim Yo-jong, the powerful sister of leader Kim Jong-un, who slammed the FBI's allegations as a "grave political provocation".

On Saturday, the FBI said a North Korea-affiliated hacking group called TraderTraitor had been behind the hack.

Experts see the incident as potentially the largest cryptocurrency theft in history, with a haul equalling "nearly a quarter of the DPRK's estimated annual GDP," Michael Bosack, founding director of the Parley Policy Institute and a former UN Command official, told the AFP news agency.

Kim Yo-jong said the FBI's claim was "absurd rhetoric and nothing new," given prior US allegations that Pyongyang was behind last year's major hack of Bybit.

Still, North Korea likely felt compelled to respond because of the "level of international attention on the incident", Bosack said.

The denial was aimed at maintaining channels through which North Korea could continue laundering and using the cryptocurrency, Bosack added.

"If external parties start closing doors based on the international heat on the matter, then the stolen cryptocurrency does nothing to support the Kim regime's near-term priorities," he said.

The hack has prompted South Korea to call for increased protections for its digital infrastructure against cyberattacks.

"Not long ago, even the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange suffered astronomical losses from a cyberattack believed to have been carried out by a hacking group," President Lee Jae-myung said.

"As hacking techniques become increasingly intelligent, sophisticated and commonplace with the development of artificial intelligence, existing security systems centred on inspections and responses after incidents have revealed clear limitations," he said.

"We must establish a comprehensive prevention system to address the cybersecurity crisis," he added.

A 2024 United Nations report said proceeds from North Korean cyberattacks had "reportedly helped to fund the country's development of weapons of mass destruction".

Monday's launch by North Korea involved strategic cruise missiles fitted with a new cluster-munition warhead, the official Korean Central News Agency (KCNA) reported on Tuesday.

"The middle-sized dispersal warhead tipped on the cruise missile is capable of turning into ashes a target area of 5 to 6 hectares with its highest-density power," KCNA said.

KCNA said the test had "perfected the capability to deliver a vertical strike against the target by improving the terminal manoeuvrability of the cruise missile".

A North Korean official was quoted as saying the test was conducted "because our armed forces should defend the security of the country and deter all sorts of threats."

North Korea is demonstrating that they are capable of disabling "key South Korean and US military infrastructure, including airfield runways, radar bases, air defence batteries and assembled troops, with a single cruise missile", Lim Eul-chul, a professor at the Institute for Far Eastern Studies at Kyungnam University, told AFP.

The launch also aims to provide a "double deterrence against South Korea by strengthening conventional asymmetric strike capabilities at a stage before the use of nuclear weapons", he said.

The nuclear-armed North recently trialled a range of weaponry including attack drones, a new precision-guided torpedo system and an intermediate-range hypersonic missile.

South Korea's military did not announce the cruise missile launch, as it had also not done with Sunday's test.

The strikes on Iran come on the eve of US midterm elections, and just hours after Trump signalled progress in negotiations with Tehran.
The United States has carried out strikes against Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz, a US official said on Monday.

The official, who spoke on condition of anonymity, described the operation as defensive strikes targeting "Iranians using surface-to-air missiles against US asset".

Meanwhile, Iranian media reported five explosions on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz.

Just hours before, US President Donald Trump signalled progress in negotiations with Tehran.

"Iran wants a settlement, as soon as possible. It could happen!," Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social.

Vice President JD Vance told reporters that both he and Trump felt optimistic about "the prospects for potentially coming to a negotiated settlement".

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Monday that indirect talks with the United States were taking place via mediators.

"Given the sensitivity and gravity of the current situation, consultations and deliberations are very intensive," Araghchi told state media.

The strikes come on the eve of midterm elections, in which the Republican party is pushing to retain control of Congress.

However, Washington's war in Iran has been a growing point of political contention and a contributing factor to Trump's declining approval ratings, as American's increasingly disapprove of the conflict, particularly over its economic consquences.

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway between Iran and Oman that normally carries around a fifth of the world's oil, has been heavily disrupted since the war began in late February.
