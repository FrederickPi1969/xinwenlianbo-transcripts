Ana Brnabic, Speaker of the outgoing parliament and a former prime minister, as well as top figure in Serbian President Aleksandar Vucic's political camp, faced the top candidate of the opposition Student List - Students win political platform and Dean of the Arts University in Belgrade, Mirjana Nikolic.

The two contenders agreed to appear together live, but not without hesitations, and engaged in a fiery one-on-one debate, confronting their views on education, the chosen theme of the debate, before descending into the wider Serbian society turmoil and political conflict.

The N1 televised debate was the most watched programme during its time slot, with a rating of over 48 points, and the most followed television show of the past month.

The N1 host invited them to present their programmes, but the contenders went into trading accusations of violence, mismanagement and intolerance which matched the heated national political discourse as the country is gearing up for early elections called after months of demonstrations and protests which led to violent incidents.

Triggered by the collapse of a concrete canopy at the Novi Sad railway station in November 2024 which killed 16 people, the protests were started by students who blocked state universities for a prolonged period of time before spreading across the country. The protesters blamed the tragedy on corruption and mismanagement. Eventually, the ad-hoc student movement registered their political list for the elections and are now seen by part of the public and by most pollsters as the main challengers to president Vucic’s rule.

The political tension was obvious even before the TV debate started, with Brnabic declaring that Nikolic would not even shake her hand when they met in the studio.

“You are constantly offending me, starting from your refusal to shake my hand. I consider that to be a basic human gesture. Generals are at war with each other and still shake hands,” Brnabic said.

She asked her contender if “is that your decent Serbia?,” to which Nikolic fired back saying “decent Serbia is yet to come.”

The debate sparked surprise and stirred significant interest in Serbia as government officials have largely avoided the N1 channel throughout multiple election cycles, claiming negative bias, a qualification N1 rejected.

At the same time, not many expected such a programme to happen at all as real debates by political opponents are rare in Serbia, with the media openly supporting one political camp or the other and thus rarely inviting politicians or activists across the partisan lines. In turn, politicians rarely agree to take part in programmes at channels they deem to be hostile and combative.

Therefore, the debate surprised Serbia and, moreover, that it was hosted by N1 television whose editorial policy has so far been seen as consistently and openly anti-Vucic. Viewers have not seen government or ruling party officials on N1 since at least 2017.

Ana Brnabic, who is in third position on Vucic's United Serbia electoral list, said she has not been on N1 since 2017, stating that “in previous election campaigns N1 would not even allow us to have paid promo clips (in their programme), that is how objective you were."

"I wasn’t thrilled about coming now either, remembering how your television tried to dehumanise my friend and my president. You criminalised him. I could hear in your programme that he was the product of a viper’s nest,” Brnabic said.

She then explained why she eventually accepted the N1 debate invitation, saying “I came because Aleksandar Vucic insisted. You changed your owner and the man said let’s give them a chance, let the people see how unbiased you are.”

Brnabic referred to N1 television recently changing its ownership. Alpac Capital, a Portuguese private equity and venture capital investment firm, which also owns Euronews, acquired the ownership of the channel as part of a wider regional media acquisition. Alpac promised the media's business transformation and no direct influence on the editorial decisions other than the request for a professional, unbiased, objective and fair reporting.

Viewers and politicians alike therefore watched the debate as a test and as one of many to come in the month leading to the elections as the channel said it would organise further thematic debates in the run-up to voting day.

Controversy surrounding the match has snowballed in recent weeks as a "Stop The Game" petition organised by the Irish Sport for Palestine group has gathered pace.

Speaking before the game, which is taking place in Hungary, Football Association of Ireland (FAI) chief executive David Courell said the players intended to wear black armbands "in recognition of all lives lost in the conflict" as he confirmed the match would go ahead following hours of talks and a vote amongst the squad.

"And also, there will be elements of the pre-match ceremony that will not be partaken in by the Republic of Ireland," he told reporters on Saturday, adding that a "significant majority" of players had voted in favour of playing.

In a statement following the announcement, the Israeli FA commended the FAI and the Irish squad on the decision, saying: "Football has won."

"The separation between football and politics must be maintained; any other approach spells chaos and a dangerous slide in a slippery slope, leading to disastrous consequences for the game we all love," the statement reads. "The Irish Football Association and the national team players did the right thing by recognizing this".

Courell confirmed that one player had "expressed their discomfort with fulfilling this fixture" and had "indicated their intent to withdraw" from the match against Israel.

Goalkeeper Gavin Bazunu announced in a post on Instagram on Saturday that he had refused to play due to his "personal belief that the killing of innocent people is wrong."

"I have reached a decision that I have not taken lightly," he wrote. "I am a man of strong faith and try my best to be a person of character and principle therefore I feel it would be wrong to participate in the upcoming fixtures against Israel".

"It is important to note that my stance is not in opposition of the Israeli people or the Jewish community but against the atrocities in the region," he added.

Asked about the impact on his team during the press conference with Courell, Ireland manager Heimir Hallgrímsson described it as "a situation you can't win."

"Obviously this affects all of them and like I said just just now, nobody is comfortable at the moment," the Icelander said.

"There's a unique and a new balance in the situation that we are trying to tackle the best way we can at the moment," he added.

Russia's recruitment drive targeting Belarusians has become a key tool to boost manpower on the battlefield and exploits economic hardship and isolation.
Russia has long regarded Belarus as a convenient reserve for topping up its manpower in the war against Ukraine, especially after suffering heavy losses at the front.

Minsk’s economic dependence, the political vulnerability of Aliaksandr Lukashenka's regime and the legal uncertainty around the Union State create conditions in which the Kremlin can act with considerable freedom.

In the Belarusian corner of the internet, in recent months a large number of adverts offering Belarusians the chance to sign a contract with the Russian army, have appeared. They are circulated everywhere – in Telegram channels, in Viber chats and in local community pages where people discuss everyday issues.

At times it looks almost surreal: nestled between posts about selling vegetables or finding a tutor, a sudden offer appears promising a "high salary, benefits and Russian citizenship" in return for going to war.

Investigations by DW, TVNET, CityDog and Current Time have documented a sharp increase in such posts. The human rights initiative Belpol regularly highlights examples of adverts in which recruiters use Belarusian phone numbers to inspire trust.

On Belpol’s Telegram channel you can see messages promising huge payouts, help with loans, "annulment of criminal convictions" and fast-track Russian citizenship.

On X, the OSB Belpol account posts screenshots of ads in which Belarusians are offered a contract with a one-off payment of 4.35 million roubles and rapid issuance of a Russian passport.

These examples are not isolated incidents but part of a systematic campaign targeting socially vulnerable people.

Belarusians are promised the equivalent of tens of thousands of euros, high salaries, housing, food, a military pension and life insurance.

Some adverts even talk about "sorting out problems with the law," which is particularly dangerous given that participation in an armed conflict abroad falls under the article on mercenary activities in Belarus' criminal code which carries a prison term of up to seven years.

Recruiters insist that the service is "legal," claiming it is not mercenary activity but "service in the army of the Union State."

Research shows that among those recruited, the majority are men from small towns and villages, people on low incomes, with debts or with existing legal problems.

Many do not realise the legal consequences and some are simply looking for quick money, without understanding that they will be sent to the most dangerous sectors of the front.

Human rights activists point out that foreign contract soldiers are often placed in assault units, where casualty rates are particularly high.

The recruitment scheme is simple and effective. A person sees an advert on social media and writes to the recruiter, who is using a Belarusian phone number. They are then told about payments, benefits and the supposed "legality" of the service.

After that they are offered rapid paperwork and travel to Russia – most often via Smolensk or Pskov. There they sign a contract and become a member of the Russian armed forces. At that point there is almost no way back.

The warning comes as EU gas storage is around 70% full, roughly 12 percentage points below the same point last year, according to the latest figures from the trade association Gas Infrastructure Europe. Analysts are warning of potential spikes above €100/MWh during peak winter if LNG export volumes from the Gulf fail to grow or if Norwegian maintenance prolongs export constraints.

The EU's top energy leader said that using less electricity at peak times can mean burning less gas in power plants, easing pressure on both gas supplies and power prices. Households and businesses are already sensitive to energy bills, while governments are under pressure to cushion consumers from another price shock.

The Commission points specifically to measures that were used during the 2022 energy crisis, including reducing electricity consumption during peak hours, encouraging consumers to shift demand through smart meters and retail tariffs, limiting temperatures in public buildings, restricting outdoor heating and switching off unnecessary public lighting at night.

"Voluntary and well-planned demand reduction measures proved useful during the 2022 energy crisis, and we can draw on the lessons from that experience in the current situation," the letter reads.

Nonetheless, the EU's energy top leader also points ministers towards their national emergency plans, which contain more serious tools that could be activated if conditions deteriorate — including interruptible gas contracts and switching power plants from gas to other fuels.

Jørgensen also encouraged capitals to use the flexibility in EU gas-storage rules to avoid panic buying and consider an 80% filling target, rather than pushing aggressively towards the 90% goal.

Normally, filling storage is about security since more gas underground means a bigger buffer when winter demand rises. But buying large quantities of gas when global markets are already tight can itself push prices higher.

The Commission has argued that reaching 80% can be sufficient for winter security under current conditions, while allowing countries to spread purchases over a longer period and avoid a late-season scramble for gas.

The Danish Commissioner suggested the EU is entering the winter with a more resilient energy system than it had before Russia's invasion of Ukraine, but not a comfortable one.

"Although EU gas storage is exceptionally low and the situation remains challenging, there are currently no immediate risks to security of supply," reads the letter.

Despite the reassurances, Brussels is no longer talking only about protecting consumers from high prices. It is asking EU governments to manage demand because consumption itself is becoming part of the supply problem.

"As we discussed in the Energy Union Task Force, we are facing a price crisis linking to a supply crisis," reads the letter.

For now, Brussels remains careful to distinguish between a deteriorating price situation and an immediate physical shortage, saying there is currently no immediate risk to security of supply.

The EU may have enough gas to get through the winter, but securing it could come at a much higher price, especially if global LNG markets remain tight and European countries have to compete with buyers.

Renewed fighting in northern Ethiopia is drawing in the country’s disputes with Egypt, Sudan and Eritrea, raising fears that its internal conflict could spread across the region.
Ethiopia’s army chief has accused Egypt and Sudan of supporting a new alliance of armed groups trying to remove Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, saying the groups could not succeed unless they were “pushed, supported and supplied” from abroad.

Field Marshal Birhanu Jula also accused Eritrea of acting as a link between foreign backers and armed groups inside Ethiopia. He made the comments in an interview with the Ethiopian army’s media outlet.

Birhanu did not provide evidence for the accusation. Egypt and Sudan had not publicly responded by Sunday morning.

The alliance announced last Sunday brings together seven groups, including the Tigray People’s Liberation Front, the Oromo Liberation Army and the Amhara Fano National Movement. Its members say they want to remove Abiy’s government and establish a transitional administration.

The accusation comes as fighting has resumed in northern Ethiopia. Tigrayan forces have seized airports and clashed with federal troops in the neighbouring Amhara and Afar regions.

UN human rights chief Volker Türk has called for an urgent end to the fighting, warning that Ethiopia could return to full-scale war.

Birhanu’s accusation against Egypt follows a new exchange between Cairo and Addis Ababa over the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, or GERD, on the Blue Nile.

Egyptian Water Resources Minister Hani Sewilam said last week that Ethiopian politicians needed to understand that building a dam on a shared river was “not like laying a carpet in their homes”.

He rejected Ethiopia’s argument that decisions about the dam fall solely under its national sovereignty.

Ethiopia’s Water and Energy Ministry responded two days later, saying Sewilam’s remarks reflected a “crude colonial mentality”. It accused Cairo of acting as though its approval was required before countries farther up the Nile could use the river’s water.

Egypt and Sudan want a legally binding agreement covering how the dam is operated, particularly during long periods of drought, as well as the sharing of information about water releases.

Ethiopia says the dam is essential for producing electricity and supporting the country’s development. It maintains that it will not cause significant harm to Egypt or Sudan.

Egypt relies on the Nile for more than 90% of its freshwater. Ethiopia formally inaugurated the GERD in September 2025 without reaching a binding agreement with the two countries.

Tensions between Ethiopia and Sudan rose in May after Sudan accused Ethiopia and the United Arab Emirates of involvement in drone attacks on Khartoum airport.

Sudan said the drones had taken off from Ethiopia’s Bahir Dar airport. Ethiopia rejected the accusation as baseless and accused Sudan of supporting Tigrayan fighters. Neither side’s accusation has been independently verified.

Eritrea fought alongside Ethiopia’s federal army against Tigrayan forces during the 2020–2022 war. Relations between Addis Ababa and Asmara later deteriorated following the peace agreement that ended the conflict.

Nearly $11 billion (€9.7 billion) worth in contracts have emerged from the Azerbaijan International Investment Forum, where some of the world’s biggest investment managers gathered for talks ranging from infrastructure and technology to energy and transport.
Azerbaijan put billions of dollars of prospective investment at the heart of its economic pitch as the second Azerbaijan International Investment Forum (AIIF) entered its final day in the capital, Baku, this week.

Azerbaijan’s Minister of Economy Mikayil Jabbarov confirmed to Euronews that almost $11 billion (€9.7 billion) worth of business was agreed over the course of the two-day event.

“The confirmed amount that we can say today is $10.8 billion. Of 26 contracts that have been executed in various fields ranging from AI to energy, from manufacturing to production,” Jabbarov said, adding that the new opportunities could come from abroad.

“We tend to look at opportunities not only within our geographical borders but also through partnerships,” he said.

“And that explains the growing visibility of Azerbaijani companies, both state-owned and private, at nearby markets, but also at markets which are geographically far from us, like the United States, like Italy, but with whom we are closely connected economically.”

According to information provided at this year's forum, the focus is firmly on whether or not Azerbaijan can convert growing international interest into an increasingly diverse range of industries.

The new investment figure comes after more than $10 billion (roughly €8.8 billion) in investment agreements were signed at the inaugural forum in 2025, more than $7 billion (around €6.2 billion) of which related to the non-oil sector.

At the 2026 event, companies represented across AIIF and the Azerbaijan Infrastructure Investment Dialogue managed a combined $30 trillion (approximately €26.4 trillion) in assets, according to SOFAZ..

Among them were BlackRock, Global Infrastructure Partners, and Brookfield Asset Management – some of the biggest names in investment.

The breadth of the agreements reflects a bigger economic challenge for Azerbaijan: attracting international capital beyond the oil and gas industry that has driven much of the country’s economic development.

In an address to the forum, President Ilham Aliyev said the non-oil sector accounted for 72% of GDP in 2025, while real non-oil GDP growth had averaged 5% annually since 2020.

He said Azerbaijan’s investment strategy went beyond simply attracting capital, highlighting manufacturing, transport and logistics, renewable energy, digital technologies, agriculture, tourism and infrastructure among areas offering investment opportunities.

"The contracts which actually led to the development in oil and gas sector signed 30 years ago still are absolutely untouched – not a single word was changed – and that was one of the factors to attract investors,” he told the audience during a closed-door session at the AIIF on Saturday.

“So already we have a very credible portfolio of being a very responsible partner,” he added.

The investment push moves into a different league with the Azerbaijan Infrastructure Investment Dialogue, which was a closed-door event hosted by Azerbaijan’s sovereign wealth fund SOFAZ and BlackRock.

BlackRock manages $15.3 trillion (approximately €13.5 trillion) in assets, according to BlackRock’s official report in July this year, while GIP — which has operated within BlackRock since 2024 — manages around $170 billion (approximately €149.6 billion).

Seventeen people were killed in a shooting at a bar to the west of Johannesburg, while 10 people were fatally shot in an attack outside of Cape Town, police said.
Two mass shootings in South Africa have left at least 27 people dead, police said on Sunday.

The SA Police Service reported that 17 people were fatally shot in an attack at a bar to the west of Johannesburg on Saturday night.

"It is alleged that patrons were drinking inside and outside the tavern when the suspects arrived and opened fire on those outside," the force said in a statement.

"The suspects then entered the tavern and continued shooting at patrons inside. The suspects were reportedly armed with AK-47 rifles and pistols. They allegedly searched some patrons and took their cellphones before fleeing the scene in two unidentified motor vehicles," the statement continued. "Two vehicles were also set alight during the incident".

It added that 13 men and four women were killed in the shooting, which took place at around 9:30 pm.

Local police have launched an investigation into the incident, with detectives and specialised units deployed to track the suspects. No arrests have so far been made.

In a separate incident, 10 people were killed and 11 were injured in a shooting in Lwandle, just outside Cape Town, in the early hours of Sunday morning, according to the SA Police Service.

"Official police reports indicate that gunmen entered an entertainment place that also sells braaied meat (shisanyama) at approximately 01:46 and shot at patrons," the service said.

"As a consequence, five individuals died on the scene while five others succumbed to injuries sustained after being transported to medical facilities. Eleven others are receiving medical treatment for injuries sustained," its statement continued.

Local police have opened investigations into murder and attempted murder. The identities of the victims are to be released after their next of kin are informed.
