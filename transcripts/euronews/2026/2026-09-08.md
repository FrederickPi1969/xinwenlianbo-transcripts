Russia and North Korea have opened their first road bridge across their shared border, linking the two countries over the Tumen River.

Russia and North Korea opened their first road bridge across their shared border on Monday, marking a new development in ties between the two countries.

The one-kilometre bridge spans the Tumen River and has two lanes, with the newly built crossing expected to handle up to 300 vehicles a day.

Footage shows officials from both countries cutting ribbons to mark the opening, followed by trucks and other vehicles crossing the bridge. The project was agreed in 2024 and construction began last year.

The decision triggered an immediate reaction in Israel. The Minister for National Security, Itamar Ben Gvir, called on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to recognise Argentine sovereignty over the Malvinas and to impose sanctions on the United Kingdom "for as long as the occupation continues", accusing London of seizing Argentine territory and oil resources.

According to the Argentine presidency, the complaint will target Navitas Petroleum Development & Production Ltd, Navitas Petroleum Atlantic United, Navitas Petroleum LP, JHI Associates Inc and Eco Atlantic Oil & Gas Ltd, as well as their executives and representatives.

Argentina maintains that these companies "allegedly hold licences granted by the illegitimate government of the United Kingdom" to explore and exploit hydrocarbons in the North Malvinas Basin, in violation of Argentine law. The case also targets directors, managers, statutory auditors and any representatives who have taken part in the project.

This is not the first time Buenos Aires has taken action over oil activity in the area. Argentina had already declared these operations illegal in 2012 and filed a similar complaint in 2015. In 2022 it sanctioned Navitas directly and, in December 2025, Javier Milei's government formally rejected investors' announcement they will pour €1.808 billion into the project to start production.

Navitas operates and controls 65% of the Sea Lion project, located around 220 kilometres north of the Falkland Islands, in waters disputed by Argentina and the United Kingdom. Its partner is the British company Rockhopper, which owns the remaining 35%. The project is in the development phase and is expected to start pumping crude oil in March 2028.

The two companies had already responded to the measures announced by Argentina before the criminal complaint was submitted. Following Milei's speech last Thursday, they issued a joint statement insisting that they operate under licences "legally granted" by the islands' government and with "the full and ongoing support" of the United Kingdom.

The companies stressed that they do not expect the Argentine measures to delay the Sea Lion project timeline. Rockhopper's shares fell by more than 6% on the London Stock Exchange after the announcement.

The complaint against the subsidiaries of an Israeli oil company comes amid rising diplomatic tension between Israel and the United Kingdom. Ben Gvir called on Netanyahu to recognise Argentine sovereignty over the territory and to adopt sanctions against London "for as long as the occupation continues".

The Israeli minister accused the United Kingdom of seizing territory and oil resources that, he argued, belong to the Argentine people. Hours earlier, Israel's Finance Minister, Bezalel Smotrich, had called for the expulsion of the British ambassador to Israel.

The remarks come amid widening rifts between London and Tel Aviv over British policy towards Palestine. For the moment, Ben Gvir's words do not amount to a change in Israel's official position on the islands' sovereignty.

President Javier Milei announced on Thursday that he intends to toughen sanctions on oil companies operating in the Falkland Islands under licences issued by the British territory. He also said he would send a bill to Congress that would extend sanctions to the suppliers of these companies and bar offenders from contracting with the Argentine state.

That same day he confirmed that he will revive plans to build a naval base in Ushuaia, around 700 kilometres from the islands. On Monday, Milei instructed Economy Minister Luis Caputo to give the project priority in the 2027 budget, which the government will send to Congress this month.

"These measures are part of a comprehensive policy aimed at strengthening the Argentine state's ability to defend its territory, protect its natural resources and safeguard its strategic interests," the government said in a statement.

US President Donald Trump's name-changing spree found an unlikely new target earlier this week when he suggested the state of New Mexico could be renamed "New America."

His proposal, which was immediately dismissed by the state's governor, comes days after he signed an order to rename Lake Ontario as "Lake America" in the middle of a trade war with neighbouring Canada.

"Many people suggested changing the name of New Mexico...to NEW AMERICA," the Republican president posted on social media.

"So much more prestigious and beautiful for the people of that potentially incredible State. Wow, I Love It!!!"

The White House reposted an image showing the Mexico part of the state's name crossed out and replaced with "America."

State governor Michelle Lujan Grisham told Fox News that the name "isn't up for debate, it's been ours since before the United States existed."

She said the president wanted to "distract Americans" from issues such as soaring gas prices.

Trump last week suggested renaming the Strait of Hormuz as "Trump Strait" as the war with Iran continues, then hours later implied he was not serious about the plan.

He renamed the Gulf of Mexico as the "Gulf of America" on his first day back in the White House in 2025 in a move rejected by Mexico.

Opening of first road bridge with Russia a sign of tighter ties, North Korea says

The opening on Monday came as Russia and North Korea have heavily boosted military and economic ties since Moscow launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
North Korea called the first road bridge across its narrow border with Russia a concrete sign of tightening bonds, state media reported on Tuesday, as Pyongyang gains advantages from its powerful neighbours Moscow and Beijing.

The bridge over the Tumen river into Russia and an under-construction span to China are part of a shift that offers Western-sanctioned Pyongyang economic and strategic options it has long sought, experts said.

North Korea "will simultaneously secure two major overland routes linking it with Russia and China, allowing it to encourage competition between the two countries and maximise its gains," said Lim Eul-chul, a professor at Kyungnam University.

He noted the bridges also "can be a message to the United States that North Korea has no reason to beg Washington for sanctions relief or make concessions on denuclearisation in order to negotiate," Lim added.

Russia noted the bridge is designed to accommodate expanded growth in freight with the capacity for up to 300 vehicles daily, which adds to the rail and air links already between the neighbours.

Ukrainian rights group Truth Hounds, however, previously warned that the new bridge could become "a direct artery for moving North Korean troops, construction brigades and military officials into Russia, with Russian technology and resources flowing in the opposite direction."

The ceremony on Monday marking the new bridge's opening came as Russia and North Korea have heavily boosted military and economic ties since Moscow launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

North Korea dispatched thousands of troops to Russia in 2024 to help ward off a Ukrainian incursion into the Kursk region, while Kyiv has accused Moscow of using North Korean missiles in its regular barrages.

South Korea's central bank estimated in July that North Korea's economy grew more than 3% for a third straight year in 2025, citing it's expansion of economic cooperation with Russia as a "major drive."

The bank's official said North Korea's gross national income also "received a boost from a sharp rise in foreign currency earnings" linked to the deployment of its troops to Russia.

"The completion of the bridge is a significant event adding a new dynamic movement to the overall bilateral cooperation," North Korean official Pak Thae-song said in comments carried by the official KCNA news agency.

North Korea and China are already connected by the Sino-Korean Friendship Bridge, which is regularly used by passenger trains, but its capacity is limited.

After a long halt, the bridge partly reopened and restored daily passenger train services with China earlier this year, but has not yet resumed issuing tourism visas to Chinese citizens, who once made up the bulk of its overseas visitors.

North Korea has long kept tight control over foreign visitors and effectively sealed its borders at the start of the COVID-19 pandemic six years ago.

The New Yalu River Bridge, intended as an alternative and eventual replacement of the Sino-Korean Friendship Bridge, is expected by experts to be completed this year.

The completion of the bridge "sends a political and geopolitical message demonstrating the formation of a "North Korea-China-Russia continental triangular bloc," said Lim.

Venice’s Grand Canal came alive on Sunday as historic boats sailed through the heart of the city for the annual Regata Storica.

Large crowds gathered along the waterfront and near the Rialto Bridge to watch the colourful procession of decorated vessels, a celebration of Venice’s rich maritime heritage.

The historic water parade was followed by traditional rowing competitions, attracting visitors from around the world.

Spectators praised the boats, their vibrant colours and the rowing crews, with one visitor describing the event as a celebration of “the Venice of the past and the Venice of today.”

The disruption, caused by the eruption of Anak Krakatau, halted nearly 3,000 flights and stranded more than 340,000 passengers.

The country's main airport serving the capital Jakarta and four other airports reopened early on Tuesday after authorities cleared volcanic ash from critical infrastructure.

Transport minister Dudy Purwagandhi told reporters it would take time for flight schedules to return to normal.

Dudy said there were around 178 aircraft at Soekarno–Hatta International Airport, Jakarta's main air travel hub.

"We will be operating these aircraft, but before we do so, we want to ensure their airworthiness. So, it may take a bit of extra time," he said.

"We ask for a little patience from passengers and the public, as we will be carrying out inspections to ensure that these aircraft are fit to fly," he added.

The transport minister said three flights departed shortly after the airport reopened, including two commercial flights to eastern Indonesia.

Airport operator Angkasa Pura Indonesia said in a statement that volcanic ash had been cleared from the runway and other areas to ensure the facilities were safe for flights.

Anak Krakatau is a volcanic island in the Sunda Strait between Indonesia’s main islands of Java and Sumatra. Its name means "child of Krakatau," and the volcano is the offspring of the infamous Krakatau, whose monumental 1883 eruption triggered a period of global cooling.

The volcano erupted at least three times on Tuesday morning alone, according to the head of the Geology Agency, Lana Saria.

A 2018 tsunami triggered by a collapse of parts of Anak Krakatau killed more than 400 people in Sumatra and Java and displaced thousands.

Indonesia has more than 120 active volcanoes and frequently experiences eruptions due to its location along the Pacific "Ring of Fire," the ring of seismic faults around the Pacific Ocean where most global seismic activity occurs.
