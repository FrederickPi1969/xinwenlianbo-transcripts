Australian model and swimwear designer Sahara Ray has died aged 34. Her family said in a statement to US celebrity news website TMZ that she died on Sunday night surrounded by family at the hospital.

The daughter of Australian surfer Tony Ray, she founded the label Sahara Ray Swim in 2014 and was photographed on holiday with Justin Bieber in 2016.

Violent clashes erupted in Albania’s capital, Tirana, on Monday as protesters threw Molotov cocktails, fireworks and stones at police, who responded with water cannon.

The package covers railways, critical minerals, finance and technology, with major US firms involved. Uzbek President Mirziyoyev outlined measures to attract investors, while business executives told Euronews that commercial activity is growing.
Uzbekistan has announced 54 agreements and proposed projects with American partners worth nearly $12bn (€10.7bn), following high-level meetings in Samarkand attended by US Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

Rubio met President Shavkat Mirziyoyev on Monday, with the two discussing trade, energy, critical minerals, aviation and technology. The two countries are seeking to expand their strategic partnership through private investment and joint industrial projects.

According to the Uzbek presidency, bilateral trade has increased by 35% since the beginning of the year, although it did not specify the comparison period.

Senior officials and American business representatives attended the second meeting of the American-Uzbek Business and Investment Council, where the economic agenda was developed.

Mirziyoyev described the council as an important mechanism for developing joint projects, emphasising its distinctive role in Uzbekistan's international economic relations.

"We do not have this kind of format with any other country of the world," he said during his meeting with Rubio.

The Uzbek president announced a package of 54 agreements and prospective initiatives valued at around $12bn and called for a comprehensive action plan to support their implementation.

He also proposed a new approach to attracting investors, under which projects would be prepared with land, infrastructure, permits and administrative requirements already addressed.

Uzbek companies would be encouraged to participate as partners, while a newly established direct investment fund could help finance eligible projects.

Mirziyoyev identified critical minerals, renewable energy, electricity storage, data centres and artificial intelligence as priority areas. He also proposed a joint trade, investment and technology cooperation programme extending to 2035.

The council is co-chaired by Head of Uzbekistan's Presidential Administration Saida Mirziyoyeva and US Special Envoy Sergio Gor.

Mirziyoyeva encouraged US companies to bring investment proposals and practical problems directly to the council, positioning it as a mechanism for resolving obstacles rather than simply discussing future cooperation.

Business representatives interviewed by Euronews in Samarkand identified opportunities in mining, agriculture and financial services.

Dan Patterson, CEO of Silver Leaf International, pointed to the potential for Uzbek fruit exports to reach US consumers and for US companies to invest in mineral development.

"So there's also tremendous opportunity for minerals and mining advancements in almost every sector," Patterson told Euronews.

Quinn Martin, president of Bluestone, said US investment activity had accelerated after several years of discussions.

"Just my firm has been involved in three transactions in the last five months regarding US investors," he told Euronews.

According to Martin, the transactions involved Uzbekistan's national investment fund, banking and hospitality. He expects US investment to increase further over the coming years.

Uzbekistan's Deputy Minister of Investment, Industry and Trade Shokhrukh Gulyamov also described discussions with US financial institutions.

"Citigroup, one of the leading financial institutions in the United States, is in discussions with our banks about providing financing for various projects," Gulyamov told Euronews.

He also highlighted cooperation with Boeing, including aircraft deliveries and the supply of spare parts needed for maintenance.

According to Uzbekistan's Ministry of Investment, Industry and Trade, the agreements and proposed initiatives cover transport, energy, critical minerals, finance, digital technologies, agriculture and healthcare.

Paris police imposed an overnight curfew on Monday in two areas of the capital, after a weekend of some of France’s worst rioting in years.

People may not enter or stay in Place de la Republique or the Les Halles shopping district from 11pm until 6am on Tuesday. Gatherings and demonstrations are barred from 8pm.

Armed groups in Narino, an illegal mining hotspot on the border with Ecuador, control the clandestine gold trade as well as trafficking drugs and engaging in extortion.
A landslide at an illegal gold mine in southwestern Colombia killed 14 people and injured seven, a regional official said on Monday.

Armed groups in Narino, an illegal mining hotspot on the border with Ecuador, control the clandestine gold trade as well as trafficking drugs and engaging in extortion.

Videos shared on social media show people and a backhoe evacuating bodies from the mine to a canoe on a nearby river.

Narino's Peace and Security Secretary Alex Gonzalez gave the revised death toll to the Caracol television channel, adding that the search for survivors had ended.

Rescue teams struggled to reach the site due to the difficult terrain and the presence of criminal groups, the governor's office said.

"Communities have no other economic alternatives than these extremely risky activities. Traditional small-scale mining has practically disappeared," a source from the office told the AFP news agency.

Locals in the municipality where the mine collapsed on Monday have reported selective killings carried out by armed groups over suspicions of collaboration with security forces and rival organizations, or of stealing gold.

Separatist leaders vow to fight on, warning the province’s governing party could split over the issue.
Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has welcomed Alberta’s decisive rejection of a move towards independence, saying the province had chosen national unity at a time of mounting pressure from Washington.

"On October 19, Albertans made a clear choice. They chose unity, to build together, and to take care of each other. Above all, Albertans chose Canada," Carney said in a statement on Tuesday.

With more than one million votes counted, 77 per cent backed remaining in Canada, according to unofficial results from Elections Alberta.

Monday’s vote was not a direct ballot on independence. A win for the separatists would instead have set the western province on a path towards a binding referendum on leaving Canada.

The result is a boost for Carney, who grew up in Alberta and had urged a decisive outcome as US President Donald Trump wages a trade war against Canada and threatens to make it the 51st US state.

Alberta, a conservative stronghold with the world’s fourth-largest proven oil reserves, has long resented the federal government in Ottawa, largely over the management of its oil industry. Premier Danielle Smith has accused Carney’s predecessor, Justin Trudeau, of trying to curb oil production to advance his climate agenda.

Separatist parties once won only 1 or 2 per cent of the vote, but anger over COVID-19 restrictions helped energise the movement.

Smith opposes independence but called the referendum under pressure from separatists within her United Conservative Party.

"Albertans have voted for a stronger Alberta in a united Canada," she posted on social media.

The separatist campaign drew interest from Trump’s allies. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent described Alberta as a natural partner for the United States, and billionaire Elon Musk posted on X that the province should separate. Separatist leaders also met officials at the US State Department.

Thomas Lukaszuk, a former deputy premier who led the Forever Canadian campaign against separation, told supporters: "We are a solid country that’s united from coast to coast to coast."

Separatists insist their cause is not finished. Jeffrey Rath, a leader of the Alberta Prosperity Project, said the push for independence would continue.

US President Donald Trump said Monday he is willing to sit down with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping in China next month, confirming media reports that the three leaders could meet on the sidelines of an Asia-Pacific summit in Shenzhen.

Trump said such a summit would centre on denuclearisation but might also touch on the war in Ukraine.

Asked by reporters whether he would join Putin and Xi to discuss Ukraine, Trump downplayed that as the main draw. “I would do it, not so much for Ukraine, because Russia, frankly, has been fine with Ukraine. They haven’t been doing much,” he said. “We would have it to talk about nuclear weapons and denuclearisation.”

Pressed on whether Ukraine would come up at a meeting with the two leaders, Trump said he would do “whatever I can do to end the war".

Earlier, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said, "Given that all three heads of state might potentially attend a summit in China, the topic of such a hypothetical meeting has indeed been mentioned."

The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) group holds a summit in the Chinese city of Shenzhen on 18-19 November. China, Russia and the United States are all members.

But Peskov added that "no substantive proposals or agreements, neither regarding the meeting itself nor its potential agenda and content, have been discussed or put forward by anyone".

China's foreign ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning said she had no information about a possible meeting while Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha bristled at the suggestion of a meeting without Kyiv.

"A trilateral meeting between the leaders of Russia, China and the US would do only the opposite: encourage Putin to continue this war," he said.

The Kremlin's Peskov said that "a final settlement is impossible without the participation of someone from Kyiv who is prepared to take on the responsibility".

But Putin has repeatedly ruled out meeting Zelenskyy until after a final peace deal is ready and has repeatedly questioned Zelenskyy's authority to sign a deal following the expiry of his five-year term under martial law.

Trump, who has renewed his swipes at Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in recent days, on Monday said it was “time for somebody else to negotiate on behalf of Ukraine.”

"Zelenskyy doesn't know what he's talking about," Trump said when asked about the Ukrainian's comments. "To be honest, I think it's time for somebody else to negotiate on behalf of Ukraine."

Australian police said they charged a man on Tuesday for what they allege was a "targeted" attack on lawmaker Helen Haines, who suffered critical injuries.

The man was charged with dangerous driving causing serious injury and intentionally causing serious injury, as well as the federal crime of harming a public official, Victoria police said.

The man, named by local media as Brendan Nugent, remains in custody and will appear in court on Wednesday, police said.

Earlier on Tuesday, local media reported that Haines had lost a leg after being struck by the vehicle.

Political violence is rare in Australia but law enforcement have documented a steady rise in threats and abuse directed towards the nation's elected officials in recent years.

Prime Minister Albanese said the attack was a "test of our character" and led an outpouring of support for Haines, widely praised for her efforts to increase transparency in politics and crack down on corruption.

She was hit by a Toyota Land Cruiser outside her office in the town of Wangaratta, just over 200 kilometres northeast of Melbourne, at around 3:35 pm (0635 CET) on Monday.

Witnesses told national broadcaster ABC they heard a bang and saw a woman pinned between two cars.

The 65-year-old was critically injured and underwent surgery overnight at the Alfred hospital in Melbourne.

"Helen remains in hospital in a critical condition. I spoke with her husband Phil last night, and she underwent surgery last night," Albanese told Australian breakfast show Sunrise.

"All Australians' thoughts are with the family, with her many friends, with her community," he added.

The 43-year-old male driver of the vehicle was restrained by local barber Ricky Jefferies, now widely hailed as a "hero".

Jefferies told journalists his first thought had been: "my wife would be disappointed if I'm injured".

"My family, they're everything, and my community is second to that," a visibly shaken Jefferies told journalists.

"The other people here were just, they did so much more than me...they deserve the credit."

The driver, Brendan Nugent, was arrested and police found a knife in his car. Australian media reported that he was known to police.

An investigation by federal police, the counter-terrorism squad and Victoria state law enforcement is ongoing and he remained in custody on Tuesday morning.

Federal police said they were working with local law enforcement on possible charges, including the offence of causing harm to a government official, which carries a maximum penalty of 10 years' imprisonment.

The alleged attacker's motive remains unknown and Albanese has refused to speculate while the investigation takes place.

But he has bemoaned the fact that political debate in Australia had grown too heated and personal over the past few years and pointed to a significant increase in threats against elected officials.

"But that doesn't mean that you have to draw into question people's motivations or their patriotism or who they are."
