## Rain to End by Monday Morning. Temperatures to Fall Tuesday

Rain across the Seoul metropolitan area and Gangwon Province is expected to taper off Sunday night, while precipitation in other regions will continue into early Monday morning.
According to the Korea Meteorological Administration, rainfall through Monday is forecast to total five to 40 millimeters in Gwangju, South Jeolla Province and South Gyeongsang Province, and five to 20 millimeters in North Jeolla, Chungcheong and North Gyeongsang Provinces.
Mountainous areas of Jeju Island could receive up to 60 millimeters of rain.
Monday morning lows are expected to range from nine degrees Celsius in Seoul and Daejeon to 10 degrees in Jeonju, up one to three degrees from Sunday.
Daytime highs will range from 14 to 20 degrees nationwide, including 15 degrees in Seoul, up three to six degrees from Sunday.
As cold air moves in from the northwest, temperatures are forecast to drop Tuesday morning, with the daily temperature swing widening to as much as 15 degrees.

## Hongdae, Itaewon Draw Large Halloween Crowds Under Tight Safety Measures

Large crowds gathered in Seoul on Halloween, which fell on a weekend for the first time since the 2022 Itaewon crowd crush, but no major incidents were reported.
According to Seoul's real-time urban data, an estimated 120-thousand and 125-thousand people were in the Hongdae area and 30-thousand to 32-thousand were in the Itaewon Special Tourist Zone as of 10 p.m. Saturday.
Hongdae was classified as "extremely crowded," while some narrow alleys in Itaewon experienced temporary bottlenecks. However, police and other safety personnel effectively managed the crowds and prevented major disruptions.
Seoul Subway Line 6 trains also bypassed Itaewon Station from 11:47 p.m. until the final train at 11:59 p.m. because of heavy crowds around Itaewon's World Food Street.
The government plans to maintain its "caution" crowd-safety alert through Sunday and focus on managing areas expected to attract large numbers of visitors.

## Foreign Minister Holds Talks with EU, UK, India and Mexico at G20

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun held a series of meetings with counterparts from the European Union, the United Kingdom, India and Mexico on the sidelines of the G20 foreign ministers meeting in Atlanta to discuss ways to strengthen bilateral cooperation, South Korea's Foreign Ministry said Sunday.
According to the ministry, Cho met Saturday with Kaja Kallas, the EU's high representative for foreign affairs and security policy, to discuss closer security and defense cooperation in areas including cybersecurity and space, as well as regional issues such as the Korean Peninsula and Ukraine.
In a meeting with British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband, Cho called for deeper cooperation in defense and artificial intelligence. The two sides also agreed to work closely on international issues ahead of their respective G20 presidencies in 2027 and 2028.
Cho also met with Indian External Affairs Minister Subrahmanyam Jaishankar to review progress on agreements reached during President Lee Jae Myung's state visit to India in April and to discuss multilateral issues.
In talks with Mexican Foreign Minister Roberto Velasco Alvarez, Cho agreed to expedite follow-up measures stemming from Lee's September state visit, including implementation of a revised investment protection agreement and expanded cooperation in artificial intelligence and space.
Cho also proposed holding the ninth South Korea-Mexico Joint Committee on Economic, Scientific and Technological Cooperation in Seoul early next year, a proposal that Velasco welcomed.
On Friday, Cho met with his German and Polish counterparts to discuss ways to strengthen bilateral cooperation, including in the defense industry.

## Foreign Minister Urges Inter-Korean Dialogue While Addressing DMZ Landmine Blasts at G20

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun outlined South Korea's position on the landmine explosions that occurred in the Demilitarized Zone(DMZ) in September during a G20 foreign ministers meeting while stressing the importance of inter-Korean dialogue and communication to ease tensions on the Korean Peninsula.
The Foreign Ministry said Sunday that Cho made the remarks at the G20 meeting held in Atlanta, Georgia, on Friday and Saturday local time.
Cho also warned that deepening military cooperation between North Korea and Russia has serious implications for security on the Korean Peninsula and across the region.
He said the South Korean government would continue efforts to promote peaceful coexistence and reduce tensions on the peninsula, while calling for support and cooperation from G20 member states.
Cho also underscored the need for international cooperation to safeguard freedom of navigation and maritime safety in the Strait of Hormuz and to address threats to food security that directly affect people's livelihoods.
In particular, he noted that South Korea recently joined an EU-led pledge opposing unlawful restrictions on passage through international straits and pledged to continue making practical contributions to ensuring free and safe navigation through the Strait of Hormuz.

## FBI Attributes $6.3 Billion Crypto Theft to N. Korea

The U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation(FBI) has formally blamed North Korea for a cyberattack that stole billions of dollars' worth of cryptocurrency from Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange.
In a statement released Saturday, the FBI said it had confirmed that North Korea was behind Thursday's theft of more than six billion dollars in cryptocurrency, marking the first time the agency has officially attributed the attack to Pyongyang.
The FBI identified the perpetrators as TraderTraitor, a hacking group believed to be linked to North Korea, and said its operatives were converting much of the stolen cryptocurrency into Ethereum.
The agency said it expects further laundering through decentralized finance, or DeFi, platforms and instant-swap services. It also released a list of Ethereum, Bitcoin and Solana wallet addresses linked to the hackers and urged cryptocurrency platforms to block related transactions.
Binance previously said the attack stemmed from an engineer's compromised laptop and a vulnerability in its transaction approval system, resulting in the unauthorized withdrawal of six-point-three billion dollars’ worth of cryptocurrency, the largest single hack in the industry's history.
The FBI said it is working with the U.S. Secret Service and the Internal Revenue Service's Criminal Investigation Division to investigate the case.

## N. Korea Tests Cluster Bomb Warhead for Tactical Ballistic Missiles

North Korea has conducted a test to assess the destructive power of a cluster-munition warhead designed for tactical ballistic missiles, state media reported Sunday.
The Korean Central News Agency(KCNA) said the Missile Administration carried out the test the previous day under the guidance of Kim Jong-sik, a member of the Workers' Party's Central Military Commission and first vice department director of the party's Central Committee.
Following the test, research institutes under the Missile Administration claimed that the Standard Type-2 cluster-munition warhead could devastate a target area of 12 to 13 hectares at maximum density.
Photos released by KCNA showed a missile launch and the warhead detonating in midair, dispersing submunitions across a broad target area that included structures resembling aircraft hangars, ammunition depots and South Korean military artillery positions.
The head of the Missile Administration said North Korea classifies cluster-munition warheads into four categories based on standard and special operational purposes and plans to deploy all of them to military units this year for use according to mission objectives and target characteristics.

## October Exports Hit Second-Highest Monthly Total on Record

South Korea's exports surpassed 110 billion U.S. dollars in October for the second consecutive month and topped 900 billion dollars for the first time in the January-October period, driven by an ongoing AI-fueled semiconductor boom.
The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources said Sunday that the country's exports surged 93-point-seven percent from a year earlier to 115-point-28 billion U.S. dollars in October, marking the second-highest monthly total on record.
The figure followed a record high of 120-point-94 billion dollars in September.
Cumulative exports for the January-October period reached 930 billion dollars, putting the country on track to surpass the one-trillion-dollar mark in November.
Semiconductor exports jumped 259-point-four percent year over year to 56-point-44 billion, exceeding 50 billion dollars for the second consecutive month.
By destination, exports increased in seven of the nine major markets, with shipments to China surging 133-point-six percent to 26-point-98 billion dollars and exports to the United States rising 185-point-eight percent to 24-point-nine billion dollars.
Imports also increased 36 percent from a year earlier to 72-point-78 billion dollars, resulting in a trade surplus of 42-point-five billion dollars.

## Rubio: Trump Open to Talks with Kim Jong-un, but No Talks Scheduled

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Saturday that President Donald Trump remains open to meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, but that no meeting has been scheduled.
Speaking at a news conference on the sidelines of a G20 foreign ministers meeting in Atlanta, Rubio said Trump was open to talks if Kim was willing to discuss issues of concern to the United States and its allies, including South Korea.
He added that Trump has repeatedly and publicly expressed his willingness to hold such a meeting.
Rubio stressed, however, that no talks had been scheduled and that there was nothing to announce at this time.
Noting that Trump met with Kim during his first term, Rubio said he believed the president would be willing to resume talks if they could produce meaningful results.
He also said that while traditional diplomacy often views meetings or invitations as concessions that confer legitimacy on the other party, Trump does not regard dialogue or meetings as concessions.
