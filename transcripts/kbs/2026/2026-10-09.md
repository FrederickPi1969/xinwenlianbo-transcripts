## RFA: Preparations for Commemorative Event Spotted at Pyongyang Airfield

North Korea appears to be preparing for a commemorative event in Pyongyang ahead of the 81st anniversary of its ruling party’s founding which falls on Saturday, according to Radio Free Asia(RFA).
RFA said Friday that images of troops and vehicles were captured in three sections of the Mirim Parade Training Ground in Pyongyang based on imagery taken by U.S. commercial satellites on Wednesday.
In one of the sections, what appeared to be troops or vehicles are lined up in a row for some 250 meters, while another image captured troops and vehicles assembled in a U-shape.
RFA said the latest preparations are extremely limited in scope and activity compared to preparations that were detected ahead of military parades.

## Opposition PPP Fears Visa Holdup Signals S. Korea-US Alliance Issues

The main opposition People Power Party(PPP) has expressed concerns over the status of the South Korea-U.S. alliance amid a delay in visa applications submitted by a ruling party lawmaker and others.
PPP spokesperson Cho Yong-sool said in a statement on Friday that unease surrounding bilateral relations with the U.S. is expanding beyond visa issues to security cooperation.
Cho pointed out that ruling Democratic Party Rep. Jung Chung-rae, a former party chair, had failed to get a U.S. visa.
He explained that Jung is regarded as a controversial figure due to his history of participating in a sit-in protest at the U.S. ambassador's residence in Seoul back in 1989.
Cho said there have been similar issues with visa issuance and approval of diplomatic status for National Intelligence Service employees who were to be assigned to the South Korean Embassy in Washington.
Cho stressed that fears of Seoul-Washington ties hitting their lowest point since establishment must not become a reality, adding that it is the responsibility of the Lee Jae Myung administration to restore trust and maintain a solid South Korea-U.S. alliance.

## Ukraine Urges Diplomacy over Emotion after S. Korea Recalls Ambassador

Ukraine's Ministry of Foreign Affairs says "diplomacy must prevail over emotions" in response to South Korea's decision to recall its ambassador to Ukraine amid a dispute over Kyiv's disclosure of the transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war to Seoul.
Ukrainian foreign ministry spokesperson Heorhii Tykhyi stated this position, saying his country remains open to "constructive dialogue," Ukrainian media outlets reported on Thursday.

Kyiv is likely to have used the word "invite" instead of "summon" due to the sensitive nature of the POW transfer issue.
Earlier on Thursday, South Korea decided to recall Ambassador Park.
Recalling an ambassador is regarded as a powerful form of diplomatic protest.

## Nearly 6 in 10 Americans Support S. Korea&#039;s Development of Nuclear-Powered Submarines

A new survey finds that nearly six in ten Americans support South Korea’s plan to build nuclear-powered submarines, but a majority oppose the Asian ally developing its own nuclear weapons.
According to the 2026 Korea Economic Institute of America(KEI) survey released on Thursday, 58 percent of Americans support South Korea's development of nuclear-powered submarines.
By political party affiliation, 64 percent of Republican supporters and 56 percent of Democratic supporters approve of South Korea’s submarine development plan.
Meanwhile, 71 percent of respondents oppose South Korea developing its own nuclear weapons.
Ninety-one percent of respondents oppose nuclear weapons development by Iran, 66 percent by Australia, 67 percent by Japan, and 69 percent by Germany.
The KEI survey, conducted with polling firm YouGov between August 21 and September 1 on one-thousand-207 U.S. adults, had a margin of error of plus or minus three-point-six percentage points.
KEI has conducted an annual survey of Americans' perceptions of foreign policy since 2020.

## N. Korea Rejects Seoul's Medical Aid, Vows to Ignore S. Korea

North Korea has vowed to ignore South Korea as it reiterated its criticism of Seoul's plan to send medical equipment over the border.

Kim claimed that Seoul’s accusations about recent landmine explosions in the Demilitarized Zone and its medical aid proposal are "farces" aimed at undermining the North’s reputation and image.

## N. Korea Classified as Tier 3 Country in US Human Trafficking Report for 24th Straight Year

The United States has designated North Korea as one of the world's worst human trafficking offenders for the 24th consecutive year.
In the "2026 Trafficking in Persons Report" released on Thursday, the State Department kept North Korea among Tier 3 countries, saying the reclusive state “does not fully meet the minimum standards for the elimination of trafficking and is not making significant efforts to do so."
The State Department assessed that in the North, "there was a government policy or pattern of human trafficking in political prison camps and labor training centers in the mass mobilizations of adults and children, and in the imposition of forced labor conditions on North Korean workers overseas."
It also stated that the North Korean "government used proceeds from state-sponsored forced labor to fund government operations."
A total of 20 countries were placed in the Tier 3 group, the lowest tier on the scale, including China, Iran, Russia, Afghanistan, Cuba and Hong Kong.
South Korea remained a Tier 1 country, the same as last year.

## Lee Hails Strength of Korean Language on 100th Hangeul Day

President Lee Jae Myung has stressed that the strength of the Korean language and writing system serves as the bedrock of South Korea's competitiveness.
Lee made the remark in a social media post on Friday as the country celebrated Hangeul Day, saying that writing is the face of a nation and language its heart.
The president noted the significance of the 100th anniversary of the holiday, which was established by earlier generations to preserve the Korean language and writing.
He also highlighted the role Hangeul, the Korean alphabet, has played in Korea's growth and development since liberation from Japanese colonial rule.
Lee said that while the past century was devoted to preserving Hangeul, the next 100 years should focus on unlocking its full potential.

## S. Korean Envoy Condemns DMZ Landmine Blast as Armistice Violation at UN

South Korea's Ambassador to the United Nations, Cha Ji-hoon, has condemned last month's landmine explosion in the western section of the Demilitarized Zone(DMZ) as a clear violation of the Korean War Armistice Agreement.
Cha called on North Korea to take responsible action over the incident during the 81st session of the UN General Assembly First Committee in New York on Thursday.
He said the explosion was confirmed to have been caused by mines deliberately planted by North Korean troops south of the Military Demarcation Line.
Cha urged Pyongyang to cease actions that escalate tensions, issue an apology, take responsibility for the incident and engage in dialogue to prevent further escalation and restore trust on the Korean Peninsula.
The ambassador also warned that Pyongyang's continued development of nuclear weapons and ballistic missiles poses a serious threat not only to the peninsula but also the global nonproliferation regime.
Regarding North Korea and Russia, he expressed grave concern over their ongoing military cooperation in violation of UN Security Council(UNSC) resolutions.
Cha called on Pyongyang to return to full compliance with its obligations under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty and relevant UN resolutions, and to respond positively to Seoul's sincere efforts to resume dialogue.
In addition, the ambassador reaffirmed South Korea's commitment to working closely with the international community to achieve the complete denuclearization of the Korean Peninsula and lasting peace.
