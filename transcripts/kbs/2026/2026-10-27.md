## Samsung Electronics Faces 20.1 Billion Won Fine for Misleading Gaming Ads

Samsung Electronics is facing a fine of 20-point-one billion won, or approximately 15-point-16 million U.S. dollars, for misleading advertisements concealing presets limiting the performance of its Galaxy smartphones during gaming, South Korea’s antitrust watchdog announced Tuesday.
The Fair Trade Commission(FTC) said it has decided to impose a corrective order and a fine on the tech giant for violations of the Act on Fair Labeling and Advertising.
From 2021 to 2022, Samsung Electronics emphasized in its marketing for the Galaxy S21, S22 and Tab8 models that the devices delivered peak performance during advanced gaming.
However, it was found that Samsung’s game optimizing service — a heat management system — imposed preset limits on CPU and GPU performance as soon as certain demanding gaming apps were launched, regardless of the device’s temperature or other conditions.
Customers were not informed of this fact, and some who purchased the devices raised complaints about freezing and lags during gaming, with several even filing lawsuits seeking damages.
The FTC said gaming performance is an important factor for consumers purchasing high-priced smart devices, one that many manufacturers have emphasized in their advertisements.
The watchdog stressed that when a company advertises a product by promoting a specific performance aspect as its key feature, important performance limitations must be clearly disclosed so that consumers are fully aware of them.
In response, Samsung Electronics explained that the game optimizing service was a feature designed to enhance perceived game performance and optimize for stable gameplay over long periods.
The company said it found the latest decision hard to understand, given that related domestic and international lawsuits have concluded and the complaint to the FTC has been withdrawn, adding that it will continue to present its position in future legal proceedings.

## S. Korean Economy Grows 1% in Q3 on Semiconductor Boom

The South Korean economy expanded at a faster-than-expected pace in the third quarter, driven by the ongoing AI-driven semiconductor supercycle.
The Bank of Korea on Tuesday estimated that gross domestic product had grown one percent from the previous three-month period, more than triple its August forecast.
The better-than-expected growth will likely prompt the central bank to raise its existing 2026 growth outlook, which is currently three-point-three percent.
Government spending rose one-point-nine percent from the previous quarter, and facility investment expanded four-point-one percent, led by chip production and other machinery.
But private consumption contracted by zero-point-two percent, marking its first decline in three years and nine months.
Exports grew two-point-three percent, with notable increases in shipments of semiconductors, machinery and electrical equipment.
