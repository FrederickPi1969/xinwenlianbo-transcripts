## Military Concludes N. Korean Antipersonnel Mines Caused Sept. 21 DMZ Blast

The South Korean military has concluded that resin-cased antipersonnel mines planted south of the Military Demarcation Line by North Korea were the cause of a blast inside the Demilitarized Zone on September 21 that injured three South Korean soldiers.
Joint Chiefs of Staff Vice Chairman Kwon Dae-won said at a press briefing Monday that the conclusion was the result of a joint investigation with the United Nations Command, including GPS data showing that the blast sites were south of the Military Demarcation Line, scientific evidence identifying the mines as North Korean antipersonnel mines and indications that they had been deliberately planted rather than displaced, including personnel testimony.
Around 12:51 p.m. on Monday, the South Korean military launched an operation to remove the North Korean mines from the area surrounding the point of the blast.
The JCS vice chairman also urged North Korea to sincerely apologize to the injured South Korean soldiers and to immediately remove the remaining mines that had been planted in violation of the armistice agreement.

## KMA: Sunny Skies Forecast Nationwide Tuesday; Mercury to Drop amid Incoming Cold Air

Sunny skies will appear nationwide on Tuesday, with a drop in mercury levels as cold air from the north moves in.

The low in Seoul is expected to hit eleven degrees.
Daytime highs are forecast to rise to between 19 and 24 degrees, similar to or slightly higher than Monday's.
Seoul's temperature will hit a daytime high of 22 degrees.
The weather agency urged the public to pay attention to the large temperature difference between day and night, which could reach up to 15 degrees.

## Police Apprehension of Drug Offenders Jumps 33% in First 8 Months of 2026

The police have apprehended a higher number of drug offenders this year thanks to the agency's crackdown on smuggling, production and sales of narcotics.
The National Office of Investigation said on Monday that 174 people suspected of smuggling or manufacturing drugs had been taken into custody between January and August, up 33-point-eight percent from the same period of last year.
Three-thousand-396 people were arrested for drug sales during the same period, up six-point-six year over year.
Police have cracked down on drug smuggling and distribution after extraditing convicted murderer and alleged drug kingpin Park Wang-yeol from the Philippines and indicting and charging him with drug smuggling and distribution earlier this year.
An agency spokesperson said investigators had arrested intermediaries and accomplices, obtained evidence and analyzed the flow of funds in connection with Park.

## N. Korea Claims Hypersonic Missile Drill Reaching &#039;Final Stages of Development&#039;

Anchor: North Korea claims it has carried out an exercise involving an intermediate-range ballistic missile and has disclosed images of a hypersonic missile launch. The missile's shape and the length of its fuselage appear different from those of one revealed two weeks ago, and Pyongyang's state media claim the weapon is in the final stages of development.
Report: A hypersonic missile that North Korea claims to have launched on Saturday has a cone-shaped tip, and its fuselage is longer than that of the one unveiled two weeks ago.
Shin Jong-woo, secretary-general of the Korea Defense and Security Forum, told Yonhap on Sunday that the missile appeared to be a version of the conical hypersonic missile that Pyongyang test-fired in 2022.
The North's state-run Korean Central News Agency(KCNA) said on Sunday that the regime had test-fired its intermediate-range strategic missile toward the East Sea the previous day.
KCNA reported that the missile had traveled one-thousand kilometers and precisely hit a designated target, of which the agency disclosed the exact coordinates. It claimed that the regime has entered a missile-deployment stage.
North Korean leader Kim Jong-un observed the exercise alongside his daughter, Kim Ju-ae, and wife, Ri Sol-ju, and reaffirmed his commitment to strengthening the North's nuclear deterrence capabilities.
In a rare move, Kim Yo-jong, the influential sister of the North Korean leader, issued a statement on the day of the missile firing Saturday and claimed that the South Korean and Japanese militaries had failed to properly track the missile due to its advanced flight capabilities.
Meanwhile, Seoul's defense ministry said Sunday that the missile North Korea had fired a day earlier was within the range of detection and interception by South Korean and U.S. defense assets, rejecting Pyongyang's claims that Seoul and Tokyo had failed to detect it.
In a statement, the ministry said its forces had closely monitored North Korea's preparations for the recent firings, tracked the missiles immediately after liftoff and accurately identified the launch site.

## AMRO Raises S. Korea&#039;s 2026 Growth Outlook to 3.3%

A macroeconomic and financial surveillance organization for the regional bloc of Southeast Asian countries and their Northeast Asian partners—South Korea, China and Japan—has upwardly revised its growth outlook for South Korea.
Seoul's finance ministry said on Monday that the Singapore-based ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office(AMRO) had raised its projection for South Korea from three-point-one percent in July to three-point-three percent in its October outlook report.
The office expects the South Korean economy to expand by two-point-seven percent next year, up zero-point-five percentage points from its previous projection.
AMRO changed its forecast for this year's inflation from two-point-six percent to two-point-seven percent and next year's rate from two-point-four percent to two-point-three percent.
Looking ahead, AMRO said risks to the regional outlook were tilted to the downside, citing a potential slowdown in AI-related demand that could weigh on exports and investment, as well as rising energy, transportation and production costs stemming from the Middle East crisis.

## Korea Financial Hacking Spreads to Nonbank Lenders, Same IP Address Linked to Attacks

Anchor: A wave of hacking attacks on South Korea's financial sector is spreading, and investigators believe many may have originated from the same source. President Lee Jae Myung has ordered a thorough investigation and countermeasures.
Report: South Korean financial regulators held an emergency meeting Sunday after personal data breaches were confirmed at several nonbank financial institutions, exacerbating a cybersecurity crisis that had already hit major commercial banks.
The Financial Services Commission convened the session, chaired by FSC Chairman Lee Eog-weon, with the CEOs of affected firms and the heads of financial industry associations in attendance.
[soundbite: Financial Services Commission Chairman Lee Eog-weon]
"The exact causes and attack methods of individual incidents still require close investigation and analysis, but the cases identified so far show that hackers exploited external web pages and servers that employees use for convenience at work, which received less management attention than core systems. The incidents show that a single overlooked gap can become a weak point in an otherwise robust security system."
The meeting follows Welcome Savings Bank’s confirmation Sunday that information related to its corporate customers had been leaked, with the number of affected records estimated at up to 2,200.
The attackers first hit four commercial banks, including Shinhan, KB Kookmin, and Hana, and have since targeted nonbank providers, including savings banks and financial service firms.
Investigators believe the attacks on all four banks may be the work of a single actor because they originate from the same IP address. The ambushes on banks and nonbanks also used similar methods.
The investigation determined that attackers did not extract data directly from databases but instead queried external web pages and servers to scrape information. Traces of a Chinese AI tool were also found.
[soundbite: Financial Services Commission Chairman Lee Eog-weon]
"I can't say for certain, but we can't rule out the possibility that AI was used in these attacks. As new types of cyberattacks are likely to continue, a security system that defends against AI attacks with AI should be put in place without delay."
Regulators warned that financial firms that neglect necessary checks and responses will face strict action.
After being briefed on the extent of the leaks, President Lee Jae Myung ordered a thorough investigation and the preparation of countermeasures.

## Reuters: Zelenskyy Believes Russia Shared Technology with N. Korea

Zelenskyy, however, did not address President Lee Jae Myung's claim that Kyiv's decision had violated a prior confidentiality agreement.

## Lee Calls for Precautions against &#039;Irresponsible Extremists&#039;

President Lee Jae Myung said that inflammatory rhetoric from “irresponsible extremists” on both sides of the political aisle, based on falsehood, is spreading like a fad and misleading the public.
Lee wrote on X on Sunday that political division has gained greater importance than the truth in an era of "post-truth" and factional confrontation, and that inciting without verifying facts has become a barometer of one's competence.
The president, however, said the truth will eventually prevail.
Lee said those in authority should take responsibility, and that most "radical claims" and "groundless incitements" stem from people's desire to take credit for the outcomes of reform measures.
Lee did not specify what he meant by "irresponsible extremists" or their "radical claims," but the comments come amid the ruling Democratic Party's protests against the nomination of Kim Ji-yong to head the new Serious Crimes Investigation Agency.
On Friday, Lee requested a parliamentary confirmation hearing for Kim, whom he had also ordered to undergo additional vetting after the nominee returned from France on Sept. 10. He said the vetting had disproved claims that the ex-prosecutor opposed prosecutorial reforms and had previously belonged to a faction close to former President Yoon Suk Yeol.
