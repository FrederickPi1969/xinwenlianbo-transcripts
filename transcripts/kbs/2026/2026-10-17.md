## Parties Continue to Clash over DP Push to Seek Impeachment of Chief Justice

The rival political parties continued to clash over the ruling party's push to seek impeachment of Supreme Court Chief Justice Jo Hee-de.
Ruling Democratic Party(DP) spokesperson Lee Yong-woo said in a written press briefing Saturday that it would be parliament's duty to verify any unconstitutionalities or unlawfulness in the allegations made against Jo and to correspondingly hold him to account.
The DP has cited Jo's refusal to make a fresh recommendation for a top court justice candidate, or to take an oath during a parliamentary audit, as well as alleged excessive spending of the court's operating expense to justify its push for his impeachment.
Main opposition People Power Party(PPP) chief spokesperson Park Sung-hoon slammed the DP for using the impeachment card to take the chief justice "political hostage," and warned a related motion would signal the end of the Lee Jae Myung administration.
PPP floor spokesperson Choi Soo-jin, in a separate statement, accused the ruling party of attempting to suspend Jo from his duties to gain control over judicial appointments and to paralyze the top court to prevent the resumption of Lee's pending trials.

## &#039;Takaichi Cabinet to Uphold Historical Perception of 1995 Murayama Statement&#039;

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi has again said her Cabinet continues to uphold the historical perception of its predecessors, including the 1995 Murayama Statement, which formally recognized and apologized for Japan's colonial rule and wartime aggression.
According to Japanese daily The Asahi Shimbun on Saturday, Takaichi reaffirmed during a session of the Upper House Budget Committee the previous day that the Cabinet's position on its perception of history would remain undeterred.
The prime minister said the stance also applies to the Murayama Statement.
Takaichi was scrutinized over a remark she had made in 1995, when she said that she felt no remorse for Japan's colonial past as a member of the post-war generation and opposed the inclusion of the term "aggression" in the statement.
The prime minister had officially stated after taking office last year that she would uphold past Cabinet positions regarding the historical awareness in the Murayama Statement.

## Anthropic Used Copyrighted Works by Korean Authors to Train AI Model

U.S. artificial intelligence company Anthropic has been found to have used English translations of books by Korean authors, including Han Kang and Hwang Sok-yong, to train its AI model Claude without authorization.
According to the Literature Translation Institute of Korea, 21 books by Korean authors were included on a list of works eligible for compensation under a copyright settlement involving Anthropic.
The institute identified the books and notified the authors in March.
Anthropic had previously faced a lawsuit over allegations that it illegally downloaded copyrighted books to train its AI models.
A U.S. court found copyright infringement involving some 500-thousand works, and the case was settled in July, with Anthropic agreeing to pay a total of one-point-five billion U.S. dollars.
Compensation is reportedly estimated at around three-thousand dollars per work.
The books include works by Han Kang, Hwang Sok-yong, Kim Young-ha and Shin Kyung-sook, among others.
Meanwhile, the Korean Publishers Society(KOPUS) has called for fair compensation amid concerns that overseas AI companies are purchasing large quantities of books for AI training.
KOPUS stressed that there would be no AI without books, criticizing the lack of transparency that leaves authors unaware of when and how their works are used, and prevents them from seeking fair compensation.

## Military Reaffirms Non-Combat Intervention amid Cheonghae Unit Deployment Controversy

The military has reaffirmed the "principle-based position" of not intervening in foreign combat under any circumstances, after media outlets reported that it was looking to expand the operational area of the anti-piracy Cheonghae Unit off Somalia to the Strait of Hormuz.
The Ministry of National Defense said in a notice to the press on Friday that the military was reviewing the state's role in protecting South Koreans and national economic interests, but that no final decision has been made.
The comments come amid controversy over the Lee Jae Myung administration's alleged review of deployment upon a U.S. request for support in the strait, while circumventing the country's non-combat intervention policy.
Earlier, media outlets reported that the ministry was weighing the operational expansion from the Gulf of Aden to include the Gulf of Oman near Hormuz, before submitting a motion to the National Assembly for consent.
The ministry was reportedly also considering the deployment of a maritime patrol aircraft to enhance the unit's air surveillance and reconnaissance capabilities.
The unit's deployment since 2009 has required annual parliamentary approval, and a motion passed last year stipulates the Gulf of Aden as its primary area of operation, in addition to contingency zones designated for the protection of South Koreans.

## N. Korea Claims Underwater Test of New Precision-Guided Torpedo System

North Korea says it had conducted an underwater test of a new precision-guided torpedo weapon system.
The North's state-run Korean Central News Agency said on Saturday that the test was carried out two days earlier to verify the technical upgrade of the "Zam-2.0" super-speed torpedo system.
The news agency said senior defense officials who oversaw the test assessed that its "great outcome" is indicative of a "significant change" in the navy's practical development.
The agency said the new torpedo is equipped with artificial intelligence technology in its target detection and guidance functions and that the test verified the system's key technology that enhances propulsion efficiency, as well as propulsion device performance.
Pyongyang has seemingly been pushing to strengthen its naval power, having test-fired two tactical cruise missiles from what was presumed to be a three-thousand-ton-class submarine in the East Sea on Wednesday.

## Firefighter Dies after Battling Factory Fire in Bucheon

A firefighter with no vital signs who was rescued from a factory fire in Bucheon, Gyeonggi Province, has died.
The Gyeonggi Nambu Provincial Police Agency said Saturday that the firefighter, in his 40s, died at around 1 a.m. while receiving treatment at a hospital.
The fire broke out at around 8:40 a.m. Friday at a metal parts  factory in Bucheon's Ojeong District and was extinguished about two and a half hours later.
The firefighter was among the first responders dispatched to the scene and became trapped as the flames spread rapidly while battling the blaze for about 20 minutes.
He was found with no vital signs at around 9:40 a.m.
Another firefighter in his 40s was seriously injured but has since regained consciousness.
A total of eight people, including six firefighters and two factory workers, sustained injuries.
A funeral altar has been set up at Soonchunhyang University Bucheon Hospital, while a joint memorial altar is scheduled to open at Sejong Central Park at 2 p.m. Saturday.

## Trump Announces Hyundai’s $560 Million Investment in Georgia

U.S. President Donald Trump says Hyundai will invest 560 million U.S. dollars to expand its manufacturing facilities in the state of Georgia.
Speaking at a midterm election campaign rally in Dalton, Georgia, on Friday, Trump cited the investment as an example of growing foreign investment in the United States.
He also touted the achievements of his second administration, saying the U.S. is doing exceptionally well under Republican leadership.
Trump was apparently referring to an investment plan by Hyundai Transys, an auto parts affiliate of Hyundai Motor Group.
Georgia Governor Brian Kemp announced the investment on October eighth, saying the company plans to create 320 new jobs as part of its ongoing efforts to support global automotive customers operating in North America.
According to the governor's office, Hyundai Transys will use the investment to modernize its two existing plants in West Point and build a third facility.
The state said the expansion will help increase production capacity for electrified powertrains used in hybrid vehicles.
The company plans to hire workers for administrative, management and manufacturing positions.
