## Huh Mimi Wins S. Korea’s First Judo Gold at Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games

The gold marked the first in judo for a South Korean athlete at this year’s Asian Games.
Born to a Korean father and Japanese mother, Huh chose South Korean citizenship in 2021 in accordance with her grandmother’s dying wish to see her compete as a Korean athlete.

## Data Ministry: Seniors Account for 21.6% of South Korea’s Population in 2026

Individuals aged 65 and older made up more than 20 percent of South Korea’s population this year, reflecting the country’s rapid aging and ultralow birth rate.
According to data released by the Ministry of Data and Statistics on Thursday, the number of senior citizens stood at eleven-point-12 million in 2026, representing 21-point-six percent of the country’s 51-point-six million people.
This demographic data categorizes South Korea as a “super-aged society,” and projections indicate that the percentage will rise to 30 percent by 2036 and exceed 40 percent by 2050.
The number of households led by senior citizens is currently six-point-five million, accounting for 28-point-eight percent of all households in the country.
Among households led by older adults, 38 percent are single-person homes, a figure that has increased steadily since 2015.
Households headed by seniors are forecast to make up 50-point-six percent of all households by 2052.
The remaining life expectancy for those aged 65 is estimated at an average of 21-point-seven years as of 2024 — 19-point-five years for men and 23-point-seven years for women.
In terms of financial stability, households led by senior citizens had an average net asset value of 493 million won, equivalent to 363-thousand U.S. dollars, in 2025, and 91-point-two percent of the total elderly population received state pension payments in 2024.

## Trump Unilaterally Announces S. Korean Investment in Alaska LNG Project

U.S. President Donald Trump has unilaterally announced that South Korea has committed to investing over 50 billion dollars in an Alaska liquefied natural gas(LNG) development project.
Trump made the announcement in the Oval Office on Wednesday surrounded by Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick, Secretary of the Interior Doug Burgum, Secretary of Energy Chris Wright, and Senator Dan Sullivan from Alaska.
The LNG project, which includes the development of a pipeline to transport natural gas from Alaska's North Slope for shipment to Asia, would be part of the 350 billion dollar investment pledge Seoul made under a year-old bilateral agreement aimed at lowering tariffs.
According to Trump, South Korea's nearly 200 billion dollar investment would also build a six-gigawatt power generation facility in Texas and eight new large-scale nuclear power plants.
Lutnick explained that more than 50 billion dollars would likely be injected into the LNG project, 120 billion dollars into the eight nuclear plants in Ohio, Tennessee, South Carolina, and Kentucky, and 22 billion dollars into the gas-fired combined-cycle power plant in Texas.
In a recent National Assembly briefing, Lee Jae Myung adminstration officials confirmed a Texas power plant as their first project, while pausing the Alaska LNG project for financial review.
The LNG project has been a high priority for Trump, especially because Alaska is likely to be a battleground state in November's U.S. midterm elections.
