## South Korea, 5 Central Asian Countries Adopt Seoul Declaration at Inaugural Summit

President Lee Jae Myung and the leaders of all five Central Asian countries have adopted the “Seoul Declaration,” which outlines a blueprint for cooperation over the next 30 years.
The announcement came Wednesday in Seoul at the inaugural South Korea-Central Asia Summit, attended by the leaders of Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan, Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan.
The Seoul Declaration sets out a vision of cooperation based on four pillars: institutionalizing a South Korea-Central Asia partnership; exerting joint efforts to establish peace and stability; pursuing a future of innovation and prosperity; and strengthening connections through people and trust.
President Lee, for his part, presented five paths to joint prosperity for South Korea and Central Asia: partnership, peace, mutual benefits, substantive cooperation, future-oriented cooperation, and connections among people.
Lee also stressed the need for South Korea and Central Asia to form a strategic relationship that goes beyond tackling independent issues and jointly prepares all parties for mid- and long-term challenges.
The South Korea-Central Asia Summit is set to be held every two years, with the next gathering taking place in Astana, Kazakhstan.

## Owner of Michelin-Starred Restaurant Fined 15 Million Won for Using Ants as Food

The owner of a two-Michelin-starred restaurant has been fined 15 million won for using ants in some dishes.
That’s around eleven-thousand U.S. dollars.
The Seoul Western District Court handed down the fine on Wednesday after convicting the owner under the Food Sanitation Act, while also hitting the corporation that operates the restaurant with a ten million won fine.
The owner and the corporation used dried ants from the U.S. and Thailand for some four years starting in 2021.
Under the Food Sanitation Act, ants can’t be used as a food ingredient as they’re not among the ten species of insects classified as edible.
According to the prosecution, the restaurant brought in profits totaling 12 million won by selling some 12-thousand-200 sherbets that used ants, adding that some 49-thousand ants were used in the restaurant’s dishes.

## Audit Finds Yoon’s Office Pressed to Advance Schedule for East Sea Gas Drilling

The state auditor says the Korea National Oil Corporation pushed ahead with drilling for deep-sea gas in the East Sea without sufficiently verifying the results of a feasibility study and failed to properly review the bidding requirements when selecting a contractor.
The Board of Audit and Inspection(BAI) announced the findings Wednesday of its audit of the gas field project, which Industry Minister Kim Jung-kwan requested last October in the public interest.
The project was unveiled by then-President Yoon Suk Yeol in a 2024 briefing based on an assessment that the waters off Pohang could contain up to 14 billion barrels of oil and gas, but its economic feasibility was effectively ruled out just 15 months later.
The audit found that the oil corporation proceeded with drilling before the final feasibility study was completed and without adequate verification, while improperly handling the technical evaluation and failing to properly review the requirements for the limited competitive bidding process.
The audit also found that Yoon’s office had repeatedly pressed the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy to bring forward the drilling schedule so the project could be carried out during Yoon’s term.
The BAI called for disciplinary action against oil corporation employees for improperly handling the bidding process, but said it would take no action on the ministry’s reports to the presidential office or on Yoon’s briefing because there were no relevant regulations governing them.

## Medical Groups Call for Legal Framework as Gov’t Moves to Introduce Abortion Pills

The government is moving to introduce abortion pills in South Korea, but medical groups have raised concerns over the lack of a legal framework and adequate safety measures.
According to the government on Wednesday, an abortion medication is currently under review by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety for import approval and use for up to 63 days into a pregnancy, or nine weeks.
The Korean Society of Obstetrics and Gynecology expressed regret that the medication is being introduced in the absence of legislation to address the legal vacuum since the Constitutional Court’s 2019 ruling that the abortion law was unconstitutional, saying a legal framework should come first.
The Korean Association of Obstetricians and Gynecologists also stressed the need for legal revisions to ensure patient safety and protect medical professionals from potential legal liability, but said it doesn’t oppose the introduction of abortion medication in principle.
The Ministry of Health and Welfare plans to require doctors to prescribe and dispense abortion pills directly for the first two years, before gradually expanding its use following reviews of its safety and side effects.
The oral abortion medication, commonly known by the brand name Mifegyne, was first approved in France in 1988 and was used in around 100 countries as of 2024, while the World Health Organization has listed it as an essential medicine.

## Over 300 S. Koreans Detained in Georgia Immigration Raid Take Legal Action against US

South Korean workers who were arrested and detained en masse last year by U.S. immigration authorities in the state of Georgia are taking legal action against the Donald Trump administration.
CNN reported on Tuesday that a group of more than 300 South Koreans who were detained in the immigration raid at the Hyundai-LG battery plant project have begun filing administrative claims — a mandatory step before a federal ​lawsuit.
The claims are against nine federal agencies, including the Department of Homeland Security, Immigration and Customs Enforcement, Customs and Border Protection, the Federal Bureau of Investigation, and the Department of Justice.
Under the Federal Tort Claims Act, if you have suffered damages due to unlawful acts by the federal government, you can seek a settlement by filing an administrative claim specifying a clear dollar amount, and if the agency denies it or fails to act within six months, you may file a lawsuit in federal district court.
Several workers interviewed by CNN said they were unlawfully arrested, detained without explanation or interpreters, shackled, forced to sign documents they couldn’t understand and held in overcrowded cells.
Four workers who requested anonymity to avoid retaliation told CNN they were working under valid visas and received no explanation for their arrest.
A Korean lawyer in Georgia who has been representing the claimants explained that the purpose of the legal action is to make the U.S. government acknowledge an “enormous, unjust mistake” and “admit it and apologize.”
The attorney said the U.S. government used the false pretext of finding four undocumented people to send hundreds of fully armed agents to storm the worksite of a South Korean company, adding that they put engineers who had come lawfully in handcuffs and shackles and paraded them like criminals, “shaming all Koreans before the world as if Korean companies were invading the U.S. labor market.”
The massive raid in Ellabell, Georgia, on September 4, 2025, resulted in 475 workers being detained — mostly South Koreans — and caused widespread public outrage in both South Korea and the U.S.
