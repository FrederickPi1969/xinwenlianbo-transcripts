## Court Orders Record Divorce Settlement for Smilegate Founder’s Wife

A South Korean court has granted a divorce to the founder of major game developer Smilegate and his wife, awarding the largest property settlement on record in a domestic divorce case.

The court said the couple’s marriage had broken down beyond repair, rejecting both sides’ claims for alimony after finding them equally responsible for the marital breakdown, in a ruling handed down about four years after the divorce suit was filed in 2022.
The court said the property settlement would amount to about two-point-55 trillion won, or roughly one-point-nine billion dollars, if paid entirely in cash, making it the largest publicly known divorce settlement in South Korea.
Given that most of Kwon’s assets are unlisted shares, the court ordered him to transfer 35 percent of his Smilegate shares to her and pay the remaining 65 billion won in cash.
The court included the shares in the assets subject to division, citing Kwon’s wife’s involvement in the company’s founding and early management, as well as her long-term contributions in the form of household duties and child-rearing.

## New Survey Shows School Violence Continues to Rise Each Year

A growing percentage of young people report experiencing school violence each year, according to new government data.
The Ministry of Education on Wednesday released the results of a nationwide survey conducted on three-point-nine million students in the fourth grade through the third year of high school that found about 91-thousand respondents, or two-point-nine percent, had been victims of school violence.
The figure is up zero-point-four percentage point compared to last year.
The victim response rate has risen steadily over the past five years from one-point-one percent in 2021, according to the survey.
Verbal abuse was the most cited form of violence, experienced by 37-point-eight percent of respondents.
Group bullying was next at 17-point-one percent, followed by physical violence at 15-point-seven percent and cyberbullying at seven percent.
An alarming 30-point-six percent of students said there was no specific reason for their perpetrator’s actions.
The most common reason victims gave for not reporting violence was that they “thought it was no big deal,” while others preferred to handle the situation themselves or believed talking about it would be pointless.
Seong Yun-sook, a senior research fellow at the National Youth Policy Institute who conducted and analyzed the survey, noted that increased digital device use leaves adolescents with fewer face-to-face relationships and declining empathy.
The ministry said there is also a large gap in perceptions between the parties involved in school violence and that it will not just focus on reporting, but will also develop educational materials aimed at building practical competencies like conflict management and self-protection strategies for students.
