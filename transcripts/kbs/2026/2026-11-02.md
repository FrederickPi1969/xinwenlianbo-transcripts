## S. Korea’s Sixth Supercomputer, ‘Hangang,’ Launches Operations

The nation’s sixth supercomputer, “Hangang,” launched operations on Monday at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology Information in Daejeon, some four years after efforts were made to establish it.
Hangang’s installment comes after bidding rounds failed on four occasions and changes were made to the project plan due to a funding shortage caused by an increase in demand that drove up the price of graphics processing units(GPUs) worldwide.
A total of 448-point-two billion won, or around 334 billion U.S. dollars, was injected into Hangang’s establishment.
The supercomputer is equipped with eight-thousand-496 GPUs, including Nvidia’s GH200 Grace Hopper Superchips, and nine-thousand-936 central processing units.
Hangang’s theoretical peak performance is 614 petaflops, its storage capacity is 205 petabytes and its network speed is over 400 gigabits per second.
Compared with the nation’s fifth supercomputer, Nurion, Hangang is 24 times stronger in theoretical peak performance, with ten times more storage space and four times greater network capacity.

## Yellow Dust Hits Seoul in November for First Time in 6 Years

Yellow dust is affecting Seoul in November for the first time in six years, pushing fine dust levels to “bad” in the capital region and parts of the west coast, and on Jeju Island.
The National Institute of Environmental Research said that as of 1 p.m. Monday, the hourly average concentration of fine dust particles measuring ten micrometers or less in diameter stood at 149 micrograms per cubic meter in Seoul, 138 on Heuksan Island and 104 on Jeju Island, all within the bad range of 81 to 150 micrograms.
The dust, which originated in Inner Mongolia and other areas on Saturday, has been carried toward the Korean Peninsula by northwesterly winds.
While yellow dust typically affects South Korea between March and May, November is also a relatively common period for such inflows, as it precedes snowfall in the source regions and weather patterns often bring northwesterly winds to the peninsula.
The impact of the current dust is not expected to last long.
The institute forecasts that additional yellow dust will arrive Tuesday night, temporarily raising fine dust levels to bad in the Seoul metropolitan area and South Chungcheong Province, although daily average levels are expected to remain moderate.

## FSC Calls Emergency Meeting with Crypto Exchanges after Binance Hack

Financial authorities have urgently summoned domestic cryptocurrency exchanges following a hacking incident at Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, involving losses of around eight trillion won, or six billion U.S. dollars.
The Financial Services Commission(FSC) said it held an emergency security meeting Monday with officials from major exchanges, including Upbit, Bithumb and Coinone, to review the incident and assess their security systems.
The meeting also discussed ways to protect users’ assets, including increasing the proportion of cryptocurrencies stored in cold wallets.
Cold wallets are offline storage systems that are less vulnerable to hacking because they are disconnected from external networks.
Under current law, exchanges must keep at least 80 percent of users’ virtual assets in cold wallets, though the actual share varies from the low 80s to as high as 99 percent among operators.
The FSC will hold another security meeting Thursday with the Financial Supervisory Service, the Financial Security Institute and the heads of the country’s five major commercial banks.

## S. Korea Targets 1-Day Stock Settlement by 2028

South Korea plans to shorten its stock settlement cycle from two business days to one by 2028.
On Monday, the Korea Exchange, Korea Securities Depository, Korea Securities Finance Corporation and Korea Financial Investment Association revealed a draft roadmap for the change, which would mark the first overhaul of the system since its introduction in 1972.
The change will cover stocks, exchange-traded funds and exchange-traded notes, allowing investors to withdraw proceeds from stock sales on the following business day.
The 2028 target is later than the previously discussed plan to align with Europe’s transition in October of next year, reflecting the need to give domestic and foreign investors more time to prepare.
In a survey of 32 foreign securities firms, custodians and investors, 84-point-four percent favored implementation in the second half of 2028 or later, while seven respondents warned that they could reduce their investments in the South Korean market if the change came before they were ready.
The shorter cycle would also require investors buying stocks to make payments one day earlier, while the deadlines for forced stock sales to cover unpaid balances and correcting erroneous trades would also be brought forward by a day.
