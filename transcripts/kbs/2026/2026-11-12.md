## Foreign Tourist Numbers Top 20 Million for First Time

More than 20 million foreign tourists have visited South Korea so far this year, a first for the country.
The Ministry of Culture, Tourism and Sports said the milestone was reached Thursday, 14 years after foreign tourist numbers first surpassed ten million in a single year.
The figure indicates that on average, some 64-thousand foreign tourists visited every day this year.
Spending by foreign visitors amounted to nearly 16 trillion won, or roughly 12 billion U.S. dollars, in the first nine months of this year.
That’s up 47-point-seven percent from the same period last year.
The ministry attributed the latest achievement to the global expansion of K-culture, the cross-ministerial coordination system on national tourism, and the reputation for hospitality that South Koreans have earned.
Culture Minister Chae Hwi-young said hitting the 20 million mark is a starting point that the country should build on to become a world-class tourist destination.

## Elementary School-Age Population Falls in 21 of Seoul’s 25 Districts

The elementary school-age population has declined sharply over the past decade in 21 of Seoul’s 25 districts, though it has remained stable or grown slightly in traditional education hubs such as Gangnam, Seocho and Yangcheon.
The Korea Educational Development Institute said Thursday that its analysis of 229 cities, counties and districts nationwide from 2014 to 2023 identified 21 Seoul districts as major urban areas experiencing sharp declines in their elementary school-age populations.
A total of 73 areas nationwide fell into this same category, including all five districts in Daejeon.
But this trend wasn’t apparent in Seoul’s Gangnam, Seocho and Yangcheon districts; Suseong District in Daegu; or Yeonsu District in Incheon — areas where the elementary school-age population either remained stable or grew moderately.
Researchers said these areas were either traditionally popular for their residential and educational environments or had attracted new residents through urban redevelopment projects.
The report also highlighted a widening regional gap, with the number of elementary school students nationwide having fallen 37-point-six percent between 2003 and 2023, from four-point-18 million to two-point-six million.
While the number fell 23-point-eight percent in the capital region over the 20-year period, it plunged 50-point-two percent in non-metropolitan areas.

On Saturday, a Kansong Art Museum researcher will deliver a lecture comparing Joseon-era Hanyang with modern-day Seoul using the landscape paintings of Gyeomjae Jeong Seon, and on Sunday the museum will introduce Joseon-era fashion and lifestyle culture through the paintings of Hyewon Shin Yun-bok.

The tech-heavy KOSDAQ lost one-point-88 points, or zero-point-27 percent, to open at 703-point-22.

## NYT Report Alleges Nvidia CEO Had Second Affair with Company Executive

Nvidia co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang, who is under investigation for having an affair with a Chinese businesswoman suspected of working as a spy for Beijing, reportedly had another extramarital relationship with an executive within the company.
In a report published Wednesday, The New York Times cited five former Nvidia employees as saying Huang had a relationship with Kimberly Powell, the company’s vice president of healthcare.
Two of those sources reportedly told the Times that Huang and Powell, who are both married to other people, were captured on video being affectionate at a hotel in San Jose, California, during an Nvidia conference in March, although the paper said it hadn’t seen the video.
When the Times inquired about the matter, Huang said the paper shouldn’t be talking to him directly and Nvidia declined to provide a statement.
The company’s code of conduct stipulates that employees must “avoid situations where actions create conflict,” including “personal relationships,” and that it “promptly investigates reports of misconduct,” the paper pointed out.
Nvidia shares fell about eight percent in after-hours trading, and The Wall Street Journal, citing people familiar with the situation, said the company’s board of directors is preparing to oust Huang.
