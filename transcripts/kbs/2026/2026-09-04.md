## Gallup: Lee’s Approval Rating Hits New Low at 40%

The poll has a margin of error of plus or minus three-point-one percentage points at a 95 percent confidence level.

## 2 Workers Rescued from Power Plant Tunnel in Nepal 9 Days after Massive Flood

## Older Adults Now Outnumber Young Children Worldwide

People aged 65 and older now outnumber children aged five and under worldwide for the first time in human history.
The global population of adults 65 and older reached 852 million last year, accounting for 10-point-five percent of the total population of eight-point-13 billion, according to a U.S. Census Bureau report released Thursday.
The report showed that longer lifespans and a plunge in fertility are creating a demographic inversion that will likely deepen rapidly in the years to come.
More than 71 percent of the world’s population lived in countries with fertility rates at or below the replacement level of about two-point-one births per woman in 2023, compared to a decade earlier when it was 45 percent.
China and India, the two most populous countries in the world, notably have fertility rates below replacement level, with Bangladesh, Mexico and Vietnam showing similar patterns.
The 65-plus population is projected to rise to two billion by 2060, which would make that demographic about 20 percent of the total population.
The report also said South Korea’s older citizens accounted for 20-point-three percent of its population as of last year, but that percentage is expected to more than double to 41 percent by 2060.
If that prediction holds true, South Korea is set to overtake Japan and become the country with the highest percentage of people over 65.
The proportion of the South Korean population aged 80 and older is also expected to increase roughly fourfold to 18-point-three percent over the cited period, which would put the country in the No. 3 spot after Monaco and Japan.
The Census Bureau report also noted that population aging reflects positive outcomes such as improvements in health and more people surviving into old age.
Andrew Scott, who contributed to the report, said the demographic shift could force societies to rethink old age and come to view it as an economic asset rather than simply a cost.

## S. Korea Posts Record July Current Account Surplus of $42 Billion

South Korea posted a current account surplus of 42-point-08 billion U.S. dollars in July — the largest ever for that month — driven by a boom in semiconductor exports.
Provisional data from the Bank of Korea on Friday shows the figure for July is second only to June’s 49-point-73 billion dollars, extending the country’s surplus streak to 39 consecutive months.
The cumulative figure for the first seven months of the year reached 233-point-09 billion dollars.
The goods account posted its second-largest surplus ever of 40-point-43 billion dollars, after exports jumped 65-point-three percent from the previous year to 100-point-45 billion dollars.
Shipments of computer peripherals surged 344-point-five percent, and those of semiconductors increased 176-point-three percent.
Imports rose 21-point-seven percent to 60-point-02 billion dollars, led by gains in capital goods, such as semiconductors and transportation equipment, and raw materials, including crude oil and gas.

## Ex-USFK Chief Predicts Trump-Kim Summit in November or December

A former U.S. military commander has predicted that a summit between U.S. President Donald Trump and North Korean leader Kim Jong-un could take place in November or December.

Brooks said both sides would likely want an "early touch point" in the fall, then a substantive follow-up discussion afterwards.

## Bad Weather Halts Helicopter Search for 9 Missing S. Koreans in Nepal

South Korea's disaster relief team in Nepal was forced to cut short a helicopter search for nine missing South Koreans on Thursday, local time, due to poor weather conditions.
Two helicopters provided by the Nepali military were sent up to conduct an aerial search, but after 30 minutes, they were forced to stop as rain reduced the field of vision to less than a meter, according to an official from the team at the Battar base camp.
The team plans to search the same area again by helicopter on Friday, before discussing next steps with Nepali authorities.
Six team members and two Nepali military officials also searched a land route between Ramche and Dhunche in the Rasuwa district, where the flash floods struck on August 26, but were unable to uncover any leads.
The nine missing employees of the Korea South-East Power Company and Doosan Enerbility had been dispatched to Rasuwa for a hydroelectric plant project.
