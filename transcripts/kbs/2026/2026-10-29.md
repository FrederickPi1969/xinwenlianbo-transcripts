The ministry says Kang and Hegseth agreed to hold the allies’ annual Security Consultative Meeting early next year, after a ministerial meeting in Washington in December.
[Sound bite: Defense Minister Kang Shin-chul and U.S. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth shaking hands before talks]
The meeting took place as Seoul and Washington remain at odds over when is the most opportune time for South Korea to assume wartime command authority of the allied forces.
After the talks in the Philippines on Wednesday, the South Korean defense ministry explained to reporters that U.S. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth reaffirmed Washington’s stance in favor of an eventual transition.
But as the two sides struggle to narrow their differences on the timing, Defense Minister Kang Shin-chul and Hegseth decided to hold the Security Consultative Meeting early next year to discuss the transfer of wartime operational control, or OPCON.
Seoul initially wanted to complete the verification of its full operational capability this year at the annual meeting in the second stage of a three-phase, conditions-based OPCON transition plan, but the two sides failed to schedule the annual security dialogue, normally held in late October or early November.

During the Korean War, South Korea handed over its operational command of the allied forces to the United Nations Command, which later transferred that authority to the South Korea-U.S. Combined Forces Command.
Seoul regained peacetime control of the troops in 1994, but wartime operations are led by the USFK chief, who also leads the South Korea-U.S. Combined Forces Command.
Instead of a date-driven timeline, Seoul and Washington agreed in 2014 to a conditions-based OPCON transfer, meaning South Korea must have the necessary military capabilities before it takes over the role.
During his campaign last year, Lee pledged to regain wartime OPCON within his term in office, calling the transfer a matter of national sovereignty and independence.

A reading above 100 indicates optimism about the overall economy, while a reading below 100 indicates pessimism.
The CBSI among manufacturers rose by six-point-one points to stand at 105-point-six, the highest since June 2022, fueled by rising production and investment in key industries such as semiconductors and shipbuilding.
The index for non-manufacturers rose four-point-three points to 103-point-two, supported by chip-related investments and an influx of foreign tourists that impacted construction, wholesale and retail, and transportation and warehousing, among other factors.
The results were based on a survey of three-thousand-450 companies in manufacturing and nonmanufacturing conducted from October 14-21.

## PHP Cyberattack Breaches 25 Firewalls Linked to S. Korea, US, Japan

In a coordinated effort, the attacker also exploited a privilege escalation flaw in the Linux operating system, gaining root privileges on 24 firewalls.
At a U.S. government-affiliated body, the attacker breached two firewalls and seized control of one internal management server, bringing the total number of compromised systems to 25.
By breaching the agency’s firewalls and taking over the central management server, the attacker likely gained access to configurations and authentication data for other managed firewalls.

## National Average Gas, Diesel Prices Drop on Thursday

The average retail price of gasoline and diesel at gas stations nationwide dropped on Thursday.
According to the Korea National Oil Corporation’s price information system, Opinet, as of 9 a.m., the national average price of a liter of gasoline was one-thousand-855-point-47, or roughly one U.S. dollar and 39 cents, down zero-point-15 won compared to the previous day.
The average price of a liter of diesel fell zero-point-32 won to one-thousand-841-point-72 won.
The average gasoline price in Seoul dropped zero-point-37 won to one-thousand-900-point-88 won per liter, while diesel rose by zero-point-08 won to one-thousand-882-point-63 won per liter.

## Special Counsel Completes Verification of 2.47 Million Ballots from June 3 Elections

An on-site inspection of roughly two-point-47 million ballots concluded on Thursday after 12 hours, led by the special counsel team investigating the ballot shortages during the June 3 elections.
Lee Tae-han, the team leader, reported at 12:38 a.m. that observers had raised objections during the inspection, but he assured them that investigators would thoroughly verify the disputed ballots.
Lee stressed that the outcome must rely on objective data and evidence rather than assumptions or political claims, adding that his team will investigate the alleged infringements of voting rights fairly and transparently.
Some members of the main opposition People Power Party(PPP) demanded another round of inspection, claiming that an alleged miscount of ballots for a Democratic Party district councilor candidate in Seoul’s Songpa District had not been fully explained.
PPP leader Jang Dong-hyeok, Supreme Council member Kim Min-soo, and other representatives who observed the inspection staged a sit-in to block the transfer of the verified ballots out of the SK Olympic Handball Gymnasium — the vote-counting center where they had been stored since the election.
The National Election Commission, with assistance from the police, began transferring the ballots around 6 a.m.
