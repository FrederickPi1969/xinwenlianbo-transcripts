## Foreign Media Speculate on Purpose of Kim's Surprise China Visit

North Korean leader Kim Jong-un's surprise visit to China on Saturday has drawn widespread attention from foreign media, with analysts and news outlets speculating about the trip's purpose.
Reuters described Kim's visit to Dandong in China's northeastern Liaoning Province via the newly opened New Yalu River Bridge as a symbolic move underscoring efforts to expand bilateral trade.
The Financial Times said the trip reaffirmed Pyongyang's close ties with Beijing amid deepening military cooperation between North Korea and Russia.
Peter Ward, a research fellow at the Sejong Institute, told the Financial Times that China would seek to draw North Korea closer, while Kim would likely try to improve relations with Beijing to secure additional economic and diplomatic benefits.
The Associated Press described the visit as an assertive diplomatic move aimed at aligning North Korea more closely with China and Russia and building a united front against the United States amid what Kim has called a "new Cold War."
Foreign media also highlighted the timing of the visit ahead of U.S. President Donald Trump's planned trip to China on November 18 and 19 to attend the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, fueling speculation about a possible meeting between Trump and Kim.
Christopher Green, a senior consultant at the International Crisis Group, told Bloomberg that Kim may have sought to align positions with Chinese President Xi Jinping before agreeing to meet Trump.

## FTC Chief Backs Tougher Penalties Over Expanded Investigative Powers

Fair Trade Commission (FTC) Chairman Ju Biung-ghi said imposing penalty surcharges of up to one percent of a company's total revenue for obstructing investigations would be an effective way to strengthen enforcement of competition laws.
Appearing on a KBS program Sunday, Ju said the FTC could effectively carry out its duties under its current administrative investigative authority, responding to a question about whether the agency should be granted compulsory investigative powers.
His remarks came amid growing calls for the FTC to receive such powers following Coupang's refusal to cooperate with an on-site inspection and Hanwha's refusal to submit requested documents.
Under other laws enforced by the FTC, obstructing an investigation can result in penalty surcharges, compulsory penalty payments and criminal sanctions.
However, under the Act on Fair Transactions in Large Retail Business, which Coupang is suspected of violating, the FTC can currently impose only administrative fines for obstructing an investigation and cannot levy penalty surcharges or seek criminal penalties.
Ju stressed that the law should be amended to allow the FTC to impose penalty surcharges and compulsory penalty payments in such cases.
Addressing continued claims from the United States that the FTC's sanctions against Coupang discriminate against American companies, Ju said the agency does not discriminate based on nationality and, in fact, applies the law more strictly to domestic companies.

## Kia-Samsung Playoff Opener Moved to Monday Due to Typhoon-Driven Rain

The South Korean baseball postseason was disrupted by a typhoon Sunday, as rain forced the postponement of the opening game of the playoff series between the KIA Tigers and Samsung Lions.
The Korea Baseball Organization (KBO) announced Sunday that Game 1 of the best-of-five series, scheduled for 2 p.m. at Samsung Lions Park in Daegu, had been postponed because of rain.
Rain began falling at the stadium in the morning under the influence of Typhoon Champi, with the Korea Meteorological Administration forecasting 20 to 60 millimeters of rain across southern Daegu and North Gyeongsang Province.
It was the first rainout of the postseason this year and the 26th rain-related postponement in KBO postseason history.
Game 1 between the KIA Tigers and Samsung Lions will be played at the same venue at 6:30 p.m. Monday.

## S. Korea’s Q2 Current Account Surplus Tops Germany, Japan Combined for First Time

South Korea's current account surplus in the second quarter exceeded the combined surpluses of Germany and Japan, two major manufacturing economies, for the first time on record, fueled by rising semiconductor prices and strong exports.
According to International Monetary Fund data available through the Bank of Korea's Economic Statistics System, South Korea posted a current account surplus of 116-point-three billion U.S. dollars in the second quarter.
The figure ranked second among 36 major economies with available data for the quarter, trailing China's 193-point-72-billion-dollar surplus.
Germany ranked third with a surplus of 52-point-28 billion dollars, followed by Japan at 50-point-62 billion dollars. Their combined surplus of 102-point-89 billion dollars was 13-point-74 billion dollars less than South Korea's.
The second quarter marked the first time since the first quarter of 1996, when comparable data for all three countries became available, that South Korea's current account surplus exceeded the combined total of Germany and Japan.
According to the IMF's October World Economic Outlook, South Korea's current account surplus is projected to reach 421-point-946 billion dollars this year, ranking second among 190 countries and regions with available data, behind China's projected surplus of 863-point-205 billion dollars.

## Seoul Closes Jamsu Bridge to Traffic for Pedestrian Conversion Project

Vehicle traffic on Seoul's Jamsu Bridge will be completely suspended beginning Monday as the city starts construction to convert it into a pedestrian-only bridge.
The Seoul Metropolitan Government said Sunday that vehicles will be barred from the bridge starting at 12 a.m. Monday, while pedestrians and cyclists will continue to have access.
The city aims to transform Jamsu Bridge, where vehicle traffic has previously been restricted only during special events, into a public space that people can enjoy on a daily basis.
If completed as scheduled in August next year, the 795-meter bridge connecting Seoul's Yongsan and Seocho districts will become the city's first pedestrian-only bridge spanning the Han River, featuring 22 riverside rest areas and transparent decks at two locations where visitors can look down at the water below.
With vehicle traffic suspended, city bus routes 740 and 405 will be rerouted to the nearby Banpo Bridge in both directions. Two bus stops near Banpo Hangang Park and Sebitseom at the southern end of Jamsu Bridge will also be closed.

## N. Korean Leader Vows to Uphold &#039;Blood-Forged&#039; Friendship with China

North Korean leader Kim Jong-un reaffirmed the "blood-forged" alliance between North Korea and China and pledged to carry forward their traditional friendship during a surprise visit to China on Saturday, according to state media.
The North's Korean Central News Agency (KCNA) reported Sunday that Kim visited the Memorial Hall of the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea in Dandong, Liaoning Province, on Saturday to mark the 76th anniversary of the Chinese People's Volunteers' entry into the Korean War.
Kim was accompanied by senior North Korean officials, including Foreign Minister Choe Son-hui and Kim Yo-jong, a department director at the Central Committee of the Workers' Party of Korea. Wang Huning, China's fourth-highest-ranking official, welcomed the North Korean leader.
After laying a wreath at a memorial tower, Kim said the North Korean people would forever remember the years they fought alongside the Chinese people and would pass down their gratitude for China's sacrifices in support of North Korea's revolution.
Kim also praised Chinese President Xi Jinping, attributing China's international stature and national dignity to the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party. Wang conveyed Xi's greetings and said Kim's visit was significant in further developing the countries' traditional friendship and military ties.
KCNA reported that Wang hosted a banquet at Xi's instruction and that Kim later visited a bus factory in Dandong before departing China via the newly opened New Yalu River Bridge, where Chinese officials saw him off at the midpoint of the bridge.
The visit marked Kim's first trip to China in one year and one month and his sixth since taking power, but it was the first time he had visited the country without meeting Xi.

## N. Korean Leader Visits China, Meets Senior Chinese Official

North Korean leader Kim Jong-un made a surprise visit to China on Saturday and met with a senior Chinese official in the border city of Dandong, reaffirming the two countries' commitment to expanding economic cooperation.
China's state-run Xinhua News Agency reported Saturday that Wang Huning, chairman of the National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference and the country's fourth-highest-ranking official, welcomed Kim and accompanied him during his visit.
During their meeting, Kim expressed gratitude for China's long-standing support of North Korea's socialist cause, saying prospects for bilateral cooperation were broad and that Pyongyang hoped to build on the countries' historic friendship and expand practical cooperation to bring tangible benefits to both peoples.
Wang said Kim's visit was significant in strengthening public support for the friendship between the two countries, deepening political trust and advancing cooperation and development.
Wang added that China hoped to expand people-to-people exchanges and economic and trade cooperation following the opening of the New Yalu River Bridge, bringing greater benefits to the people of both countries.
The two countries officially opened the cross-border bridge on October 15, more than a decade after construction was completed.
Xinhua reported that Kim and Wang visited an automobile factory in Dandong, where they toured production facilities and received briefings. Wang later saw Kim off at the cross-border bridge as the North Korean leader departed the city.
