## 20th Asian Games Kick Off in Japan for 16-Day Run

The 20th Asian Games Aichi-Nagoya officially kicked off on Saturday for a 16-day run in Japan.
The opening ceremony was held at Nagoya City Mizuho Park Athletic Stadium, with Emperor Naruhito and Empress Masako in attendance.
Under the theme “Imagine One Asia,” the games will see the participation of some ten-thousand athletes from 45 countries and the Asian Refugee Team vying to win gold in 469 events across 43 sports.
Led by two flag bearers, table tennis player Shin Yu-bin and rowing athlete Kim Dong-yong, the South Korean delegation was the 16th to march into the stadium.
Team Korea has sent one-thousand-51 athletes and officials to Japan to compete in 41 sports with the aim of finishing third in the overall medal tally.
Meanwhile, North Korea has sent 107 athletes to compete in 14 events.
This marks the third time Japan has hosted the Asian Games, after games in Tokyo in 1958 and in Hiroshima in 1994.

## Defense Authorities from S. Korea, US to Strengthen Cooperation on Expediting OPCON Transfer

Defense authorities from South Korea and the United States have issued a joint statement on strengthening cooperation toward expediting Washington's planned conditions-based transfer of wartime operational control(OPCON) to Seoul.
According to Seoul's defense ministry and the U.S. Department of War on Saturday, the statement was issued following the allies' regular high-level Korea-U.S. Integrated Defense Dialogue(KIDD) in the southeastern city of Busan between Wednesday and Friday.
The statement said that the two sides assessed joint efforts to modernize their alliance in line with the changing security environment, inspected progress in the OPCON transfer plan and agreed to reinforce cooperation for its acceleration.
KIDD serves as a prelude to the annual Security Consultative Meeting of defense ministers in November, and Seoul intends to complete the second verification stage of the plan and fix the year of the actual transfer at the upcoming meeting.
The Lee Jae Myung administration seeks to retake wartime OPCON before the end of Lee's presidency in June 2030.

## S. Korea's Lee Jun-suk Advances to Asian Games Men's Teqball Singles Final

South Korea's Lee Jun-suk has advanced to the final of men's singles in teqball at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, after his Kuwaiti rival withdrew from the semifinal match due to a left thigh injury.
Lee was declared the winner of the semifinal game at the Nagoya City Higashi Sports Center on Saturday when Kuwait's Zaid Eidan retired during the second set.
Lee is scheduled to compete for the gold medal against Iraq's Ali Jalil Mezher Alelayawi on Sunday.
If Lee wins, he would become the first Asian Games gold medalist in teqball, which is making its debut as an official sport at Aichi-Nagoya 2026.
A sport originating from Hungary, teqball is played on a curved table with combined elements of various sports including football and table tennis.
Players are allowed to use almost every part of the body except the hands and arms.

## Kim Seung-won Withdraws as Justice Minister Nominee

Anchor: South Korea's nominee for justice minister withdrew his candidacy, just 20 days after being tapped for the post. Kim Seung-won, of the ruling Democratic Party, announced his resignation as nominee Saturday morning, saying he did not want to burden the government. Yun Sohyang has the details.
Report: Justice minister nominee Kim Seung-won withdrew his candidacy on Saturday morning, 20 days after President Lee Jae Myung nominated him.
He said he chose to resign after realizing during President Lee Jae Myung's press conference the previous day that he should not add burden to the government as it looks after people's everyday lives and completes reforms.
Kim said as the justice minister, he had wanted to protect democracy, defend the socially vulnerable with fair law enforcement, and establish a new criminal justice system in a stable manner.
He pledged to complete prosecutorial reforms and to find facts behind its targeted investigations under former President Yoon Suk Yeol, as well as independent Rep. Han Dong-hoon's alleged use of internal prosecution files to attack him during the confirmation process.
This comes after the ruling party adopted the confirmation hearing report for the nominee on Thursday amid the opposition's boycott over allegations that he had illegally lobbied the drug safety ministry to approve a clinical trial for a COVID-19 drug in 2021.
The presidential office later on Saturday said it respects Kim's decision to resign, and that it will proceed according to established rules. It did not say when a replacement would be named. The statement came a day after President Lee Jae Myung said during Friday's press conference that he had not yet reached a final decision on whether to appoint Kim.
The ruling Democratic Party backed Kim's decision. Chief spokesperson Park Sung-joon said in a written briefing that the party respects what he called an agonizing decision, and described it as Kim's resolve not to add to the burden on state affairs.
The main opposition People Power Party said the resignation was long overdue, but that it does not clear Kim of the allegations against him. Party leader Jang Dong-hyeok said Kim was in effect forced out, and argued that President Lee's press conference on Friday fanned the flames of public anger.

## Pres. Office Denies Lee Turned Down Trump's Request for Hormuz Deployment

The presidential office has denied that President Lee Jae Myung had turned down U.S. President Donald Trump's request to send troops to the Strait of Hormuz through the statement Lee made during Friday's press conference .
An official from the top office on Saturday refuted a related report by The New York Times from the previous day, and said Lee's press conference on Friday had not declined Trump's request for Seoul's cooperation toward defusing the crisis in the Middle East.
The official added that Seoul was looking at ways to practically contribute,  protecting the South Korean people and national interest while closely communicating with Washington and others in the international community.
During a nationally televised news conference on Friday, Lee did not rule out the possible deployment of South Korean troops to the region, but cautiously set limits on what Seoul can do, stating that there will be no deployment that involves participation or intervention in the war.
The president had also said that the government was considering ways to strengthen the role of South Korea’s anti-piracy Cheonghae naval unit, which is operating in waters close to the strategic shipping lane.

Amid Washington’s continued calls for Seoul to send troops to the waterway, the South Korean president had said on Friday that the country won’t deploy troops to the region in a way that could draw it into the U.S.-Iran war.
Cho and Rubio agreed to continue their close communication and coordination on North Korea issues, as the South Korean minister highlighted Seoul's resolve to actively support efforts to renew dialogue between Washington and Pyongyang as the "pacemaker."

## AG Committee Apologizes after N. Korean Anthem Played ahead of S. Korea&#039;s Hockey Game

The organizing committee of the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games has apologized to the Korean Sport and Olympic Committee(KSOC) after the national anthem of North Korea was played before a men's hockey group match between South Korea and Bangladesh.
KSOC said on Saturday that two officials from the organizing committee had visited the Team Korea office the previous night to deliver the apology.
The North's national anthem was mistakenly played instead of the South Korean anthem, "Aegukga," before the Group A game held at Gifu Prefectural Green Stadium on Friday.
The South Korean side had demanded an explanation and sent a formal letter of protest to the organizing committee.
KSOC had also called for an official apology, fact-finding and preventive measures.
