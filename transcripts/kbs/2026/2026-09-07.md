## S. Korea, France to Actively Invest in Film, Audiovisual Industries via Lumière Partnership

South Korea and France have agreed to launch a global investment and cooperation initiative for the film and video industry, dubbed the “Lumière Partnership.”
President Lee Jae Myung unveiled the plan in his opening address at the Lumière Summit in the Saint-Paul-de-Vence commune in southern France on Monday.
Through the partnership, Lee said South Korea will invest 800 billion won and France will invest 500 million euros in the film and audiovisual industries over the next five years.
He expressed hope that this partnership will become a beacon of light that illuminates a new future for the global film and video industry.
Citing the sharp decline in the number of moviegoers since the emergence of streaming services, the president stressed the need to gather knowledge to turn this situation into an opportunity for the film industry.
Lee then stressed that ensuring diversity among independent films is also important, saying that while the advancement of artificial intelligence presents opportunities to innovate, it also brings challenges such as copyright concerns and fewer jobs in the industry.
The president promised that South Korea will become a reliable partner in the making of independent films as he stressed the importance of multilateral cooperation to make such films steadily and in greater numbers.
With regard to local and global content, he said South Korea will work to ensure that local and global industry players can coexist and fuel each other’s growth.

## S. Korea, US, Japan Kick Off Annual Freedom Edge Exercise

South Korea, the United States and Japan kicked off their annual Freedom Edge multidomain exercise in waters east and southeast of Jeju Island on Monday.
South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff said Monday that the exercise, which runs through Friday, involves major naval and air assets from the three countries, with six warships, maritime patrol aircraft, fighter jets and aerial refueling aircraft reportedly taking part.
The South Korean Navy is reportedly deploying its seven-thousand-600-ton Aegis destroyers, the ROKS Seoae Ryu Seong-ryong and ROKS Yulgok Yi I, while no U.S. aircraft carrier is expected to participate for the second consecutive year.
The Joint Chiefs said the three countries will operate a joint exercise control group to ensure the drills are conducted systematically and safely, while strengthening their operational and joint response capabilities across multiple domains, including maritime, air and cyber operations.
The Joint Chiefs stressed that the exercise is an annual defensive drill conducted in accordance with international law and norms to improve deterrence and response capabilities against North Korea’s nuclear and missile threats and help safeguard peace and stability in the region.
The Freedom Edge exercise has been held annually since the three countries agreed at the 2023 Camp David summit to conduct regular multidomain exercises, with this year’s drills marking the fourth edition and aimed at enhancing interoperability among the three militaries.
