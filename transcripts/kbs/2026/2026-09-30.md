## Bloomberg: Trump to Announce S. Korea's $54 Bln Investment in Alaska LNG Project

U.S. President Donald Trump is reportedly expected to announce South Korea's planned investments in America, including a 54 billion U.S. dollar pledge in Alaska's liquefied natural gas(LNG) development project.
Bloomberg reported on Tuesday, citing sources familiar with the matter, that Trump is likely to disclose details of Seoul's 200 billion dollar investment package at the White House on Wednesday under an agreement last year aimed at lowering tariffs.
Bloomberg said the LNG development in Alaska would be a part of the investment package.
When Seoul recently briefed the South Korean parliament on its first investment project involving a gas-fired combined-cycle power plant in Texas, it had set aside the LNG project with the commercial viability yet to be assessed.
The LNG project, including a pipeline to transport natural gas from Alaska's North Slope for shipments to Asia, has been strongly pursued by Trump, especially as the state has become a contested battleground ahead of the U.S. midterm elections in November.

## National Average Gas, Diesel Prices Slide Wednesday

The average retail prices of gasoline and diesel at gas stations nationwide continued to fall on Wednesday.
According to the Korea National Oil Corporation’s Opinet price information system, the national average price of a liter of gasoline was one-thousand-858-point-01, or roughly one U.S. dollar and 37 cents, as of 9 a.m., down zero-point-17 won from the previous day.
The average price of diesel also slid zero-point-47 won to one-thousand-843-point-39 won per liter.
In Seoul, the average price of a liter of gasoline slipped zero-point-two won to one-thousand-903-point-75 won, and that of diesel fell zero-point-04 won to one-thousand-886-point-52 won.

## Kim Jong-Un&#039;s Sister Denies N. Korean Mines Caused DMZ Blast, Threatens Retaliation

Kim Yo-jong, the influential sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, has denied South Korea's claim that the North's land mines were the source of a recent explosion inside the demilitarized zone and threatened immediate retaliation against any live rounds fired along the border.
In a statement to the North's state-run Korean Central News Agency on Tuesday, Kim said the explosion had clearly been staged and claimed that its timing and method were similar to that of a wooden-box mine blast that took place more than ten years ago.
Two South Korean soldiers suffered serious injuries in the 2015 explosion, which Seoul concluded that Pyongyang had planted below the Military Demarcation Line.
Kim went on to accuse "armed gangsters" in the South of fabricating groundless investigation results to justify political and military provocation on the peninsula.
The leader's sister claimed that the North Korean military's ongoing work to build barriers along the border is a "thorough exercise of sovereignty unrelated to any military objective," and denied that the regime's soldiers have ever crossed into South Korea.
She threatened to instigate "immediate and merciless" retaliation if South Korea were to conduct live fire along the border and vowed to hold those who'd staged the latest blast accountable.
