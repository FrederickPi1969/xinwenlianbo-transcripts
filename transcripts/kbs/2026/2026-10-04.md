## 2026 Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games End after 16-Day Run; S. Korea Ranks Third in Medal Tally

The 2026 Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, the 20th edition of the quadrennial summer sporting event in Asia, have ended their 16-day run.

## Rain Expected Through Monday Morning, Temperatures to Fall

Rain is expected to spread across much of the nation overnight Sunday and continue into Monday morning, bringing gusty winds, thunderstorms and cooler temperatures.
According to the Korea Meteorological Administration, rain will begin in the greater Seoul area Sunday evening before spreading overnight to other parts of the central region, as well as parts of Jeolla and Gyeongsang Provinces.
The rain is expected to continue through Monday morning, accompanied by strong winds, thunder, lightning and possible hail in some areas.
Rainfall of five to 30 millimeters is forecast for the greater Seoul area and western Gangwon Province, while other regions are expected to receive around five millimeters.
Cloudy conditions are expected to persist after the rain ends Monday, with morning lows ranging from 11 to 17 degrees Celsius, similar to Sunday's levels, with Seoul forecast to record a low of 15 degrees.
Daytime highs will range from 19 to 25 degrees, 3 to 6 degrees lower than on Sunday, while strong winds will make temperatures feel even cooler.

## Data Breaches Spread, Prompting Emergency Regulatory Meeting

South Korean financial regulators called an emergency meeting Sunday after personal data breaches were confirmed at several non-bank financial institutions, widening a cybersecurity crisis that had already affected major commercial banks.
The Financial Services Commission will hold an emergency inspection meeting at 2 p.m., chaired by FSC Chairman Lee Eog-weon, with the CEOs of affected financial institutions and the heads of financial industry associations in attendance.
Yegaram Savings Bank, a Taekwang Group-affiliated savings bank, said Friday that it detected signs of a personal data breach on Wednesday and immediately suspended the affected services.
The bank estimates that about 40-thousand people were affected, with names, dates of birth and contact information among the leaked data. It notified customers of the breach by text message on Saturday, three days after detecting the incident.
At around the same time, a Hyundai Capital website used by loan consultants was also targeted in a cyberattack, resulting in the leakage of the names, resident registration numbers and contact information of 146 mortgage loan agents.
With data breaches now confirmed at savings banks and credit finance companies in the secondary financial sector, following similar incidents at Shinhan, Kookmin, Hana and Busan banks, the entire financial industry is on high alert.

## South Korea Captures Six Golds Ahead of Asian Games Finale

South Korea won six gold medals on the penultimate day of the 2026 Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, claiming titles in soccer, archery, sport climbing, golf, sailing and taekwondo as the competition neared its conclusion.
Archer Lee Woo-seok won the men's individual recurve final on Saturday, defeating Japan's Junya Nakanishi 6-2. South Korea, however, failed to win a gold medal in the women's individual recurve event for the first time.
Fifteen-year-old sailor Choi Ji-un won the boys' dinghy event with 25 points after 12 races, giving South Korea its first Asian Games gold medal in a youth sailing event since the 2014 Incheon Games.
PGA Tour player Kim Joo-hyung captured the men's individual golf title after shooting a six-under 64 in the final round for a four-round total of 18-under 262.
Song Da-bin won gold in the women's over-67-kilogram taekwondo event in her Asian Games debut, while Seo Chae-hyun claimed the women's lead climbing title after becoming the only competitor to complete the route within the time limit.
South Korea's final gold medal came in men's under-23 soccer, as the team defeated Japan 1-0 to secure its fourth consecutive Asian Games title, a streak dating to the 2014 Incheon Games.
South Korea is expected to finish the Games with 39 gold, 43 silver and 69 bronze medals, remaining third behind China and Japan. The total would represent the country's lowest gold-medal haul since it won 28 golds at the 1982 New Delhi Games.

## N. Korea Claims S. Korea, Japan Failed to Track Missile

North Korea claimed Saturday that it conducted a launch drill involving an intermediate-range strategic missile earlier in the day, alleging that South Korean and Japanese military authorities failed to properly track the missile because of its advanced flight capabilities.
Kim Yo-jong, the influential sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, made the claim through the Korean Central News Agency (KCNA) about 12 hours after the launch, saying the strategic weapons drill had been conducted over the East Sea early Saturday.
Kim Yo-jong said an intermediate-range strategic missile was used in the drill and that Kim Jong-un directly observed the exercise.
She also criticized South Korean and Japanese authorities for estimating that the missile flew more than 700 kilometers and about 680 kilometers, respectively, claiming the differing assessments demonstrated their inability to accurately track its flight path.
Kim said North Korea had already observed four similar cases and claimed the superior flight characteristics of its missiles had been demonstrated in practice, adding that the missiles could alter their flight paths at low altitudes and follow a wave-like trajectory, causing adversaries to lose track of them after the initial boost phase.
However, she did not disclose the missile's specific flight distance. South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said it detected a ballistic missile launched from the Wonsan area toward the East Sea at around 6:30 a.m. and assessed that it flew more than 700 kilometers.

## KCNA: Kim Oversees Hypersonic Missile Launch, Vows to Strengthen Nuclear Deterrent

North Korean leader Kim Jong-un has reaffirmed his commitment to strengthening the country's nuclear capabilities, saying the continued testing and improvement of weapons systems constitute an exercise in deterrence.
According to the Korean Central News Agency (KCNA) on Sunday, North Korea conducted a launch drill of an intermediate-range strategic missile toward the East Sea from its eastern region early Saturday under Kim's supervision.
The agency said the drill was aimed at improving missile troops' ability to operate a hypersonic strategic weapons system and assessing the reliability, efficiency and operational readiness of the country's war deterrent.
Kim reportedly praised the missile troops for demonstrating their proficiency and readiness to launch counterattacks at any time, calling for continued efforts to repeatedly demonstrate the reliability and lethality of the country's strategic offensive weapons to its adversaries.
Describing such efforts as a form of exercising deterrence, Kim said they were intended to further strengthen the country's nuclear war deterrent.
The KCNA said the missile was launched in a northeastern direction and struck a designated target about one thousand kilometers away over the East Sea.
Photos released by North Korean state media showed Kim's daughter, Ju-ae, and his wife, Ri Sol-ju, also observing the drill.
