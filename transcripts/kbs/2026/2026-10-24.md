## N. Korean Leader Kim Jong-un Makes Surprise Visit to China

North Korean leader Kim Jong-un has reportedly made a surprise visit to China for the first time in more than a year.
A South Korean government source told Yonhap News Agency on Saturday that Kim arrived in Dandong, in China's northeastern Liaoning Province, via the New Yalu River Bridge.
Earlier, Japan's Kyodo News reported that strict traffic restrictions had been imposed in parts of Dandong, citing diplomatic sources who suggested the measures were linked to a visit by a senior North Korean official.
Kim's unexpected visit comes as U.S. President Donald Trump seeks to resume dialogue with North Korea before the end of the year, ahead of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation(APEC) summit in Shenzhen.
The trip also coincides with recent social media rumors regarding Kim's death.
The New Yalu River Bridge, connecting North Korea's Sinuiju with China's Dandong, officially opened on October 15, more than a decade after its completion in 2014.

## S. Korea's Gasoline Prices Fall for 23rd Consecutive Week

South Korea’s pump prices extended their downward trend into a 23rd consecutive week, though the decrease was only marginal.
According to the Korea National Oil Corporation's oil price information service, Opinet, on Saturday, the national average price of gasoline was one-thousand-857-point-one won per liter in the third week of October, down zero-point-five won from the previous week.
Seoul recorded the highest average price at one-thousand-902-point-three won per liter, while Daegu had the lowest at one-thousand-828 won.
The average price of diesel also edged down zero-point-three won to one-thousand-842-point-five won per liter.
Meanwhile, global oil prices rose this week amid growing supply concerns fueled by escalating tensions in the Middle East.
The benchmark Dubai crude price climbed two U.S. dollars and 90 cents from the previous week to 111 dollars and ten cents per barrel.
Changes in international oil prices typically take two to three weeks to affect domestic pump prices.
The government has maintained price caps on petroleum products to cushion the impact of fluctuations in global oil prices.

## IAEA Chief Forecasts Global Nuclear Power Demand to More than Triple by 2060

The head of the International Atomic Energy Agency(IAEA) forecasts that global demand for nuclear power will more than triple by 2060, spurred by advancements in artificial intelligence(AI) and the construction of data centers.
The IAEA’s 2025 annual report, submitted to the UN General Assembly by Director General Rafael Grossi on Friday, raised global nuclear power generation estimates for the fifth consecutive year.
Under the high-growth scenario, the IAEA projected the world's nuclear power operating capacity to reach 992 gigawatts by 2050, or two-point-six times the capacity in 2024.
In the long term, the IAEA expected global power demand to more than triple by 2060.
Grossi projected that small modular reactors will make up 28 percent of future capacity, driven by big tech's search for stable power to fuel AI data centers
The IAEA chief, meanwhile, expressed deep regret regarding North Korea's continued expansion of its nuclear program outside the agency's supervision and control in violation of UN Security Council resolutions.

## S. Korea Convenes Security Officials from 7 Regions to Boost Overseas Korean Protection Network

South Korea invited security officials from seven regions this week to strengthen its network to protect citizens abroad.
The Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced on Saturday that eight officials from the Netherlands, East Timor, Malaysia, Mexico, Guam, Vietnam, and Cambodia participated in the year's second "Friends Korea" event.
The participants include those who cooperated with Seoul in protecting South Korean nationals during the 2026 FIFA World Cup co-hosted by the United States, Canada and Mexico, and others involved in Seoul's response to transnational online scams.
The security officials visited the ministry to receive a briefing on the 24-hour operation of the overseas safety situation room and consular safety call center, and to exchange ideas on bolstering cooperation.
They also toured one of the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency's emergency call centers and the immigration inspection and security systems at Incheon International Airport.

## Won Yoo-min Elected to APC Athletes' Committee

In a major boost to South Korea's sports diplomacy, Won Yoo-min, second vice-chair of the International Paralympic Committee(IPC) Athletes' Council, has been elected to the Athletes' Committee of the Asian Paralympic Committee(APC).
The Korea Paralympic Committee announced on Saturday that Won and four other representatives from Uzbekistan, Japan, China, and Hong Kong were elected as new members.
The election concluded on Friday on the sidelines of the 2026 Aichi-Nagoya Asian Para Games.
The 38-year-old wheelchair rugby player and para nordic skier's term on the committee runs until 2030.
The APC Athletes' Committee advocates for athletes with disabilities across Asia, and works to reflect their voices in the development of para sports.
Won, who immigrated to Canada and represented the country in wheelchair basketball at the 2016 Rio Paralympics, restored his South Korean nationality and played for Team Korea in nordic skiing at the Winter Paralympics in 2022 and 2026.
He was elected the second vice-chair of the IPC Athletes' Council in 2024.

## VOA: US Notifies N. Korea of Court-Ordered Compensation for USS Pueblo Crew

The United States has notified North Korea of a U.S. court ruling ordering Pyongyang to compensate crew members of a U.S. Navy vessel captured and released by the regime in 1968.
According to Voice of America(VOA) on Friday, the State Department informed the District Court for the District of Columbia on October 14 that it had delivered relevant court documents to the North's permanent mission to the United Nations in New York on August 8.
Pyongyang seized the USS Pueblo intelligence vessel in waters off the eastern coast of the Korean Peninsula on January 23, 1968, and released the 82 surviving crew members — along with the body of a sailor who died during the ordeal — eleven months later.
The North, however, did not return the vessel and it is currently displayed along the Pothong River in Pyongyang.
The crew members were subject to months of abuse and torture in captivity, and many complained of mental distress following their release.
The U.S. federal court ordered the North to pay 400 million U.S. dollars in damages to 15 crew members and relatives.
This follows two earlier cases that awarded victims and families 97 million dollars and two-point-31 billion dollars, respectively.
VOA stated that although the North is unlikely to pay the damages, the victims will be able to file for compensation through the State Department's Victims of State Sponsored Terrorism Fund and seek to seize the regime's frozen overseas assets.

## Expert Voices Uncertainty Over N. Korea&#039;s Growth Sustainability

Despite experiencing its best economic conditions under leader Kim Jong-un, North Korea may still struggle to generate sustainable long-term growth, according to a U.S. expert on the region's economy.
Troy Stangarone said Friday at the North Korea Economic Forum in Washington that the North Korean regime is experiencing its best economic period since Kim took power in 2011, and perhaps even the best period of his life.
He cited the North's military cooperation with Russia amid the war in Ukraine, expanded economic cooperation with China, and increased revenue from cybercrime as factors behind the country's economic boom.
Stangarone projected that the revenue from cooperation with Russia would not immediately decline even if the war in Ukraine ends, and that Pyongyang would be able to generate money for two to three more years with Moscow seeking rearmament.
The expert noted that the key question is how Kim will utilize the favorable environment to build a sustainable economic structure, adding that the regime's chronic energy shortages remain a primary constraint on industrial growth.

## Lee Vows to Push for Life in Prison Without Parole for Violent Offenders

President Lee Jae-myung has vowed to expedite the implementation of life imprisonment without parole for violent offenders and establish a special police unit to patrol near victims' homes.
Lee posted the pledge on social media following a pan-government meeting on Friday, which brought together relevant ministries and agencies to discuss policy responses to growing public safety concerns amid a rise in violent crime.
The president promised to reinforce a response system that is centered on preventing violence through early detection.
According to chief presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung, the National Police Agency had briefed the president on launching a special unit to patrol areas surrounding the homes of victims of violence.
The justice ministry said it would actively push to introduce life without the possibility of parole to permanently isolate offenders of heinous crimes from society, and to better protect the rights of victims and their families.

## Lee, German Chancellor Merz Set for Seoul Summit Next Week

President Lee Jae Myung and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz will meet in Seoul next week for a summit that's expected to focus on cooperation in trade, people-to-people exchanges and future industries, including semiconductors, artificial intelligence and defense.
The two leaders are slated to meet at the presidential office on Thursday afternoon for talks, followed by a joint press announcement and an official dinner, according to briefing by chief presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung.
The summit is part of the German chancellor's two-day official visit to South Korea, timed to coincide with his attendance at the 19th Asia-Pacific Conference of German Business in Seoul.
It will mark Merz's first visit to the country as chancellor.
The spokesperson emphasized that Germany is South Korea's largest European trading partner and a key partner for cooperation, particularly for South Korean companies with regional headquarters on the continent.
Lee and Merz previously met on the sidelines of the G20 summit in November 2025 and the G7 summit in June this year.
