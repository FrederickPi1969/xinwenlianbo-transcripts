## Gov’t to Decide within November How Much to Compensate Oil Refiners Hit by Price Caps

The government plans to notify oil refiners this month of how much compensation they will receive for losses incurred due to the oil price cap system.
According to the government on Monday, the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources plans to wrap up its calculations for the first settlement by the end of the month and notify the refiners of the results.
The period covered by the first settlement runs from March 13, when the price cap system was introduced, through June 30.
The companies can raise objections to the proposed amounts within 30 days of being notified.
The government is aiming to pay compensation within the year, but payment could be delayed as oil refiners disagree with its calculation methods.
In June, when it disclosed draft regulations on the subject, the government said it would base compensation on production costs.
​Those costs include crude import expenses, such as purchase prices for crude oil and petroleum products, transportation and insurance, along with production and sales expenses like depreciation, labor costs, fuel and domestic distribution costs.
The government had also said it could consider a reasonable profit margin.
However, the industry wants the amounts calculated on the basis of the Mean of Platts Singapore, which serves as the benchmark for refined petroleum product prices in the Asia-Pacific region.

## 4 Students Caught Cheating on Middle, High School Exams with Smart Glasses

Four cases of cheating using smart glasses were uncovered this year at middle and high schools across the country.
The Ministry of Education revealed on Monday that four students had been caught cheating on major school assessments using artificial intelligence-enabled smart glasses, according to the results of a nationwide survey.
One of the students was in middle school, and the others were in high school.
In one of the cases, a student tipped off staff about the cheating, and the cheater was caught switching out their regular glasses for smart glasses during the exam.
All four students received zeroes in accordance with academic performance regulations.
The three high schools also imposed separate disciplinary actions, such as suspension, in line with their respective school policies.
The ministry said it plans to strengthen student evaluation management to deter cheating via smart glasses and ensure the integrity of school record assessments.
With the all-important Suneung, or College Scholastic Ability Test, just ten days away, the ministry has assigned a dedicated agent to each of the approximately 13-hundred test sites nationwide to screen for smart glasses.
During the Suneung, simply possessing smart glasses will result in the student’s exam being invalidated, while wearing or using them will also bar the student from taking the test next year.

## Gov&#039;t to Conduct Simulation Drill to Tackle High Concentrations of Ultrafine Dust

This will be based on the scenario that the concentration of ultrafine particulate matter in the nation stood at over 150 micrograms per cubic meter for more than two hours and is set to exceed 75 micrograms per cubic meter the next day.

They will also inspect the reduction in operating hours at public places of business, illegal emissions from these sites and the operation of street sweeper vehicles.
The joint drill comes as the government is set to implement its seasonal fine dust management efforts from next month.

## Obesity Rates Hit 50% for South Korean Men in 30s and 40s

New analysis revealed that half of South Korean men in their 30s and 40s are obese.
The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) on Monday released an in-depth analysis of adult obesity and health behaviors, using data from the 2025 Community Health Survey, which shows that one in three adult citizens is obese.
The obesity rate has increased by 26-point-nine percent over the past decade, according to the data.
South Korea defines obesity as a body mass index(BMI) of 25 or higher.
The obesity rate among men was 42-point-five percent, nearly twice as high as the rate among women, which stood at 22-point-nine percent.
The rate was highest among men in their 30s and 40s, at 52-point-one percent and 53-point-one percent, respectively. Women in their 70s had the highest rate at 27.4 percent.
The analysis highlighted poor sleep, skipping breakfast and a lack of strength training as habits linked to obesity. A lack of regular walking and habitual drinking were also associated.
KDCA Commissioner Im Seung-gwan emphasized the necessity of adopting healthier lifestyles, warning that obesity significantly raises the risk of chronic conditions like hypertension and diabetes.

## Samsung Lions Reclaim Korea Series Title after 12 Yrs.

The Samsung Lions have reclaimed the Korea Series championship title after 12 years.
The Lions defeated KT Wiz 12-1 in Game Five of the Korean Series at Daegu Samsung Lions Park on Sunday, their fourth win in the best-of-seven series.
It is the Lions' first Korea Series title since 2014 and their ninth overall.
The Lions finished the regular season in second place after the Wiz, becoming the first No. 2 team to win the Korea Series championship following the SK Wyverns' victory in 2018.
After dominating the regular season, the Wiz had high hopes of winning the Korea Series title for the first time in five years, but fell short of that goal.

## Committee Chief: Existing OPCON Transfer Conditions Remain in Place

The chair of the National Assembly's National Defense Committee has confirmed that the existing agreement between South Korea and the United States on the conditions of the transfer of wartime operational control (OPCON) remains unchanged.
Committee chair and ruling Democratic Party Rep. Jin Sung-joon, who had just returned from a trip to the U.S. Pacific Command along with a parliamentary delegation, said Sunday that he had conveyed Seoul's strong commitment to OPCON transfer to the nation.
Jin said that although Washington recognized that certain policy factors could affect the process, the two sides confirmed that their existing agreement on a conditions-based OPCON transfer remains unchanged.
Jin said the U.S. side explained that the October 28 postponement of the allies' annual Security Consultative Meeting to next year was unrelated to any differences over the OPCON transfer, but was instead due to scheduling considerations involving the two countries' defense ministers and military leadership.
The remarks come after United States Forces Korea Commander Gen. Xavier Brunson said during a seminar in Washington last month that “conditions have changed” regarding the OPCON and that the allies now have “new standards” to uphold.
