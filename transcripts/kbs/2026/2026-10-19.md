The association’s remarks come hours after the health ministry announced it would issue an advance notice of the revisions from Monday to November 9.

## USFK Chief Stirs Sovereignty Controversy in S. Korea with DMZ Remarks

Gen. Xavier Brunson, commander of U.S. Forces Korea and the United Nations Command, has sparked controversy for saying South Korea begins at the southern boundary of the Demilitarized Zone(DMZ), an assertion critics argue disregards the ally’s sovereign territory.
Brunson made the remark during a seminar hosted by the Gold Institute for International Strategy in Washington on Wednesday of last week as he was discussing the UN Command’s jurisdiction in the DMZ.
Brunson told the audience that when you look at the military demarcation line(MDL), which is the actual border between South and North Korea, tactical fence lines are located two-thousand-500 meters north and two-thousand-500 meters south of the MDL, with the UN Command managing the area up to the southern line.
Brunson said that in his mind, through the lens of the UN Command, South Korea begins at that southern fence line.
Referring to South Korea by its official name, he said, “That’s where the Republic of Korea begins because I administer that area in order to maintain a state of armistice.”
The DMZ was established under the 1953 Korean War Armistice Agreement as a military buffer between the two Koreas.
However, the area from the MDL to the southern limit line in the southern half of the DMZ includes South Korean villages such as Daeseong-dong.
This issue has reignited concerns over whether the UN Command’s authority is being equated with the legal boundaries of South Korean territory.
Critics argue that the authority granted to the UN Command by the armistice agreement is limited to DMZ access permits and administration, without infringing on territorial sovereignty.
On Sunday, the UN Command issued a statement saying, “The Republic of Korea’s sovereignty is not in question.”
It explained that Brunson’s remarks described the responsibilities the armistice agreement assigns to the UN Command commander in the DMZ, including military and civilian administration as well as civil relief in the portion of the DMZ south of the MDL.
The UN Command said those responsibilities are “military and administrative,” stressing that they do not alter and were not intended to address the sovereignty of South Korea.
It said the UN Command remains committed to strengthening the alliance and to the defense of the South Korean people.
Seoul’s defense ministry said it views Brunson’s remark as a “personal opinion” and that the government’s stance on territorial sovereignty remains firm.
Brunson has also expressed what has been interpreted as pushback over the timing of Seoul’s move to retake wartime operational control from Washington, citing changed conditions driven by North Korea’s advancing military capabilities.
The growing rift between the allies in recent months appears to be spreading across broader security issues.
