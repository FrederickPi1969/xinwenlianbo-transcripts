## Cryptocurrency Tax Delayed Again, Now Set for 2029

The government has decided to delay taxation on cryptocurrency income by another two years, pushing its implementation to 2029 from next year as originally scheduled.
Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il announced the decision at a briefing Thursday, saying the government would postpone the January 1, 2027, start date by two years, marking the fourth delay since introducing the tax system in 2020.
Under the original plan, annual cryptocurrency gains exceeding two-point-five million won, or nearly 19-hundred U.S. dollars, will be subject to a 22 percent tax, including the local income tax.
The government said the latest delay is partly because exchanges of information with the United States on cryptocurrency transactions are not scheduled to begin until 2029, warning that immediate taxation could put South Korea’s market at a disadvantage compared with overseas markets.
It also cited expected changes to the digital asset regulatory framework under the proposed Digital Asset Act, saying an additional two years would allow more time to build the necessary tax infrastructure, particularly for overseas transactions.
The government also plans to allow investors to carry forward losses for up to three years and deduct them from gains generated during that period.

## Financial Authorities to Inspect Cybersecurity at 580 Financial Firms

Financial authorities will conduct comprehensive inspections of about 580 financial institutions, electronic financial service providers and virtual asset operators, focusing on external access points to all IT assets and internal security controls.

## Bill to Probe Fabricated Indictments Passes Standing Committee

A bill to appoint a special counsel to investigate allegations of fabricated indictments under the previous Yoon Suk Yeol administration has passed a parliamentary standing committee.
The Legislation and Judiciary Committee, led by the ruling Democratic Party, approved the bill on Wednesday, triggering a walkout by the main opposition People Power Party, which argued that the legislation is designed to scrap criminal indictments against President Lee Jae Myung.
The bill seeks to investigate claims that the Prosecution Service, the National Intelligence Service, and the Board of Audit and Inspection abused their powers and targeted the opposition under the previous administration.
The ruling party, however, deleted a provision that could be interpreted as giving the special counsel the power to cancel indictments.
Although the bill is expected to come to a plenary vote on Thursday, the proceedings may be stalled by an opposition filibuster.

## N. Korea Reports New Air Defense System Test, Live-Fire Cruise Missile Training

​North Korea claims it test-fired a new air defense missile and staged a live-fire strike drill using long-range strategic cruise missiles.
The state-run Korean Central News Agency(KCNA) on Thursday reported that the Missile Administration carried out the missile test the previous day to verify the "technical specifications" of the new air defense missile system.
The test was overseen by Kim Jong-sik, a member of the Central Military Commission, and confirmed the reliability of the projectile's multiple-guided mode and the system's target identification, tracking capability and information processing.
According to the report, the same day a subunit of the North's eastern cruise missile unit a conducted a live-fire drill as an extension of efforts to boost the technical advancement of combat systems and reliability of strategic weapons.
South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said Wednesday it had detected multiple missiles fired from the North's Sondok area in South Hamgyong Province toward the East Sea.
