## N. Korea Remains Silent on Latest Missile Launches

North Korea remained silent on its latest launch of multiple short‑range ballistic missiles into the East Sea, with state media making no mention of the launch as of Sunday morning, a day after it occurred.
North Korean outlets, including the Korean Central News Agency for external audiences and the Rodong Sinmun for domestic readers, had not reported on the launch.
The North has now refrained from reporting on all four of its ballistic missile launches into the East Sea over the past month, including launches on August 6, 12 and 20.
The country typically reports missile launches through state media early the following morning, describing them as weapons tests or missile test‑firings, and uses the reports to tout its military capabilities and rally domestic support, particularly when leader Kim Jong‑un observes the launches.
While North Korea sometimes does not report launches when they are routine drills or technical checks with limited military or propaganda value, its continued silence on the latest four launches is considered unusual, as all were detected by South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff and publicly reported.
The latest launch came 23 days after the previous one on Aug. 20 and was North Korea's 13th ballistic missile launch of the year.

## S. Korean Container Ship Arrives in Britain via Arctic Route in 21 Days

The PanStar Acro, the first South Korean container ship to undertake a trial voyage through the Arctic shipping route, arrived in Britain on Saturday after sailing across the Arctic Ocean.
The Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries said on Saturday that the two-thousand-758‑TEU container ship entered the Port of Felixstowe in Britain at around 5 a.m. Korea time, 21 days after leaving Busan on August 22.
The vessel is expected to arrive in Rotterdam, the Netherlands, on Sunday and make a port call in Gdansk, Poland, on Wednesday before returning to South Korea on October 12.
The Arctic shipping route has drawn attention as the shortest maritime link between Asia and Europe amid rapidly shrinking sea ice due to climate change, and the government said data and experience from the trial voyage will help South Korea develop the route.
President Lee Jae Myung also congratulated the captain and crew, calling their voyage the opening of a historic new route and asking the public to wish them a safe return and success in developing Arctic shipping routes.

## Gender Equality Minister Nominee Withdraws from Consideration amid Controversy

Yong Hye-in, President Lee Jae Myung's nominee for gender equality minister, withdrew from consideration on Sunday, 14 days after being nominated.
Yong, the sole lawmaker of the minor Basic Income Party, announced her decision at a press conference at the National Assembly, saying she regretted being unable to fulfill the responsibilities entrusted to her and would now focus on her parliamentary duties.
She said the ruling Democratic Party conveyed that it respected her situation and that of her party but asked her to step down, citing a lack of broad public support for her simultaneously holding both parliamentary and ministerial positions.
Yong had faced pressure to resign from the opposition and from some members of the ruling party over controversy surrounding her concurrent service as a proportional‑representation lawmaker and ministerial nominee, as well as allegations involving her husband's appointment to a post within the Basic Income Party.
Under current law, lawmakers may serve concurrently as Cabinet members, though proportional‑representation lawmakers have customarily given up their seats when joining the Cabinet.
Yong said she ultimately concluded it would be difficult to serve as a Cabinet member while concerns persisted within the ruling party, adding that stepping down was also necessary to maintain solidarity within the broader progressive camp.
She became the second sitting lawmaker to withdraw as a ministerial nominee, following former Democratic Party lawmaker Kang Sun‑woo, who also voluntarily stepped down as the nominee for the gender equality minister post.

## Jobs for 20‑Somethings Decline at Fastest Pace Since 1998

The number of employed people in their 20s has fallen by the largest margin since the 1998 Asian financial crisis.
According to the Korean Statistical Information Service, the monthly average number of employed people in their 20s stood at three million 279-thousand from January through August, down 193-thousand from a year earlier.
The decline was the largest for the eight-month period since 1998, when employment in the age group fell by 550-thousand and exceeded the decreases recorded during the global financial crisis in 2009 and the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.
Employment among people in their 20s has dropped every year since 2023, with the annual decline widening from 91-thousand in 2023 to more than 100-thousand in 2024, 177-thousand last year, and more than 190-thousand this year.
Even accounting for the shrinking population, the employment situation remained weak, with the average population of people in their 20s falling three-and-a-half percent to five-point-55 million during the period, while employment dropped five-point-six percent, pushing the employment rate down one-point-three percentage points to 59-point-one percent – below 60 percent for the first time since 2021.

## Korea Exchange to Extend Trading Until 8 pm

Starting Monday, investors will be able to buy and sell stocks in real time on the Korea Exchange until 8 p.m., four hours after the regular trading session ends.
The exchange said Sunday it will operate an after-hours market from 4 to 8 p.m., allowing trading in a wider range of stocks than the alternative platform NEXTRADE.
Previously, orders were collected and matched at a single price every 10 minutes between 4 and 6 p.m. Under the new system, trades will be executed in real time whenever buy and sell orders match.
The change extends trading by two hours compared with the exchange's single-price after-hours session, which ran from 4 to 6 p.m.
Eligible securities include stocks listed on the KOSPI and KOSDAQ markets, while management-designated stocks, investment-warning and investment-risk stocks, and shares that saw no trading during the regular session will be excluded.
Exchange‑traded funds and exchange‑traded notes will also be excluded due to concerns that after‑hours trading could increase market volatility, and the same 30 percent daily price limit and volatility‑interruption measures used during regular trading will apply.

## Lee Chang-dong Wins Venice Jury Grand Prize for 'Possible Love'

Director Lee Chang-dong has won the Silver Lion Grand Jury Prize, the second-highest honor, at the Venice International Film Festival for his new film "Possible Love."
The film, starring Jeon Do-yeon, Sol Kyung-gu, Zo In-sung and Cho Yeo-jeong, was named the winner of the prize at the closing ceremony of the 83rd Venice International Film Festival on Saturday.
It marks the first time a South Korean film has won an award in the festival's competition section since Kim Ki-duk's "Pieta" won the Golden Lion in 2012 and the first Jury Grand Prize for a Korean film.
The award also marks Lee's return to the Venice winners' circle 24 years after he won the Silver Lion for best director with "Oasis" in 2002.
In his acceptance speech, Lee said he made the film to ask what cinema could and should do in an era when labor is disappearing and relationships between people are breaking down, adding that he would take the award as a sign that he should continue asking those questions.
"Possible Love," Lee's first film in eight years since "Burning" and his first Netflix project, follows a laid-off worker couple and a documentary filmmaker couple who meet and confront their different lives and hidden desires.
