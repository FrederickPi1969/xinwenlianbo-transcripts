## S. Korea, US Have Yet to Set Date for Security Consultative Meeting

South Korea and the United States have yet to set a date for this year’s Security Consultative Meeting, according to South Korean military sources on Wednesday.
The sources said South Korea proposed holding the annual gathering in mid-November as in previous years, but the U.S. has yet to give an answer.
They said talks on setting a date have failed to make progress as Washington maintains that necessary conditions for the second phase of the verification process for the OPCON transfer — transferring wartime operational control from Washington to Seoul — have not yet been met.
The three-stage process entails evaluating and verifying initial operational capability, full operational capability and full mission capability.
The defense ministry in Seoul said no date has been set for the meeting due to the political calendars in both nations.
In a statement on Wednesday, the ministry said it is consulting with the U.S. Department of War and the U.S. Joint Chiefs of Staff, taking into account factors such as mutually acceptable schedules.

## Top Court Overturns Reduced Sentence for Shaman in Fatal Exorcism

The Supreme Court has overturned an appeals court ruling that reduced an 81-year-old shaman’s sentence to seven years for causing her niece’s death during an exorcism ritual involving burning charcoal.
According to legal sources on Wednesday, the top court quashed the ruling and sent the case back to the Seoul High Court on September 23.
The shaman, surnamed Shim, was originally indicted on murder charges, and a court found her guilty and sentenced her to life in prison.
But the appellate court overturned the murder conviction and handed down a seven-year sentence for causing death by injury, ruling that the prosecution had failed to prove intent to kill.
Shim and others tied the woman, in her 30s, to a metal structure at a restaurant in Incheon in September 2024 and exposed her to intense heat from burning charcoal for three hours to drive out an evil spirit.
They continued the ritual despite the victim repeatedly losing consciousness and called emergency services later, but the woman died two days later from multiple organ failure caused by burns.
In delivering its ruling, the Supreme Court said the defendants were aware of the real risk of death and that their intention to drive out an evil spirit could not rule out murderous intent.

## Presidential Committee Unveils 5-Year Demographic Plan

## Seoul City to Boost Express Subway, Bus Services to Ease Morning Rush

Starting next month, Seoul Subway Line 9 will increase its morning rush-hour express services to better serve commuters.
The Seoul Metropolitan Government announced the change on Wednesday as part of improvements to public transportation in the capital.
From 5:40 a.m. to 7 a.m., Line 9 will run two express trains for every one local train from VHS Medical Center Station toward Gaehwa Station, replacing the current one-to-one ratio.
The peak train deployment time during the morning hours will be moved up from 6:30 a.m. to 5:40 a.m., and the peak operation time during the evening commute will be pushed up by half an hour to 4 p.m. until 6:30 p.m.
Accordingly, the number of trains operating during the morning rush will increase by eight, shortening dispatch intervals.
Congestion during the morning and evening commutes is expected to decrease by up to 20-point-four percentage points, which is equivalent to adding 24 more trains.
The city will also pilot trunk express buses that stop only at major downtown points, while preserving existing service in the outskirts, cutting travel time by about ten percent.
The pilot service will affect four routes including Seoul Blue Bus No. 150 from Dobong Garage to City Hall Station, Bus No. 370 from Gangdong Garage to Chungjeongno Station, Bus No. 470 from Sangam Garage to City Hall Station, and Bus No. 605 from Gangseo Garage to Yeongdeungpo Station.

## White House: S. Korea to Invest 54 Billion in Alaska LNG Project

The United States said South Korea would invest 54 billion U.S. dollars in an Alaska liquefied natural gas(LNG) development project as part of the trade deal the two sides reached last year.
White House Press Secretary Katie Zacharia said during her first news briefing on Tuesday that a 200-billion-dollar South Korean energy investment package that U.S. President Donald Trump announced last month was an "amazing deal."
Zacharia said the package includes up to 120 billion dollars to build eight large-scale nuclear power plants, and that 54 billion dollars are earmarked for the Alaska LNG pipeline and terminal to serve the Pacific Rim.
She also cited a previously announced 22-point-three billion investment in a six-gigawatt Texas natural gas facility.
The spokesperson said the investment package was part of Trump's election pledge and that the president had once again made good on a promise.
The South Korean government, for its part, has only confirmed its participation in the construction of the power plant in Texas. It is still reviewing the other two projects.
