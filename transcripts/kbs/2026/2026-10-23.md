## S. Korea Sends Letter to UNSC President regarding Sept. 21 DMZ Blast

South Korea has sent a letter to the president of the United Nations Security Council(UNSC) regarding a recent North Korean mine blast inside the Demilitarized Zone and Pyongyang's tension-escalating acts near the Military Demarcation Line(MDL).
The Permanent Mission to the UN said on Thursday that Ambassador Cha Ji-hoon delivered a two-page letter to the envoy from Greece, which currently holds the council presidency.
Seoul said a joint investigation with the UN Command had found mines planted by Pyongyang on the south side of the MDL caused the blast on September 21, injuring three South Korean soldiers, and that it is a violation of the Korean War Armistice Agreement.
The letter also demanded Pyongyang apologize, take responsible measures, and engage in talks aimed at preventing an escalation of tensions and restoring trust between the two Koreas.
According to a South Korean government official, the UNSC president has since approved circulation of the letter among the council member states and it is likely to be posted on the council website.
The letter comes after the North threatened to resume anti-Seoul loudspeaker broadcasts and claimed that the South was moving to infringe on its security interests regarding its ongoing work to "fortify" the southern border.
