## S. Korea to Cut Middle East Oil Dependence to 50% by 2035, Expand Reserves

The government plans to reduce its reliance on crude oil from any particular region to 50 percent or less by 2035 while expanding oil storage facilities.
The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources on Wednesday held a meeting chaired by Minister Kim Jung-kwan and finalized four agenda items, including the first basic plan for resource security.
The plan is the first governmentwide mid- to long-term strategy for resource security since the Special Act on National Resource Security took effect in February last year.
Under the plan, the government aims to reduce dependence on Middle Eastern crude, which accounted for 70 percent of imports last year, to 50 percent or less by 2035, while also cutting reliance on any single country for natural gas below 30 percent.
It also plans to expand oil storage capacity by 20 million barrels by 2030, increasing government reserves and jointly held international stockpiles, and secure additional condensate to support stable domestic naphtha production.
The number of designated critical minerals will increase from 38 to 51, as the government plans to add germanium, phosphorus, fluorspar and ten rare earth elements, to meet rising demand from high-tech industries such as semiconductors and batteries.

## Gov’t Drops 2-Line Escalator Campaign, Focuses on Safety Rules

The government will no longer promote or enforce a uniform “stand on both sides” policy on escalators, instead focusing on three key safety rules, including holding the handrail.
The Ministry of the Interior and Safety said Wednesday that it will implement follow-up measures based on the results of a public discussion held at Yonsei University on August 29, attended by more than 200 citizens and experts.
During the discussion, experts explained that people form one or two lines at a similar rate, and said individual movement efficiency does not necessarily coincide with the efficiency of all users.
Reflecting the outcome of the discussion, the ministry plans to revise its first Basic Plan for Elevator Safety Management to focus on three key safety rules and a voluntary safety culture instead of promoting standing on both sides of escalators.
The government will no longer pursue a uniform campaign or regulations on forming two lines, but may deploy public workers or volunteers to help ensure safety on long escalators or in crowded areas.
The ministry will promote holding the handrail, not walking or running, and staying behind the safety line through public service announcements, short videos, and expanded safety education for children and older adults.
