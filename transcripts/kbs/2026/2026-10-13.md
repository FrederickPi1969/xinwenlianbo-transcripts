## AI-Based System to Identify Wild Animals Captured on Camera in Nat’l Parks

The Korea National Park Service has developed an AI-based system to identify the nation’s wildlife species.
The agency announced on Tuesday that it expects the new system to drastically reduce the time spent analyzing images of wild animals in national parks, handling 100-thousand images in just eight hours instead of 208 days.
The system automatically identifies 17 species of mammals captured on motion-sensor cameras, including Asiatic black bears, foxes, otters, wildcats, roe deer and wild boars.
Some 13-hundred motion-sensor cameras installed in national parks across the country gather some four-point-seven million images on average every year.
The Korea National Park Service plans to further refine the system and make public its AI model to enable both domestic and foreign research institutes and organizations to use it to analyze images of mammals.

## All 9 Cube Satellites Deployed by Nuri Rocket Make Contact with Ground Stations

All nine CubeSats deployed into orbit during the Nuri rocket’s fifth launch have communicated with ground stations.
The Korea AeroSpace Administration and the Korea Aerospace Research Institute announced on Tuesday that all nine secondary payload satellites that entered their target orbit made contact and were undergoing checks ahead of their missions.
On Wednesday of last week, the rocket successfully deployed nine of the ten domestically developed CubeSats that it was carrying.
The one that failed to deploy was PERSAT-02, developed by the South Korean company Quaternion.
Of the nine satellites, six of them, including Space Lintec’s BEE-1012 and Chosun University’s orbital verification satellite for space optical communications, achieved two-way contact less than two hours after liftoff.
The remaining three, including KAIST’s GBSat, sent status signals or data to ground stations, confirming they were in good condition.
Each satellite is scheduled to begin its full mission after completing basic status checks and an initial operational phase to ensure all systems are functioning properly.
The space agency said in the fifth Nuri launch, contact was established with the CubeSats more quickly compared to the last because the satellites were placed into their target orbits more precisely, with more accurate positioning data, pointing to advancement in their space tech capabilities.

Brunson pointed out that the allies conduct two big training exercises every year in South Korea focused on large command posts and said, “We’ve got to move away from that."

Brunson added that he and Gen. Kim Seoung-min, Deputy Commander of Combined Forces Command, discussed training differently to "operate in a distributed fashion" and avoid becoming fixed targets.

"What would it look like if we trained from Jeju all the way up to Gyeonggi Province in the north? Imagine training across the entirety of the peninsula and reaching a point where we could say: "You cannot use the lessons you learned on the battlefield in Ukraine against us, because we learned the same lessons and distributed ourselves in a way that does not make us a target." We will not be fixed in one place. That is what I mean by positional advantage."
A South Korean military source said that Brunson’s comments emphasize the need to implement additional distributed training methods rather than altering existing large-scale command post exercises.
