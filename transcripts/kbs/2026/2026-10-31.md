## S. Korea Pledges Nationwide Support for World Youth Day 2027 in Seoul

South Korea's National Assembly, the government and the Catholic Church have pledged to work together to ensure the success of World Youth Day 2027 in Seoul.
The event, organized by the Catholic Church every two to three years, brings together young people from around the world to promote unity, peace and hope.
At a ceremony held at the National Assembly on Saturday, lawmakers, government officials and Catholic Church leaders adopted a joint declaration pledging nationwide support for the event.
The declaration calls for a government-wide support system, legislative and budgetary assistance, and close cooperation with local governments and the private sector.
The participants said the event would provide an opportunity for young people to reflect on universal values while enhancing South Korea's international standing.
Archbishop Peter Chung Soon-taick said the gathering would help young people share their concerns and find hope.
Pope Leo XIV is expected to make his first visit to South Korea for the event, with KBS serving as the host broadcaster.

## N. Korea Reports Outcome of Ministerial Talks with Visiting Swiss Official

North Korea reported on the outcome of ministerial talks with Switzerland's top diplomat, who visited Pyongyang this week to mark the opening of the country's diplomatic office.
The state-run Korean Central News Agency said on Saturday that North Korean Foreign Minister Choe Son-hui and Swiss Federal Councillor Ignazio Cassis discussed enhancing the two sides' diplomatic dialogue and cooperation the previous day.
They also exchanged views on a range of global issues in what the news agency described as a "friendly atmosphere."
Cassis wrote on X that he and his North Korean counterpart discussed the new diplomatic office, ways to normalize the two sides' political relations, and wider security issues on the Korean Peninsula and across Eurasia.
The Swiss official also met with Jo Yong-won, secretary of the ruling Workers' Party's Central Committee, who is known to be one of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un's closest aides.
After attending an opening ceremony for the diplomatic office on Friday, Cassis traveled to South Korea for talks with National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac and Vice Foreign Minister for Strategy and Intelligence Jeong Yeon-doo.

## N. Korea Slams Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team, Fresh Sanctions

North Korea protested a statement by a multilateral mechanism to monitor and report violation and evasion of UN Security Council(UNSC) sanctions on the North that called for continued enforcement of sanctions against the regime's nuclear and weapons programs.
A spokesperson from the North's foreign ministry said in a statement on Saturday that the joint call by the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team and the European Union(EU) is an "ill-intended conspiratorial racket" to justify hostile policies of the U.S. and its followers.
The spokesperson accused the monitoring team and the EU of infringing upon its sovereignty and said their move was a "strategic failure" and that their sanctions and pressure would have no impact on its status as a nuclear weapons state.
On Wednesday, eleven countries participating in the monitoring and the EU released the joint statement, reaffirming their commitment toward the North's denuclearization and monitoring of its violation of UNSC sanctions.
They also welcomed new independent sanctions imposed by the U.S., Australia and the EU over the North's illicit cyber activities and dispatch of workers overseas to fund its nuclear weapons and missile programs.

## Lee Reaffirms Opposition against Reverse Discrimination in Supporting Multicultural Families

President Lee Jae Myung has reaffirmed his earlier position that state programs designed to support multicultural families should not result in reverse discrimination.
Lee wrote on social media on Saturday that while multicultural families should be supported by the government, such programs cannot justify specific policies that are deemed irrational or discriminate against the majority.
The president said a general claim cannot support all of the specific claims and that there's a need to consider animosity toward remarks based on political correctness, hypocrisy, and factionalism.
Lee said the more sensitive the matter, it should be publicly discussed for a resolution, and that admitting faults and correcting mistakes would further cement public support.
Earlier, the president came under fire after he questioned fairness and rationality in giving the same level of priority to mothers from multicultural families and others recognized as persons of national merit for publicly-subsidized postpartum care centers.

## US Issues Visa for DP Rep. Jin Sung-joon after Application Denial

The United States has issued a visa for ruling Democratic Party Rep. Jin Sung-joon for a trip to the U.S. Pacific Command, days after the lawmaker said his application was denied.
Jin wrote on his social media account on Saturday that he had received the visa from the U.S. Embassy in Seoul.
The three-term lawmaker, who chairs the parliamentary National Defense Committee, expressed relief that the U.S. State Department belatedly issued the A-2 visa for diplomats and government officials.
Jin said he will depart on Sunday afternoon for talks with the commander of the U.S. Pacific Command and other military leaders on strengthening bilateral security cooperation and developing a future-oriented alliance.
Earlier in the week, Jin said the U.S. had denied his visa application and expressed regret that the National Assembly's official activity was restricted.
The reason behind the denial was unclear, but observers have speculated that Jin's conviction in 1991 for violating South Korea's National Security Act may have been a factor.

## Global Oil Prices Surge amid Concerns over Renewed US-Iran Conflict, Crude Supply Disruptions

Global oil prices surged on Friday amid concerns about a renewed conflict in the U.S.-Iran war and crude delivery disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz.
Brent crude for December delivery ended Friday at a six-month high of 117 U.S. dollars and 64 cents per barrel, up two-point-four percent from the previous session's closing.
West Texas Intermediate for December delivery closed at 104 dollars and 12 cents a barrel, the highest since September 15, following a two-point-08-percent increase from the previous day.
The U.K. Maritime Trade Operations said on Friday that a small boat attached two limpet mines to a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz the previous day, causing an engine-room fire and temporarily disabling the vessel.
There is also growing concern that the U.S. may resume strikes on Iran after the midterm elections.

## Trump: US Verifying N. Korean Involvement in Binance Hacking

U.S. President Donald Trump says Washington is verifying if North Korea was behind the recent hacking of the world's largest crypto exchange, Binance.
When asked about the potential North Korean involvement at the White House on Friday, Trump said that they don't know and that officials are looking into it.
As for possibly recovering the assets, the U.S. president said Binance has said that they're going to get them back and that they have the situation "pretty much under control."
Trump added that he'd always felt crypto trading was "a little bit dangerous."
Earlier, Binance said six-point-three billion dollars worth of virtual assets were stolen following a hack on Thursday, the largest single crypto heist to date.
Industry analysts suspect that the North's hacking group, TraderTraitor, orchestrated the hacking attack.

## Hanwha Defense USA Wins First Contract to Build Vessel for US Navy

A U.S. subsidiary of South Korea's Hanwha Aerospace has won its first contract to build a vessel for the U.S. Navy since acquiring the Philly Shipyard in 2024.
Hanwha Defense USA said on Friday, local time, that it had secured a deal to construct a medium landing ship for the U.S. Navy and Marine Corps, valued at 269 million U.S. dollars.
The ship would be built at the Hanwha Philly Shipyard in Philadelphia, with options for construction of three additional vessels.
U.S. maritime services firm Tote Services selected the Hanwha subsidiary in a competitive bidding process under its vessel construction management program, and the construction is set to begin in the fourth quarter of 2027.
The contract comes after South Korea agreed to invest 150 billion dollars into America's shipbuilding industry as part of the 350-billion-dollar investment pledge under last year's bilateral trade deal aimed at lowering tariffs.

## S. Korea, US, Japan Reaffirm Commitment to N. Korea&#039;s Complete Denuclearization

Top diplomats from South Korea, the United States and Japan have reaffirmed their commitment to achieving North Korea's complete denuclearization.
The commitment was reaffirmed during talks on Friday among South Korean Foreign Minister Cho Hyun, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi on the sidelines of the G20 Foreign Ministers' Meeting in Atlanta, Georgia.
According to the U.S. State Department, the three ministers reaffirmed their commitment to North Korea's complete denuclearization and the enforcement of UN Security Council resolutions aimed at preventing the regime from generating revenue for its nuclear and ballistic missile programs.
The three sides also emphasized the importance of maintaining peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait, while Rubio expressed concerns over China's attempts to assert control over the South China Sea and its coercive actions in the East China Sea and the Yellow Sea.
The ministers also signed a memorandum of cooperation establishing a Trilateral Arctic Pacific Partnership to explore maritime cooperation and economic opportunities in the Pacific Arctic.
