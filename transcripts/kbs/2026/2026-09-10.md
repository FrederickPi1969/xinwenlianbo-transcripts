## Companies That Repeatedly Leak Personal Info to Face Tougher Penalties

A new penalty system will go into effect on Friday, making it possible for companies that leak personal information to face fines equivalent to ten percent of total sales if the leaks are repeated or if they affect ten million people or more.
The Personal Information Protection Commission announced on Thursday that the tougher penalties follow changes to the Personal Information Protection Act and the enforcement decrees and notices containing detailed standards.
The revisions aim to strengthen accountability for companies or agencies involved in data breaches and prevent such incidents in advance, as the nation has recently witnessed a series of large-scale data breaches that affected people’s everyday lives.
The new penalties will affect companies that repeatedly leak personal information, whether intentionally or via gross negligence, within a three-year period.
Companies that cause data breaches affecting ten million people or more or cause them after failing to fulfill corrective orders will also be subject to the new penalties.
But companies that have made active investments in protecting personal information will be eligible to have their fines reduced by up to 40 percent.

## Baseball Legend Jang Hoon Dies at Age 86

Baseball legend Jang Hoon, known in Japan as Isao Harimoto, has passed away at the age of 86.
Japanese broadcaster TBS reported that Jang died in a hospital in Tokyo on Wednesday.
A native of Hiroshima, Jang overcame a right-hand disability caused by burns from the atomic bombing, achieving remarkable success in Japan’s Nippon Professional Baseball, or NPB.
His accomplishments include seven batting titles, nine highest on-base percentage records, and 16 Best Nine selections.
Jang holds the NPB record for the most career hits, with three-thousand-85, and has a career batting average of .319, ranking third of all time.
He also hit 504 home runs, placing him seventh, and amassed one-thousand-676 RBIs, which is the fourth-highest number in league history.
His career began in 1959 with the Toei Flyers, which later became Nittaku and Nippon-Ham.
He played there until 1975, then continued with the Yomiuri Giants and the Lotte Orions, retiring in 1981.
Jang was enshrined in the Japanese Baseball Hall of Fame in 1990.
Beyond his playing career, Jang also played a pivotal role in the establishment of the Korean Baseball Organization, or KBO, serving as a special adviser to the KBO commissioner from 1982, the inaugural year of professional baseball in South Korea, until 2005, maintaining strong ties to South Korean baseball throughout his life.

## NBS: Survey Shows 45% Oppose Troop Deployment to the Strait of Hormuz

A recent poll by the National Barometer Survey shows that more than four out of ten citizens oppose deploying military forces to the Strait of Hormuz.
According to the survey, released on Thursday, 45 percent of respondents oppose any plan to send military assets, such as maritime patrol aircraft and support ships, to the region.
The percentage in favor of deployment stood at 36 percent, while 19 percent of respondents answered they “don’t know” or did not respond.
Embrain Public, K-Stat Research, Korea Research and Hankook Research conducted the survey from Monday to Wednesday, involving one-thousand-two adults aged 18 and older.
By age group, opposition to deployment was strongest among people in their 40s and 50s, with 54 percent and 59 percent opposing it, respectively.
On the other hand, support for deployment was higher among those in their 60s and those in their 20s or younger, at 42 percent and 40 percent, respectively.
By political affiliation, 55 percent of Democratic Party supporters opposed deployment, while 31 percent favored it.
Among People Power Party supporters, 46 percent favored deployment, while 40 percent opposed it.
The survey had a confidence level of 95 percent, with a margin of error of plus or minus three-point-one percent.
