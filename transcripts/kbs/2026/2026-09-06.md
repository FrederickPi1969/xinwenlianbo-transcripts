## Chuseok Ritual Table Costs Estimated at over 300,000 Won at Traditional Markets

The cost of preparing a traditional Chuseok ancestral ritual table at traditional markets has risen slightly from a year earlier, with a four‑person household expected to spend just over 300-thousand won, or about 220 U.S. dollars.
Korea Price Information, a professional price research agency, said Sunday that a four‑person household would need to spend 302-thousand-500 won, up 35-hundred won, or one-point-two percent, from last year.
The estimated cost at large supermarkets was 397-thousand-700 won, an increase of six-thousand-350 won, or one-point-six percent, from a year earlier.
The rise was driven mainly by higher prices for chicken and eggs following the summer heat wave, which pushed up livestock product prices.
To stabilize prices ahead of Chuseok, the government plans to supply a record 183-thousand tons of key holiday food items, including more than three times the normal supply of fruit and up to two-point-four times the usual supply of eggs.
The government will also provide a record 103 billion won in discounts on agricultural, livestock and fishery products.

## Strong Winds, High Waves Expected Along Southern, Eastern Coasts

Skies will be mostly clear nationwide, though cloudy conditions and rain are expected through Monday in parts of the east coast, Jeju Island and the Gyeongsang region.
According to the Korea Meteorological Administration on Sunday, strong wind advisories are in effect for Jeju and the southern coast of South Gyeongsang Province, with winds potentially exceeding 20 meters per second, raising concerns over falling objects and damage to facilities.
Waves could reach up to five meters along the southern coast and the southern East Sea, while powerful swells are also expected along parts of the east coast, the southern coast of South Gyeongsang Province and Jeju.
Monday morning lows will be similar to Sunday, with Seoul and Daegu at 19 degrees, while daytime highs are forecast to range between 24 and 31 degrees, with Seoul reaching 29 degrees.
Temperatures will remain near seasonal averages, but the perceived temperature could climb to around 31 degrees in Gwangju and the Gyeongsang region, making conditions feel hot during the day.

## Seoul Fireworks Festival Draws 1 Million

The annual Seoul International Fireworks Festival lit up the skies over Yeouido in western Seoul on Saturday night, drawing an estimated one million people, according to organizers.
Under the theme "Finding Your Light," teams from Britain, the United States and South Korea took part in the event hosted by Hanwha Corp.
The British team opened the show with a dynamic fireworks display set to a song from the soundtrack of the Netflix megahit "KPop Demon Hunters," while the South Korean team closed the festival with an emotional performance accompanied by Lee So‑ra's “The Wind Blows.”
This year's festival was held about a month earlier than usual to account for the Chuseok holiday period.
With massive crowds expected along Yeouido Hangang Park, authorities strengthened safety measures and traffic controls around the area.
Yeouinaru Station on Subway Line 5 was bypassed by trains until 10 p.m., and traffic was restricted on major roads around Yeouido, including both directions of Wonhyo Bridge.

## S. Korea&#039;s Deficit Debt to Top 1,300 Trillion Won by 2030

South Korea's deficit government debt, which must ultimately be repaid by taxpayers, is projected to exceed one-thousand-300 trillion won, or about 963 billion U.S. dollars, by 2030.
According to next year's budget proposal from the Ministry of Planning and Budget, deficit debt is expected to rise from one-thousand-25-point-two trillion won this year to one-thousand-117-point-one trillion won next year.
The debt is projected to continue increasing to one-thousand-192-point-four trillion won in 2028, one-thousand-245-point-eight trillion won in 2029, and one-thousand-312-point-three trillion won in 2030, a rise of 287-point-one trillion won over four years.
Its share of total government debt is also forecast to grow from 72-point-six percent this year to 75-point-seven percent in 2030.
Unlike financial debt, which can be repaid using corresponding financial assets, deficit debt has no matching assets and must effectively be covered by taxpayers.
Interest payments on government debt are expected to rise from 36-point-five trillion won this year to 53-point-three trillion won in 2030, while the government debt‑to‑GDP ratio is projected to fall from 50-point-six percent this year to 48-point-three percent next year before reaching 49 percent in 2030.

## Iran Warns S. Korea Against Military Involvement in Strait of Hormuz

A senior Iranian official has reportedly warned that Iran would regard any South Korean involvement in efforts against Iran in the Strait of Hormuz as direct participation in the war.
According to Hezbollah‑affiliated Lebanese outlet Al Mayadeen, the official said Friday that South Korea should not give in to United States pressure to cooperate in what he called illegal military operations.
He urged Seoul not to "sacrifice its national interests and reputation" for U.S. policies he said destabilize regional security, stressing that South Korea should not overlook what he described as U.S. and Israeli aggression as the root cause of instability in the Strait of Hormuz.
The official warned that Tehran would consider any South Korean action against Iran in the strait as direct military aggression and participation in the conflict.
The warning comes as Seoul weighs a possible contribution to efforts to secure the key waterway amid growing pressure from Washington.
An official at Seoul's presidential office said Friday that South Korea could consider deploying troops as a way of contributing to operations in the Strait of Hormuz, provided doing so would not significantly undermine readiness for contingencies on the Korean Peninsula.

## Search Continues for 6 Missing After Busan Tugboat Capsizes

The search for six people missing after a tugboat capsized off the coast of Busan entered its fifth day on Sunday, with rough seas and strong winds hampering the operation.
The Busan Coast Guard said six vessels and eight aircraft would be deployed for the fifth day of daytime search operations.
Vessels weighing more than one-thousand tons are being used as a gale warning remains in effect in waters off Busan, with the Coast Guard focusing its search on an area measuring 65 kilometers by 31 kilometers.
Underwater searches remain impossible due to the weather, with waves reaching three to four meters and northeasterly winds blowing at 20 to 22 meters per second.
The gale warning is expected to be lifted on Tuesday.
The 286‑ton vessel TNS Catcher overturned at about 1:30 p.m. Wednesday approximately nine kilometers east of the Oryukdo islets, leaving one of the eight crew members rescued, another dead and the remaining six missing.

## President Lee Heads to France for State Visit, Summit with Macron

President Lee Jae Myung has begun a five‑day state visit to France.
Lee departed Seoul Air Base in Seongnam, just south of Seoul, on Sunday morning for Nice, France.
He is scheduled to arrive in the evening and attend the Lumière Summit, an international film and audiovisual forum, as co‑chair alongside French President Emmanuel Macron on Monday in Saint‑Paul‑de‑Vence in southern France.
National Security Adviser Wi Sung‑lac said Friday that South Korea, as a co‑host with France – a country that has played a central role in the development of the global film industry – will help lead discussions on the future of film and audiovisual production worldwide.
Lee will travel to Paris on Tuesday for a two‑day stay, during which he will hold an expanded summit meeting with Macron and attend a state dinner.
The two leaders are expected to discuss cooperation in artificial intelligence and nuclear energy, as well as coordination on the situation in the Strait of Hormuz, including potential troop deployments.
Lee is also scheduled to attend a South Korea-France business roundtable with about 20 companies from both countries.

## Chinese Survivor Found in Nepal Tunnel Says Another Person May Be Trapped

A Chinese man rescued after being trapped for 10 days in the same area where nine South Koreans went missing following massive flooding in Nepal told authorities another person was nearby, though it remains unclear whether that person is alive.
The Korea Disaster Relief Team (KDRT) said Saturday it obtained the account after rescuing the survivor from a tunnel at the construction site of the Upper Trishuli-1 (UT-1) hydropower plant in Rasuwa District, Bagmati Province, in central-northern Nepal.
The man was pulled out at about 1:15 p.m. Saturday local time by the Nepalese Army and the South Korean relief team from a point roughly 225 meters inside the hydropower plant tunnel.
KDRT said it plans to conduct an additional search of the tunnel Sunday morning in coordination with the Nepalese Army.
Nine South Koreans who were at the UT-1 hydropower plant construction site when flooding struck on Aug. 26 remain missing. Six are employees of Doosan Enerbility, and three work for Korea South-East Power.
