## Report: S. Korea Continues to 'Push Back' after Trump Announces LNG Investment

South Korea is continuing to "push back" after President Donald Trump's unilateral announcement that the nation would invest more than 50 billion U.S. dollars in the state's liquefied natural gas(LNG) development project.
Democratic Senator Bill Wielechowski of Alaska told Alaska Public Media on Friday, local time, that South Korea had stated that any potential investment would come after developer Glenfarne Group reaches a final decision.
Wielechowski said South Korean officials had told state lawmakers in Anchorage earlier in the week that they would then consider the project's commercial viability and that no decision had been made "at this point."
The outlet said the 807-mile, or nearly one-thousand-300-kilometer, pipeline project, which the state has pursued for decades, had failed to secure firm commitments from gas buyers at prices sufficient to support its immense costs.
Jason Feer, head of global business intelligence for the energy consulting firm Poten & Partners, told the outlet that there haven't been buyers willing to pay "substantially above market" for LNG from Alaska.
