## Ex-Envoy Stephen Biegun: Trump, Kim Might Meet for ‘Performative’ Summit

A former U.S. envoy for North Korea foresees the possibility of a summit between U.S. President Donald Trump and North Korean leader Kim Jong-un but says any such meeting would only be a superficial display.
At a seminar hosted by the Sejong Institute and the Stimson Center in Washington on Monday, former Special Representative for North Korea Stephen Biegun speculated that both Trump and Kim would readily participate in what he called “performative summitry” since they both enjoy being at the center of attention.
But Biegun said the ongoing wars in Ukraine and Iran, combined with Trump’s crushing defeat in the midterm elections, make it very difficult for the U.S. to restart diplomacy with North Korea.
Kurt Campbell, former U.S. deputy secretary of state under former President Joe Biden, told media on Monday that he believes the North has demanded recognition of its nuclear weapons state status as a prerequisite to considering a summit with the U.S.
Campbell said that given the current circumstances, it would be almost impossible for Trump to accept the North’s conditions and that any contact between Trump and Kim would be limited to “just a handshake of sorts.”
On the other hand, Center for Strategic and International Studies Korea Chair Victor Cha said at a separate online press event that Trump may meet with Kim during his trip to Asia later this month out of a desire to build his legacy, especially following the election loss.

## Lee Stresses Need for Thorough Preparations for Changing Environment after US Midterms

Following last week’s U.S. midterm elections, President Lee Jae Myung has instructed officials to prepare thoroughly for changes in the external environment to minimize their impact on the domestic economy.
Presiding over a Cabinet meeting Tuesday, Lee urged the officials to assess the variables and put forth response measures for different scenarios.
The president also told them to monitor markets carefully in line with the U.S. and global economies and respond promptly as needed.
Lee called for efforts to ensure a robust economy by fostering core strategic industries, such as semiconductors and artificial intelligence.
He also stressed the importance of reinforcing cooperation and mutual trust between Seoul and Washington, and of implementing the allies’ diplomacy and security agreements while also protecting national interests.

## N. Korea Test-Fires New Torpedo in East Sea, Plans Early 2027 Deployment

North Korea says it has test-fired a new type of torpedo in the East Sea and plans to deploy the weapon starting early next year.
The state-run Korean Central News Agency(KCNA) said on Tuesday that the regime's Academy of Defense Sciences, Missile Administration and torpedo weapons research bureau had verified the new system's combat effectiveness the previous day.
The news agency said the test was an "obligatory" part of the technical advancement of its weapons systems and that the North plans to deploy the torpedo with its Navy in early 2027.
The KCNA did not share additional information about the torpedo.
South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said the military continues to closely monitor North Korea's weapons development.
Last month, Pyongyang tested a new high-speed, precision-guided torpedo equipped with artificial intelligence and fired two types of cruise missiles from what South Korean defense experts believe was a three-thousand-ton submarine.

## Cabinet to Deliberate on Special Counsel Bill Next Week

The Cabinet is set to deliberate next week on a bill to appoint a special counsel to investigate whether prosecutors fabricated evidence to indict political opponents during the former Yoon Suk Yeol administration.
The presidential office said in a press notice on Monday that the bill would not be introduced at Tuesday's Cabinet meeting.
It will instead be submitted at next week's session on November 17.
The presidential office said the timing of Cabinet reviews typically depends on when the National Assembly sends over legislation and how urgent it is, and that the Cabinet is not obligated to consider a bill at its next meeting.
The office said the National Assembly had sent the special counsel bill to the executive branch on Friday afternoon, after preparations for this week's Cabinet meeting had already concluded.

## Iran Urges S. Korea Not to Intervene in Hormuz Situation

Iran expressed deep concerns regarding South Korea's possible naval deployment to the Strait of Hormuz and urged the nation not to intervene.
Tehran's foreign ministry spokesperson, Esmaeil Baghaei, said during a news briefing on Monday that Seoul should not send troops to help reopen the strategic waterway, as it would complicate the regional situation.
The spokesperson cited more than 60 years of friendly ties between Seoul and Tehran, based on mutual respect and shared interests, and stressed that Iran did not want other countries to side with the United States, Israel and some of their allies that had supported recent military aggression.
He then said he hoped that South Korea and other parties would recognize the consequences of the military operation launched by the U.S. and Israel.
Last week, the South Korean military announced plans to expand the operational area of its Cheonghae anti-piracy unit toward the Strait of Hormuz and deploy additional military assets to help restore stability at the chokepoint.
