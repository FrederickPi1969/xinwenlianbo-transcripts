## Lee Calls for Preemptive Steps to Prevent Hacking, Suggests Establishing ‘Cybersecurity Czar’

President Lee Jae Myung has called for a comprehensive prevention system to protect the nation’s economy and security from the rising threat of cyberattacks.
Presiding over a Cabinet meeting Tuesday, Lee said the development of artificial intelligence has facilitated advances in hacking technology and exposed the limitations of existing response-centered security systems.
The president raised the concern in reference to the latest heist at the world’s largest crypto exchange, Binance, worth six-point-three billion U.S. dollars, which the U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation has blamed on a North Korean hacking group.
Lee called for a system that can detect vulnerabilities in advance and respond appropriately to early signs of an attack, and for the fundamental reform of existing security systems to preemptively counter evolving cyber threats.
The president urged officials to look into establishing a “cybersecurity czar” that could effectively oversee security functions across both the public and private sectors.

## NIS to Launch Emergency Security Inspection of Central Ministries, Local Governments

The state intelligence agency will launch an emergency security inspection of central government ministries and local governments in response to a recent series of hacking incidents in the financial sector.
Government sources said Tuesday that the National Intelligence Service(NIS) had requested that approximately two-thousand-100 public institutions each conduct a security inspection based on a distributed checklist.
The NIS asked that the inspections focus on security surrounding external contact points, the operation of security systems and the use of security patches.
The intelligence agency is likely to complete its own inspection of weak spots in the information systems of core public-sector infrastructure by mid-November.
The NIS also began a security check in May with the interior ministry and other relevant agencies to better prepare for digital threats stemming from advances in artificial intelligence.

## Top Office Discusses Data Breach Linked to Presidential Delegation

The presidential office has convened an emergency meeting regarding a reported leak of personal information connected to President Lee Jae Myung, first lady Kim Hea Kyung, presidential aides, secret service agents and members of the press corps.
Presidential Chief of Staff Lim Kwang-hyun on Tuesday called for a thorough investigation into how the data was exposed and the scope of the leaked information, and for those responsible to be held to account, according to chief presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung.
Lim also instructed an inspection of security management for presidential trips and directed officials to implement effective preventive measures.
Earlier, KBS reported that a 168-person passenger list for South Korea’s presidential aircraft during Lee’s visits to Belgium, France and Italy in June had been posted on a subscription-based cybersecurity platform on June 2, a week before his departure.
The list included the names, job positions, birth dates and passport information of the president, first lady, top office aides, Cabinet ministers, and even officials whose identities have not been disclosed to the public, including secret service agents and members of the president’s medical team.
The National Police Agency, meanwhile, has launched an internal probe into the case.

## Police Disclose Identity of Suspected Yeoksam Arsonist

Police have disclosed the personal information of the person they suspect set a series of fires that injured 26 people in Gangnam District last month.
The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency revealed on its website on Tuesday that the suspect is a 77-year-old named Lee Myeong-soo and included his mug shot in the announcement.
The data will remain public until December 3.
A police review committee decided to disclose the suspect’s identity last Wednesday, taking into account the severity of the damage, the brutality of the crime, and the evidence obtained.
Lee was referred to the Public Prosecution Service on the charge of arson resulting in injury and attempted murder in connection with fires that broke out in two separate buildings in the Yeoksam area on October 20.
The police said that during the interrogation, Lee said he’d begun planning arson attacks after disputes with two patent firms over registration certificates, and had intended to intimidate employees and draw public attention to his grievances.

## [KBS EXCLUSIVE] Personal Info. of Pres. Lee, First Lady, Aides, Secret Service Agents Exposed Online

Personal information connected to President Lee Jae Myung, first lady Kim Hea Kyung, officials at the presidential office, Cabinet members, secret service agents and members of the press corps has been exposed online for five months.
KBS has learned that the 168-person passenger list for South Korea's presidential aircraft during Lee's visits to Belgium, France and Italy in June had been posted on a subscription-based cybersecurity platform on June 2, a week before his departure.
The list included the names, job positions, birth dates and passport information of the president, first lady, then-Director of National Policy Kim Yong-beom, National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac, Science and ICT Minister Bae Kyung-hoon and Foreign Minister Cho Hyun.
It also included data linked to presidential officials whose identities have not been disclosed to the public, including secret service agents, the president's medical team and those in charge of the president's and first lady's hair and makeup.
A cybersecurity industry source told KBS that organizations should scan sensitive files for malware on isolated networks or establish internal screening systems to prevent data leaks, warning that hostile states can exploit such information if it's exposed ahead of important events.

## Pyongyang Protests Claims That N. Korean Hackers behind Binance Heist

North Korea has protested U.S. claims that its hackers were behind a heist at the world's largest crypto exchange, Binance, worth six-point-three billion U.S. dollars, accusing Washington of conducting a "grave political provocation."
Kim Yo-jong, the influential sister of the North's leader Kim Jong-un, made the statement Monday to the state-run Korean Central News Agency.
Kim said the U.S. was misleading the international community into believing that the North was behind all the cyber fraud around the world and that Pyongyang viewed the move as a political provocation.
Kim denied the Federal Bureau of Investigation's conclusion that the North Korean hacking group, "TraderTraitor," had carried out the Binance heist, and claimed that the U.S. was infringing upon the regime's sovereignty by trying to brand it as a "cyber criminal state."
The influential sister said Pyongyang was monitoring Washington's persistent provocations against the regime, and that there were "issues to reconsider" regarding its "very unpleasant" attitude.
North Korea has been accused of involvement in crypto thefts in the past, but it is rare for the regime leader's sister to deny such claims.
