## JCS: N. Korean Land Mines Highly Likely Caused Last Week's DMZ Explosion

The South Korean military says North Korean land mines had highly likely caused an explosion inside the Demilitarized Zone last week and that it would take "corresponding measures" if confirmed.
The remark came from Joint Chiefs of Staff Vice Chairman Kwon Dae-won on Monday as part of preliminary findings from the military's joint investigation with the United Nations Command.
Kwon said North Korean mines are presumed to have caused the explosion, as he cited the blast crater, flame patterns and the extent of injuries by three South Korean soldiers.
Traces of trinitrotoluene and brown synthetic material were found in a forensic analysis of the injured soldiers' military equipment, which are consistent with elements of North Korean antipersonnel mines covered with plastic resin.
The explosion took place approximately ten meters south of the Military Demarcation Line(MDL), and also about ten meters away from where the North is suspected of having planted mines recently.
Kwon said if North Korean mines are confirmed to be the cause, Pyongyang should be held to full account for injuring South Korean soldiers lawfully engaging in operations south of the MDL and that Seoul would take "appropriate and corresponding measures."

## Ukrainian Outlet Slams Kyiv's Disclosure of N. Korean POW Repatriation

A Ukraine-based English-language outlet has criticized its government's decision to disclose that two North Korean prisoners of war(POW) had been sent to South Korea.
The Kyiv Independent wrote on Monday that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's actions had "angered" a partner that sits on an "arsenal of weapons that Kyiv wants," adding to the government's "string of diplomatic mishaps."
The online outlet mentioned that just over a month ago, Zelenskyy had requested South Korean air defense weapons to protect Ukraine from Russian ballistic missiles.
Moscow's state-run RIA Novosti news agency published an op-ed on Monday suggesting that Kyiv had deliberately reneged on what it characterized as a previous promise to Seoul not to disclose the POW repatriation in retaliation against South Korea's longstanding refusal to provide weapons to Ukraine.
In a recent interview with digital news outlet NewsForce, Kyrylo Budanov, head of Zelenskyy's office, said North Korea was upgrading its weapons based on combat experience gained while supporting Russia in its war against Ukraine.
Referring to "another incident on the demarcation line" between the two Koreas, an apparent reference to last week's suspected mine blast inside the demilitarized zone, Budanov warned that Pyongyang may be preparing for "active combat operations in its region."
