## S. Korea Conducts First Test Launch of Hypersonic Glide Vehicle

South Korea has successfully conducted the first test launch of a domestically developed hypersonic glide vehicle, a strategic weapon capable of changing its flight path unpredictably at high speeds.
According to presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung, President Lee Jae Myung observed the test launch Thursday afternoon and was briefed on its successful outcome, calling for the rapid development and deployment of the weapon.
Lee said the country’s security must be ensured through its own capabilities and pledged government support to develop and deploy advanced weapons to keep the public safe.
The presidential office said the test was aimed at verifying key technologies, including ultra-high-temperature-resistant materials, guidance and control capabilities, and the vehicle’s ability to perform irregular flight at low altitudes under hypersonic conditions.
The top office said the vehicle successfully separated after reaching its target altitude and completed its planned flight, including an irregular low-altitude glide that involved changing its trajectory vertically and horizontally before accurately hitting the designated target.

U.S. Vice President JD Vance has attributed South Korea's uncertain commitment to an Alaska liquefied natural gas(LNG) development project to "political dynamics" inside the country, while pledging to secure the investment.
Vance told Alaska's News Source on Tuesday that the Donald Trump administration intends to "get this done," whether it's with the current or next government in South Korea, without elaborating on what he meant by "political dynamics."
Vance also said the U.S. could work with Japan or other nations on the Alaska natural gas pipeline.
He said people are "desperate" for the gas that comes out of Alaska, describing it as cheaper, higher in quality and more reliable as opposed to what comes from "some tin-pot dictatorship."
On Monday, Vance said some details of South Korea's commitment still needed to be "worked out," after Seoul denied Trump's earlier announcement of its investment, citing the need for further assessment of the project's commercial viability.
