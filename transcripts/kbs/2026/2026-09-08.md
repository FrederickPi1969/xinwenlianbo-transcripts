## OECD Study: Reading Skills See Sharp Decline among 15-Year-Old Students in S. Korea

New data shows that reading skills among 15-year-old students in South Korea have seen a sharp drop over the past three years.
The Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development(OECD) released on Tuesday its Programme for International Student Assessment(PISA) for the year 2025.
The study found that South Korea’s 15-year-old students scored an average of 501 in reading, down 14 points since the last study, conducted in 2022.
Of all the skills measured, the report said reading recorded the steepest decline, with scores falling by 28 points across OECD countries and by 16 points among non-OECD participants between 2015 and 2025.
Singapore, which ranked No. 1 in reading both in 2022 and 2025, saw its average score drop from 543 to 535 in three years.
An education ministry official in Seoul said though it is difficult to pinpoint one reason behind the drop in reading performance among students of that age worldwide, the widespread use of digital media and social media appear to have affected students’ attitudes toward reading and their motivation to read.
In 2025, more than 760-thousand students, representing around 33 million 15-year-olds across 91 countries and economies, participated in the PISA test.

## IAEA: S. Korea Has Given Notice of Intent to Begin Consultations on Nuclear-Powered Subs

The head of the International Atomic Energy Agency(IAEA) said South Korea has officially notified the agency of its intent to begin consultations on safeguard arrangements for its envisioned nuclear-powered submarines.
IAEA Director General Rafael Mariano Grossi made the remarks during a Board of Governors meeting at the agency’s headquarters in Vienna, Austria, on Monday.
Grossi told board members that South Korea has reiterated its commitment to fulfill its obligations under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty(NPT), the comprehensive safeguards agreement(CSA) and the Additional Protocol, and has already provided the secretariat with the relevant declarations.
Grossi said he looks forward to the continuation of the consultations in the same spirit of transparency.
The NPT, to which South Korea is a party, prohibits non-nuclear-weapon states from diverting nuclear materials like uranium for weapons development.
To guarantee compliance, the treaty requires states to conclude agreements to accept safeguards such as routine inspections.
However, Article 14 of the CSA allows a country to use nuclear material for nonnuclear military purposes, like nuclear-powered submarines, through a separate agreement with the IAEA.
The South Korean government believes concluding an agreement under Article 14 of the CSA will help dispel concerns that it might use enriched uranium for the development of nuclear weapons.
A Seoul official said South Korea is following procedures to demonstrate its commitment to nonproliferation and transparency.
South Korea has set a goal of launching a nuclear-powered submarine in the mid-2030s and deploying it to the Navy by late 2030.

## Police Tighten Rules on Family-Related Conflicts of Interest

Police will introduce new guidelines to prevent family-related conflicts of interest during investigations.
The National Police Agency said Tuesday that any case involving a close relative of an officer at one police station will be transferred to another station, with the scope of the rule now expanded to include siblings in addition to spouses and direct ascendants and descendants.
Under the guidelines, investigators must recuse themselves from any case involving the family of a fellow officer at the same station, or an officer who has worked there within the past three years.
The case must then be transferred to another police station within the same court jurisdiction under the direction of the relevant metropolitan or provincial police agency.
Exceptions will require approval from the head of the relevant metropolitan or provincial police agency and will be subject to monthly reviews.

President Lee Jae Myung will nominate former prosecutor Kim Ji-yong for the first head of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency on his interior minister's recommendation.
Presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung announced the president’s decision on Monday during a briefing in Paris, where Lee is currently making a state visit.
Kang said the president will request that parliament hold a confirmation hearing for Kim.
The spokesperson stated that the nominee, an expert in criminal investigations, is the most suitable figure to help the new agency, scheduled to launch operations next month, get settled quickly.
She said the top office anticipates that Kim, with his extensive investigative experience and management skills, will successfully guide the agency through the new criminal justice system that separates the prosecution’s current investigative and prosecutorial functions.
Kang’s remarks come after Interior Minister Yun Ho-jung announced earlier Monday that he had selected Kim as his nominee and would recommend him to the president.
