## Autumn Chill Set to Arrive from Thursday

## 2026 Tax Revenue Likely to Beat Forecast by Over 50 Trillion Won

The government is expected to collect more than 50 trillion won, or roughly 34 billion U.S. dollars, in additional tax revenue this year beyond the amount projected in its supplementary budget.
According to government sources Sunday, the Ministry of Economy and Finance will announce its 2026 tax revenue reestimate at the end of this month, with the revised figure expected to exceed the 415-point-four trillion won estimate set in April by more than 50 trillion won.
If the estimate holds, annual tax revenue would surpass 465-point-four trillion won, exceeding the previous record of 395-point-nine trillion won set in 2022.
The government expects higher-than-anticipated corporate tax payments from semiconductor companies in August and September to significantly boost revenue. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix posted combined operating profits of nearly 245 trillion won in the first half of this year.
The increase in tax revenue could push funding for a new Future Response Fund planned for next year above 200 trillion won, although it has yet to be decided whether all of this year's additional revenue will be allocated to the fund.
Planning and Budget Minister Park Hong-geun said earlier this month that the fund is intended to serve as a financial reserve that the government can draw on when tax revenue falls short.

## S. Korea Wins Equestrian Dressage Gold After 12 Years

South Korean equestrian rider Nam Dong-heon won gold in the individual dressage event at the 2026 Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games on Sunday, reclaiming the title for South Korea for the first time in 12 years.
Nam scored 75-point-190 points with his horse, Leonidas, in the final at the Equestrian Park in Tokyo to finish first overall.
His victory marked South Korea's first Asian Games gold medal in individual dressage since the 2014 Incheon Games.
It was Nam's second medal of the Games after he helped South Korea win silver in the team event Thursday alongside Kim Shi-woo, Lee Myeong-seok and Do Hyun-woo.
Nam posted the highest score among all competitors in the team event, earning 70-point-559 points and signaling his strong form ahead of the individual final.
Often described as "ballet on horseback," dressage involves riders guiding their horses through a set course while performing a series of prescribed movements.

## Seoul Mobile Rates Among Highest: Japan Survey

Seoul's mobile phone rates are among the highest in six major cities worldwide, comparable to those in New York, according to a Japanese government survey.
Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications on Sunday released a report comparing 4G and 5G mobile phone rates in Seoul, Tokyo, New York, London, Paris and Dusseldorf, Germany.
The survey compared consumer costs based on data usage, using the cheapest plans offered by the leading mobile carrier in each country for new subscribers as of the end of last year.
For 4G plans with 20 gigabytes of monthly data, customers in Seoul paid six-thousand-916 yen, or about 60-thousand-846 won, to SK Telecom, the highest among the six cities, followed by New York at six-thousand-684 yen.
Seoul's rate was two-point-three times that of Tokyo and two-point-eight times that of Paris, which ranked fifth and sixth, respectively, among the six cities. Paris charged two-thousand-495 yen for the comparable plan.
For 5G plans, Seoul's mobile rates remained relatively high, although the gap was smaller than for 4G plans. Seoul ranked second at four-thousand-902 yen, or about 43-point-127 won, for 20 gigabytes of data, behind New York at six-thoussand-684 yen.

## Heavy Traffic Nationwide on Final Day of Chuseok Holiday

Traffic congestion is building on highways across the country as more vehicles head back to the capital region on the final day of the Chuseok holiday.
According to the Korea Expressway Corporation, as of noon Sunday, the estimated travel time to Seoul was six hours from Busan, four hours and 30 minutes from Gwangju, three hours and 20 minutes from Gangneung, and two hours and 20 minutes from Daejeon.

About five-point-24 million vehicles were expected to be on the roads Sunday, including 370-thousand traveling toward the Seoul metropolitan area and 490-thousand heading away from the capital.

## President Lee Heads Home After UN General Assembly, Mexico State Visit

President Lee Jae Myung is heading home after wrapping up his trip to the United States for the United Nations General Assembly and a state visit to Mexico.
Lee departed Felipe Angeles International Airport in Mexico City on Saturday morning local time.
He became the first South Korean president in 16 years to make a state visit to Mexico, where he held a summit with Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum.
The two countries concluded seven years of negotiations to revise their investment protection agreement and signed 17 memorandums of understanding and other cooperation documents covering the economy, industry, resources, artificial intelligence, defense and space, culture and tourism.
They also agreed to continue cooperating to ensure a stable supply of and access to crude oil.
Earlier, Lee visited New York to attend the 81st UN General Assembly, where he delivered a keynote speech recalling that the simultaneous admission of the two Koreas to the United Nations 35 years ago laid the groundwork for peaceful coexistence on the Korean Peninsula. He also outlined a three-point blueprint for peaceful coexistence.
Lee also held a roughly 30-minute summit with U.S. President Donald Trump, reaffirming their shared commitment to deepen cooperation on nuclear-powered submarines, the enrichment and reprocessing of spent nuclear fuel, shipbuilding, and the transfer of wartime operational control from the United States to South Korea.

## S. Korean Military, UN Command Gear Up for DMZ Mine Blast Investigation

South Korea's military and the United Nations Command have begun preparations for a joint on-site investigation into a suspected landmine explosion that recently occurred in the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ).
The Joint Chiefs of Staff (JCS) said Saturday that the two sides jointly secured a route to the site and assessed operational conditions in preparation for the investigation.
The investigation team consists of a JCS joint on-site investigation task force, the UN Command Military Armistice Commission, relevant investigative agencies and about 20 military personnel, including soldiers who carried out operations at the time of the explosion Monday.
Saturday's assessment included evaluating the safety of the route and checking surveillance and communications conditions, while the JCS said it plans to provide the public with an objective and transparent account based on the findings of the subsequent investigation.
The military has also collected protective clothing and mine-protective overshoes worn by the soldiers at the time of the incident and sent them to the National Forensic Service for analysis to determine whether the explosion was caused by a North Korean or South Korean mine. Initial results are expected this week.
Two suspected mine explosions occurred at about 10:50 a.m. Monday near the Military Demarcation Line in Paju, Gyeonggi Province, leaving two soldiers seriously injured and another with minor injuries.

## S. Korea Claims 3 Golds in Fencing, Esports, Basketball with Wins over Japan

South Korea swept three gold medals Saturday with victories over Japan in fencing, esports and basketball at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games.
The men's sabre team of Oh Sang-uk, Park Sang-won, Do Gyeong-dong and Hwang Hee-geun edged Japan 45-44 in a thrilling final at Aichi Sky Expo in Tokoname, Aichi, securing the country's fourth consecutive Asian Games title in the event following victories in 2014, 2018 and 2023.
Oh captured his second gold medal of this year's Asian Games after winning the men's individual sabre title Wednesday.
In esports, South Korea defeated host Japan 5-2 in the fighting game final at Aichi Sky Expo to win its first Asian Games gold medal in the event.
The gold-medal sweep was completed by the women's basketball team, which beat Japan 75-70 in the final at Aichi International Arena.
The victory marked South Korea's first Asian Games women's basketball title since the 2014 Incheon Games. It also marked the first time in 12 years that both the men's and women's teams won Asian Games gold medals.
