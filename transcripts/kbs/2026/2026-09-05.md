## South Korea&#039;s Exports Surpass Last Year&#039;s Total, On Pace for $1 Trillion by December

South Korea's exports have already surpassed last year's full-year total, with this year's cumulative figure crossing the 700 billion U.S. dollar mark.
According to data released Saturday by the Korea Customs Service, cumulative exports this year reached 709-point-four billion dollars as of 1 p.m. Saturday.
That means the country hit the mark 117 days earlier than last year, when annual exports totaled a then-record 709-point-three billion dollars.
Semiconductors led the growth, surging 169-point-six percent from a year earlier to account for 40-point-six percent of total exports.
Exports grew broadly across major markets, including China, the United States, Vietnam and the European Union.
Officials project South Korea will reach one trillion dollars in annual exports by early December, a mark only the U.S., China and Germany have hit so far.

## Man Sentenced to 5 Yrs. for Spying for N. Korean Agent

A man has been sentenced to five years in prison for collecting information on North Korean defectors and members of the South Korean military upon orders from a North Korean spy.
According to sources in the judicial community on Saturday, the Seoul Central District Court sentenced the man in his 40s to five years for violating the National Security Act.
The man, who ran a crypto asset investment firm, was instructed by a North Korean agent via Telegram between May and June 2021 to collect information about anti-Pyongyang defectors from the North and their activities.
He had received cryptocurrency worth approximately 660 million won, or around 489-thousand U.S. dollars, from the agent.
In July 2021, the agent ordered the man to obtain information of seven active-duty South Korean military officers and to recruit them for espionage.
The Supreme Court previously upheld a four-year sentence for the man for leaking classified military information following orders from the same North Korean agent.

## FM Cho to Visit Nepal to Seek Support for Search of 9 Missing S. Koreans

Foreign Minister Cho Hyun is set to visit the flash flood-stricken Nepal, where nine South Korean people have gone missing.
According to Seoul's foreign ministry, Cho is scheduled to visit the country between Saturday and Monday, and hold talks with his Nepali counterpart, Shisir Khanal.
The South Korean minister, who had spoken to Khanal twice on the phone, is expected to request Nepal's support to ensure that the search by Seoul's disaster relief team proceeds without any obstacles.
Cho also intends to meet with the families of the missing workers and representatives of the Korea South-East Power Company and Doosan Enerbility that dispatched them to Nepal for a hydroelectric plant project.
After his trip to Nepal, the minister is scheduled to travel to France to accompany President Lee Jae Myung during his state visit.

## Search for 6 Crew Members Enters 4th Day after Tugboat Capsized Off Busan

Searches continue for six people who went missing after a tugboat capsized in waters off the southeastern port city of Busan on Wednesday.
The Busan Coast Guard Station began its daytime search near the site of the accident at 9 a.m. Saturday, with mobilization of five vessels from the Coast Guard and the Navy, along with nine aircraft.
The search is likely to face some difficulty as the area has observed strong winds measured by a speed of around 20 meters per second and high waves of up to five meters in height under activation of a wind wave advisory.
The 286-ton vessel, "TNS Catcher," overturned at around 1:30 p.m. Wednesday approximately nine kilometers east from Oryukdo islets.
Out of eight crew members aboard, one person was rescued alive, while another died after being found unconscious.

## Trump Downplays Iran War, Presses S. Korea on Hormuz Troop Deployment

Anchor: U.S. President Donald Trump has downplayed the ongoing war in Iran, saying it is more of a military conflict than a war. He also threatened to strike Iran's Pickaxe Mountain, which is suspected of housing an underground nuclear site.

At the Oval Office on Friday, Trump said there was no fighting between the two sides and that the U.S. was controlling the Strait of Hormuz "very strongly" after removing all the mines placed by Iran.
"I call it a military conflict. It's small potatoes for us. It's not a big thing."

It is the first time that the Trump administration has publicly stated its position on South Korea's deployment, though the U.S. leader had repeatedly complained about Seoul declining to send troops.
On Truth Social Friday, Trump shared a link to an opinion piece on The Washington Post that urged Washington to press Seoul to sell its homegrown Cheongung-II medium-range surface-to-air missile defense system to Ukraine, without any additional comment.
The column said South Korea, which cites its legal preclusion from selling arms to countries at war, could make up for angering Trump in its refusal to assist in Hormuz and use the opportunity to test its intercepting capabilities against North Korea.

## Nepali Military Disapproves Ground Search by S. Korean Team over Safety Concerns

The Nepali military has not approved Seoul's disaster relief team to conduct a ground search for nine South Koreans who went missing during flash floods on August 26.
An official from the team at the Battar base camp said on Friday, local time, that a regional military commander refused to give permission to search the Ramche area due to safety concerns.
The nine employees of the Korea South-East Power Company and Doosan Enerbility had been dispatched to work in the region's Upper Trishuli-1 Hydropower Project.
The military, meanwhile, approved a helicopter and drone search near the plant's powerhouse, though the team was unable to uncover any leads.
Earlier on Friday, two Nepali workers were rescued from an area of the Trishuli 3A Hydropower Project in Bagmati Province, raising hopes of saving hundreds of others presumed to be trapped inside plant tunnels.

## Traffic, Security Tightened for Seoul Fireworks Festival, Marathon

Traffic restrictions will be imposed in Seoul's Yeouido and downtown areas throughout the weekend as the city hosts a major fireworks festival and a marathon, with police also deploying additional security measures.
The 2026 Seoul International Fireworks Festival takes place Saturday in the Yeouido and Ichon Hangang Park areas, with the main event running from 1 p.m. to 10 p.m. in Yeouido, following Friday's pre-event.
The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency said it expects around 100,000 spectators to attend the festival.
Police plan to station officers at major entry and exit points and subway stations, while cracking down on illegal parking, scalping, and the collection of unauthorized fees from spectators.
For counterterrorism purposes, police special forces will conduct searches for suspicious objects, including possible explosives. Vehicle barriers will also be set up at key locations to guard against vehicle-ramming incidents.
Yeouidong-ro between the southern end of Mapo Bridge and the 63 Building will be closed to vehicles from 3 p.m. Saturday until 12 a.m. Sunday.
Wonhyo Bridge will also be closed in both directions from 9 a.m. Saturday to 9 a.m. Sunday, while access to some roads will be limited to local residents and event vehicles.
On Sunday, the YTN Seoul Tour Marathon will take place in central Seoul from 5:30 a.m. to 10:30 a.m., with roads along the race route closed in stages.
About 16-hundred traffic officers and volunteer traffic guides will be deployed over the weekend to manage traffic and guide drivers around restricted areas.
More information on weekend events and traffic conditions is available through the Seoul Transport Operation and Information Service (TOPIS), the Seoul police traffic information hotline (02-700-5000), the Seoul Traffic Information Center website (www.spatic.go.kr), and the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency's traffic information KakaoTalk channel.
