## KBS Survey Finds 51% of Respondents Disapprove of Lee's Job Performance

A recent KBS poll ahead of the Chuseok holiday shows President Lee Jae-myung’s approval rating for his administration at 42 percent, far below the 51 percent disapproval rating.
Compared to the Lunar New Year survey in February, the approval rating fell 23 percentage points from 65 percent, while disapproval rose 24 percentage points from 27 percent.
The poll, commissioned by KBS, surveyed one-thousand-one adults nationwide on Tuesday and Wednesday and was released Thursday.
Respondents cited economic and livelihood policies as the primary reason for their disapproval at 38 percent, followed by cabinet and staff appointments at 19 percent.
By age group, 18- to 29-year-olds showed 37 percent approval and 46 percent disapproval. Those in their 30s had 37 percent approval and 60 percent disapproval.
Disapproval was highest among those in their 60s, reaching 62 percent.
In contrast, people in their 40s and 50s reported higher approval than disapproval, with the 40s at 51 percent approval and 43 percent disapproval, and the 50s at 52 percent approval and 45 percent disapproval.
Regionally, the liberal stronghold of Jeonnam-Gwangju and North Jeolla Province had the highest approval rating at 68 percent. In Seoul, approval was 37 percent, while disapproval stood at 56 percent.
Regarding the president’s press conference held last Friday, approval and disapproval were evenly split at 44 percent each.
The survey conducted by Embrain Public has a 95 percent confidence level and a margin of error of plus or minus three-point-one percentage points.

## S. Korea Nabs Gold in Men&#039;s Kayak Four 500m at Asian Games

South Korea has won the gold medal in the men's kayak four-person 500-meter race at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, marking the country's first gold in canoe sprint at this competition.
The team, comprising Cho Gwang-hee, Kim Hyo-bin, Jang Sang-won and Choi Min-kyu, completed the finals on Thursday at Miyoshi Lake Canoe Course in Miyoshi, just east of Nagoya, with a time of one minute 22-point-587 seconds.
This victory also marks the country's ninth gold medal in Aichi-Nagoya.
South Korea finished point-255 seconds ahead of China, while Japan came in third, trailing three-point-322 seconds behind the champions.

## S. Korea Investigates Two Explosions in DMZ Blast

The South Korean military says two consecutive explosions occurred during Monday's suspected landmine incident that critically injured two soldiers south of the Military Demarcation Line within the demilitarized zone(DMZ) separating the two Koreas.
An official with the Joint Chiefs of Staff told reporters on Wednesday that the incident took place tens of meters away from an area suspected of containing landmines planted by the North Korean military.
The military has sent evidence collected from the scene to the National Forensic Service to determine whether the landmine involved was planted by North Korean or South Korean forces.
It also plans to conduct a joint investigation of the site with the United Nations Command to figure out the exact circumstances surrounding the incident.
Military officials confirmed that one battalion commander suffered a severe ankle injury, while a noncommissioned officer sustained a leg fracture.
The soldiers were engaged in clearing a path for a United Nations Command(UNC) inspection of North Korea's suspected landmine activities, initially scheduled for early next week.
According to initial reports, the explosions occurred as the team was returning after completing its route-clearing operation for the day.

## N. Korean Foreign Minister Reaffirms Pyongyang’s Commitment to Nuclear Weapons

North Korea has reiterated its commitment to maintaining its nuclear program and arsenal.
In a statement carried by the North's state-run Korean Central News Agency on Thursday, Foreign Minister Choe Son-hui declared that the regime will firmly pursue its nuclear weapons ambitions, asserting that this resolve will remain unchanged “for a hundred or a thousand years.”
Choe slammed the joint statement issued on Monday by the foreign ministers of South Korea, the United States, and Japan, which reaffirmed their dedication to the “complete denuclearization” of North Korea.
She criticized what she described as the "anachronistic confrontational nature" of the three nations and their "unrealistic goal" of denuclearizing the North.
While Choe's remarks targeted the three countries' joint statement, observers noted that it followed President Lee Jae Myung's announcement of a vision for a nuclear-free Korean Peninsula.
In a speech at the UN General Assembly in New York, President Lee said Seoul aims to halt the advancement of Pyongyang's nuclear and missile capabilities, then reduce them in the medium term, ultimately seeking to achieve a nuclear-free Korean Peninsula in the long term.

## Trump Rolls out Red Carpet for Xi in Rare Reception ahead of Summit

U.S. President Donald Trump met Chinese President Xi Jinping in a rare welcome at a military base near Washington as Xi began his three-day state visit to the United States.
Trump and First Lady Melania Trump welcomed Xi and Chinese First Lady Peng Liyuan on the tarmac at Joint Base Andrews in Maryland on Wednesday on the eve of the leaders' summit at the White House.
This is their third in-person meeting, following previous talks in Busan last October and Beijing in May.
Xi’s U.S. arrival comes three years after the 2023 APEC summit in San Francisco, marking his first state visit to the country in eleven years.
The summit comes at a crucial juncture for both leaders, as Trump seeks stable relations with Xi ahead of the November midterm elections, while Xi aims to show a good outcome before the fifth plenum of the Communist Party's Central Committee, scheduled for next month.
A main agenda item for the summit is the trade truce between the two countries, expiring in November.
U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in an interview with Fox News that Washington and Beijing have agreed to extend “the Busan agreement” by two months, until January 10.
The current truce between the U.S. and China was reached during their summit in Busan last year, with Washington agreeing to suspend tariff increases on Chinese goods and Beijing agreeing to pause new export controls on rare earth elements and other critical minerals.
North Korea is also expected to be a key topic as Trump plans to resume diplomacy with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un later this year.
Observers speculate that Trump may seek a meeting with Kim during his next trip to Asia, likely for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen in November.

## Foreign Ministry Effectively Confirms Ukraine’s Transfer of N. Korean POWs to S. Korea

## S. Korea&#039;s Four-Day Chuseok Holiday Begins Thursday

Traffic congestion begins in earnest across the country as the four-day Chuseok holiday kicks off.
Heavy traffic has been reported on expressways nationwide since early Thursday morning as travelers head to their hometowns for the holiday.
According to the Korea Expressway Corporation, an estimated six-point-16 million vehicles will hit the roads throughout the day.
Around 510-thousand vehicles are projected to leave greater Seoul, while 380-thousand travel toward the capital region.
As of noon on Thursday, estimated travel times from Seoul reached eight hours and 20 minutes to Busan, seven hours and 20 minutes to Daegu, seven hours and 30 minutes to Gwangju, four hours and 20 minutes to Daejeon, and six hours and 40 minutes to Gangneung.
Expressway tolls across the country will be waived through Sunday.
Throughout the holiday period, a diverse lineup of cultural events is available to both local residents and international visitors.
Major royal palaces in Seoul, including Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung and Deoksugung, as well as Jongmyo Shrine and the royal tombs of the Joseon Dynasty, will be open free to the public.
The National Folk Museum of Korea and local governments are also offering hands-on programs for visitors to experience traditional Korean holiday customs, including making songpyeon rice cakes and trying on hanbok.
This year’s Chuseok harvest moon will reach peak fullness at 1:49 a.m. on Sunday.

## Oh Sang-uk Wins Gold, Defends Asian Games Sabre Title

South Korean fencing star Oh Sang-uk has won South Korea’s eighth gold medal at the 2026 Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games.
Oh defeated China’s Shen Chenpeng 15-13 to defend his title in the men’s individual sabre final at Aichi Sky Expo in Tokoname City on Wednesday.
The victory marked Team Korea's second medal in fencing, following Song Se-ra’s gold in the women’s individual épée event a day earlier.
South Korea has dominated the individual men's sabre event for five consecutive Asian Games, thanks to Gu Bon-gil's three titles from 2010 to 2018 and Oh’s back-to-back wins.
Oh will return for the men’s team sabre event on Saturday, seeking a second consecutive double gold at the Asian Games.
As of 9 a.m. Thursday, South Korea was in third place in the medal standings behind China and Japan, with eight gold, eight silver and 25 bronze medals.
