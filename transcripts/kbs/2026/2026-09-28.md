## 51% of Children in S. Korea Start Using Smartphones before Age 2

A staggering 51 percent of infants and toddlers now interact with smartphones before their second birthday, according to government data released Monday.
The National Center for Mental Health’s Mental Health Research Institute conducted a survey on 400 parents nationwide asking them at what age their children started using smartphones.
The most common response was 12 to 23 months of age, given by 37 percent of parents in the survey, which was released on Monday.
Another 26-point-seven percent said 24 to 35 months, 13-point-eight percent said zero to eleven months, 12-point-four percent said 36 to 47 months, and 13 percent said 48 to 59 months.
In terms of daily usage time on weekdays, 57 percent of respondents said their children used smartphones for less than one hour, 33 percent said they did not use smartphones at all, and nine percent said they used them for one to three hours.
For weekends, 55 percent said their children spent less than an hour on smartphones, similar to weekdays, but 23 percent said they spent between one and three hours on the devices, which is more than double the screen time recorded for weekdays.
Experts say smartphone use by young children should be avoided as much as possible.
The World Health Organization advises that infants and children under two years old should avoid screen time, while those aged two to four should be limited to one hour per day to ensure better sleep, more active play and healthy growth.

South Korea accused Ukraine of violating the terms of a nondisclosure agreement after its president disclosed at the United Nations on Wednesday that the nation had facilitated the transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war(POW) to South Korea.
In a statement on Sunday, Senior Presidential Secretary for Public Relations and Communications Seong Ghi-hong said Kyiv had requested that the repatriation remain confidential, fearing that critics at home might accuse the government of forgoing the opportunity to exchange the prisoners for Ukrainian ones held by Russia.
Seong said South Korea had agreed to keep the matter under wraps out of respect for the safety of the North Korean POWs and their families, as well as its own diplomacy and national security.
President Lee Jae Myung posted to X on Thursday that it was regrettable that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had disclosed the transfer during his United Nations address, violating what Lee characterized as a prior vow to keep the matter confidential. Ukrainian outlet RBC-Ukraine then reported on Friday, citing a source in the Ukrainian presidential office, that Kyiv had made no such promise to Seoul.
The presidential aide strongly criticized Ukraine for unilaterally disclosing the POW repatriation and denying the existence of a confidentiality agreement.
Seong then demanded an explanation and apology from Kyiv and said Seoul would review further action.
