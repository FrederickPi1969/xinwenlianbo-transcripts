## Gov’t Confirms Google’s Compliance with Security Requirements for High-Precision Map Data

Google is now one step closer to using South Korea’s high-precision map data, having met the security requirements for the transfer of the data overseas.
The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport said Friday that a government-private sector consultative body has verified Google’s compliance with the security criteria, a condition of its approval to transfer one to five-thousand scale map data abroad.
Google applied for the transfer in February last year and received conditional approval in February this year, subject to restrictions on coordinate displays, security processing of satellite images, domestic data processing, and security incident response measures.
Google submitted its final compliance report on Wednesday of last week and has also established a system to block app updates if necessary to prevent the exposure of restricted coordinates.
The data approved for transfer is limited to basic map and road network information, which must be processed by local partner T Map Mobility on domestic servers, while users’ personal information and location data are excluded.
The government will monitor compliance and may revoke approval in the event of serious violations.
Google is now expected to expand its services in South Korea as early as the beginning of next year.
The tech giant already has ten million users of its map app in South Korea, and combining navigation with search and artificial intelligence could reshape competition in the domestic market, long dominated by Naver Map and KakaoMap.

## S. Korea Holds Separate Sado Mine Memorial for Third Straight Year

The South Korean government held its own memorial ceremony in Japan for the third year in a row to commemorate Koreans forced to work at Japan’s UNESCO-listed Sado mines during World War II.
The event took place on Friday morning at a hotel on Sado Island, Niigata Prefecture, with nine family members of Korean victims in attendance.
In a memorial speech, South Korean Ambassador to Japan Lee Hyuk paid tribute to the Korean workers who were forcibly mobilized against their will during Japan’s colonial rule and endured harsh labor and poor living conditions on Sado Island.
South Korea boycotted a memorial event hosted by Japan for the third consecutive year in response to Tokyo’s failure to recognize the forced mobilization of Korean nationals.
Japan held its own ceremony on September 12 but, as in previous years, made no mention of forced labor.
South Korea and Japan agreed to hold an annual joint ceremony at the site when the mine was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2024.
However, the two countries have held separate ceremonies, as Japan has not acknowledged that it subjected Koreans to forced labor at the mines.

## Exhibition on Last Emperor, Sunjong, Opens at National Palace Museum of Korea

A special exhibition on the life of Sunjong, the last emperor of the Korean Empire, opened Friday at the National Palace Museum of Korea to mark the centennial of the monarch’s death.
The exhibition showcases two portraits of Sunjong, one from when he was the crown prince and another from after he became emperor.
The portrait of him as crown prince is reported to have been produced alongside that of his father, Emperor Gojong, in 1902 to mark the 40th anniversary of Gojong’s reign.
The portrait of Emperor Sunjong was created in 1928, two years after his death, by royal artist Kim Eun-ho, who used photographs of the late emperor as a reference.
Both portraits were damaged in fires after the Korean War broke out, but authorities verified their origin based on Sunjong’s dress and accessories, the mat patterns, and the furniture in the paintings.
