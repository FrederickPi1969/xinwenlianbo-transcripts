## Southern Regions and Jeju to See Showers on Monday

Rain clouds are expected to move southward on Monday, bringing showers to the southern regions and Jeju Island.
According to the Korea Meteorological Administration, rainfall of up to 30 millimeters is forecast for Jeju, while Gwangju, South Jeolla Province and the Gyeongsang Provinces are expected to receive up to ten millimeters. Other southern regions and Gangwon Province are forecast to receive around five millimeters.
The rain is expected to largely stop by Monday evening, with skies gradually clearing afterward.
Monday morning lows will be similar to or slightly higher than Sunday's, with temperatures forecast at four degrees Celsius in Seoul, six degrees in Daegu and seven degrees in Jeonju.
Daytime temperatures are expected to rise by three to seven degrees from Sunday's levels, reaching 12 degrees Celsius in Seoul and Gwangju, 13 degrees in Chuncheon and 14 degrees in Busan.
Waves are forecast to reach two to four meters in all coastal waters.

## S. Korea&#039;s Tourism Deficit Widens to Nearly $500 Million in September

South Korea posted a tourism deficit of nearly 500 million U.S. dollars in September, marking the third straight month in the red as the shortfall widened nearly tenfold over the three-month period.
According to data released Sunday by the Korea Tourism Organization, the tourism deficit reached 498 million dollars in September, up nearly 100 million dollars from August.
The country had recorded monthly tourism surpluses for four consecutive months from March through June, with the June surplus approaching 600 million dollars, the second-highest on record.
However, the balance swung back to a deficit of 50-point-three million dollars in July, and the shortfall widened for three consecutive months through September.
With the number of foreign visitors expected to exceed 20 million for the first time this year, industry officials and experts say South Korea needs to strengthen regional tourism offerings and develop more attractions to encourage repeat visits.
An official at a travel platform company warned that without sufficient tourism infrastructure and attractions, the current boom could prove temporary and largely driven by the popularity of K-pop.

## PPP Begins Outdoor Protest Campaign Against Special Counsel Bill

The main opposition People Power Party (PPP) has launched a street protest campaign following the passage of a special counsel bill targeting allegations of malicious prosecution during the administration of former President Yoon Suk Yeol.
At a rally near Gyeongbokgung Palace on Saturday, PPP leader Jang Dong-hyeok denounced the legislation as an abuse of legislative power designed to shield President Lee Jae Myung from criminal prosecution. He argued that any wrongful indictment should be challenged through the courts like any other case.
PPP floor leader Jeong Jeom-sig also accused the government of committing what he described as an act of insurrection by allowing the president to handpick prosecutors to investigate cases involving himself.
The PPP said it plans to continue its street protests, including holding a parliamentary meeting outside the presidential office and staging one-person demonstrations.
The ruling Democratic Party (DP), meanwhile, accused the opposition of trying to mislead the public out of fear that allegations of fabricated investigations and indictments would be exposed. The party argued that the PPP was effectively shielding prosecutors who served under the Yoon administration.
DP spokesperson Shin Hyun-young also noted that a controversial provision allowing the special counsel to decide whether to maintain existing indictments had been removed from the bill. She challenged the opposition to recommend candidates for special counsel if it was confident the previous investigations had been legitimate.

## Seoul Sees First Snowfall of Winter, 26 Days Earlier than Last Year

Seoul recorded its first snowfall of the season on Sunday.
The Korea Meteorological Administration said the season's first snowfall was confirmed by weather observers at the Seoul Weather Station in Jongno District in central Seoul on Sunday morning.
In Seoul, snowfall is officially recorded when weather observers visually confirm snow at the station.
This year's first snowfall came 26 days earlier than last year's and 12 days earlier than the seasonal average for the 1991-2020 period.
As cold air moves in from the northwest, Seoul's daytime high is forecast to reach just seven degrees Celsius, seven-point-eight degrees below the seasonal average.
The weather agency forecast up to five millimeters of rain Sunday in inland and mountainous areas of the Seoul metropolitan region and Gangwon Province, with some areas expected to see snow or sleet.

## Elementary School Enrollment to Fall Below 300,000 for First Time Next Year

South Korea is expected to see the number of children entering elementary school fall below 300-thousand for the first time next year, highlighting the growing impact of the country's declining birthrate.
According to data released Sunday by the Korean Statistical Information Service and the Korean Educational Development Institute, the number of children set to receive elementary school enrollment notices next month is expected to fall into the 200-thousand range, marking a record low.
The decline is largely attributable to a sharp drop in births in 2020, the year next year's incoming students were born. The number of newborns fell into the 200-thousand range that year for the first time since related statistics began in 1970.
The number of incoming elementary school students is expected to fall below 300-thousand just three years after dropping below the 400-thousand mark, leaving enrollment at roughly one-quarter of its peak level about 60 years ago.
The projected figure would represent a sharp decline from this year's 301-thousand-442 students, which was already the lowest number since enrollment statistics began in 1964.
The number of new elementary school students peaked at one-point-05 million in 1968 before falling to 353-thousand 828 in 2024, the first time enrollment dropped into the 300-thousand range.
The shrinking school-age population is expected to accelerate school closures and mergers, particularly outside the Seoul metropolitan area. It is also raising concerns about deteriorating educational conditions and a potential wave of university closures in the years ahead.
