## Naver Cloud Consortium Picked to Develop AI Foundation Model for Cybersecurity

The government has selected a Naver Cloud consortium as the operator of a project to develop an AI foundation model specialized in cybersecurity.
The science ministry announced the decision Thursday, saying two consortiums took part in the bid.
The Naver Cloud consortium will see the participation of 32 organizations — companies, universities and research centers.
The science ministry commended the consortium’s composition and its development strategy, which used a number of AI agents.
The government will provide the consortium with 256 B200 graphics processing units from Nvidia for ten months starting this month.
The science ministry plans to enter into an agreement with the consortium before the end of the month.

## Trump Again Mentions S. Korea, Japan in Connection with Alaska LNG Project

U.S. President Donald Trump has again mentioned South Korea and Japan in connection with Alaska’s liquefied natural gas(LNG) project.
Speaking to reporters at the White House on Wednesday, Trump said South Korea and Japan are going to Alaska to buy oil in large volumes and build a pipeline.
Trump made the remark as he unveiled plans to visit Alaska to help Dan Sullivan of the Republican Party, who is seeking a third term in the Senate in the November election.
Earlier, Trump said in January that the U.S. had launched a massive Alaskan pipeline project to export natural gas to Asia and had secured unprecedented funds after making deals with South Korea and Japan.
Alaska’s LNG project is one of Trump’s key policy priorities, and Trump has repeatedly raised the possibility of it getting injections of funds from Seoul and Tokyo.
The project aims to transport natural gas produced in northern Alaska to the state’s southern coast via a one-thousand-300-kilometer gas pipeline, then liquefy it before shipping it overseas.

## Proposed Defense Budget Reflects Sharp Increase Related to OPCON Transfer, Self-Reliance

Defense spending is set to expand significantly next year if the government’s budget proposal is approved, in anticipation of the transfer of wartime operational control(OPCON) from the U.S.
In materials distributed on Thursday detailing the government’s proposed defense budget for 2027, the defense ministry said spending related to the OPCON transfer and self-reliant defense capabilities would account for around 12-point-two trillion won, or nearly nine billion U.S. dollars.
Compared to last year, that’s up 34-point-nine percent.
The ministry said the increase is intended to enable the nation to defend itself on its own and to preemptively secure the capabilities to establish a South Korean-led coalition defense system.
The proposed budget also covers the launch of the nuclear-powered submarine program, the mass production of domestically developed KF-21 fighter jets, the early operational deployment of long-range surface-to-air missiles, and the mass production of 230-millimeter-class unguided rockets for Cheonmu multiple rocket launchers.

## N. Korea Removes Words ‘Peaceful Reunification’ from Ruling Party’s Charter

North Korea has removed the term “peaceful reunification” from the rules governing its ruling Workers’ Party of Korea.
The Institute for National Security Strategy disclosed the full text of the party’s revised charter during a media briefing in Seoul on Thursday.
Though intelligence authorities in the South had already revealed the change of party rules, indicating updates were made in June 2024 to reflect the view that the two Koreas are separate and hostile states, the North never released the full text until Thursday.
The institute said the North adopted the revisions during the ruling party’s Ninth Congress in February.
Instead of highlighting the achievements of the current leader’s father and grandfather, the revised party rules strengthen the power and authority of the party’s general secretary, a title held by North Korean leader Kim Jong-un.

## Justice Minister Nominee Promises Not to Order Charges Dropped in Individual Cases

Justice Minister nominee Kim Seung-won says he has no intention of directing the prosecutor general to drop charges against anyone, nor would he have the authority to do so as minister.
Kim made the remark on Thursday before entering the office of a preparation team for his confirmation hearing in Seoul, when asked if he would exercise his right to direct investigations should he become justice minister.
Previously, Kim served as co-chair of a lawmakers’ gathering tasked with seeking the dismissal of multiple criminal charges against President Lee Jae Myung.
The nominee stressed that the trial prosecutor is the one who has the authority to decide whether or not to proceed in individual cases, and not the justice minister.
Kim also said he felt wronged with regard to a 2021 case in which he was accused of interfering in the pharmaceutical research process by lobbying the drug safety ministry to approve a clinical trial for a new COVID-19 drug.
Prosecutors chose to defer prosecution for Kim, a decision that suggests lenient treatment despite grounds for indictment, instead of dismissing the case outright.
The nominee said he has filed a constitutional challenge to the prosecution’s decision and that the Constitutional Court is reviewing his appeal.

## KEPCO Proposes Samsung Electronics, SK hynix Pay Electricity Bills 5 Years in Advance

The Korea Electric Power Corporation(KEPCO) has proposed that Samsung Electronics and SK hynix pay for five years’ worth of electricity in advance.
According to industry insiders on Thursday, KEPCO requested up-front payments of 20 trillion won from Samsung Electronics and five trillion won from SK hynix.
The combined amount, worth about 18-point-four billion U.S. dollars, is equivalent to five years’ worth of electricity bills for both companies calculated on the basis of what they paid last year.
KEPCO apparently made the proposal to secure funding for new power grid investments as it faces accumulated deficits and debt.
As of the end of June, KEPCO’s total debt stood at nearly 211 trillion won, with the daily interest alone amounting to some eleven-and-a-half billion won.

## Central Gov’t Bodies to Begin Relocating to Sejong from Metro Seoul in First Half of 2027

A number of central government bodies are set to relocate to Sejong City from the Seoul metropolitan area next year.
The relocation will take place in phases, starting with the justice and gender ministries in the first half of 2027.
The National Police Agency and the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency will move once new offices are built in Sejong.
Prime Minister Han Seong-sook said Thursday that priority will be given to those government bodies whose relocation would have the greatest impact.
The government will finalize the list of organizations to be relocated in the fourth quarter of this year.
Interior Minister Yun Ho-jung said plans to move the foreign and unification ministries are also under consideration.
Han said Sejong is the symbol of balanced national development and that a presidential office will be built there by August 2029.
The government will also support the completion of a National Assembly building in the city in 2030.
The relocation is part of efforts to reduce the concentration of government functions in Seoul and promote balanced regional development.
The government also plans to relocate public institutions outside the Seoul metropolitan area.
It will announce relocation plans later this year for some 350 public institutions and begin moving them next year.
The government also plans to reduce the number of public institutions by 109 through restructuring and consolidation.
Han said ministries will draw up roadmaps to reform the functions of public institutions.
The latest plan follows earlier rounds of government relocations that began in 2012 with the prime minister’s office.
Since then, 44 central government organizations have fully relocated to Sejong.

## US Weighs ‘Targeted, Thoughtful’ Tariffs on Semiconductors

U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick says the Trump administration is considering tariffs on a broad range of semiconductor products.
Speaking to CNBC on the sidelines of a G20 ministerial meeting in North Carolina on Wednesday local time, Lutnick said the tariffs would be “targeted” and “thoughtful.”
He said companies that manufacture semiconductors in the United States would not face tariffs.
But he said those that produce them elsewhere should be prepared to pay tariffs to gain access to the U.S. market.
Lutnick also confirmed reports that the tariffs could be steep and tied to semiconductor investment in the United States.
Politico earlier reported that the administration is considering significantly expanding the range of products subject to semiconductor tariffs.
The measures could cover not only chips but also products containing them, including laptops, game consoles and data center servers.
The report said the administration is also considering country-specific tariff rates and quotas.
Lutnick also said that as early as next week Taiwan could announce investments in the United States worth an additional 20 billion to 30 billion U.S. dollars.
The Trump administration began imposing tariffs on a limited range of semiconductor products in January.
U.S. President Donald Trump signed a proclamation imposing a 25 percent tariff on certain chips, including Nvidia’s H200 AI chips, which are imported into the United States and then reexported.
At the time, the White House said broader tariffs on semiconductors and related products could follow to encourage manufacturing in the United States.
