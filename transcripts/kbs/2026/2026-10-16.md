## Former US Diplomat Accused of Defaming President Lee Denies Allegations in Court

Tan allegedly spread false information during a news conference in Washington in June of last year by saying President Lee spent time in a juvenile detention center after taking part in a gang rape and murder in his teens.
He is also accused of defaming Lee by repeating the same allegations at a church gathering in Seoul’s Eunpyeong District in July of last year.
The prosecution previously verified that there was no record of Lee’s being incarcerated at a juvenile detention center and concluded that the rumors about his incarceration were false.
During Friday’s hearing, Tan said he is innocent because he believes Lee is guilty.
Tan’s legal team has asked the court to dismiss the indictment, claiming a South Korean court has no jurisdiction over remarks made in the United States.
Tan was indicted without physical detention in July after entering the nation in late May and failing to comply with a police summons to appear for questioning.
Police, who had initially decided not to hand over Tan’s case to the prosecution, launched a reinvestigation at the prosecution’s request.

## Mount Baegak in Seoul to Open Fully after Decades of Restricted Access

Mount Baegak in Seoul, where access has been restricted ever since North Korean commandos attempted to raid the presidential office of Cheong Wa Dae in January 1968, will be fully open to the public starting Saturday.
The Korea Heritage Service said Friday that an additional one-point-32-kilometer trail along the mountain’s southwestern foothills will open at 9 a.m. Saturday, connecting previously opened routes into a single trail network.
Access to the mountain was closed off after the 1968 incident, but a two-point-two-kilometer section of the Hanyangdoseong trail on the mountain’s northern side reopened in 2020, followed by a three-kilometer forest trail along the southeastern foothills in 2022.
The newly opened section runs from Dolgorae Shelter to Mansedongbang Spring and a pine tree bearing traces of a firefight during the 1968 incident.
Four rest areas with panoramic views of Mount Inwang, Mount Nam, Cheong Wa Dae and Gyeongbok Palace have also been built along the new trail.
