## 5 Sailors Hurt After Freon Gas Leaks on Russian Boat Docked in a Busan Port

Five Russian sailors were injured after there was a freon gas leak on their fishing boat docked in waters off Busan.
According to the Busan Metropolitan City Fire and Disaster Headquarters, the gas leak occurred on the 769-ton Russian ship at around 8:37 a.m. Tuesday, injuring five sailors who were in the ship’s freezer.
Two of them were in cardiac arrest but have since regained spontaneous circulation.
The ship with a crew of 15 members was docked in Gamcheon Port in Busan’s Saha District for repairs.
The incident apparently occurred while the sailors were working on the refrigerant system.
Freon gas, which is colorless and odorless at room temperature, is used as a refrigerant in air conditioning and refrigeration systems.
Though low in toxicity, freon gas can cause asphyxiation when inhaled in an enclosed space.
The Korea Coast Guard plans to conduct an investigation on the incident.

## Nuri Rocket to Carry 1st Microsatellite Constellation in October Launch

The country’s homegrown Nuri space rocket is set to carry multiple satellites that will form part of the country’s first domestically launched microsatellite constellation when it launches next month.
The Korea AeroSpace Administration and the Korea Aerospace Research Institute said Tuesday that on its fifth launch, the rocket will carry a total of 15 satellites, consisting of New-space Earth Observation Satellite Constellation for National Safety(NEONSAT) as primary payloads and ten CubeSats as secondary payloads.
The launch will mark the first time the Nuri carries multiple primary payloads in a single mission and the largest number of satellites ever carried by the rocket, with all 15 to be placed into a sun-synchronous orbit at an altitude of 570 kilometers.
The launch is scheduled for October 7 between 12:23 p.m. and 1:23 p.m. from the Naro Space Center in Goheung, South Jeolla Province, with the exact time to be decided on launch day.
Final assembly of the rocket is under way at the space center, with the rocket scheduled to be moved to the launch pad on October 6 after final checks.

## Korean Air-Asiana Mileage Integration Plan Finalized

The mileage integration plan for Korean Air and Asiana Airlines has been finalized, with measures in place to protect Asiana customers and ensure miles remain after the two carriers merge in December.
The Fair Trade Commission(FTC) ordered the airlines to submit the plan when it conditionally approved the two airlines' merger in 2022, to ensure that their combined mileage, reportedly worth around four trillion won, or some three billion U.S. dollars, can continue to be used normally.
Under the plan, Asiana mileage will be managed separately during the ten years following the merger date, meaning current expiration periods will continue to apply even after Asiana ceases to exist as a corporate entity.
If converted to Korean Air miles, flight-earned Asiana miles will carry over at a one-to-one rate, and partner miles at a rate of one to zero-point-82.
The plan also requires award-seat flight levels to remain at or above the airlines’ combined 2024 level, with separate targets for popular long-haul routes such as those to North America and Europe. Total mileage usage must increase by six percent next year and 21 percent in 2030, compared with last year.
The Fair Trade Commission will monitor compliance for ten years through an oversight committee.

A U.S. federal judge has pumped the brakes on the Donald Trump administration’s new visa rules limiting how long foreign students and media personnel could stay in the country.
Reuters reported on Monday that Judge Dennis Saylor IV of the United States District Court for the District of Massachusetts said the Department of Homeland Security’s(DHS) rules were irrational, and that its justification was “exceptionally weak.”
The ruling, handed down in response to a lawsuit filed by labor unions and groups concerned with higher education, came just a day before the regulations were set to take effect.
The judge indefinitely blocked DHS from implementing the rules.
In July, DHS imposed a four-year cap on the duration of stay for F-1 and J-1 visa holders, while also limiting foreign media personnel on I visas to 240 days.
Those wishing to remain in the country longer were required to apply for extensions.
The department said at the time that the new rules closed a loophole allowing foreign students and exchange visitors to commit “visa abuse” by remaining in the U.S. indefinitely.
