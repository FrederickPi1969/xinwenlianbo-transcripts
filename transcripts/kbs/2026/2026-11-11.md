## Space Agency Pursues Hybrid VTOL Aircraft Development

South Korea's space agency plans to launch a one-trillion-won research and development project to develop a five-seat-class hybrid vertical takeoff and landing aircraft, while also pursuing further development of a solar-powered stratospheric drone.
The Korea AeroSpace Administration(KASA) announced the plans Tuesday at a seminar on the aircraft development projects in central Seoul.
The hybrid aircraft, powered by both an engine and batteries, is designed to carry a range of passengers and cargo without requiring a runway.
KASA aims to develop four aircraft with a flight range of at least 200 kilometers, a cruising speed of at least 250 kilometers per hour and a payload capacity of at least 600 kilograms.
The five-year project is scheduled to run from 2028 to 2032, with a proposed budget of one trillion 86-point-one billion won, or 813 million U.S. dollars.
The agency also said the EAV-4 solar-powered stratospheric drone, which is being developed by the Korea Aerospace Research Institute, successfully completed a continuous flight of 170 hours and five minutes this year, with further development to be led by private companies.
The drone can fly for extended periods at altitudes of 10 to 20 kilometers and could be used for disaster monitoring and as an airborne telecommunications base station in areas with limited network coverage.

## Suneung Proctors Receive Training to Identify Smart Glasses

With the all-important Suneung, or College Scholastic Ability Test, just eight days away, education authorities conducted a special training program for proctoring personnel to prevent cheating using smart glasses.
The Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education said the training was conducted Wednesday for 239 personnel tasked with spotting such glasses, who will be assigned to exam venues in the capital next Thursday.
The personnel were educated on the key features of smart glasses and how to tell them apart from ordinary eyewear.
Smart glasses have thicker temples than ordinary glasses and have small camera lenses on the front part of their rims or on top of their nosepads.
Some have speaker or microphone holes or charging ports on the temples.
The education office decided not to reveal the content of the training program out of concerns that the information could be misused.
There are concerns that instances of cheating using such glasses will be missed this year, given that most exam venues will have to rely on visual inspections since specialized radio wave detectors for smart glasses have not been supplied.
During the Suneung, simply possessing smart glasses will result in the student’s exam being invalidated, while wearing or using them will also bar the student from taking the test next year.

## Data Shows 1 in 6 Pedestrians Cross Street while Looking at Smartphones

New data finds that one in six pedestrians crossed the street while looking at their smartphones and roughly 40 percent of these accidents involved people aged 20 or younger.
The Samsung Traffic Safety Research Institute, under the Samsung Fire and Marine Insurance Company, released the data on Wednesday, which marked Pedestrian Day.
The institute compiled the data after observing 14-thousand-21 pedestrians at eight crosswalks in Seoul’s downtown area, conducting a poll on 400 people, and looking at pedestrian accidents involving smartphone users that were reported to its auto insurance claims database during the past five years.
The institute also found that in 67-and-a-half percent of accidents involving “smombies,” a portmanteau of “smartphone” and “zombie,” the driver was deemed to be 100 percent at fault.
The institute said this is because current standards for determining fault do not consider the possibility of smartphone use as a contributing factor.
The Samsung Fire and Marine Insurance Company said the Road Traffic Act must stipulate that pedestrians have an obligation to be cautious while crossing the street.

## FTC Fines 15 Financial Firms over 1.6 Trillion Won for Bond Auction Collusion

The Fair Trade Commission(FTC) has decided to impose more than one-point-six trillion won in fines on 15 domestic financial firms for colluding in the government bond bidding process for years.
The FTC announced on Wednesday that it levied fines and corrective orders on five banks and 10 brokerages totaling about one-point-63 trillion won, or roughly one-point-two billion U.S. dollars, the largest amount ever in a South Korean collusion case.
Seven of the entities will be referred to the Serious Crime Investigation Agency due to their prolonged collusion and the high volume of bids involved.
According to the FTC, the 15 primary dealers exchanged information on their bidding yields by phone or the Telegram messaging app minutes before deadlines across 150 government bond auctions between January 2020 and June 2023.
Some firms even coordinated their interest rates and bidding volumes.
Government bonds are loans the state uses to raise money, paying back regular interest over time.
Only select financial firms are allowed to bid for these bonds at auction, where heavy competition often forces them to accept lower interest rates as well as smaller profits.
The entities involved in the collusion included Kookmin Bank, NongHyup Bank, Industrial Bank of Korea, Hana Bank and Korea Development Bank.
The brokerages included Kyobo Securities, Daishin Securities, Meritz Securities, Mirae Asset Securities, Samsung Securities, Shinhan Investment Corp., NH Investment & Securities, KB Securities, Kiwoom Securities and Korea Investment & Securities.
The FTC stated that this case constitutes a serious legal violation that effectively nullified the competitive bidding system for government bonds and caused losses to the national treasury.
The regulator pledged to continue taking strict action against bid-rigging in the public sector.

The nominee declined to comment on whether he would direct prosecutors to withdraw the president’s charges, saying it would be inappropriate to state an opinion.
He said he had neither been part of a group of ruling camp figures who sought to cancel the president’s indictments nor served as legal counsel in any of the cases under investigation, and he vowed to observe the proceedings objectively.
His perspective appears to differ from those of former Justice Minister Jung Sung-ho and former Minister nominee Kim Seung-won, both of whom said prosecutors should decide whether to withdraw the charges.

## Gas, Diesel Prices Fall Wednesday

The average retail prices of gasoline and diesel at gas stations nationwide fell Wednesday.
According to the Korea National Oil Corporation’s price information system, Opinet, as of 9 a.m., the national average for a liter of gasoline dropped zero-point-one won to one-thousand-855-point-03, or approximately one U.S. dollar and 38 cents.
The average price of diesel slid zero-point-39 won to one-thousand-841-point-46 won per liter.
The average price of gas in Seoul edged down zero-point-32 won to one-thousand-899-point-66 won per liter, while that of diesel slipped by zero-point-18 won to one-thousand-882-point-11 won.

## Top Diplomats from S. Korea, Ukraine Meet after N. Korean POW Dispute

Top diplomats from South Korea and Ukraine met in Paris on the sidelines of the Group of Seven Foreign Ministers' Meeting.
According to Seoul's foreign ministry, Foreign Minister Cho Hyun told Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha on Tuesday, local time, that mutual respect, trust and a sincere enforcement of agreements are prerequisites to the development of bilateral ties.
Cho then expressed hope to amicably resolve all issues between the two sides.
The remark comes after Seoul demanded that Kyiv apologize and promise preventive measures regarding its public disclosure that it had transferred North Korean prisoners of war(POW) to South Korea, in violation of a prior confidentiality agreement.
Sybiha called for continued diplomatic efforts to manage and develop Seoul-Kyiv relations in a stable manner and reaffirmed an earlier request for Cho to visit the country.
It was the first meeting of the two ministers since Seoul recalled its ambassador to Ukraine, Park Ki-chang, due to the POW dispute on October 8 and pulled out half of its embassy employees on October 20.
