## Expert Stresses Need to Preserve Culture of Jeju Haenyeo

A former South Korean ambassador to UNESCO says it’s necessary to conduct research on the culture of Jeju haenyeo, or women divers, and document their life stories to preserve it.
Park Sang-mi, a professor at Hankuk University of Foreign Studies, spoke Friday at a forum on Jeju Island marking the tenth anniversary of the inscription of the culture of Jeju haenyeo on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
Park said that over the past decade, the Jeju haenyeo culture has faced three structural challenges: aging; climate change and environmental changes; and commercialization and tourism.
She said that to preserve the women divers’ experience and knowledge, their life stories must be documented and digitized.
She also stressed the need to quickly establish a national haenyeo hall of fame.

## Fuel Price Ceilings Frozen for 4 Weeks

The ministry said the country has secured crude supplies at roughly 90 percent of last year’s average for September and October, and over 70 percent for November.

## IAEA Adopts Resolution Reaffirming N. Korea Can’t Have Nuclear Weapons State Status

The International Atomic Energy Agency(IAEA) has reaffirmed that North Korea cannot have the status of a nuclear weapons state in accordance with the Nuclear Nonproliferation Treaty(NPT).
At its 70th General Conference in Vienna on Thursday, the agency adopted a resolution on implementing the NPT safeguards agreement between the IAEA and North Korea.
It passed with 103 countries in favor, six opposed and 14 abstentions, with Russia and China among those opposed.
The resolution called on Pyongyang to immediately suspend all activities related to its nuclear weapons program, saying the North’s nuclear activities are persistently causing serious concerns.
In response, Kim Yo-jong, the powerful sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, stressed that the North’s status of a nuclear weapons state cannot be reversed by any means.
North Korea announced its withdrawal from the NPT — overseen by the IAEA — on January 10, 2003.
The IAEA has been monitoring North Korea’s nuclear activities using only satellite imagery and publicly available information since IAEA inspectors were expelled from the country in 2009.

## Suspected Flu Cases Surge 4.6-Fold amid Early Outbreak

The nation is seeing an unusually early flu season, with suspected influenza cases reaching four-point-six times the level recorded during the same period last year, particularly among elementary schoolchildren and younger children.
According to the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency on Friday, weekly monitoring of about 800 clinics found that the rate of influenza-like illness stood at 31 cases per one thousand people in the 37th week of the year, from September 6 to 12.
The rate has risen steadily, having posted nine-point-two cases per thousand in the 34th week, 13-point-three in the 35th and 25-point-three in the 36th.
The latest figure is four-point-six times last year’s and more than double that recorded during the 2023-2024 season, when flu activity persisted for an extended period.
The rate increased across all age groups, with children aged seven to 12 recording the highest rate at 79-point-six cases per one thousand people, followed by those aged one to six at 55-point-two and those aged 13 to 18 at 39-point-eight.
As the flu season began unusually early, health authorities issued an influenza epidemic advisory for the 2026-2027 season on Friday last week and moved up the start of the national vaccination programs for children and older adults.
Vaccination for children aged six months to 14 years starts September 21, a week earlier than usual, while vaccination for older adults will begin up to six days earlier, depending on age.

## N. Korea’s Nuclear Tests May Have Reactivated Fault Zones, Increased Earthquakes

North Korea’s six underground nuclear tests may have reactivated fault zones, increasing the prevalence of earthquakes in the region, according to a new study.
A joint team of researchers led by professor Kim Gwang-hui of the department of geological sciences at Pusan National University and professor Pan Xingli of Chengdu University of Technology analyzed seismic wave data in South Korea and northeastern China between 2008 and May 2025.
In a report published Friday in the international journal Science, the researchers said earthquakes were not observed in the studied areas before the North’s first nuclear test at its Punggye-ri site in North Hamgyong Province in 2006.
They wrote that seismic activity started being detected after the North conducted its third nuclear test in 2013, and around 60 earthquakes were recorded from that time until 2017, when the regime conducted its sixth test.
After the last test, which also marked the largest detonation, one-thousand-330 quakes were identified, with the magnitude of seismic activity gradually rising above three-point-zero between 2023 and 2024.
Kim estimated that the region could experience a powerful six-point-four magnitude earthquake if two fault zones where the tremors were concentrated — each one 24 kilometers in length — were to be energized at once.
