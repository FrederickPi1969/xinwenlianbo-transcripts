## Several European Newsrooms Receive Bomb Threats in Latest Wave of Hoaxes

Several European media outlets were forced to evacuate their offices after receiving bomb threats, amid a recent wave of hoaxes that has also disrupted flights across the continent.
Irish public service broadcaster RTÉ and Bulgaria's Nova TV were among the newsrooms that received the threats via email on Friday local time.
The purported sender, using the name "Mikhail Andropov," claimed that bombs inside the buildings would detonate in 45 minutes.
The sender stated they represented the leadership of an organization responsible for the recent wave of bomb threats against passenger flights across Europe.
No explosives were found at the targeted locations, but the evacuations disrupted broadcasting schedules.
Reuters, citing an airport source in Italy, reported that some of the emails sent to several airlines and airport operators in Rome and Milan had purported to be from "fighters of the Male State," a far-right anti-feminist movement banned in Russia.
Russia has denied involvement following claims that the hoaxes are part of a "hybrid" disruption campaign by Moscow targeting countries that support Ukraine.

## Average Gas, Diesel Prices Drop for 25th Straight Week

Average gasoline and diesel prices in South Korea fell for the 25th consecutive week, though week-on-week changes ground to a near standstill.
According to data from the Korea National Oil Corporation’s price information system on Saturday, the average price of a liter of gasoline in the first week of November fell zero-point-three won from the week prior to one-thousand-855-point-four won, or approximately one U.S. dollar and 36 cents.
The week-on-week decline has remained under one won per liter since the third week of September.
The average price of diesel dropped zero-point-one won to one-thousand-842 won per liter, with the week-on-week drop staying under one won since the fourth week of August.
Global oil prices fell this week amid expectations for a diplomatic resolution to the U.S.-Iran conflict and the easing of geopolitical risks stemming from trilateral peace talks between the U.S., Russia and Ukraine.
Seoul continues to maintain price caps on refiners' supplies to gas stations, keeping gasoline at at one-thousand-784 won per liter and diesel at one-thousand-773 won per liter to minimize the impact of global oil price fluctuations on domestic prices.

## S. Gyeongsang, Changwon Ship First-Ever Batch of Sweet Persimmons to China

The governments of South Gyeongsang Province and the city of Changwon have exported sweet persimmons to China for the first time.
An agricultural cooperative in Changwon held a ceremony Friday to mark the shipment of ten tons of sweet persimmons, alongside an additional nine-point-six tons from another cooperative in South Gyeongsang.
The provincial government said the shipment signifies the inaugural export of the fruit from a South Korean local government to China, following the conclusion of 17 years of quarantine negotiations last November.
The two local governments plan to hold promotional events next month at distribution markets in Shanghai and Nanjing.
South Gyeongsang Province leads South Korea in sweet persimmon production, and Changwon ranks as the province's top producer.

## Ex-US Envoy: Democratic Victory May Stall Seoul’s Sub, OPCON Goals

However, Yun noted that new initiatives like the OPCON transition and the nuclear-powered subs could be put on the back burner over the next two years of the Donald Trump administration due to a lack of momentum on either side.
The former envoy doubted the election would impact a potential summit between Trump and North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, as a meeting has yet to materialize in Trump's second term primarily due to Kim's lack of enthusiasm.
Yun said Trump's trade and tariffs policies are expected to continue, backed by full congressional support for securing investments from friendly nations.

## Early-Winter Chill Brings Season&#039;s First Ice to Seoul

An early winter cold wave brought the season’s first ice to Seoul on Saturday, driving morning temperatures across the country to their lowest points this fall.
According to the Korea Meteorological Administration, Seoul's first ice arrived ten days later than last year and four days later than the seasonal average.
First ice was also reported in Incheon, Daejeon and Cheongju.
Morning lows dropped by as much as ten degrees Celsius from Friday, with temperatures plunging to minus nine degrees on Mount Seorak in Gangwon Province as cold air rushed in from the northwest.
The inter-Korean border areas of Cheorwon and Paju recorded minus five-point-three and minus five degrees, respectively, while Seoul hovered around zero.
The cold weather is expected to continue into Sunday morning.

## Mirae Asset Reports Data Breach Affecting Customers, Employees

In the latest of a growing wave of cyberattacks targeting South Korea’s financial sector, Mirae Asset Securities has confirmed that hackers breached its systems, compromising the personal data of thousands of customers and employees.
The brokerage said on Saturday that unauthorized external access to its mobile web phone-authentication login service and internal groupware system occurred Wednesday and Thursday.
The account numbers of approximately three-thousand-800 clients and the personal information of 35-thousand-255 current, former and contract employees were leaked.
Mirae Asset said the breach compromised names, employee numbers, email addresses, mobile phone numbers and encrypted passwords, but noted that client account names, resident registration numbers and passport numbers were not exposed.
Following the incident, the firm immediately blocked the affected access points and triggered an emergency response system.
Prior to the Mirae Asset case, financial authorities stated that 37 financial companies had reported 40 data breaches between late September and November 3, during which the personal information of roughly 470-thousand records was compromised.
