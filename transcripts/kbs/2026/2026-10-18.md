## IMF Backs S. Korea's Policy Mix, Supports Future Response Fund

The International Monetary Fund has endorsed the South Korean government's mix of fiscal and monetary policies, including its plan to establish a Future Response Fund.
The Ministry of Economy and Finance said Sunday that IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva made the assessment during a meeting with Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il in Bangkok on Saturday on the sidelines of the International Monetary and Financial Committee (IMFC) meeting.
Georgieva noted that the IMF had significantly upgraded its growth outlook for South Korea and said the country's policy mix of moderately tight monetary policy and neutral fiscal policy was appropriate.
She added that setting aside surplus funds in the Future Response Fund without adding pressure on monetary policy would help create a buffer against future economic shocks and said the issue could be discussed in greater detail during the IMF's annual consultations with South Korea in November.
Lee and Georgieva also signed a letter of intent to continue cooperation on South Korea's participation in the IMF's global artificial intelligence hub and on capacity-development initiatives.
Separately, Lee met with Georgieva and IMF First Deputy Managing Director Dan Katz on the sidelines of the IMFC meeting and briefed them on South Korea's efforts to pursue sustainable growth and reduce economic disparities.

## Won Hits 2-Year High Against Dollar, 19-Year High Against Yen

The South Korean won has strengthened against the U.S. dollar this month, pushing the average exchange rate to its strongest level in more than two years amid robust semiconductor exports and increased dollar supply.
According to the Bank of Korea's Economic Statistics System, the average won-dollar exchange rate over the first 12 trading days of October stood at one-thousand-342-point-six won per dollar, the lowest monthly average since September 2024, when it averaged one-thousand-332-point-eight won.
The exchange rate climbed to one-thousand-528 won in June amid rising oil prices and foreign investors' stock sell-offs but has since fallen as exporters, particularly semiconductor companies, increased dollar sales on the back of a sustained trade surplus.
On October 16, the won-dollar rate fell as low as one-thousand-328-point-nine won, its lowest level since October 2, 2024, even as the U.S. Dollar Index rose from about 99 to 102 over the past month amid higher U.S. interest rates.
The won has also strengthened against the Japanese yen. The average won-yen exchange rate fell to 849 won per 100 yen in October, its lowest level since December 2007, when it stood at 829-point-two won.
Analysts say South Korea's economic fundamentals are supporting the won's strength, although exchange-rate volatility is likely to persist amid uncertainty in the global economy.

## Heavy Rain to Sweep Nationwide Before Temperatures Plunge

Cloudy skies and heavy rain accompanied by thunder and lightning are expected across most of the country Sunday, with precipitation easing in many areas by Monday morning.
According to the Korea Meteorological Administration, rainfall through Monday is forecast at 30 to 80 millimeters in the Seoul metropolitan area and the Chungcheong and Jeolla regions, 20 to 70 millimeters in inland Gangwon, and up to 60 millimeters of rain in the Gyeongsang region.
Through early Monday, some areas could receive downpours of 20 to 30 millimeters per hour, accompanied by strong winds and thunderstorms, while coastal and mountainous areas could experience wind gusts exceeding 20 meters per second.
The rain is expected to taper off across most regions by Monday morning, ushering in cooler autumn weather.
Monday morning temperatures are forecast to range from seven to 19 degrees Celsius, with Seoul expected to see a low of 12 degrees. Daytime highs will range from 19 to 24 degrees, including 20 degrees in the capital.

## National Pension Service Suffers 182 Trillion Won Stock Loss in 3 Months

South Korea's National Pension Service is estimated to have incurred more than 182 trillion won, 13-point-six million U.S. dollars, in stock valuation losses in just over three months amid a recent market downturn.
According to financial data provider FnGuide on Sunday, the combined value of shares held by the pension fund in more than 200 listed companies in which it owns stakes of at least five percent stood at an estimated 333-point-53 trillion won as of Thursday.
The figure was down 182-point-three trillion won from 515-point-92 trillion won at the end of June.
The decline marked the first quarterly drop in the value of the pension fund's stock holdings in seven quarters, dating back to the fourth quarter of 2024.
SK hynix and Samsung Electronics accounted for more than 131 trillion won of the losses, with the value of the pension fund's holdings in the companies falling by about 74-point-two trillion won and 56-point-eight trillion won, respectively.
During the period, SK hynix shares plunged from two-point-65 million won to one-point-26 million won, while Samsung Electronics shares dropped from 334-thousand won to 210-thousand won.

## S. Korea&#039;s Potential Growth Rate Set to Rebound for First Time in 4 Decades

South Korea's potential growth rate is projected to increase for a second consecutive year, marking the first such rebound in four decades, driven by a boom in the semiconductor industry, according to the International Monetary Fund's latest forecasts.
In its October World Economic Outlook, the IMF projected South Korea's potential growth rate at two-point-14 percent this year and two-point-two percent next year, up from one-point-94 percent last year, reversing a long-term downward trend despite structural challenges, including an aging population and a shrinking labor force.
Potential growth refers to the pace at which an economy's potential gross domestic product can expand sustainably without generating inflationary pressure.
Unlike actual GDP growth, which can receive temporary boosts from fiscal or monetary stimulus, potential growth reflects an economy's underlying capacity for long-term expansion.
South Korea's potential growth rate is expected to rise for two consecutive years for the first time since the "three lows" boom of 1986 and 1987, when low oil prices, low interest rates and a weak U.S. dollar helped fuel economic growth.
Among 27 economies with comparable IMF estimates, South Korea is projected to rank seventh in both 2026 and 2027, up from 12th place last year.

## Police Launch Full Investigation Into Deadly Bucheon Factory Fire

Police have launched a full-scale investigation into a factory fire in Bucheon, Gyeonggi Province, that killed one firefighter and injured seven others.
The Gyeonggi Nambu Provincial Police Agency said Sunday that its provincial investigative unit was questioning people involved in the case, including the factory owner and employees, as part of a 33-member task force established to investigate the blaze.
Investigators are examining the cause of the fire, the handling and storage of hazardous materials, and whether adequate fire prevention measures were in place.
They are also investigating whether the company failed to meet its safety management responsibilities and whether those found responsible could face charges of professional negligence resulting in death or injury, as well as negligent fire-related offenses.
Separately, special judicial police officers from fire authorities are investigating the types and quantities of hazardous materials at the factory and whether the company obtained the required permits.
The fire broke out at about 8:40 a.m. Friday at a metal injection molding and printing factory in Bucheon, and was extinguished about two and a half hours later.

## President Lee Pays Tribute to Firefighter Killed in Bucheon Factory Fire

President Lee Jae Myung paid tribute Saturday to a firefighter who died while battling a factory fire in Bucheon.
Lee visited the funeral hall at Soonchunhyang University Bucheon Hospital on Saturday evening to pay his respects to firefighter Kim Jin-seok, who died in the line of duty earlier that morning.
In a message in the condolence book, Lee wrote that the public would forever remember Kim's sacrifice while living the safe, everyday lives he helped protect.
Lee placed the posthumously awarded Okjo Order of Service Merit before Kim's portrait, offered incense and a moment of silence, and expressed condolences to the bereaved family.
Lee also wished a speedy recovery to another firefighter injured in the blaze and instructed Interior and Safety Minister Yun Ho-jung to provide all necessary support for the firefighter's treatment and recovery.
Kim was among the first firefighters dispatched to a factory fire in Bucheon on Friday. He died at about 12:55 a.m. Saturday after being transported to a hospital for treatment.

## S. Korea Voices &#039;Disappointment and Regret&#039; over Takaichi&#039;s Yasukuni Offering

The government expressed disappointment and regret over Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's offering of a ritual gift to Yasukuni Shrine, which honors Japan's war dead, including Class-A war criminals convicted after World War II.
In a statement Saturday, South Korea's Foreign Ministry said it was deeply disappointed and regretful that Japanese leaders had once again sent offerings to or paid respects at the shrine, which Seoul says glorifies Japan's militarist past and enshrines convicted war criminals.
The ministry urged Japanese leaders to confront history directly and demonstrate sincere reflection and remorse for their country's past through concrete actions, stressing that such efforts are essential to building future-oriented relations based on trust between the two countries and their peoples.
Takaichi offered a masakaki, a ritual tree, in her capacity as prime minister Saturday to mark the shrine's autumn festival but did not visit in person.
Other Cabinet members and senior figures in Japan's ruling coalition also sent offerings or visited the shrine.
