## S. Korea Military, UN Command Created Task Force Early This Year to Discuss MDL Standard

South Korea’s military and the UN Command have been holding discussions since early this year in an effort to address discrepancies in how the two Koreas and the UN Command lay out the military demarcation line(MDL).
The head of the defense ministry’s policy planning bureau, Woo Kyung-suk, said in a briefing Tuesday that the South’s military and the UN Command formed a task force early this year, intending to first resolve their differing standards before tackling North Korea-related issues.
The South’s military apparently decided to pursue the formation of the task force after North Korea failed to reply to the South Korean defense ministry’s proposal, made last November, to hold military talks on how to map out the MDL.
Woo was quick to add, however, that aligning the MDL with the UN Command’s standard is not a prerequisite for the inter-Korean military talks.
South Korea uses an MDL marked on military maps under a standard the U.S. National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency established in 2004, while the UN Command uses a line connecting the coordinates created under its MDL Project in 2016.
The South’s military believes the North uses its own MDL baseline.
The long-standing discrepancies in how South Korea, North Korea and the UN Command map out the MDL has been cited as the one of the reasons for the instability in the Demilitarized Zone.

## Financial Authorities Advise Caution via Consumer Alert System amid Hacking Attacks

Amid a wave of hacking attacks in South Korea’s financial sector, financial authorities have advised caution via their consumer alert system.
The warning followed an emergency meeting of financial firms on Tuesday convened by the Financial Supervisory Service and the Financial Services Commission, which have also decided to implement special response measures for the next month in case of secondary damage from personal information leaks.
During the monthlong period, financial institutions will operate desks tasked with providing consultations on personal data breaches.
If they detect or suspect secondary damage, such as any theft of customer funds, they must promptly report it to financial regulators.
The financial industry is enhancing its fraud detection systems and will swiftly share information on suspected data breaches via a platform dubbed ASAP, short for the AI-Based Anti-Phishing Sharing and Analysis Platform, as part of efforts to protect customers.
So far, financial authorities have not confirmed any instances of secondary damage from the latest data breaches.
Authorities warned consumers that even though no passwords were leaked, the hackers behind the latest attacks could use personal information that was previously leaked to pass themselves off as legitimate loan consultants, or could lure victims by pretending to offer compensation for data breaches.

## Justice Ministry: Nation Has 52 Inmates on Death Row, Aged 58.7 on Average

The justice ministry disclosed the figures Tuesday, four days ahead of World Day against the Death Penalty.
The ministry said ten of the inmates are in their 60s, seven are in their 70s, 29 are in their 50s, five are in their 40s, and one is in their 30s.
The figures show that the population of death row inmates is aging while the nation continues to mull whether to keep or abolish the death penalty.
South Korea has been categorized as an “abolitionist in practice” country by Amnesty International since 2007, having not carried out an execution since late 1997, but capital punishment is still possible under domestic law.
Observed every year on October 10, World Day against the Death Penalty aims to promote and consolidate awareness of the worldwide movement against the death penalty.
The day was first organized by the World Coalition Against the Death Penalty in 2003.
