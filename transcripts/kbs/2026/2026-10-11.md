## Scattered Showers Forecast Nationwide Monday

Scattered showers are forecast across the country on Monday, while skies will remain partly cloudy nationwide.
According to the Korea Meteorological Administration on Sunday, inland areas are expected to receive between five and ten millimeters of rain on Monday.
Dry conditions in inland parts of the central region are expected to heighten the risk of wildfires and other fires.
Morning lows will range from 13 to 20 degrees Celsius, up one to four degrees from Sunday, while Seoul is expected to start the day at 17 degrees.
Afternoon highs are forecast to reach 22 to 28 degrees, similar to Sunday's levels, with Seoul expected to peak at 26 degrees.
Waves will remain relatively low, ranging from zero-point-five to two meters across all coastal waters.

## Lee Reaffirms Commitment to Public Engagement Amid Criticism

President Lee Jae Myung said he will continue engaging directly with the public through social media and in-person visits with minimal security, rejecting criticism that such efforts undermine the dignity or authority of the presidency.

Lee said there was little reason to remain in politics unless the goal was to build a better society where more people could live happier lives.
Recalling his first presidential campaign, Lee said his determination to stay close to the public had never changed.
He stressed that politics and power should serve ordinary and underprivileged people rather than the powerful and privileged.

## Interior Minister Orders Inspection of Agency Official Over Criticism of Crime Agency Chief Nominee

Interior and Safety Minister Yun Ho-jung has ordered an inspection of Im Eun-jeong, head of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency's Gwangju office, over her public criticism of Kim Ji-yong, the nominee to lead the agency.
In a post on X on Sunday, Yun said he would instruct the agency to launch an inspection into Im, criticizing her for treating the agency as a political organization rather than a law enforcement body.
Yun said it was troubling that a senior official at an agency established to uphold social justice by combating corruption, economic crime, cybercrime and drug offenses appeared focused on criticizing the agency chief nominee.
He also expressed concern that prosecutors' past practices of political intervention and collective action could carry over into the new agency.
The order, issued under the law governing the agency's organization and operations, marks Yun's first use of supervisory authority over the agency since its launch.
Yun said the nominee's vetting should be handled by the president and the National Assembly through a confirmation hearing, in accordance with the Constitution and relevant laws.

## Houthi Attack on Saudi Airport Kills 12, Injures Over 300

An international airport in Saudi Arabia's capital, Riyadh, came under attack for the third time in four days on Saturday, leaving 12 people dead and more than 300 others injured.
Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation said the attack struck King Khalid International Airport on Saturday afternoon.
The Associated Press reported that a missile hit a building housing Terminals 3 and 4, prompting authorities to suspend operations and divert or cancel all incoming flights.
A senior Houthi official claimed responsibility for the attack, saying it was retaliation for Saudi airstrikes on civilian facilities in Yemen, after the Saudi-led coalition launched an operation last Sunday with Yemeni government forces to retake Houthi-controlled territory.
The Houthis have since responded with missile attacks targeting Saudi airports and oil facilities, with four people, including pilots, killed in the two previous attacks.
The United States, Britain and Germany have advised their citizens to avoid the airport. U.S. President Donald Trump said he would review whether the United States should join Saudi military operations, signaling the possibility of direct U.S. military involvement.

## Low Birthrate Drives Sharp Decline in S. Korea Childcare Centers

The number of childcare centers in South Korea has fallen by more than 40 percent from its 2013 peak, underscoring the impact of the country's prolonged low birthrate on early childhood education and care services.
According to the Ministry of Health and Welfare on Sunday, South Korea had 26-thousand-64 childcare centers last year, down four-point-eight percent, or about 13-hundred facilities, from a year earlier.
The number of childcare centers stood at about one-thousand in the 1990s before surpassing 40-thousand in 2012. However, it began a steady decline after reaching a peak of 43-thousand in 2013.
The total number of centers fell by 40-point-five percent, or 17-thousand-706 facilities, between 2013 and last year, largely because declining enrollment and financial difficulties linked to the low birthrate have led to more closures than new openings.
Kindergartens, which serve slightly older children, have also been in decline. Their number fell for an eighth consecutive year last year to eight thousand-140, down from a peak of nine-thousand-29 in 2017.
Amid the deepening demographic crisis, the government is pursuing the integration of early childhood education and childcare under a unified system and aims to complete the reform by 2030 through legislative changes.

## NIS: US Intelligence Sharing Remains Broad Despite Some Restrictions

South Korea's spy agency said Sunday that intelligence cooperation with the United States remains active across a wide range of areas, rejecting reports that Washington has limited sharing on North Korea to missile-related information.
In a statement Sunday, the National Intelligence Service (NIS) said reports claiming the United States was providing only information on North Korean missiles because of intelligence-sharing restrictions were "entirely untrue."
The agency said intelligence cooperation remains active in such areas as North Korean missile and conventional military capabilities, developments inside the North, international affairs, cyber threats and counterintelligence.
The NIS, however, acknowledged that some information tied to the issue the South Korean government had previously identified as the cause of the restrictions remains subject to limitations.
The United States is understood to have restricted the sharing of information on North Korean nuclear facilities and related matters following remarks by Unification Minister Chung Dong-young in March about a uranium enrichment facility in Kusong, North Korea.
The NIS said the overall volume of intelligence provided by the United States had not declined despite restrictions in specific areas. It added that intelligence sharing between the allies remains reciprocal and mutually beneficial across multiple fields.

## N. Korea Marks Workers&#039; Party Anniversary Without Military Parade

North Korea marked the 81st anniversary of the founding of its ruling Workers' Party on Saturday with celebratory performances but did not hold a military parade as it did last year.
The North's state-run Korean Central News Agency reported Sunday that a commemorative performance was held at Pyongyang Indoor Stadium and attended by leader Kim Jong-un, senior party and government officials, and foreign diplomats stationed in the country.
The celebrations contrasted with last year's 80th anniversary, a major milestone, when North Korea staged a military parade attended by senior officials from China and Russia, as well as the leaders of Vietnam and Laos.
State media photographs showed Kim's wife, Ri Sol-ju, and their daughter, Ju-ae, watching the performance alongside him.
State media did not report any speech or public remarks by Kim. At last year's anniversary parade, he reiterated his commitment to strengthening the country's defense capabilities.

## US Official: US Expects S. Korea to Uphold Alliance Standards on Intelligence, Counterintelligence

A senior United States official said Washington expects South Korea to uphold the standards of their decades-long alliance amid strains in intelligence cooperation between the two countries.
Responding to a KBS inquiry on Saturday, the official said Washington expects Seoul to uphold the standards of their decades-long alliance, particularly on intelligence and counterintelligence matters; however, the official did not specify the standards or say whether South Korea had violated them.
The United States has restricted intelligence sharing with South Korea on North Korea-related issues for more than six months after Unification Minister Chung Dong-young publicly mentioned a uranium enrichment facility in Kusong, North Pyongan Province, in March.
During a parliamentary audit on Thursday, Joint Chiefs of Staff Chairman Jin Yong-sung said some key U.S. intelligence on North Korea's nuclear activities remained subject to restrictions.
He added that the limitations had not affected South Korea's military readiness and said a swift resolution would be preferable.
It also recently emerged that South Korea's National Intelligence Service (NIS) was excluded, at Washington's request, from a trilateral working-group meeting on North Korean cyber threats held in the United States in June.
The NIS said there had been some misunderstandings several months earlier but added that the issue had already been resolved.
