## Ukraine Says N. Korean POWs Sent to S. Korea After Direct Talks

The Ukrainian presidential office said Thursday that the transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war to South Korea was carried out through direct talks with Seoul, describing the matter as “highly sensitive.”
It said the two men are ethnic Koreans and that South Korea considers them its citizens, while denying reports that Russia had repeatedly requested their transfer and saying Moscow had shown no interest in them.
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy disclosed the previous day in his U.N. General Assembly speech in New York that Ukraine had recently sent the two POWs to South Korea, while Seoul effectively confirmed their transfer without directly denying it.
Asked whether Ukraine and South Korea had agreed to keep the transfer confidential, the Ukrainian presidential office said it would have been unusual for an open country to secretly send captured prisoners somewhere, adding that although the full circumstances were lengthy and detailed, Ukraine had only disclosed the fact that the two men had been sent to South Korea.
President Lee Jae Myung criticized Zelenskyy for publicly disclosing the transfer, saying on social media that he did not know the circumstances behind the decision but found it regrettable that Zelenskyy had violated an agreement to keep the matter confidential.
The two North Korean soldiers were reportedly captured by Ukrainian forces in Russia’s Kursk region in January last year, and South Korea has said it would accept them as South Korean citizens under the Constitution after they expressed their intention to defect.

## S. Korea, Mexico Adopt 2026-2030 Joint Action Plan to Expand Cooperation

South Korea and Mexico adopted a joint action plan to further advance bilateral cooperation and exchanges on the occasion of the summit talks between their leaders.
President Lee Jae Myung on Thursday announced the singing of the South Korea-Mexico Joint Action Plan 2026-2030 in a joint press conference with Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum after their summit in Mexico City.
The action plan included an agreement to promote regular high-level exchanges and hold regular vice-ministerial policy consultations between the two foreign ministries, while also strengthening cooperation in multilateral forums such as Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation(APEC) and the G20.
The two leaders also agreed to expand cooperation in artificial intelligence and digital transformation and adopted an AI cooperation declaration to that end, while pledging to work together on the implementation of the APEC AI Initiative when Mexico chairs APEC in 2028.
On defense cooperation, Lee expressed hope that South Korean defense products with strong performance and competitiveness could contribute to Mexico's military modernization, while the two sides also agreed to strengthen cooperation in other areas including aerospace.
The summit also resulted in the final agreement on revised text for the bilateral investment protection agreement after seven years of negotiations, while the two sides shared the need to conclude a social security agreement to ease the burden of double payment of pension insurance contributions for workers dispatched between the two countries.
The leaders also agreed to build on the popularity of K-pop and K-food in Mexico to expand cultural exchanges and discussed establishing a K-Culture Center in Mexico City.

## Lee Pledges to Work Harder for All Citizens in Chuseok Greeting

President Lee Jae Myung said that he would work harder to ensure that a bright light could shine on the lives of every citizen, regardless of their qualifications, conditions or differences in views.
Lee made the remarks in a holiday greeting video released Friday, filmed with his wife Kim Hea Kyung to mark Chuseok, saying it is a time when South Koreans can see the same full moon wherever they are in the world.
He also expressed his deep gratitude and respect to citizens quietly going about their daily lives, including sanitation workers, farmers and fishermen, military personnel, police officers, firefighters and medical workers, as well as overseas Koreans working hard to represent the country.
Lee said that although people live in different places, do different jobs and have followed different paths, they share a common affection for their country, adding that he was grateful and honored as president to be with the “great” Korean people.”
Kim also wished everyone a meaningful Chuseok holiday filled with sharing greetings with loved ones and happiness with those they cherish.
Lee left South Korea on Monday for a trip to the United States and Mexico and is scheduled to return home on Sunday.

## S. Korea, 79 Nations Call for Immediate Reopening of Strait of Hormuz at UN

South Korea and 79 other countries have issued a joint statement calling for the immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and condemning armed provocations by Iran and the Iran-aligned Houthi rebels in Yemen, as well as disruptions to international trade.
Bahraini Foreign Minister Abdullatif bin Rashid Al Zayani Thursday read out the statement at a press conference at the United Nations headquarters in New York.
In the statement, joined by South Korea, Gulf and Arab countries and major Western nations, the 80 countries said Iran's actions in the Strait of Hormuz continued to threaten international security and the rights and freedom of navigation.
They also strongly condemned the Houthis' decision to resume the conflict in Yemen, their continued attacks on Saudi Arabia and their reckless attacks in the Red Sea.
The countries reiterated their call for the urgent and full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and safe, secure and unimpeded passage of vessels without tolls, conditions or illegal charges in accordance with international law under the U.N. Convention on the Law of the Sea.
The countries then called for the full restoration of free transit without delay in accordance with navigational rights and freedoms.

## S. Korea, Mexico to Launch Oil, Supply Chain Dialogue

President Lee Jae Myung said Thursday that South Korea and Mexico have agreed to launch an intergovernmental dialogue channel on oil and other critical resources as part of efforts to strengthen supply chain cooperation.
Speaking at a joint press conference after his summit with Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum in Mexico City, Lee said the two countries would continue working together to ensure stable oil imports and explore ways to expand bilateral trade and investment, stressing the need for a trade agreement to create a stable business environment.
He also said the two sides successfully concluded seven years of negotiations to revise their bilateral investment treaty and would work to bring the revised agreement into force as soon as possible after completing necessary procedures, including parliamentary ratification.
On advanced industries, Lee said a South Korean public-private delegation would visit Mexico in November to discuss digital cooperation and identify specific projects aimed at strengthening the competitiveness of the aerospace industries of both countries through the South Korea-Mexico Space Day event in Mexico.
The president said the two countries would also explore ways for South Korea to contribute to Mexico's military modernization and jointly enter third-country markets through local defense production.
Lee added that the two countries had agreed to strengthen cooperation in areas including culture, agriculture, public security and anti-corruption, while establishing an institutional foundation to jointly tackle transnational crimes such as online fraud, human trafficking and drug trafficking.
On the Korean Peninsula, Lee said Sheinbaum expressed clear support for South Korea's policy of peaceful coexistence, adding that the two countries would continue to work closely for peace and stability on the peninsula and in the international community.
