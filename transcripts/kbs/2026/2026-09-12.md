## Pres. Office Says Gender Minister Nominee Should Get Chance to Address Allegations

The presidential office says it believes Gender Equality Minister nominee Yong Hye-in should be given an opportunity to address the allegations against her during a parliamentary confirmation hearing.
Chief presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung on Friday relayed the top office's position in response to a media inquiry about the possibility of President Lee Jae Myung withdrawing Yong's nomination.
Kang said the presidential office continues to pay heed to various concerns raised by the public.
Yong, who is serving a second term in the National Assembly as a proportional representative, has come under fire for insisting that she keep her seat as the only lawmaker of the minor Basic Income Party, even after she assumes the Cabinet role.
Although all lawmakers are legally permitted to serve as Cabinet members, it is customary for proportional representatives to give up their seats to allow the next candidate on the proportional representation list to take over.
Yong has also been accused of nepotism regarding her husband's appointment to a paid party position and faces allegations of neglecting to address sexual discrimination inside an unofficial political faction of the Labor Party, of which she was previously a member.

## Cloudy Skies, Big Temperature Swings Forecast for Sunday

Cloudy skies are expected nationwide on Sunday, with a big swing in temperatures from day to night.
According to the Korea Meteorological Administration, expect morning lows to range between 12 and 21 degrees Celsius, and daytime highs to peak between 23 and 31 degrees.
The weather agency has advised the public to watch for temperature fluctuations of up to 15 degrees, particularly in inland regions.
Areas in Seoul, Incheon, northern Gyeonggi Province and central and northern Gangwon Province may receive a light sprinkle of rain in the early hours of Sunday, while mountainous areas of Jeju Island are expected to receive up to 10 mm of precipitation later in the afternoon.

## Unionized Workers of Seoul's Intracity Buses Vote to Strike for Higher Wages

Unionized workers of Seoul's intracity buses voted to launch a strike to demand for a wage increase.
The union said that of its 18-thousand-296 members, 16-thousand-716 of them cast their ballot on Friday, with 96-point-25 percent of them voting in support of a walkout scheduled for next Wednesday.
Labor and management failed to reach a deal following nine rounds of negotiations since March, leading the union to request labor dispute mediation late last month.
The union wants basic wages to be calculated on a monthly standard of 176 work hours and to increase the hourly rate by seven-point-85 percent, which would result in higher allowances for employees.
If mediation falls through again next Tuesday, the union will likely push ahead with the strike.

## Local Zoo Unveils Baby Giant Panda Shu Bao

Anchor: Giant pandas are immensely popular in South Korea, sparking a massive cultural phenomenon known locally as "panda fever." It all started with a single superstar cub named Fu Bao and the deep bond she shared with her primary zookeeper, Kang Cheol-won. Fu Bao has since returned to China, and her old zookeeper is now caring for a new giant panda cub.
Report: The Everland theme park has unveiled the latest addition to its Panda World — a giant panda cub named Shu Bao, who celebrated her 100th day of life on Friday.
Kang Cheol-won, a veteran zookeeper at Everland who is widely known as "panda grandpa" for his loving care of the park's celebrity pandas, introduced the cub to the media and fans for the first time.
[Sound bite: Kang Cheol-won, Zookeeper, Everland theme park (Korean-English)]
"Shu Bao was born on June 3, and today marks her 100th day. Her name means 'a treasure as bright as the dawn light.' Like her older sisters and her parents, her name includes 'Bao' and she has now received her own name."
Shu Bao was born at the theme park near Seoul, and her name was selected through a public naming event that drew some ten-thousand participants, according to the zoo.
[Sound bite: Kang Cheol-won, Zookeeper, Everland theme park (Korean-English)]
"Unlike her older sisters, our fourth Bao seems especially gentle and easygoing. We're hoping that she will grow up to be an exceptionally kind panda."
She is the fourth cub born to giant pandas Ai Bao and Le Bao, who were brought to South Korea from China in March 2016, in line with agreements reached during a South Korea-China summit two years earlier.
Fu Bao, the panda couple's first and most popular cub, born in South Korea in 2020, returned to China in April 2024.
They had twins Rui Bao and Hui Bao in 2023.
An Everland official said they are considering revealing Shu Bao to the general public within the year after closely monitoring her growth and how well she adapts to the outside environment.
Under the global CITES international conservation agreement, all giant pandas—even those born outside of China—are the property of the Chinese government, and must be sent back before they turn four years old.

## Lee to Host Inaugural South Korea-Central Asia Summit Next Week

President Lee Jae Myung will host the first South Korea-Central Asia summit next Wednesday, bringing together the leaders from Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan.
During a meeting with his key aides on Friday, Lee discussed the plan to deepen economic ties with the resource-rich region.
[Sound bite: President Lee Jae Myung (Korean-English)]
"Next week, the first South Korea–Central Asia summit will be held in Seoul. Central Asia is the geopolitical center of the Eurasian continent and is also a region with very high growth potential, possessing abundant energy resources and critical minerals. ...Through this summit, relations between South Korea and Central Asia will be consolidated into a leader-level multilateral cooperation framework, and the New Northern Policy we have pursued is expected to gain momentum."
Lee is slated to hold bilateral talks with the leaders of the five Central Asian nations from Monday through Wednesday, ahead of the multilateral gathering.
The president's chief spokesperson said the gathering will provide an opportunity to diversify South Korea’s global supply chains by linking its energy exploration and mineral processing technology with Central Asia’s abundant critical mineral and energy resources.

## Pres. Office Condemns N. Korea's Missile Launch as Violation of UNSC Resolutions

The presidential office has called North Korea's latest ballistic missile launch a violation of United Nations Security Council resolutions.

## First Relief Team Returns Home after 10-Day Search Mission in Flood-Stricken Nepal

South Korea's first disaster relief team has returned home following ten days of search and rescue work in flood-ravaged Nepal.
The foreign ministry stated that the 44-member team arrived at Seoul Air Base in Seongnam, Gyeonggi Province, around 1:30 a.m. on Saturday aboard a KC-330 military transport aircraft.
The team cooperated with Nepali authorities in the search for nine missing South Koreans from the Korea South-East Power Company and Doosan Enerbility who were working on the Upper Trishuli-1 hydropower project in the country's Rasuwa District.
While one Chinese survivor was found during the search, rescue teams failed to locate any trace of the missing South Koreans.
Nepal has since shifted the focus of its response to reconstruction and recovery, and South Korea has sent another team to support those efforts.
The second team of eight officials from the ministry, the National Fire Agency, the Korea International Cooperation Agency, and the National Medical Center is expected to work in Nepal for around one week.

## North Korea Fires Multiple Ballistic Missiles a Day after 3-Way Freedom Edge Drills End

Anchor: North Korea fired multiple ballistic missiles toward the East Sea on Saturday. The South Korean military said it detected the early morning launch, which came a day after trilateral military drills between South Korea, the United States and Japan wrapped up near the Korean Peninsula.
Report: The short-range ballistic missiles were fired at around 5:20 a.m. from an area near Wonsan on North Korea's eastern coast, and flew around 250 kilometers towards the East Sea, according to South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff(JCS).
South Korea and the U.S. are analyzing radar and other data to determine details about the projectiles, which appeared to fly toward mock targets on an island in the East Sea.
Sources suggest that the latest projectiles consist of the North's Hwasong-11 line short-range ballistic missile as well as ammunition from 600-millimeter multiple rocket launchers.
The JCS said South Korean and U.S. intelligence authorities tracked developments from the initial stage of the launch and shared information with Japan.
Pyongyang's action came a day after Seoul, Washington and Tokyo wrapped up their five-day Freedom Edge trilateral military exercise in waters off the southern island of Jeju.
Earlier on Monday, North Korea's new Defense Minister Kim Song-gi issued a statement condemning the joint military drills, calling them a U.S.-led war rehearsal.
South Korea's presidential National Security Office convened an emergency meeting and urged the North to stop ballistic missile launches that violate UN Security Council resolutions.
