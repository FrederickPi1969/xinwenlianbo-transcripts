## Presidential Message on National Cybersecurity Awareness Month

Presidential Message on National Cybersecurity Awareness Month
The White House
October 2, 2026
In an age of rapid digital advancement, cyberspace has emerged as the new frontier of American innovation, prosperity, and strength.  As new technologies open doors to discovery and opportunity that were once unimaginable, a secure cyberspace is essential to ensuring that America continues to lead the world in finance, military power, emerging technology, and manufacturing.  During National Cybersecurity Awareness Month, my Administration reaffirms our dedication to bolstering our Nation’s cybersecurity, protecting the American People, and unleashing the Golden Age of Cyberspace.

Every American relies on the digital world for information, economic opportunity, and the simple routines of daily life.  Freedom and security in cyberspace, however, can never be taken for granted.  For too long, adversaries and cybercriminals exploited our connected way of life to target our citizens, communities, and businesses without consequence.  My Administration has put an end to this era of weakness.

As President, I signed an Executive Order to combat cybercrime, fraud, and predatory schemes and stop foreign criminal networks from preying on vulnerable Americans.  In March, I released my Cyber Strategy for America—a bold vision for the future of American cybersecurity and innovation.  My Administration is already putting this strategy into action through the creation of “GOLD EAGLE,” a partnership between the Federal Government and leading American companies to harness the power of American Super Intelligence to identify cybersecurity vulnerabilities at rapid speed and stay ahead of our adversaries, defend the American People from emerging threats, and safeguard the critical infrastructure vital to our national security and global competitiveness.

As we secure America’s digital future, we are also preparing the next generation to lead it.  Through First Lady Melania Trump’s leadership on the Presidential Super Intelligence Challenge, we have inspired young Americans to embrace emerging technologies, develop new skills, and find solutions to challenges in their own communities.  The First Lady’s Fostering the Future Together initiative also continues to empower students, parents, and educators to learn, grow, and thrive through the safe and innovative use of advanced technology. This month, I encourage every American to do their own part to stay safe online by creating strong passwords, keeping software up to date, and remaining vigilant against scams.  Together, we can strengthen our Nation’s cybersecurity, safeguard the technologies that power our daily lives, and secure America’s place as the leader in the world’s digital age.

## Presidential Message on National Domestic Violence Awareness Month

Presidential Message on National Domestic Violence Awareness Month
The White House
October 2, 2026
In communities across our great Nation, domestic violence destroys families, harms innocent children, and leaves lasting pain in our neighborhoods.  For America to be strong, children must feel safe in their homes, spouses and significant others must be respected, and it must be a universal truth that violence in any form is unacceptable.  This National Domestic Violence Awareness Month, my Administration reaffirms its commitment to permanently ending domestic violence and ensuring every American can live free from the threat of violence.

For as many as 10 million Americans, the unrelenting fear of violence in our homes, places of work, and other social settings make it impossible to enjoy life and foster healthy relationships.  Behind every one of these numbers is a fellow American robbed of the safety and peace that our Nation promises to all its people.  As President, I will always uplift survivors of domestic violence and make certain that shelters, counselors, advocates, and hotlines have the resources they need to answer each and every call for help.  Help is always within reach, and you are never alone.  If you are in need of immediate and confidential support, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233.

This month, we commit that for our mothers, our fathers, our daughters, and our sons, we will end the scourge of domestic violence.  My Administration remains steadfast in safeguarding the safety and security of the American People, giving law enforcement the tools and resources they need to prevent domestic violence, and ensuring that those who commit acts of violence are held accountable.  Together, we will protect the innocent, defend the vulnerable, and build a safer and stronger America.

## Presidential Message on National Breast Cancer Awareness Month

Presidential Message on National Breast Cancer Awareness Month
The White House
October 2, 2026
During National Breast Cancer Awareness Month, we celebrate the resilience and courage of our Nation’s four million breast cancer survivors, extend our unwavering support to those currently in the fight, and honor the memory of beloved family members and friends lost to this terrible disease.

In an instant, a breast cancer diagnosis forever changes the life of the patient and the lives of those around them.  This year in the United States, more than 380,000 women will receive this devastating news, and approximately 40,000 souls will be lost to breast cancer.  Thanks to medical breakthroughs, increased public awareness, and early detection, survival rates have drastically improved—but these numbers represent shattered lives and families, and that is unacceptable.

Through our historic mission to Make America Healthy Again, my Administration is committed to conquering chronic illness and advancing cutting-edge research, treatment options, and innovative therapies that will enhance the quality of life for men and women battling breast cancer and other diseases.  Under my leadership, we will never stop working to deliver the breakthroughs, treatments, and cures that American patients and families deserve.

Breast cancer has caused immeasurable pain, fear, and uncertainty on too many American families, and throughout this month, we stand in compassionate solidarity with all cancer warriors.  The First Lady and I encourage every person to know their family medical history and risk factors and to become a fierce advocate for their own health.  May God comfort those who mourn, give strength to those suffering, and offer grace to the caregivers as we anticipate, with hope and confidence, the day this horrific disease is defeated once and for all.
