## First Lady Melania Trump and Madame Peng Liyuan Visit Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art Highlighting Sino-American Artwork, Culture, and the Next Generation

First Lady Melania Trump and Madame Peng Liyuan visited the Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art in Washington, DC today, where they explored works spanning centuries of artistic achievement and cultural exchange and shared a special moment with students from the Washington, DC area.

Upon arrival, the First Lady and Madame Peng were welcomed by leadership of the Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art before beginning their tour of the museum’s collections.

The First Lady and Madame Peng then viewed the Smithsonian’s celebrated Peacock Room, one of the museum’s most distinctive and immersive works of art.

Created by American artist James McNeill Whistler in 1876–77, the Peacock Room transforms an entire interior into a dazzling composition of blue, green, and gold. Inspired in significant part by Chinese porcelain and Japanese design, Whistler covered the room with shimmering patterns and fanciful depictions of peacocks, creating a singular work in which painting, architecture, and decorative arts converge. Purchased by American collector Charles Lang Freer in 1904, the room was transported from London to the United States and has been a centerpiece of the museum in Washington since 1923.

Today, the Peacock Room stands as a remarkable meeting point of American art and Asian artistic traditions, reflecting the ways artists and collectors have found inspiration across cultures and generations.

The First Lady and Madame Peng continued their visit with a viewing of a Qianlong-period Chinese porcelain vase, dating to the 18th century. Produced in Jingdezhen during the Qing dynasty, the vase features elaborate floral decoration executed in colorful famille rose enamels, gold, and iron red over a pale glaze. The work is part of the Freer Gallery of Art’s collection and reflects the extraordinary technical and decorative sophistication of imperial Chinese porcelain during the Qianlong era.

Museum leadership who served as guides for the visit were Jan Stuart, Melvin R. Seiden Curator of Chinese Art at the National Museum of Asian Art, and Chase Robinson, Dame Jillian Sackler Director of the Arthur M. Sackler Gallery and Freer Gallery of Art.

The visit concluded with a musical performance by the youth choir from Hope Chinese School, who performed “Jasmine Flower” and “Songs & Smiles” for the First Lady and Madame Peng. The young students’ performance offered a joyful and memorable conclusion to the visit, bringing together the beauty of artistic expression, the appreciation of cultural heritage, and the shared experience of young people discovering the enduring power of art and music.

## Presidential Message on the Beatification of Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen

Presidential Message on the Beatification of Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen
The White House
September 24, 2026
Melania and I join all those celebrating the life and legacy of Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen—the Catholic priest who pioneered the use of media to speak the truth, preach the Gospel, and bring many souls closer to Christ.  Today, Archbishop Sheen will become the first-ever American-born bishop to be beatified by the Catholic Church.

Born on the plains of central Illinois at the twilight of the nineteenth century, Fulton Sheen was blessed with extraordinary intelligence, good humor, and eloquence.  After his ordination to the priesthood, he spent more than two decades teaching, lecturing, and broadcasting on the radio before he was consecrated Auxiliary Bishop of New York in 1951.  With a growing reputation as a world-class preacher and evangelist, he then launched his renowned national television series, Life Is Worth Living, which soon captivated the entire country.

On his show, Archbishop Sheen lectured before a live audience on an array of theological, philosophical, and political topics.  Dressed in striking priestly robes and armed with only a chalk stick and blackboard, he quickly emerged as a primetime sensation—boldly bringing the teachings of Christianity to a world threatened by the titanic forces of communism.

Archbishop Sheen shocked the entertainment industry by besting American legends like Milton Berle and Frank Sinatra in weekly ratings, winning an Emmy award for Most Outstanding Television Personality, and appearing on the cover of Time magazine.  For years, his sharp wit and piercing intellect filled millions of American living rooms and converted thousands—including national politicians, Hollywood celebrities, and communist leaders—to the Catholic faith.  His program attracted an estimated 30 million weekly viewers, ultimately becoming one of the most watched religious programs in the history of television.  Through it all, Archbishop Sheen used his platform not for his own sake but to strengthen our Nation with the good news of the Gospel.

In the 1960s, against the rising tide of anti-God extremism in American culture, Archbishop Sheen turned to the camera to identify a “new type of violence that’s sweeping our country, our schools, and our streets.”  This violence, he warned in his later career The Fulton Sheen Program, “involves destruction of everything that is in the past.”  He continued, “Our Nation is too full of those that are crying, “Down… Down with schools.  Down with the churches.  Down with teachers.  Down with government.  Down with the police.  Can you build anything down?” he implored the Nation.  “You cannot… Let’s begin now to use the word ‘Up.’  Up from all of this filth.  Up from this violence… Up—up to God.”

Sheen was later appointed as the Bishop of Rochester, New York, and the titular Archbishop of Newport, Wales.  In October 1979, Pope John Paul II, the leader of the Roman Catholic Church, embraced Archbishop Sheen in Saint Patrick’s Cathedral in New York City, telling him, “You have written and spoken well of the Lord Jesus.  You are a loyal son of the Church.”  Archbishop Sheen died just two months later while praying in his private chapel.

Archbishop Fulton Sheen’s legacy endures in homes, churches, and communities all across our country.  Countless Americans still watch recordings of his broadcasts and read his books.  The Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen Museum in his native Illinois attracts thousands of visitors every year.  And today, as thousands of Americans gather in St. Louis, Missouri, for his Beatification Mass, we pray that Archbishop Sheen’s heroic commitment to nourishing the soul of our country will inspire others to follow his example and lift our Nation upward and ever higher to God.

## First Lady Melania Trump Releases Details on the State Dinner with His Excellency Xi Jinping, President of The People’s Republic of China, and Madame Peng Liyuan

President Donald J. Trump and First Lady Melania Trump will welcome His Excellency Xi Jinping, President of The People’s Republic of China, and Madame Peng Liyuan for a State Dinner in the East Room of the White House this evening. The First Lady led the preparations for the dinner, which will be attended by the official delegations as well as business leaders and government officials.

The menu showcases American ingredients with subtle Chinese influence, bringing the culinary traditions of both nations together. The meal begins with a toast of Schramsberg “Blanc De Noir,” honoring the historic “Toast to Peace” shared by President Nixon and Premier Zhou Enlai in Beijing in 1972. The same American sparkling wine served at that historic gathering continues to be enjoyed at White House functions today.

First Course
Yellow Squash Velouté
Wild Mushroom FricasséeCrispy Pancetta
Scallion Oil

The first course offers a refined and silky-smooth velouté made of sweet yellow squash. This course is gently blended to highlight its delicate seasonal flavors of earthy wild mushrooms, sautéed with fresh herbs. Together, these ingredients create a rich fricassee, complemented by crispy pancetta for a savory and smoky contrast. A finishing drizzle of scallion oil adds vibrance and a subtle allium brightness to the dish.

Second Course
Sesame Crusted Sea Bass
Braised Baby Bok ChoyRoasted Garden Eggplant
Sauce Verte

The second course is prepared delicately with fresh seabass and coated in toasted sesame seeds. This combination creates a fragrant nutty crust while keeping the fish tender. The fish is served with crisp baby bok choy and roasted garden eggplant, creating lightly caramelized flavors. The dish is finished with a vibrant sauce verte of fresh herbs, which add brightness, acidity, and a garden character.

Dessert
Vanilla Crémeux & White House Honey Ice Cream
Walnut FrangipaneSour Cherry Confit The dessert course is a smooth vanilla crémeux with a vibrant sour cherry confit center, set a top a rich walnut frangipane. The dessert’s fruit shaped form is complimented by a delicate ice cream made with harvested White House honey.

There will be several performances during the formal dinner, which will be headlined by Mr. Christopher Macchio, a classically trained tenor who performed at the 2025 Presidential Inauguration. The evening’s entertainment will feature performances by military musicians from the United States Marines Corps and the United States Army, including “The President’s Own” United States Marine Band, “The Commandant’s Own” United States Marine Drum and Bugle Corps, the United States Army Chorus, and the United States Army Strings.

The décor for the evening is an expression of warmth and celebration and draws on the color red’s longstanding cultural significance throughout Chinese history. Urns showcase overflowing red blossoms. Tables are dressed in rich red linens and adorned with dahlias and ranunculus. Among the seasonal blooms, the tables feature vermeil from the White House collection. The Reagan State Service is featured on the tables. These details blend Chinese artistry and American tradition together, showcased within the timeless elegance of the White House.
