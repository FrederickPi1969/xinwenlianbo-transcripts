## Trump names AI czar to head White House task force

https://p.dw.com/p/5NjSQ Jay Clayton has been tapped to lead the new task forceImage: Nathan Posner/Anadolu/picture allianceAdvertisementUS President Donald Trump on Sunday announced a White House task force to coordinate the administration's response to artificial intelligence (AI).

The group will be led by director of national intelligence Jay Clayton, chairman of the Federal Trade Commission Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon chief technology officer Emil Michael and director of the Office of Personnel Management Scott Kupor.

The task force, named the "Super Intelligence Force" or SI, in accordance with the term that the president prefers over AI.

"The Super Intelligence Force will coordinate the federal government’s engagement with consumers, public interest groups, religious organizations, critical infrastructure providers, and super intelligence companies," Trump said in Truth Social post on Sunday.

Trump in September said he would create a new AI force to oversee the development of the fast-moving technology, but has so far opposed any strict regulation that codifies pillars of AI safety into law.

"We will not in any way hinder or stifle the growth of this incredible industry. Rather, we will cherish it, help it, and watch over it, as it grows!" Trump said in the September post where he announced he was going to choose an AI czar.

Leading up to the midterm elections in November, Trump has been under pressure to address concerns about AI risks. AI researchers and tech CEOs have sounded the alarm about the existential threat that the technology can pose.

Anthropic's chief executive Dario Amodei, for example, published an essay last month in which he called for a global slowdown in the race to develop AI. His call was met with agreement from OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and SpaceX CEO  Elon Musk.

The US president hosted a White House lunch for nearly 20 tech titans at the end of September, where he said there was an agreement in the room that there should be "tremendous self-regulation" by tech companies.

The tech leaders signed onto a one-page document that relies on companies to voluntarily follow through on their safety commitments. But the accord is largely symbolic since it is not an official order.

## Torrential rain and flooding leave 2 dead and 1 missing in Spain&apos;s Catalonia region

https://p.dw.com/p/5NjCgPeople watch the swollen Besos River after it exceeded its danger level following torrential rain near BarcelonaImage: Kike Rincón/Europa Press/abaca/picture allianceAdvertisementTwo people were reported dead on Sunday, with at least one person missing, after torrential rain triggered flooding in Spain's northeastern Catalonia region.

Authorities have warned of an "extreme danger" in parts of Barcelona and Girona, with rivers at risk of overflowing and residents in some areas urged to stay indoors.

One person died when a wall collapsed at a campsite in Tordera, north of Barcelona, while another drowned in a flooded garage in Montornes del Valles, Catalan firefighters said.

Emergency services were also searching for a missing person in Pineda de Mar, about 52 kilometers (32 miles) north of Barcelona.

Catalan regional leader Salvador Illa urged residents to exercise "the greatest caution" and follow instructions from emergency services.

More than 100 millimeters (3.9 inches) of rain fell overnight in numerous locations, according to Spain's AEMET weather agency.

Civil protection authorities sent alerts to people living near the Besos River warning of a risk of it bursting its banks. Residents were told to move to upper floors if flooding occurred.

Homes were flooded and vehicles submerged in towns near Barcelona, while local rail services were disrupted.

Emergency crews rescued people trapped in vehicles on flooded streets in Granollers and other communities.

Flights at Barcelona airport were also canceled or delayed, while shops closed early and sporting and cultural events were called off.

The storms are linked to a weather phenomenon known in Spain as a DANA, traditionally called a "gota fria," or "cold drop."

It occurs when cold air high in the atmosphere meets warm, moisture-laden air rising from the Mediterranean, producing intense thunderstorms.

Spain's geography can amplify the effect, as moist air from the Mediterranean is forced upwards by mountain ranges separating the coast from the country's elevated interior.

The latest flooding comes almost two years after catastrophic floods linked to a DANA killed 238 people in eastern Spain, mostly in Valencia, in October 2024.

A study published in 2026 found that human-caused climate change intensified the rainfall during that disaster.

The findings apply only to the 2024 Valencia floods, and a separate analysis would be needed to determine the influence of climate change on the latest flooding in Catalonia.

## Voting in Brazil&apos;s 2026 presidential election has begun, with polls showing Lula and Bolsonaro nearly tied

https://p.dw.com/p/5NiO9Supporters of Lula da Silva lined the main avenue in Sao Paulo for a final rally on SaturdayImage: Andre Penner/AP Photo/picture allianceAdvertisementVoting began in Brazil's presidential election on Sunday, with leftist President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva seeking a fourth non-consecutive term and Senator Flavio Bolsonaro seeking to continue the right-wing populist movement started by his father, Jair Bolsonaro.

Polls have predicted a tight race between the two leading candidates, with neither likely to win more than 50% of the vote to avoid a runoff that would take place on October 25.

Lula and Bolsonaro wrapped up their campaigns on Saturday in Brazil's economic powerhouse state of Sao Paulo.

The 80-year-old Lula capped off his campaign with a tour through the city of Sao Paulo, vowing to "defeat the far right."

Organizers said some 150,000 of Lula's supporters turned up for the rally in Brazil's largest city.

Lula has campaigned on continuing his left-leaning Workers' Party platform, comprising welfare programs, state-funded development and income redistribution.

"The people will have to choose between strengthening the democratic regime and barbarism ... between progress and backwardness," he said as he cast his ballot on Sunday.

Flavio Bolsonaro, 45, has served as the Liberal Party's senator for Rio de Janeiro since 2019. On Saturday, he led a motorcycle parade through Sao Paulo's countryside cities of Santa Barbara d'Oeste and Americana.

He also held up a portrait of his father, while the crowd shouted "mito" — a Portuguese word for "legend" that Jair Bolsonaro's supporters use to refer to him.

The right-wing Bolsonaro has campaigned on hardline security policies and tax cuts, emphasizing high cost of living under Lula, who he has slammed as "incompetent."

"Brazil needs change," the son of the former president said as he cast his vote. "Brazil cannot bear any more suffering, such as high taxes, so many women killed."

The elder Bolsonaro is currently serving a 27-year sentence in prison for attempting a coup to overturn his loss to Lula in the 2022 presidential election. Flavio Bolsonaro has vowed to pardon his father if elected.

Lula's team has cast the Bolsonaro movement as a threat to Brazil's sovereignty, pointing to Flavio and Jair's ties with US President Donald Trump, who last year slapped a 50% tariff on Brazilian imports in response to Jair Bolsonaro's coup trial.  The US president called the case a "witch hunt."

The younger Bolsonaro has said in interviews he is "more moderate" than his father, who he has nevertheless said will remain "my compass, my point of reference."

Policies where both Bolsonaros are similar include relaxed gun control laws and skepticism of human-caused climate change.

Trump has not endorsed the senator, but has said he is watching the election "very closely."

Brazil finds itself politically divided, and campaigning has been bitter, with online operations from both sides seizing on every gaffe, accusation and rumor, quickly producing slick videos or AI-generated images for social media.

In September, it was revealed Bolsonaro is under investigation for alleged corruption, money laundering and tax evasion tied to the financing of a biopic about his father. He has denied any misconduct.

## Japan: US Marine arrested in Okinawa on suspicion of murder

https://p.dw.com/p/5NjCdThe US Marine Corps confirmed that a serviceman based at Marine Corps Air Station at Futenma, Okinawa, was in Japanese custodyImage: Chermaine Lee/DWAdvertisementA United States Marine was arrested on suspicion of murder in Okinawa, Japan, police said Sunday, prompting an official protest from the Japanese government.

The 20-year-old lance corporal is suspected of robbing and murdering a 39-year-old woman in a hotel in Naha, the capital of Japan's most southernmost prefecture.

Japanese public broadcaster NHK reported that CCTV footage showed a foreign man entering a hotel room with a woman and later leaving with her belongings.

Police then found a 20-year-old man who matched the description of the suspect seen in the footage and who voluntarily accompanied them to a local police station where he was arrested.

"The occurrence of ​such an extremely brutal and vicious incident is extremely regrettable," said Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi ​in a statement as Tokyo registered an official protest with Washington.

"The government of Japan strongly protests to the US side and strongly seeks maximum cooperation with the investigation," she continued, also calling for "stricter discipline among US military personnel" and "thorough measures" to prevent a recurrence.

According to the local Asahi Shimbum newspaper, the brother of the victim said: "We have lost a family member and are deeply saddened. My parents, in particular, are utterly devastated at the loss of their daughter."

The US Marine Corps confirmed that a serviceman assigned to the III Marine Expeditionary Force and based at Marine Corps Air Station at Futenma, Okinawa, was in Japanese custody.

"The Marine Corps takes these allegations very seriously and expects every Marine to adhere to the ⁠highest ​standards of behavior," it said in a statement.

The US Ambassador to Japan, George Glass, said he had spoken with Japan's Foreign Ministry and Okinawa's governor, offering "sincere condolences and pledging full cooperation as the investigation continues."

The United States has around 54,000 military personnel, including 18,000 Marines, stationed in Japan. Most are based in Okinawa, which the US captured during the Second World War and held until 1972.

Located only 100 kilometers (60 miles) from Taiwan at the closest point, Okinawa is considered by both Tokyo and Washington to be key in deterring potential aggression from China in the region.

But the US presence regularly stirs resentment among local residents over issues such as noise, aircraft crashes and the conduct of personnel, with crimes including rapes, assaults, hit-and-run incidents and drunk-driving accidents.

"Incidents and accidents involving US military personnel cause great anxiety among the local community and must not be allowed to happen," said Japanese Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi.

According to the Yomiuri Shimbun newspaper, around 6,000 US military personnel have been arrested in Okinawa since 1972. In 2024, that number hit 80, the highest in 20 years.

Last year, a Japanese court sentenced a US Marine to seven years in prison after finding him guilty of choking and attempting to rape a young woman.

In 2017, a former US Marine and civilian military base worker was sentenced to life in prison for the rape and murder of a local woman in Okinawa.

## Bosnia-Herzegovina votes amid divisive campaigns, EU bid

https://p.dw.com/p/5Nim0Preliminary results are expected to be available late Sunday after polls closeImage: Armin Durgut/AP Photo/picture allianceAdvertisementPolling began in Bosnia and Herzegovina on Sunday, with around 3.4 million voters eligible to take part in the Balkan country's general election.

Citizens are electing officials across multiple tiers of government, including members of the multiethnic, tripartite presidency.

They will also choose lawmakers for the federal parliament and the heads of institutions for the country's two largely autonomous entities —  the Muslim-Croat Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and the Serb-run Republika Srpska.

The election comes at a time when the country is struggling with a number of challenges. There's been an economic slowdown, sluggish progress toward EU membership, and a long-drawn-out political gridlock, with the ethnically based dominant parties from the two entities repeatedly stalling decisions by the weak central government that links them.

The election will see adversary ethnic parties, the Bosniak ​SDA, Serb SNSD and Croat HDZ BiH, pitted against multi-ethnic opposition coalitions led by the Social Democratic Party.

Polls close at 7 p.m. local time (1700 GMT), with preliminary results expected to be released late Sunday.

The economic, administrative and political responsibilities in Bosnia and Herzegovina are divided between the multiethnic central government, the self-governing neutral Brcko district and the two semi-autonomous entities.

The complicated framework — divided along ethnic lines — was set up during the US-brokered 1995 Dayton Peace Accords, following the 1992-95 Bosnian war which was triggered by the breakup of Yugoslavia.

Currently in Bosnia and Herzegovina's populace, around half are Muslim Bosniaks, while 31% are Orthodox Serb and 15% are Catholic Croat.

The country has been peaceful in recent years but its ethnonationalist parties have kept the divisions alive even as voters say they want to move on from the war-era rhetoric and division.

In Republika Srpska, secessionist leader ​Milorad Dodik of the SNSD party remains the key figure despite not being on the ballot.

Dodik, the former president of the Serb-majority entity, is barred from holding public office for six years after being convicted of defying the top international envoy overseeing the 1995 peace process.

Dodik wants close ‌ties with Serbia and Russia. He has just returned from Moscow where he met Russian President Vladimir Putin.

"Bosnia and Herzegovina is dead, it is an impossible country," Dodik told supporters at the final rally of his in Banja Luka on Friday.

In the Muslim-Croat federation, Bosniak parties are advocating for a stronger central state.

The main Bosniak SDA party is focused on efforts to keep the country united against Serb separatism.

Bosnian Croats are also largely backing their ethnic parties, including the Bosnian branch of Croatia's ruling conservative Croatian Democratic Union.

Meanwhile, Denis Becirovic, the incumbent Bosniak presidency member, has vowed a renewed path to the EU and NATO.

But the actual start of membership talks depends on the prior implementation of numerous reforms, including strengthening judicial oversight and passing anti-corruption ​laws.

## Latvia election: Pro-Ukraine PM Kulbergs set to retain power after historic victory for his United List party

https://p.dw.com/p/5NgfdCentrist Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs (right) is on track for a strong win in the electionsImage: Wojtek Radwanski/AFPAdvertisementLatvian Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs appeared set to remain in power on Sunday after his centrist United List secured 35.3% of the vote in the country's parliamentary election.

The result gives United List 42 of 100 seats and puts Kulbergs in a strong position to form another pro-Ukraine coalition.

With 97% of ballots counted, United List had secured the country's biggest parliamentary election victory since Latvia regained independence from the Soviet Union in 1991.

The party could secure a parliamentary majority with either the nationalist National Alliance or the left-leaning Progressives, political scientist Roberts Kits said.

Kulbergs, a 47-year-old businessman and amateur rally driver, only came to power in May after the previous coalition collapsed over its handling of incidents involving stray Ukrainian military drones.

An exit poll put the the populist Latvia First party in second place with 11.3% — nearly double its share at the last election in 2022. It had courted the country's substantial ethnic Russian minority.

Kulbergs said on Saturday night that he would not cooperate with any pro-Russian parties and specifically ruled out forming a coaltition with Latvia First.

Some 1.5 million people were eligible to vote in the country's 11th parliamentary election since independence in 1991.

The election, which saw 100 seats in the Saeima being contested by 14 parties using a proportional system, was dominated by rising living costs and national security concerns in the small Baltic country which borders both Russia and Belarus.

Russia's ongoing invasion of Ukraine is seen as a direct threat to national security in Latvia which, like Ukraine, is a former Soviet state with a sizeable ethnic Russian and Russian-speaking minority.

Riga is one of Kyiv's closest partners and supporters, having committed itself by law to spending at least 5% of GDP on defense – leaving less room for spending elsewhere.

Many Latvians complain of high prices and low wages, the economy is sluggish, the health system is under strain, and emigration and an ageing population remain persistent challenges.

Populist movements such as the Sovereign Power/Young Latvians Alliance and the Latvia First party have attempted to exploit discontent by taking a less stringent line towards Moscow and arguing that a complete economic decoupling from Russia is damaging.

Nevertheless, no parties proposed any major deviation from Latvia's clear foreign policy alignment with the EU and NATO.

Indeed, four German Eurofighter jets were deployed to protect Latvian airspace during the election in response to a request from Riga.
