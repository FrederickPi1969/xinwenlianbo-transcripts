09/04/2026September 4, 2026'Suspicious objects' found near substation in Lower Saxony: reports In the northwestern region of Lower Saxony, police are investigating "suspicious objects" found in the pre-dawn hours Friday morning at a power substation near the small town of Ganderkesee outside of Bremen, the Bild tabloid reported.

"A discovery was made that required a police investigation," a police spokeswoman was quoted by the newspaper as saying. A helicopter had been called in to illuminate the area from above.

09/04/2026September 4, 2026Police suspect another sabotage attempt near Dormagen substationPolice gather evidence near a power substation in Dormagen early Friday Image: Alexander Franz/dpa/picture alliancePolice in western Germany say a suspicious device found at an electricity substation in the small city of Dormagen between Cologne and Düsseldorf was likely intended to "cause damage."

"Someone apparently tried to deliberately cause a short circuit," a Cologne police spokesperson told AFP news agency.

The object was discovered late Thursday by a passer-by. A police spokesperson said it may have been a "suspicious launching device."

The Bild tabloid reported that homemade rockets with launching devices, including timers and cooper wires, were discovered. Police have not confirmed the report.

The new discovery Friday is the latest this week in what police say are attempts at damaging critical infrastructure.

The first incident on Tuesday near the Jänschwalde coal power plant in the eastern state of Brandenburg involved homemade projectiles resembling New Year's Eve fireworks that can short out high-voltage power lines by shooting a grounding wire over them.

A day later, similar objects were found near a power station in Bergheim, in the western state of North Rhine-Westphalia.

## Argentina: Milei reignites dispute over Falkland Islands

https://p.dw.com/p/5L2ZmThe Falkland Islands have been the subject of a dispute between Britain and Argentina [FILE: January 2009]Image: Dreamstime/IMAGOAdvertisementArgentine President Javier Milei said the United States' position on the Falkland Islands could be changing.

He added that his country would impose sanctions on any oil companies operating in the British overseas territory.

In a nationally televised speech on Thursday, Milei reiterated Argentina's stance that the Falkland Islands are part of its territory. This move could further escalate the long-standing dispute with Britain over the islands.

The Argentine leader also praised US President Donald Trump's comments regarding the US potentially revisiting its largely neutral position on the Falklands, saying this is a result of his close relationship with Trump.

According to Milei, the US is considering this change in position due to Argentina being a "reliable partner, aligned with Western values" that can be "a valuable ally in the decades to come."

Milei announced several moves aimed at stopping the Sea Lion oil project, which is located about 140 miles from the islands — an area that Argentina considers its own. Two companies, one from the UK and one from Israel, plan to begin drilling in the area in the coming months with the goal of producing oil by 2028.

"Argentina will not stand idly by," Milei said, adding that any further advance on the Islands will be viewed as a "violation of our national security."

The Falklands issue is a point of consensus in an Argentine society divided by political differences in other areas.

Meanwhile, British Prime Minister Andy Burnham's spokesperson said the British government's position was "long-standing and unwavering."

"Sovereignty rests with the UK, and the islanders' right to self-determination is paramount,” he said.

Any change in the US' neutral approach towards the dispute between Britain and Argentina would be a major shift and could contribute to further deterioration in already strained transatlantic relations.

Asked about whether his administration could change its position on the Falkland Islands, Trump said he "always" reviews positions.

In another interview, Trump was asked whether the US would come to help Britain should it be involved in an armed conflict over the islands.

"Your country was not there to help me," he answered, referring to Britain's refusal to take an active part in the joint US-Israeli campaign against Iran.

## FIFA accuses UEFA of &apos;smear campaign&apos; against Infantino

https://p.dw.com/p/5L2TpUEFA President Aleksander Ceferin (left) and FIFA President Gianni Infantino have been at odds [FILE: June 2024]Image: Nick Potts/PA Images/IMAGOAdvertisementWorld football governing body FIFA took a lash at Europe's UEFA for starting a "smear campaign" and "fishing expedition" against FIFA and its President Gianni Infantino.

This comes after a dispute between the two organizations due to FIFA's plan to sell a stake in its commercial rights, with UEFA submitting an ex parte application to the US District Court in New York to obtain testimony and documents from US-based entities. These documents will be used in potential criminal proceedings in Switzerland, where both FIFA and UEFA are based.

"While these applications are styled as legal pleadings with captions and docket numbers, they are little more than press releases in further support of UEFA’s smear campaign against FIFA and its leadership,” FIFA said in a filing in US District Court in Colorado on Thursday.

FIFA's filing in Colorado was a motion to intervene, requesting that the discovery process be postponed or dismissed.

According to UEFA, the FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE), an entity which would have had the commercial rights linked over the FIFA World Cup to investors was developed without adequate consulting FIFA's 211 member associations, as well as FIFA's own governing bodies.

FIFA argued that the association representing European football had tried to scupper the proposal with a bid to keep European football's dominance as is.

Pressure has been mounting on both FIFA and its President Gianni Infantino since the FFE plans were made public.

This resulted in several countries saying they will not support Infantino's reelection for a fourth term as president in March 2027, when the Swiss-born FIFA boss looks for a fourth term in office.

Both CONCACAF, representing North and Central America and the Caribbean, and the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) voiced their objections to Infantino. Only the South American and African confederations still support him.

UEFA also demanded a "genuinely independent external review" of how the proposal came about.

"FIFA cannot investigate itself and expect the football community to simply accept its conclusions," UEFA said.

## Volkswagen to cut 100,000 jobs by end of decade

https://p.dw.com/p/5L2U7Volkswagen's Zwickau plant is expected to be affected by the cuts [FILE: June 2026]Image: Hendrik Schmidt/dpa/picture allianceAdvertisementGerman automobile giant Volkswagen announced on Thursday that it would be cutting a total of 100,000 jobs by the end of the decade in what is set to be the biggest restructuring ever seen in the global car industry.

The company said that management and trade unions had approved a plan involving the reduction of a further 50,000 jobs, in addition to 50,000 redundancies already agreed.

"It is essential to systematically align workforce levels with economic realities," read a statement from the multi-brand Volkswagen group which, apart from VW itself, also includes Audi and Porsche.

The total of 100,000 cuts amount to about 15% of Volkswagen staff worldwide. The cull eclipses the 50,000 job cuts General Motors made after it declared bankruptcy in 2009.

Unions and management had publicly rowed before signing off on the plans, with the former accusing the latter of not being honest with the workforce after the figure of 100,000 possible job cuts surfaced in the media before being communicated internally.

What's more, Volkswagen also said the long-term future of four major German plants – in Hannover, Emden, Zwickau and Neckarsulm — could not be guaranteed. Their closure would mark the first full-scale shuttering of Volkswagen factories in its home country.

"If this plant really were shut down, you could put a big black spot on the map," one long-term employee in Zwickau, Saxony, told the AFP news agency.

"The plant and the jobs outside it, our suppliers, they're the motor of the entire region."

Caught between US tariffs, Chinese competition and a sluggish growth in demand for electric vehicles, Europe's largest carmaker is in trouble.

However, CEO Oliver Blume insisted that the decision to cut 100,000 jobs sent "a strong signal for the future of the Volkswagen Group."

Volkswagen did not give details as to the timing of the cuts and how they would be distributed across the different regions where it has plants and offices, but the company said its focus will turn to North America and that it is looking to expand exports to the Global South.

The plan also includes a three-digit-billion sum investment over the coming years as VW looks to improve technologically through research and development and become more competitive.

Among the moves is a structural change that should bring about faster decision-making and a cut to the number of businesses and holdings it owns by about one-third.
