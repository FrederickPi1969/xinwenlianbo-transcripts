## Palestinian president postpones elections until late 2027

https://p.dw.com/p/5O7Q3Mahmoud Abbas reiterated his pledge to hold elections in a video address the UN General Assembly last month [FILE: May 2026]Image: Thaer Ganaim/APA Images/SIPA/picture allianceAdvertisementPalestinian President Mahmoud Abbas has postponed legislative elections for almost another year, according to a decree issued on Friday.

Abbas had previously scheduled legislative elections for November 28, and presidential elections for early next year. Now both elections have been pushed back to September 11, 2027.

In his decree, Abbas cited "obstacles ⁠posed by Israeli measures" as the reason for the postponement of "free, fair and inclusive elections."

He pointed to Israeli settler violence, military attacks and restrictions of movent in the occupied West Bank and East Jerusalem, as well as Israel's continued withholding of tax revenue.

In Gaza, where Israeli strikes continue after three years of war, the decree cited "exceptional security and humanitarian conditions," the incomplete withdrawal of Israeli forces and a lack of progress towards reconstruction, among other issues.

Israel's Foreign Minister Gideon Saar responded to the news on X, calling the decree "yet another ridiculous lie designed to evade responsibility and accountability."

Abbas had committed in writing to French President Emmanuel Macron and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman that elections would be held as part of France's recognition of the State of Palestine last year.

The delay comes just weeks after Abbas reaffirmed this commitment to hold elections in November when he delivered a video address to the UN General Assembly.

## Saudi Arabia: 12 killed, hundreds injured in Riyadh airport attack

https://p.dw.com/p/5O76UMajor airlines have suspended operations in Riyadh amid security threats [FILE: February 15, 2023]Image: Markus Mainka/picture allianceAdvertisementAt least 12 people were killed and hundreds were injured in an attack on Riyadh airport in Saudi Arabia on Saturday.

"King Khalid International Airport was targeted by an attack, amid continued attacks targeting civilian aviation facilities in the kingdom," the Saudi General Authority of Civil Aviation said in an initial statement.

Hours later, the authority announced that 12 people had been killed, including four Saudis, two Bangladeshis, one American, one Egyptian, one Jordanian, one Syrian, one Palestinian and one Sudanese national.

At least 309 others were injured in the attack, the aviation authority said, adding that "all injured individuals are receiving the necessary medical care at several hospitals."

The Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis in Yemen also announced that "a projectile" had also landed near King Fahd International Airport in the eastern city of Dammam.

On Sunday, the Saudi-led military coalition supporting Yemen's internationally recognized government accused the Houthis of carrying out the deadly attack on Riyadh's international airport. The coalition vowed to respond "decisively."

In a statement, coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki described the attack as a "war crime" and vowed the coalition "will respond decisively to this terrorist attack."

Meanwhile, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio ‌said that ​the United States ​strongly condemns recent ​attacks ‌on ‌Saudi Arabia, confirming that ​an American citizen was killed ​in the strikes.

"These reprehensible ⁠attacks ​demonstrate ​a blatant ​disregard for innocent ‌civilian life. ​They ⁠must stop now," ⁠Rubio ​wrote on X.

A Saudi official, speaking on the condition of anonymity, told the Associated Press that many people were wounded after a missile hit a passenger terminal.

The AFP news news agency also reported that a number of people were injured, citing a local medical source who also spoke on the condition of anonymity.

"ER doctors are being called to hospitals across Riyadh," the source told AFP. "We were told we are dealing with a 'serious emergency situation'. Injured are being moved to hospitals across Riyadh."

Several Western embassies in Riyadh, including those of Germany, the United States, Canada and Spain, urged citizens to avoid the airport.

The US embassy said it was "aware of attacks" at the airport. Spain's embassy referred to a "new attack" by the Houthi rebels in Yemen while Canada's embassy warned of "ongoing military activity."

However, after the attack, Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree retweeted his own post from a day earlier in which he said that only pre-approved humanitarian flights would be allowed to land at Riyadh airport.

When asked about the attack, US President Donald Trump said the US would soon decide whether to join Saudi strikes against the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen.

"We may. We're going to look at it. We just found out about the recent attack, so we'll make a decision," Trump told reporters at the White House. "We move very quickly."

The Iran-backed Houthis have been launching strikes on targets in Saudi Arabia, including several this week on the airport in Riyadh that killed four people, injured several others, and damaged planes.

Etihad Airways and Qatar Airways canceled multiple flights between their ​respective hubs and Riyadh, with some cancellations extending into Monday. Kuwait Airways also cancelled its flights on Saturday.

Last month, Houthi rebels made advances on important strategic locations along Yemen's Red Sea coast, with a Saudi-led coalition providing air support for Yemeni government forces seeking to take back those gains.

The Houthis have responded by launching attacks into Saudi Arabia, which represents a significant escalation in their multi-year conflict with Yemen's Riyadh-backed government after a peace deal unraveled in July.

The Saudi-backed government in Yemen last week announced a counter-offensive aimed at retaking all areas held by the Houthis, especially those around the Bab al-Mandab strait.
