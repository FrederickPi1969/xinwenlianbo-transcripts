## German youth plan France-style mass protests

https://p.dw.com/p/5OWfVA person carrying a placard that says "I want to live, not just survive" [FILE: December 2023]Image: Ying Tang/NurPhoto/picture allianceAdvertisement"Gen Z regelt" — or Gen Z's Got It — a youth initiative, has called for mass protests in 21 cities across Germany on Saturday, October 31; including Berlin, Cologne, Hamburg and Munich.

Thousands are expected to participate in demonstrations protesting "broken schools and austerity," as well as the cost of living, social injustice, military conscription and climate change.

French protests that exploded in violence and rampant destruction of private and public property this weekend also grew out of student frustration over the national school system and soon included numerous unions, groups and individuals voicing anger over a number of issues.

Gen Z regelt's website clearly states that the initiative is "against all violence," whether directed at people or property.

Police say they are "vigilant and relaxed," adding that they intend to protect people's right to congregate and express their opinions.

Still, images of massive damage and public violence from France, Italy and Spain have raised concerns among domestic policymakers and police, who have warned against allowing the protests to be hijacked by left-wing extremists.

Andreas Roßkopf, head of the German Federal Police's GdP union, made clear that officers would deal harshly with riots and rioters, telling the Rheinische Post newspaper on Wednesday, "state, but also federal police, will offer a firm and decisive response" to any trouble.

Roßkopf also warned that radicals and extremists could insert themselves among demonstrators in order to "attack police" or "simply be destructive."

## France bans protests in parts of Paris as Macron slams Trump

https://p.dw.com/p/5OWfdLocalized curfews extend into Thursday (PHOTO: October 19)Image: Kyodo/picture allianceAdvertisementAs France braces for more protests on Thursday, Paris police said participants will be banned from gathering at the Elysee presidential palace, the Bastille Square, both houses of parliament, and several tourist areas, including the Louvre and the Champs-Elysees.

French authorities have sought to reassure the public after the country was swept by violent unrest last weekend. The ongoing crisis started with students protesting high prices and demanding education reform, but it soon escalated, with masked rioters looting shops and clashing with police. On Monday, Interior Minister Laurent Nunez admitted the authorities "lost control" and failed to anticipate the scale of the violence.

Large-scale protests are also expected in Paris this weekend. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu has already pledged a "massive" security deployment for the event.

While the European nation has seen two relatively quiet nights in a row, images of burning cars, broken store windows and ransacked buildings have been used by politicians inside and outside of France to increase pressure on the current government, led by President Emmanuel Macron.

Notably, US President Donald Trump attributed the unrest to "migrants, jihadists, and communists," saying they had "conquered, looted, and burned (France) to the ground." While campaigning for the US midterm elections, Trump also said he had warned Macron about immigration, but the French president refused to close the borders.

While visiting Qatar on Wednesday, Macron fired back at Trump, implying that the US leader and possibly Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu were trying to gain political capital from the unrest in France. Netanyahu also spoke of an "invasion of extremist Islam" earlier this week.

"There are people who campaign at France's expense — leaders who are in campaign mode, whether here in the region or on the other side of the Atlantic," Macron told reporters on Wednesday.

He also said information about the crisis has been manipulated, including "AI-generated images depicting scenes that never actually happened." This, according to Macron, prompted comments that are "as unacceptable as they are stupid, from many people, including foreign leaders."

"We've had people claiming that we were invaded by hordes of migrants and Islamists, which is completely false,” Macron said.

Later on Wednesday, Macron's far-right rival Marine Le Pen sought to dismiss his account as "conspiratorial."

"The images we saw last Saturday are not generated by artificial intelligence: they show the sad reality of what France has become after 10 years of Macronist rule," said Le Pen.

The far-right leader is currently the favorite to win the 2027 presidential election in France, followed by far-left candidate Jean-Luc Melenchon.
