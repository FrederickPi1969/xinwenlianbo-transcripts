## [Press Statement] Disrupting Iran’s Pursuit of Man-Portable Air-Defense Systems — Thomas &#8220;Tommy&#8221; Pigott

Today, the Department of State is imposing sanctions on two individuals and one entity based in Belarus, Russia, and Nigeria, respectively, for their involvement in the Iranian regime’s efforts to procure man-portable air-defense systems (MANPADS).

Consistent with Operation Economic Outcast, this is the latest in a series of U.S. actions against Iran’s military procurement networks involving MANPADS.  It follows designations on May 8, June 10, and July 15, 2026.  Each round makes it harder and more costly for Tehran to arm itself and reinforces the UN restrictions restored in September 2025.

Iran’s procurement efforts threaten U.S. national security, and the United States will take all necessary action to hold the Iranian regime accountable.

The United States will continue to work with partners to implement the restored UN measures and to stop the flow of weapons that threaten U.S. forces and regional security.  Anyone who helps the regime build its arsenal should expect to be held accountable.

The Department of State’s action was taken pursuant to E.O. 13949, which targets certain persons with respect to the conventional arms activities of Iran.  For more information about today’s actions, see the Department of State’s fact sheet.

## [Fact Sheet] Disrupting Iran’s Pursuit of Man-Portable Air-Defense Systems Fact Sheet </a> <div class="collection-result-meta" dir="ltr"> <span dir="ltr">November 4, 2026</span> <!-- <span>date</span> --> </div> </li> <li class="collection-result"> <!-- add label if a report custom post in inculded in collection--> <p class="collection-result__date">Press Statement</p> <a href="https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesman/2026/11/u-s-sanctions-fraudulent-ship-registries-and-aviation-support-for-iranian-regime/" class="collection-result__link" target="_self"> U.S. Sanctions Fraudulent Ship Registries and Aviation Support for Iranian Regime — Thomas &#8220;Tommy&#8221; Pigott

Today, the Department of State is designating two individuals and one entity that have been involved in efforts to help the Iranian regime obtain man-portable air-defense systems (MANPADS).

These designations continue a sustained U.S. campaign against Iran’s military procurement networks involving MANPADS.  They follow actions on May 8, June 10, and July 15, 2026.  Together, these actions close off more of the channels Tehran uses to try to rebuild its arsenal and raise the risk for third-country brokers and suppliers.  Today’s action also supports enforcement of the UN arms embargo on Iran restored in September 2025.  Those restrictions bar UN Member States from supplying such weapons to Iran.

The United States will keep exposing the networks that arm Iran’s military and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and will keep denying the regime the resources that sustain its destabilizing activities.

All three targets are being designated pursuant to Executive Order (E.O.) 13949, which targets certain persons with respect to the conventional arms activities of Iran. Designations Pursuant to Section 1(a)(iii) of E.O. 13949

As a result of today’s sanctions actions, and in accordance with E.O. 13949, all property and interests in property of the designated persons described above that are in the United States or in possession or control of U.S. persons are blocked and must be reported to the Department of Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC).  In addition, all entities that are owned, directly or indirectly, individually or in the aggregate, 50 percent or more by one or more blocked persons are also blocked.

All transactions by U.S. persons or within (or transiting) the United States that involve any property or interests in property of designated or otherwise blocked persons are prohibited unless authorized by a general or specific license issued by OFAC or exempt.  These prohibitions include the making of any contribution or provision of funds, goods, or services by, to, or for the benefit of any blocked person and the receipt of any contribution or provision of funds, goods, or services from any such person.

The power and integrity of U.S. government sanctions derive not only from the U.S. government’s ability to designate and add persons to the Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons (SDN) List, but also from its willingness to remove persons from the SDN List consistent with the law.

Petitions for removal from the SDN List may be sent to: OFAC.Reconsideration@treasury.gov.  Petitioners may also refer to the Department of State’s Delisting Guidance page.
