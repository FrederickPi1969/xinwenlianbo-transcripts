		Secretary Rubio’s Call with Foreign Minister Gerapetritis — Marco Rubio

The text of the following statement was released by the Governments of the United States of America, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the Republic of Rwanda, the State of Qatar, the Republic of Togo (as the African Union mediator), and the African Union Commission on the occasion of the sixth Joint Security Coordination Mechanism meeting held this week in Geneva, Switzerland.

On August 12-13, 2026, representatives from the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and Rwanda (the Parties), along with the United States, Qatar, Togo (as the African Union mediator) and the African Union Commission convened the sixth meeting of the Joint Security Coordination Mechanism (JSCM) at the U.S. Mission in Geneva, Switzerland.

The DRC and Rwanda agreed to advance two initiatives to strengthen monitoring and verification of Washington Accords implementation on the ground. The parties requested that the United States consider these two initiatives and provide feedback at the next JSCM. The parties agreed to a trilateral planning conference in October 2026 that will culminate in a revised Operations Order guiding their coordinated efforts.

The African Union Commission presented on the outcomes of the African Union High-Level Meeting in Gaborone, Botswana, held July 24-25, 2026.

The DRC presented its Disarmament, Demobilization, and Reintegration (DDR) Plan for the Forces Démocratiques de Libération du Rwanda (FDLR) and responded to questions from JSCM members. Following the DRC’s presentation, the JSCM held a virtual session with the United Nations Organization Stabilization Mission in the DRC (MONUSCO) to draw on its operational experience conducting DDR in the region.

The Governments of the DRC and Rwanda expressed their appreciation to the United States, Qatar, Togo, and the African Union Commission for their support throughout the peace process and agreed to reconvene for the next JSCM on September 16 –17 in Geneva.

		Joint Statement by Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy and Secretary of State Marco Rubio: United States to Serve as Secretariat of the Geneva Consensus Declaration — Marco Rubio

At the Second Pax Silica Summit in Washington, D.C. on June 25-26, Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg announced a new Pax Silica AI Assistance Project to more efficiently and rapidly move high-value AI inputs through Panama.  The Department has published a competitive Notice of Funding Opportunity for this ambitious new platform that has the potential to transform how trusted partners transship rapidly critical goods that power the AI economy of the 21st century.  Working with Congress, the Department seeks to commit up to $50 million in foreign assistance funding to strengthen global AI supply chain security by developing an AI supply chain credentialing and provenance platform that expedites the shipping of semiconductors, AI infrastructure, critical minerals, and related products in participating Pax partner countries and economies. We are putting our resources behind our vision and demonstrating that trusted partnership is backed by real investment.

The Pax Silica AI Assistance Project would begin as a pilot, implementing an AI supply chain credentialing platform with Panama’s ports and customs authorities. If the pilot with Panama is successful, a second phase of the project would expand the geographic reach of these platforms to other Pax Silica countries and economies.

Interested bidders are encouraged to apply for this Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) at Opportunity Listing – Pax Silica Artificial Intelligence Assistance Project before the NOFO closes on August 20.  This deadline is firm and will not be extended.  Any questions from potential bidders regarding the NOFO should be directed to PaxFund-NOFO-DL@state.gov.
