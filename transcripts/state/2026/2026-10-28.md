## [Press Statement] The United States Announces Sanctions in Response to Rwanda’s Destabilizing Activities in Eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo — Thomas &#8220;Tommy&#8221; Pigott

Today, the United States is sanctioning Rwanda’s Ministry of Defense and Zigama Credit and Savings Society, a financial institution that serves Rwanda’s security services, for supporting the March 23 Movement (M23) armed group in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). Thousands of Rwanda Defense Force troops fight alongside M23 in North and South Kivu provinces and have supplied the group with weapons and training. Rwandan troops have also brought in surface-to-air missiles and jamming equipment that endanger civilian aviation.

On December 4, 2025, President Trump brought the leaders of the DRC and Rwanda together to sign the Washington Accords for Peace and Prosperity (Washington Accords). Both governments agreed to end the conflict and expand trade and investment across the Great Lakes region of Africa. Rwanda’s Ministry of Defense’s support for M23 violates those obligations and undermines the Washington Accords’ goals of peace and prosperity. Rwanda must stop supporting M23 and any successor armed groups and withdraw its forces from eastern DRC. Moreover, armed groups and their state sponsors must not profit from the DRC’s natural resources. In the gold and coltan mining areas under M23 control, including Rubaya in North Kivu, civilians face forced labor, dangerous working conditions, and illegal taxation. We urge our partners and the private sector to examine their supply chains closely. The United States calls on all actors to respect the DRC’s sovereignty and territorial integrity and will use every available tool to hold accountable those who prolong this conflict.

The Rwandan Ministry of Defense is being designated pursuant to E.O. 13413, as amended, for having materially assisted, sponsored, or provided financial, material, logistical, or technological support for, or goods or services in support of the RDF. Zigama is being designated pursuant to E.O. 13413, as amended, for having materially assisted, sponsored, or provided financial, material, logistical, or technological support for, or goods or services in support of, the RDF, M23, and AFC. For more information on today’s action, please see the Department of the Treasury’s press releases.

The U.S. Department of State congratulates the Socialist Republic of Vietnam on joining the Artemis Accords.  Space exploration is entering a new era of discovery and opportunity, with growing importance for scientific advancement, economic growth, and the technologies that will shape the future.  As the 78th signatory, Vietnam joins a growing coalition of nations dedicated to space policy that will extend the reach of human discovery and innovation using safe, transparent, and responsible space exploration and use.  Vietnam will help put these shared principles into practice as a committed partner in the peaceful, cooperative use of space.

Vietnam’s decision extends the U.S.-Vietnam Comprehensive Strategic Partnership into a new frontier.  Built on more than 30 years of cooperation, our partnership spans defense, economic, law enforcement, war legacy, and technological cooperation, and it advances peace, stability, and prosperity in a free and open Indo-Pacific.

Vietnam’s Minister of Science and Technology Vũ Hải Quân signed the Accords on behalf of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam on October 28, 2026.  U.S. Ambassador to Vietnam Jennifer Wicks witnessed and celebrated the signing at a ceremony on the margins of Vietnam International Digital Week in Ha Long, Vietnam.

The United States and seven other countries established the Artemis Accords in 2020 during President Trump’s first term to serve as a set of practical principles to guide responsible space exploration.  The U.S. Department of State and NASA lead the United States’ outreach and implementation of the Accords.

For more information, including a full list of signatories to the Accords, please visit the Artemis Accords webpage.  For media inquiries, please submit a request.
